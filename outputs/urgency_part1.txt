THE FINDING THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING ELSE (section 3)

The dispatch round's M8 trigger said that 11 of 13 detected-victim deaths were
"feasible-critical". We re-read those deaths one by one on the 64 dp0 runs.
- Two independent passes reproduced M8 and every victim's waiting record.
- One pass classified the mechanism of each death; a reviewer re-checked it.

The re-reading does NOT show that the ORDER of service cost those lives:
- In today's dispatch, none of the 13 victims who died ever waited for a single
  step while a FREE firefighter had a fire-free route to her. This was checked
  two ways: per-step samples, and a reconstruction of every re-dispatch "kick"
  at bind time.
- 10 of the 13 were bound at their detection step to the nearest unit, or to a
  unit within 4 route steps of it.
- 12 of the 13 had their unit's route closed by fire before they died.

Of the 11 FC deaths:

  | Mechanism                                                 | Deaths |
  |-----------------------------------------------------------|--------|
  | Reachable by ordering (U1)                                | 0      |
  | Reassignment of a stalled unit (the dispatch round's      | 1      |
  |   Limit 3)                                                |        |
  | Movement layer: greedy-mover loops while a fire-free path | 8      |
  |   existed, the survival retreat before pickup, one        |        |
  |   carrying-leg livelock (one of the 8 possibly futile)    |        |
  | Futile in practice                                        | 2      |

M8 counts BUSY units and uses a lower-bound route measured at detection. It
measures "reachable in principle", not "lost to dispatch order".

How often today's dispatch faced a scarce choice:
- 14 of its 285 binds in 64 runs had more waiting victims than free units.
- All 14 were at the two kick points, all with exactly one free unit, and all
  went to the lowest victim index.
- Passed over at those points:
    - 10 victims who survived (13 pass-overs in all);
    - 2 who died. Neither had a viable route at that moment.

THE ESTIMATOR (section 4)

Front-priority's fire-arrival estimate RANKS well:
- Harrell C 0.86 against the first burn of the victim's cell (seed-level 95 %
  interval [0.82, 0.90]);
- today's victim-index order scores 0.46, which is chance.

As a CLOCK it is poor, and wrong in both directions:
- late in 100 of the 101 detection cells that burned by step 360, by a median of
  74-91 steps;
- early in 24 of the 126 cells it said would burn before 360 but did not (by at
  least 8 steps, median at least 95).
Fleeing kept dying victims alive 6-204 steps after their detection cell burned.

RECOMMENDATION (section 5): form U1, the minimal one.
- It changes only which waiting victim a single newly free unit takes at a kick.
- It applies only when the unit can reach her before the fire along a route that
  stays clear of the fire's estimated edge.
- It is attributable with two arms: the first divergence in each cell must be a
  U1 decision (structural gate Z1).
- Its no-flip-flop proof is short: U1 adds no unbind.
- Its switch-off path is today's loop verbatim.

U2, by comparison:
- needs the failed joint dispatcher and three fixes to it;
- needs four arms to attribute anything to urgency;
- its urgency term would act on 14 of 491 fill decisions;
- on sets 1-2, the deaths it reached came from reassignment, not urgency.

EXPECTED OUTCOME, stated before any run (17.4)
- On the PRIMARY (detected-victim deaths) the screen will most likely be NOT
  SHOWN. A chance rise of one must-not-rise guard could make it a FAIL. Either way
  the switch ships 0.
- The round is still worth running:
    - it gives a measured answer to the supervisor's question, "how does the
      system treat an easy rescue versus a critical one?";
    - it leaves a tested critical-first policy;
    - it supports a paper statement naming the movement layer as the binding
      constraint on detected-victim deaths.
- Decision U-0 asks whether to build anyway. The recommendation is yes. The
  alternative is a movement-layer round first.

DECISIONS NEEDED: U-0 .. U-14 (section 19).

PAPER: the urgency question gets two answers.
- Today's system has no notion of urgency. It serves waiting victims in
  victim-index order, a hidden function of ring geometry.
- On the recorded data, detected-victim deaths were not decided by service order.

2.4 FIREFIGHTER AND VICTIM FACTS THE DESIGN RESTS ON
    FIREFIGHTER DEATH
    - A firefighter dies only if its own cell burns at the post-move casualty
      sweep (WM:5747-5793).
    - Fire changes only in Fire.advance, first in the activation order
      (AG:2526-2532). So a unit that moved this step cannot die.
    - A death therefore needs one of: an enclosed unit with no non-burning
      neighbour (AG:3729-3738); an idle unit with no retreat candidate
      (AG:3326-3334, 3389-3440); or a return from absence placed on its exit
      cell (WM:2952-2955).
    ROUTE TEST: _path_exists_avoiding_fire (AG:3554-3589)
    - a 4-connected BFS; only burning cells are impassable;
    - returns a bool. NO function returns a firefighter path LENGTH.
    UNCLEAN CELLS
    - A cell is unclean when it burns, carries active Fire-agent smoke
      (_cell_has_active_smoke, AG:3160-3174), or is 4-adjacent to a burning cell
      (AG:3176-3181).
    - The mover's risk weights: fire 1e6, fire-adjacent 100, smoke 10
      (AG:3184-3192).
    - Approach tiers 1-3 use only clean cells (AG:3746-3760). An unclean cell is
      entered only when no other neighbour exists (tier 4, AG:3770-3779).
    SURVIVAL RETREAT
    - An assigned unit retreats when its cell burns, is smoky, or lies within
      Manhattan 1 of fire (AG:3203-3225).
    - Pickup is tested only after the survival check (AG:3005-3007). So no pickup
      happens on an unclean victim cell.
    VICTIMS
    - A victim dies only when her own cell burns at the sweep (WM:5674-5745).
    - She flees when a burning cell is within Manhattan 3 (CFV:369):
        - leash 6 cells, re-anchored when safe (CFV:370);
        - at most 1 cell per step;
        - never while in custody (AG:2523-2643).
    - A DETECTED victim is tracked at her true live cell (assumption A7;
      snapshot WM:3629-3632; the bound unit re-targets every step,
      AG:2904-2943).
    FIRE, WIND, GRID
    - The burning set is a fresh scan by TRUTHINESS (WM._active_burning_cells,
      WM:5255-5269; Firefighter._fire_cells, AG:3136-3146). It is identical
      across P3-P6.
    - A cell burns for 21-30 steps, and units remove fuel. So the burning set can
      be EMPTY later in a run.
    - The wind is constant (FIXED_WIND, CFV:10). Its vector is
      cfv.wind_vector_from_direction (CFV:47-58).
    - The grid is MultiGrid(HEIGHT, WIDTH) (WM:195). grid.width IS model.HEIGHT,
      the x extent (WM:201-205), which is what FAE expects as its first argument
      (FAE:75-99).
    PAPER: a firefighter dies only when the fire encloses it (or in two edge
    cases). An urgency guard must therefore bound ROUTE-CLOSURE EXPOSURE, not
    "stepping into fire", which never happens.

4. THE ESTIMATOR - A GOOD RANKER, A POOR CLOCK
================================================================================
4.1 WHAT IT IS (FAE:1-117; bayesprep_part1.txt 2.2-2.3)
      T(c) = min over burning b of ( |v| - e (v . u) ) / ( R_H (1 - e) ),
      where v = c - b and u is the downwind unit vector.
    This is an elliptical Huygens envelope built from published rates:
    - R_H = 10 % of U10 (Cruz & Alexander 2019), divided by a walking speed of
      1.34 m/s (Weidmann 1993), giving 0.4146 cells/step;
    - length-to-width z = 1 + 0.25 U_mf (Andrews 2018, after Anderson 1983),
      giving z = 2.081 and e = 0.877;
    - U10 = 20 km/h.
    It reads no simulator fire constant, draws no random number and keeps no
    cache. At these values it predicts about 2.4 steps per cell downwind, 19.6 on
    the flank and 36.8 upwind.

4.2 MEASURED ON dp0
    Harrell C is computed within runs. Intervals come from a SEED-level bootstrap:
    the ring and uniform runs of a seed share the fire, so there are 32
    independent fires.
      Predictor -> target                              C      95 %
      T_fire -> first burn of the detection cell       0.861  [0.82, 0.90]
        (205 comparable pairs)
      victim index (today's order) -> first burn       0.459  [0.36, 0.55]  chance
      T_fire -> the victim's death (13 pairs)          0.846  [0.54, 1.00]
    - dp1 gives the same values. That agrees with dp0 but is not a replication:
      same seeds, and 32 identical cells.
    - CLOCK ERROR, BOTH WAYS:
        - late in 100 of the 101 detection cells that burned by step 360, by a
          median of +91.3 steps (set 1, n 54) and +74.2 (set 2, n 47);
        - early in 24 of the 126 cells with a finite estimate that did NOT burn by
          360 although detection + T_fire < 360. The error there was at least 8
          steps, with a median of at least 95.
      DPR's "+155 / +164 including censored cases" treats step 360 as the burn
      step. It bounds the lateness; it does not estimate it.
    - FLIGHT: the 13 dying victims died 3-42 cells from their detection cell, and
      6-204 steps after that cell burned (median 36).
    - The test "min_d + 1 <= T_fire" at detection passes 220 of 225 victims,
      including 10 of the 12 deaths that had a living unit.

4.3 CONSEQUENCES FOR THE DESIGN
    (a) T is a sound ORDERING key, far better than the index order it replaces.
        Its validation against deaths is thin (13 pairs).
    (b) An absolute "in time" test errs both ways:
        - the clock is mostly late (too generous) and sometimes early;
        - a fleeing victim outlives the estimate at her current cell (too harsh).
        So an "unreachable in time" verdict must never write a victim off (8.4).
    (c) T must be re-evaluated at the victim's CURRENT cell at every decision
        (7.2).
    (d) The estimator knows nothing about fireproof depots (case 4d: victim_1
        sheltered in the NW depot) or about fuel. This is stated as a limitation,
        not patched: patching it would mean using simulator knowledge.
    PAPER: "the dispatcher ranks by a published-rate fire-arrival estimate that
    orders victims well (C = 0.86) but is a poor clock (late by a median of 74-91
    steps where the fire arrived, and sometimes early). No victim is ever written
    off on it."

WHERE

    K1  a unit returned or recycled;
    K2  a unit's route reopened.
- These are exactly where today's dispatch made all its scarce choices (3.2).
- Untouched: detection drains, route_blocked replacements and casualty
  replacements (U-2).

WHEN - only if ALL of these hold:
  (i)   DISPATCH_URGENCY is on;
  (ii)  exactly ONE unit is free at the kick: |F| = 1, which was the case at all
        14 recorded scarce kicks;
  (iii) two or more victims wait: |W| >= 2;
  (iv)  at least one cell burns.
- W is the set of victims today's loop would consider, by today's three
  predicates.
- F is the set of units passing _firefighter_available_for_dispatch
  (WM:3584-3596). That predicate includes select's availability filter.
- Otherwise the kick runs today's loop verbatim (U-3).

  "initial") per victim, as today. With one free unit, exactly the first victim
  in the order gets it.
- Only the ORDER of W changes:
  1) PROMOTABLE victims first. A victim is promotable when the free unit f can
     reach her along a SAFE-IN-TIME ROUTE (8.2) and arrive before the fire is
     estimated to reach her current cell:  c(f, v) + 1 <= T(v).
     The safe-in-time route:
       - runs over CLEAN cells only: not burning, not smoky, not 4-adjacent to
         fire. The victim's own cell must be clean too.
       - never enters a cell that the fire is estimated to reach, or to make
         fire-adjacent, before the unit gets there.
     Promotable victims are ordered by T ascending (the fire reaches her soonest),
     then by c ascending, then by integer victim index.
  2) Every other waiting victim follows, in TODAY'S victim-index order.

NOT CHANGED
- Which unit is sent (the only free one), how many victims a kick binds, and the
  reason string "initial".
- Every release, write-off and timer; movement; firefighting.
- The stage order, and stdout when the switch is off.

NOBODY IS ABANDONED. A non-promotable victim:
- stays waiting;
- is served at the next kick at which no promotable victim competes;
- is re-judged at every kick;
- is logged ([UrgencyTriage], 13.3).
U1 issues no unassign, no release and no mark_unreachable (8.4).

NO FLIP-FLOP, BY CONSTRUCTION. U1 adds no unbind. Every change of a victim's
binder goes through an outside event (fire, latch, death, rescue, isolation timer),
so the urgency key can never send a unit back (section 10).

SWITCH: DISPATCH_URGENCY ships 0 and is on only on an exact 1.

PAPER, in four sentences:
  (1) "When firefighters are scarce, a newly free firefighter is sent to the
      waiting victim that the fire is expected to reach soonest, among those it
      can reach first along a route that stays clear of the fire's estimated edge."
  (2) "Every other waiting victim keeps the baseline's order and is never written
      off by this rule."
  (3) "Nothing else in dispatch changes."
  (4) "A bound firefighter is never redirected."

7. URGENCY
================================================================================
7.1 DEFINITION
    At a qualifying kick at step t, compute T(.) = FAE.arrival_time over the
    whole grid. The inputs:
    - x size model.HEIGHT and y size model.WIDTH (WM:195, 201-205: grid.width is
      HEIGHT, which is FAE's first argument);
    - the TRUE current burning set, read by truthiness exactly as the units read
      it (A3);
    - the downwind unit vector of the constant wind,
      cfv.wind_vector_from_direction(model.wind.wind_direction) (A4);
    - the published parameter values FrontPriorityParams(): U10 20, ROS fraction
      0.1, walk 1.34, WAF 0.4, 1.15, LW 0.25 / 8 (FAE:35-45).
    The parameters are FROZEN IN CODE. They are NOT read from the SEARCHER_FP_*
    knobs, so the measurement round's FP sensitivity points cannot move dispatch
    (U-6). kappa and tau are not used.
    T(v) = T at v's CURRENT true cell. T is also used on route cells (8.2).
    NEVER READ: the simulator's spread constants (FIRE_SPREAD_*, MU, fuel), the
    flee rule, undetected victims.
    If NO cell burns, the kick runs verbatim. U1 makes no claim without a fire.
    PAPER: "urgency is the estimated fire arrival time at the victim's cell, from
    published spread rates, the current fire perimeter and the wind".

7.2 A FLEEING VICTIM'S CHANGING CELL
    - T and the route are recomputed from her current true cell at EVERY
      qualifying kick (A7: a detected victim is tracked).
    - There is no memory, no smoothing and no prediction of her flight.
    - The flee rule is the simulator's and is unknown to the team. The search
      belief's flee model is an operator assumption (A8) for search probability,
      not for deadlines.
    - U1 decides only when a free unit is offered and never revisits a binding.
      So a moving T cannot reverse anything. The bound unit re-targets to her live
      cell every step, as today (AG:2904-2943).
    KNOWN BIAS
    - A fleeing victim stands within about 3 cells of the fire. So T at her cell
      is small, especially downwind (3 cells is about 7 steps), and she often
      stands on or next to an unclean cell.
    - Flight often keeps her alive far longer than that (4.2: 6-204 steps after
      her detection cell burned).
    - The rule therefore tends to DEFER threatened downwind victims (3.4: s1 ring
      D_W 51). That is why "deferred" never means "abandoned" (8.4), and why
      every verdict is recorded with its outcome (MU3).
    PAPER: "urgency is re-estimated from the victim's current cell at each
    decision; the estimate does not model her flight, which tends to make a
    fleeing victim look less reachable than she is".

7.3 TIES
    - T is a float from a deterministic formula with no RNG.
    - Exact ties fall to c, then to the integer index.
    - No tolerance band is used: any band would be a tuned constant. 18.4 records
      the cost (re-orderings between victims whose estimates are hundreds of
      steps away).
    PAPER: "the order uses the estimate as computed, with deterministic
    tie-breaks; no tuned constant".

================================================================================
8. REACHABLE IN TIME
================================================================================
8.1 THE UNIT
    - U1 acts only when |F| = 1. The unit judged is the unit that will be sent:
      the only free unit, f.
    - Two or more free units under scarcity never occurred in 64 runs. If it
      happens, the kick runs verbatim and is counted (MU1).
    PAPER: "the reachability test is about the firefighter that is actually sent".

8.2 THE SAFE-IN-TIME ROUTE c(f, v)
    CLEAN CELL: a cell where an assigned unit does not trigger its survival
    retreat (AG:3203-3225). It is:
      - not burning (truthiness);
      - not smoky (active Fire-agent smoke: the units' own predicate,
        AG:3160-3174, and NOT _fix3b_true_fire's wider visibility smoke);
      - not 4-adjacent to a burning cell.
    T*(x) = the minimum of T over x and its in-grid 4-neighbours.
    BFS FROM f's cell (hop 0; f's own cell is exempt):
      - a cell x is entered at hop k >= 1 iff x is CLEAN now AND T*(x) > k;
      - "T*(x) > k" means the fire is not estimated to reach x, or to make x
        fire-adjacent, before the unit stands on it.
      - c(f, v) = the hop at which v's cell is entered. v's own cell must
        satisfy the same test, so a victim standing on an unclean cell is
        unreachable now.
      - c = infinity if no such route exists.
    Correctness:
      - Passability only tightens as k grows. Arriving earlier is therefore never
        worse, and BFS gives the exact earliest arrival under these constraints.
      - The unit's own cell may be unclean (it retreats anyway).
    Cost: one BFS from the single free unit gives c to every waiting victim.
    WHY THIS ROUTE, AND NOT DP1's NON-BURNING ROUTE d
    - d is an admissible lower bound, so an in-time test built on it is
      optimistic.
    - The units' own survival rule retreats from unclean cells. The mover ranks
      fire-adjacent cells 100 times worse than clean ones, and picks up only on a
      clean cell.
    - 22 of 26 dp0 firefighter deaths followed ROUTE CLOSURE: the fire reached a
      unit's route, and the unit was stranded (12.1).
    - The time-expanded condition is the parameter-free way to require "a route
      the fire is not expected to close before the unit has passed".
    - It uses the same frozen estimator. No new constant is introduced.
    LIMITS, stated:
    - The shipped mover is L1-greedy with risk tiers and does NOT follow paths
      (AG:3686-3779). So c is a LOWER BOUND on its travel time along that route,
      and "in time" is optimistic in exactly the MOV cases of 3.1.
    - T is mostly late, so "T*(x) > k" is permissive.
    - The non-burning d is still recorded for every victim (instrument), which
      keeps M8 comparable.
    Ruling U-5 offers simpler variants.
    PAPER: "travel time is measured along a route that the fire is not expected
    to close before the firefighter passes; for the greedy mover it is a lower
    bound".

8.3 THE TEST
    v is PROMOTABLE iff c(f, v) is finite AND c(f, v) + 1 <= T(v). The "+1" is
    the pickup step, as in M8 (DP1 11.6).
    Otherwise v is DEFERRED, because either:
      - no safe-in-time route exists (including: her own cell is unclean now); or
      - the route is too slow for the estimate.

8.4 A DEFERRED VICTIM IS NOT ABANDONED, AND NOT SILENTLY DEPRIORITISED
    (a) She stays in W. There is no state change, no flag and no write-off of any
        kind. U1 has no code path to unassign, release or mark_unreachable (T-SRC).
    (b) With one free unit, only the first victim in the order is served at a
        kick. A deferred victim is served at a kick where she comes first, which
        happens when:
          - no victim is promotable (the kick is then exactly today's); or
          - she is the lowest-index waiting victim and no promotable victim
            competes.
    (c) She is re-judged at every later kick, from her new cell, the new fire and
        the newly free unit. She becomes promotable as soon as the estimate admits
        it.
    (d) Every deferral is printed on the ON path ([UrgencyTriage], 13.3), with T,
        c and the victim served instead. The instrument records it with its
        outcome (MU2, MU3). It is never silent.
    (e) The ESCAPE SWEEP is unchanged. It can write her off only after 30
        consecutive steps in which she is both:
          - not productively served (no assigned unit got closer, WM:5476-5490);
            and
          - unreachable by a fire-free path from EVERY living, on-grid,
            non-exiting unit (RP:447-459, 527-528).
        Deferral can contribute to a write-off only by leaving her unbound during
        such a stretch, while she is unreachable in any case. This is gated:
          - escape-sweep write-offs of detected victims must not rise (F8);
          - every write-off of a victim who had been deferred is diagnosed (DIAG).
    JUSTIFICATION
    (1) The verdict rests on a poor clock (4.2) and on a current-cell estimate
        that ignores flight. In the evidence cases, victims whom an in-time test
        would have judged hopeless survived 99 to 200+ more steps (u2assess:
        cases 2, 3, x and 4g). Writing victims off on such a verdict would kill
        victims that today's dispatch saves.
    (2) Deferring only changes WHO GOES FIRST when one unit is free. Serving a
        deferred victim is what today's dispatch would do.
    (3) Dispatch in this project writes nobody off (the dispatch round's D-6
        principle). A triage rule that gives people up would need an ethical
        statement the paper cannot support with this estimator.
    PAPER: "a victim the dispatcher judges unreachable in time is deprioritised,
    never abandoned: she keeps her place in the baseline's order, is re-judged at
    every decision, every deferral is logged, and write-offs were a gated outcome".

9. THE TRIAGE RULE, WITH TIE-BREAKS, AND WHY
================================================================================
9.1 THE RULE
    At a kick with DISPATCH_URGENCY = 1, |F| = 1, |W| >= 2 and at least one
    burning cell:
      key(v) = (0, T(v), c(f, v), idx(v))   if v is promotable
               (1, idx(v))                  otherwise
    - idx(v) is the INTEGER victim index ("victim_10" after "victim_2").
    - Sort W ascending by key, then run today's loop body over the sorted W.
    - The other managed victims are skipped by today's predicates, as now.
    Any other kick: today's loop verbatim.

9.2 WHY EACH PART
    - |F| = 1 (U-3).
        - Under scarcity the kick then binds exactly one victim, so urgency
          decides only WHO goes first. It never re-pairs.
        - "The unit judged is the unit sent" is exact.
        - The divergence definition (Z1) is exact.
        - It covers all 14 recorded scarce kicks.
    - PROMOTABLE FIRST. This is the brief's "AND that can still be reached in
      time": the one free unit is not spent on a victim it is not expected to
      reach before the fire if another victim can be reached.
    - T ASCENDING. This is earliest-deadline-first ("the fire will reach
      soonest").
        - EDF is the classic rule for meeting deadlines on one resource
          (Jackson's rule: it minimises maximum lateness).
        - T is the best-validated ordering information the team has: C 0.86,
          against 0.46 for the index.
    - c, THEN INDEX, AS TIE-BREAKS.
        - Of two equally urgent victims, the quicker rescue frees the unit sooner
          (shortest-processing-time).
        - The index is today's own order, and it makes the order total and
          deterministic.
    - DEFERRED VICTIMS IN TODAY'S ORDER.
        - The estimate says the unit cannot be expected to reach any of them
          safely before the fire, so it gives no basis to rank them.
        - Ordering them by T would send the unit toward the front where the
          chance of a pickup is lowest, which is the safety concern of section 12.
        - Keeping today's order is the minimal choice. Test T-U10 enforces it.
    PAPER: "critical-first among the reachable, the baseline order for the rest,
    only when one firefighter is free and others are needed".

9.3 WORKED CASES (one free unit; c is the safe-in-time route)
    (a) v0: T 400, c 30; v2: T 60, c 40. Both promotable; U1 serves v2, today
        serves v0.
    (b) v0: T 400, c 30; v2: T 20, c 40. v2 is deferred; U1 serves v0, as today.
    (c) v1: no safe route; v3: T 25, c 15. v3 is promotable; U1 serves v3, today
        serves v1.
    (d) v0 and v1 both deferred: U1 serves v0, as today.
    (e) Nothing burns: the kick runs verbatim.

9.4 WHAT THE RULE DOES NOT DO
    - It does not pre-empt or cross-swap.
    - It does not act at a detection.
    - It does not act at a route_blocked or casualty replacement. This is
      deliberate. If the casualty replacement's free unit went elsewhere, the
      dead unit's victim would meet an empty pool and be WRITTEN OFF permanently
      (RP:661-667). T-CAS enforces this.
    - It does not decide for units freed by a victim's death, the escape sweep or
      a stale clear: these trigger no kick (2.2(b)). In dp0, those units' idle time
      (13 unit-steps) never preceded a bind. Recorded as risk 18.10 and offered
      in U-2.
    PAPER: "the rule applies only when a firefighter becomes free through a
    return or a reopened route".

================================================================================
10. NO FLIP-FLOP - PROOF
================================================================================
SETTING
- DISPATCH_URGENCY = 1; the scenario teams as shipped.
- A victim's BINDER SEQUENCE lists the units bound to her in order. A unit's
  VICTIM SEQUENCE likewise.
- OUTSIDE EVENTS are those dispatch does not choose:
    o1  the unit's FIRST route_blocked raise on that pair (handler unassign)
    o2  its REPEATED raise: the unit stays bound but latched, hidden from
        _find_active; observed as route_blocked while bound
    o3  its death
    o4  the victim becomes terminal: rescued, dead, or unreachable (escape sweep
        or casualty write-off)
    o5  the completer's recycle or absence, after an o4 rescue
    o6  a stale clear (mode 1 clears flags only; reached only when no detected
        live victim remains)

LEMMA 1 (U1 adds no unbind)
- U1's code is: the ordering function (pure, reads only), the qualifying-kick
  predicate, and the [UrgencyTriage] print.
- It changes only the iteration order of the loop at WM:3051. The loop body
  either binds through the executor or returns a no-op delay. With reason
  "initial", select never returns mark_unreachable (2.1).
- So every unassign, release and write-off in a run with U1 on comes from a path
  in 2.3, each triggered by o1-o6.
- Tests: T-FLIP-U, T-SRC and the reason mutant in T-U5.

LEMMA 2 (U1 binds only waiting victims, and exactly as many as today)
- W is formed by today's three predicates, unchanged. The executor's guards are
  unchanged (RE:380-398; WM:4914-4924).
- So U1 binds the free unit only to a detected, needy victim with no ACTIVE
  binder. It never creates a second ACTIVE binder.
- A latch-held victim can receive a second claimant exactly as today, because
  the latched unit is invisible to _find_active (WM:4044-4046).
- At a qualifying kick, exactly one victim is bound in either order: the
  selected assign always succeeds, and binding one victim does not change
  another victim's predicates.
- Elsewhere the kick is today's loop.
- So the number of binds per kick is identical (Z8).

THEOREM (no reversal by an urgency comparison)
- Suppose a victim's binder sequence is A ... X ... A, or a unit's victim
  sequence is v ... w ... v.
- A new binder joins a victim only when her previous binder was:
    - REMOVED by an unbind: o1, o3, or an escape-sweep unassign, which ends her
      sequence (o4); or
    - LATCHED (o2): a second claimant joins beside the latched unit, as today.
- By Lemma 1, U1 issues no unbind. Hence every return of a unit to a victim, and
  every return of a victim to a unit, is preceded by an outside event (o1-o6) on
  the intermediate binder, or on the unit while it served w.
- The urgency key moves every step with the fire and the victim's flight. But it
  is consulted only when a FREE unit is offered to WAITING victims.
- It cannot send a unit back: that would need an unbind or a latch, which only
  the fire, a death, a rescue or the isolation timer can cause.

BOUNDS: binds per victim are bounded as today.
- o1 happens at most once per pair (the permanent _blocked_replacement_attempted,
  WM:425-426).
- There are at most N_ff deaths.
- A qualifying kick binds one victim.
- U1 adds no bind opportunity: kicks happen at the same events.

STILL POSSIBLE, AS TODAY (none chosen by urgency)
  R1  A raises (o1); later its route reopens; K2 offers A to the waiting victims,
      possibly v again.
  R2  A dies (o3); B takes v; B raises; and so on.
  R3  A latches (o2); a kick binds B as a second claimant; A recovers, giving two
      active binders. This is today's double claim (DP1 1.3).

IMPOSSIBLE UNDER U1
- any unbind, release or write-off issued by the urgency code;
- a return whose intermediate binder was neither removed nor latched by an
  outside event;
- a kick binding a different NUMBER of victims than today's loop would on the
  same state.
Under DISPATCH_URGENCY = 0, today's dispatch runs verbatim.

GATED IN PART 3: Z2 (U1-issued unbinds = 0) and Z5 (returns without an outside
event = 0) are structural zeros.
- Z5 uses the outside-event set above. A post-phase return whose intermediate
  binder X is route_blocked in rows_ff at the return step counts as a same-frame
  latch (o2), WHATEVER the bind's reason.
- DPR's G-T(a) counted that only for J's joint_replace_latched reason. Under DPR's
  definition, today's code shows 1 such return (set 2 uniform A_N, step 118, a K2

- Under the amended definition there are 0 returns without an outside event in

PAPER: "the urgency rule cannot make a firefighter oscillate between victims. It
never unbinds anyone, so every change of assignment follows a fire event, a

12. FIREFIGHTER SAFETY - THE GUARD
================================================================================
12.1 THE RISK, MEASURED
    dp0's 26 firefighter deaths, by leg:
      approach                                      8
      stranded after a route_blocked unassign      11   died a median 5 steps
                                                        later (range 2-9)
      idle                                          6   3 of them soon after
                                                        their own route_blocked
                                                        cleared
      carry                                         1
    - 22 of the 26 follow ROUTE CLOSURE: units die near the front when the fire
      closes their route.
    - Only 2 of the 26 died after a bind made at a scarce choice. One of these
      died at the same cell and step in dp1, where the other victim was chosen.
    - Urgency deliberately prefers victims the fire reaches first, which is where
      routes close.
    - Illustration of that exposure (not an urgency decision). Case 2 with J on,
      at step 46, sent ff_unit_0 on a 64-step route (Manhattan 18) to victim_2,
      the only waiting victim. The route closed at 63, the unit died unbound at
      87, and the victim died at 144 (201 with J off).
    PAPER: "most firefighter deaths follow route closure near the front; an
    urgency rule must not add such legs".
12.2 THE GUARD (each part structural, each with a test)
    G1 SAFE-IN-TIME ROUTE (8.2)
       - Urgency may move a victim ahead of today's order only if the free unit
         has a route to her over clean cells that the fire is not estimated to
         reach, or to make fire-adjacent, before the unit passes. The unit must
         also be estimated to pick her up before the fire reaches her cell.
       - Urgency never prefers a victim who can be approached only along, or
         through, the fire's present or estimated edge, or who stands on an
         unclean cell.
       - At case 2's OFF-arm kick (step 51), victim_2's route was 55 or more,
         against a stale remaining estimate of about 2: G1 refuses the
         promotion.
       - Tests T-U2 and T-U3. Mutants: the cut removed; a static clean route; the
         victim's cell exempt.
    G2 NO NEW KIND OF LEG
       - U1 sends the one free unit to a victim in today's W, through today's
         pairing and reason.
       - Its DESTINATION can differ from today's, so its exposure can differ:
         this is measured by G4 and MU4.
       - U1 never redirects a bound unit, never pre-empts, and never acts on a
         route_blocked or casualty replacement.
       - Tests T-U4, T-U5, T-FLIP-U, T-CAS, T-DRN.
    G3 THE SAFETY LAYERS STAY: movement, the survival retreat, the route_blocked
       raise and handler, the casualty sweep, idle firefighting and its retreat.
       Test T-NT (NOT-TOUCHED source hashes).
    G4 MEASURED, AS A GATE
       - Firefighter deaths must not rise, pooled (F1).
       - Reported: per set and placement; by leg; unit-steps on unclean cells;
         route_blocked raises; strandings (an o1 unassign followed by that unit's
         death within 15 steps). Each is split into legs bound at a U1-divergent
         kick versus all others (MU4).
       - Every firefighter death in a diverged cell is diagnosed.
12.3 WHAT THE GUARD DOES NOT GUARANTEE
    - G1 judges the route with an estimate that is mostly late (4.2), so
      "not estimated to close" is optimistic.
    - The greedy mover may leave the route (8.2).
    - The guard bounds the DECISION, not the outcome. That is why firefighter
      deaths are a gate.
    PAPER: "the urgency rule may prefer a victim only along a route the fire is
    not expected to close before the firefighter passes, and only when the
    firefighter is expected to arrive first; firefighter deaths were a
    pre-registered guard".

- Dispatch has no notion of urgency.

  reopens, and then in victim-index order. That order is a hidden function of
  ring geometry, and it ranks victims at chance against the fire (C 0.46).

THE SUPERVISOR'S QUESTION, MEASURED
- Today's system treats easy and critical rescues the same.
- On the recorded data, scarce choices are rare (14 in 64 runs, always with one
  free unit), and none of them decided a death.
- Detected victims died after assignment, because the assigned unit did not get
  through: loops, the retreat before pickup, route closure and stranding, and the
  carrying leg.
- The 80 % / 88 % "feasible-critical" share counts busy units and lower-bound
  routes. It means "reachable in principle".

THE DISPATCH ROUND, CORRECTED

  route-aware feasibility (5.2).

THE RULE, IF BUILT
- Critical-first among the victims the one free firefighter can reach before the
  fire, along a route the fire is not expected to close; today's order for the
  rest.
- Only when one firefighter is free and several victims wait.
- It never redirects a bound unit and never writes anyone off.

  reads, and one switch whose off state is the baseline.
