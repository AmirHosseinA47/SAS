0. THE HEADLINE - READ THIS BEFORE THE REST
================================================================================
Four findings, all measured before spending a wave. Together they change what the
four arms can measure, and one of them is a defect in the shipped feature. I am
putting them first because arm B as literally specified is a null arm, and the
maintainer should know that before approving the round.

0.1  THE RETURN-LEG SHARE IS ARITHMETIC, NOT A TUNING CONSTANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One return trip costs d steps, d being the Manhattan distance from wherever the
UAV was to its berth. A reserve of 60 produces exactly one trip per UAV per run.
So

      return share  =  mean d / 240

The measured mean d was 41.8 and the measured share 17.5%. 41.8/240 = 17.4%. The
17.5% is not a number to be tuned away - it is the mean distance to a corner
depot divided by the horizon. That leaves exactly two levers: fire fewer trips,
or make d smaller.

0.2  THE FIRST LEVER DOES NOT WORK. THE LITERAL DISTANCE RULE IS A NULL ARM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Replaying candidate triggers against the recorded per-step battery and position
(outputs/_dc_replay.py), canonical 13 x 4 UAVs = 52 UAV-runs:

  rule                                          trips fired    return share
  max(60, 0.3d + 5)     the shipped rule           52 / 52        17.41%
  0.3d + 16.40          fuel-derived (3.4)         22 / 52         3.13%   none completes
  0.3d + 3              fuel, small margin          0 / 52         0.00%
  0.3d + 39.23          horizon-derived (3.5)      52 / 52        17.88%

A trigger derived from FUEL ALONE either never fires or fires so late that no trip
completes. The reason is not subtle: a UAV drains 0.2983 points per step
(measured, 3.1), so it ends a 240-step run at battery 28.7, while the fuel a
return needs is at most 0.3 x 92 = 27.6 points. The battery never gets low enough
for distance to bind.

Related, and it sharpens the point: THE DISTANCE TERM IN THE SHIPPED TRIGGER HAS
NEVER ONCE BEEN THE MAX. max(60, 0.3d + 5) selects the distance term only when
d >= 183.3, and the largest d reachable on a 50x50 grid is 92. The round is
proposing to promote a term that is currently dead code.

0.3  THE SECOND LEVER IS THE WHOLE EFFECT, AND IT IS PLACEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same replay, at the derived margin that keeps returns completing (3.5), 52/52
trips in every row:

  depots           mean d over the grid   predicted return share    vs 17.41%
  NW (shipped)              41.40                 17.88%              +0.5
  NW+NE                     29.10                 13.58%              -3.8
  NW+SW                     29.10                 13.86%              -3.6
  NW+SE                     25.12                 11.79%              -5.6
  CENTRAL                   21.16                  9.39%              -8.0

  (NW+NE and NW+SW are the same geometry by symmetry - grid mean 29.10 and
  D=65 for both - and their 0.28-point difference here is trajectory noise,
  not a preference between them. Both are clearly worse than NW+SE.)

Making the reserve distance-based AT A SINGLE NW DEPOT does not reduce the cost -
it is half a point WORSE, because the trigger fires from further out. Every point
of improvement in this round comes from where the depot is.

That is the honest framing: this is a DEPOT PLACEMENT round wearing a
reserve-derivation hat. The reserve still has to be re-derived, because a flat 60
tuned for a 90-cell worst case is wrong for a 45-cell one, but deriving it is what
makes the placement comparison fair, not what delivers the gain.

0.4  A DEFECT FOUND WHILE MEASURING: THE MODE-2 RETURNS DO NOT DEADLOCK-FREE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The previous round reported its five non-arriving mode-2 legs as
"'En route at 240' is NOT stranding: the run ended while the UAV was still flying
home, with battery in hand. Every trip either completed or was still in progress
at the horizon; none failed."

That is wrong, and the record shows it. All five are the SAME UAV in the SAME
cell, permanently stuck:

  seed            UAV    stuck at   stuck for   final battery   berth
  east/half/101   2502   (3,44)     34 steps       39.6          (3,46)
  east/half/303   2502   (3,44)     36 steps       40.0          (3,46)
  east/half/404   2502   (3,44)     43 steps       37.2          (3,46)
  east/half/505   2502   (3,44)     63 steps       40.8          (3,46)
  south/half/404  2502   (3,44)     66 steps       41.6          (3,46)

MECHANISM, fully diagnosed. At mode 2 nothing recharges, so a docked UAV never
leaves its berth. UAV 2502's berth is (3,46); it approaches from (3,44), one cell
OUTSIDE the depot (the depot is y >= 45).
  - _rtb_direction (agents.py:350-376) computes dx = 0, dy = +2, so x_dir is None
    and the primary direction is y_dir = 3.
  - The target cell (3,45) is UAV 2501's berth and 2501 is docked on it, so
    not_UAV_adjacent refuses the move and the position does not change.
  - The stall sidestep swaps primary and secondary - but secondary is None,
    because dx = 0. The function then falls through to `if secondary is not None`
    and returns the ORIGINAL blocked direction. The sidestep cannot fire on a
    pure-axis approach, which is exactly the case that needs it.
  - The docking fallback at agents.py:447-449 requires _rtb_inside_depot(here);
    (3,44) is outside, so the fallback never fires either.
Both escape hatches miss the same case, and the UAV retries the same blocked move
for 34 to 66 steps with 37-42 battery in hand.

AND THE SIDESTEP IS TESTED ONLY ON THE CASE THAT WORKS. tests/test_base_station.py
:273-278 exercises _rtb_direction from (10,48) to (4,45) - dx = -6, dy = -3, BOTH
axes non-zero, so a secondary direction exists and the swap succeeds. The
pure-axis case (dx = 0 or dy = 0), which is the one that deadlocks in production
and the only one where the swap has nothing to swap to, is not covered. Whatever
round fixes this should add exactly that case.

WHAT I AM DOING ABOUT IT. Not fixing it in this round: a fix changes mode-2
behaviour and would confound the placement comparison this round exists to make,
and this campaign's practice is to report a defect and diagnose it before
bundling a fix into an unrelated round. What this round DOES do:
  - the "no UAV stranded" gate is redefined for Part 3. "Zero battery outside the
    depot" would have passed all five of these. The gate becomes: no UAV ends the
    run rtb_active with an unchanged position for more than N steps, whatever its
    battery. Part 3 reports the stuck count per arm.
  - arms C and D change the berth geometry, so the deadlock's frequency changes.
    That is measured, not assumed.
  - a one-line follow-up (make the sidestep pick any legal direction, or let the
    docking fallback fire from a cell adjacent to the depot) is recommended as its
    own round with its own gate.

WHAT BREAKS, and every one of these is a silent no-op or a crash rather than a
loud failure - which is why they are enumerated rather than discovered in Part 2:

  (a) rtb_berth IS WRITTEN EXACTLY ONCE IN THE TREE, at wildfire_model.py:175,
      and never re-assigned. "Return to the nearest depot" has NO existing code
      path at all. It has to be added, and 2.5 says how without breaking the
      distinct-berth invariant.
  (b) THE DOCKING FALLBACK DOCKS AT THE WRONG DEPOT. _rtb_inside_depot tests the
      UNION, so with two depots a UAV whose own berth is momentarily occupied can
      dock inside the depot it was NOT returning to - and at mode 3 recharge and
      re-launch from there. _rtb_inside_depot must become "inside the depot this
      UAV is currently returning to", not "inside any depot".
  (c) THE BERTH RANKING INTERLEAVES THE BLOCKS. A single sort over the union of
      NW+SE at 50x50 hands out uav_berths as 3 NW / 1 SE and firefighter_berths as
      1 each, purely as an artifact of the (c[0], c[1]) tie-break. The contiguous-
      prefix argument that keeps UAVs and firefighters from colliding is a
      PER-BLOCK argument and only survives if the ranking stays per block.
  (d) DUPLICATE BERTHS. wildfire_model.py:578 sorts a plain LIST, not a set. Two
      depot regions that overlap or touch put the same cell in the ranking twice
      and hand it out as two different berths, stacking two UAVs on one cell.
      Depot regions must be asserted pairwise disjoint at build time.
  (e) THE CAPACITY CHECK BECOMES VACUOUS. It compares against len(ranked), the
      total berths across all depots - 50 for two 5x5 blocks. The real constraint
      under nearest-depot return is PER DEPOT, since every UAV needs a berth in
      every depot it may return to.
  (f) THE OUT-OF-BOUNDS SPAWN FALLBACK REPAIRS spawn_pos BUT NOT rtb_berth
      (wildfire_model.py:174-182), leaving a UAV with a berth pointing off the
      grid. Unreachable at the four hardcoded corners; reachable the moment
      origins are configurable. An off-grid berth is a UAV that returns forever.
  (g) BOTH RENDERERS EMIT ONE BLOCK. Canvas_Grid_Visualization.py:144 unpacks
      `origin_x, origin_y = station["origin"]` and raises ValueError if origin
      becomes a list; serve_dashboard.py:213 does int(station["size"]) and would
      raise TypeError. Worse, the browser guard `if (fr.depot) {...}` is TRUTHY
      FOR A LIST and then reads .x / .size off it, yielding NaN rect coordinates
      that canvas draws as nothing - a silent no-op with no error anywhere.
  (h) THE HARNESS CRASHES MID-WAVE on int(model.base_station["size"]) if size
      becomes a list. A crash is the good case; see 6.5 for the drift case.
  (i) TEN of the 23 tests in tests/test_base_station.py hardcode single-depot
      facts - the exact berth tuples, the scalar origin, the dict shape, the
      global capacity check. They must be widened, not deleted.
  (j) base_station_corner() maps any unrecognised value to NW with no error

  (k) A CONSTRAINT ON WHERE THE LAYOUT CODE MAY LIVE: tests/test_base_station.py

1.2  The berth ranking across two blocks, and what it means for a central depot
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The ranking key - descending distance from the nearest grid edge, then (x, y) -
must stay PER BLOCK for the reason in 1.1(c). Verified reproduction of the shipped
ranking (outputs/_dc_geom.py):

  NW       (4,45) (3,45) (3,46) (4,46) (2,45) (2,46) (2,47) (3,47)
which is exactly the report's "UAV berths (4,45) (3,45) (3,46) (4,46) /
firefighter berths (2,45) (2,46)". Same key on the other anchors:
  NE  (45,45) (45,46) (46,45) (46,46) ...   SW  (4,4) (3,3) (3,4) (4,3) ...
  SE  (45,4) (45,3) (46,3) (46,4) ...       CENTRAL (24,24) (24,25) (25,24) (25,25) ...

The ranking's stated RATIONALE - "the berths handed out first are the ones with
the most legal moves out of the block", matching the executor's margin-3 edge
filter - is a CORNER rationale. Every cell of a central block is 22-24 cells from
the grid edge, so the key is flat across it and the (x, y) tie-break does all the
work. Nothing breaks (an interior cell has all four moves legal) but the rationale
carries no information there and must not be repeated for arm C.

1.3  Proposed internal representation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  model.base_station = {
    "depots": (                      # ordered, one entry per block, disjoint
      {"origin": (ox,oy), "size": n, "cells": frozenset(...), "ranked": (...)}, ...
    ),
    "cells":  frozenset(union),                   # unchanged semantics
    "uav_berths":         ( (berth_in_depot0, berth_in_depot1, ...), ... ),
    "firefighter_berths": ( (berth_in_depot0, ...), ... ),
    "uav_home":           (depot_index_per_uav, ...),
    "firefighter_home":   (depot_index_per_ff, ...),
    # aliases kept so the existing readers and the shipped tests are untouched
    # in the single-depot case:
    "origin": depots[0]["origin"],
    "size":   depots[0]["size"],
  }

Every UAV owns a berth in EVERY depot. That is what preserves the invariant the
source calls load-bearing - "distinct berths are what make the return leg
deadlock-free" - because within any one depot no two UAVs share a berth, whichever
depot they choose. Keeping "origin"/"size" as aliases for the FIRST depot means
the renderers and the harness keep working unchanged when there is one depot; both
renderers additionally LOOP over ["depots"], and Part 2 must assert the emitted
portrayal count per depot, exactly as the previous round asserted 20 portrayals at
Layer int 2. An invisible second depot is the silent no-op this design is most
exposed to.

2. SPAWN WITH TWO DEPOTS

2.1  Roles, lanes and sectors are index-based and constant - measured
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From the recorded uav_steps of the mode-0 arms, identical at steps 1, 120 and 240:

  roles=half  2500 tracker  2501 tracker  2502 SEARCHER   2503 SEARCHER
  roles=def   2500 tracker  2501 tracker  2502 tracker    2503 SEARCHER

(Scenario D's evaluate_scenarios default is 4 UAVs / 4 victims / 2 firefighters,
and the ROLE default is the legacy 3 trackers + 1 searcher; "half" is the round's
explicit 2+2 override. Both are in the wave.)

A searcher's crosswind lane is a pure function of its index among searchers, the
wind and the grid bounds - no RNG, no position
(local_adaptation_generator.py:332-363). Recorded at step 1:

  east  wind   2502 -> y in [0,24]     2503 -> y in [25,49]
  south wind   2502 -> x in [0,24]     2503 -> x in [25,49]

So a split by UAV INDEX is a split by ROLE, and never_detected - the metric this
round exists to fix - is a SEARCHER metric.

2.2  The lane arithmetic rules out every wind-blind index rule
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Against NW (x 0-4, y 45-49) and SE (x 45-49, y 0-4):

  east    NW (high y) lies in 2503's lane;   SE (low y)  lies in 2502's lane
  south   NW (low x)  lies in 2502's lane;   SE (high x) lies in 2503's lane

THE ASSIGNMENT THAT PUTS EACH SEARCHER IN ITS OWN LANE IS THE EXACT OPPOSITE FOR
THE TWO CANONICAL WINDS. No wind-blind index rule is right for both. That is a
computation, not a preference, and it eliminates options (ii) and (iii) below.

The consequence is already visible in the shipped arm's own record. Taking the
recorded trigger_distance field directly (bsfull, roles=half, 10 runs - no replay,
so no indexing question arises):

  searcher whose lane CONTAINS the NW depot   n=10   mean d 29.6   min 10  max 46
  searcher whose lane does NOT                n=10   mean d 54.9   min 30  max 68
  trackers                                    n=20   mean d 35.5   min 10  max 68

The far-lane searcher's return leg is 1.85x the home-lane searcher's, and which
searcher that is flips with the wind.

2.3  The options
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (i)   ALL AT THE FIRST DEPOT. Deterministic, and identical to today when there
        is one depot. Wastes the second depot's spawn value - but NOT its return
        value, since return is to the nearest depot regardless of spawn.
  (ii)  ALTERNATE BY INDEX. Deterministic, balances roles across depots at
        roles=half, but per 2.2 gives BOTH searchers the far depot at east wind.
        Measurably worse than the status quo on a replay bound.
  (iii) BLOCK SPLIT (first half / second half). Role-segregated: both trackers at
        NW, both searchers at SE. A wash on the replay bound, because it moves a
        tracker to SE and trackers are nearer NW at trigger under every wind.
  (iv)  SPLIT BY ROLE. Same as (iii) at roles=half; degenerate at roles=def.
  (v)   PARTITION-NEAREST: home each UAV to the depot nearest the centroid of its
        own initial partition - crosswind lane for a searcher, sector for a
        tracker. Correct at both winds by construction, and deterministic: the
        lane is a pure function of (searcher index, wind, bounds) and the sector
        of (tracker index, bounds).

2.4  CHOSEN: (v) PARTITION-NEAREST, with (i) as the default and the fallback
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - never_detected is a searcher metric, and 2.2 shows the partition-aware rule is
    the only one that starts every searcher in its own lane at both winds.
  - the measured asymmetry in 2.2 says what it is worth: the far-lane searcher
    pays roughly twice the return leg, and under east wind spends ~86 of 240 steps
    reaching its own lane before it can search at all.
  - it is deterministic and RNG-free, the invariant the whole feature rests on.
  - it costs nothing at one depot: every UAV is nearest the only depot, so the
    rule degenerates to today's behaviour exactly.

CONSTRAINTS THIS IMPOSES ON THE IMPLEMENTATION, both from 1.1(k):
  - the home assignment may NOT live in _build_base_station, which the geometry
    tests bind onto a stand-in exposing only HEIGHT / WIDTH / NUM_AGENTS. It lives
    in the spawn loop, where the model is real.
  - it must call the SAME resolve_victim_searcher_uav_ids /
    _searcher_crosswind_lane the rest of the model uses, never a re-derivation.
    A second copy of the role split is a worse defect than a suboptimal spawn.
    _searcher_crosswind_lane returns None for n <= 1 and for an unknown id; both
    must fall back to (i) explicitly rather than silently.
IF THAT PROVES IMPOSSIBLE BEFORE THE UAVs EXIST, take fallback (i) and say so in
the report rather than shipping a duplicate rule.

FIREFIGHTERS. They never return to base - the depot is spawn-only for them, and
nothing in the Firefighter class reads rtb_*. They have no lane and no sector, so
partition-nearest does not apply. THEY STAY AT NW IN EVERY ARM. The previous
round's measured firefighter benefit (+2 rescued, -3 unreachable, -2 ff deaths) is
a benefit of the NW depot specifically; firefighters are NOT fire-immune and the
SE block burns in 19 of 20 distinct fires (4.3), so moving them there opens a new
casualty channel and confounds the placement comparison with a firefighter-risk
change. Keeping them at NW in all four arms keeps that variable fixed.

2.5  Nearest-depot selection, and the one rule that makes it safe
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE TARGET BERTH IS CHOSEN ONCE, AT THE TRIGGER, AND LATCHED FOR THE TRIP.
Re-evaluating the argmin every step would make a UAV equidistant from two depots
flip its target each step; _rtb_direction would reverse with it and the stall
sidestep - already shown broken on a pure-axis approach in 0.4 - would not save
it. Latching also gives the record something to report: rtb_log gains the chosen
depot index and the target berth, which is what makes "mean return distance per
arm" attributable in Part 3.

With one depot the selector returns the existing rtb_berth unchanged, so the
single-depot path is bit-for-bit what it is today.

2.6  Determinism, to be asserted by a test in Part 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The depot set, every berth, every home assignment and every spawn cell must remain
a pure function of the grid extent, the agent counts, the role counts and the
wind, and must draw from NO RNG - so no agent is added, unique_agents_id is not
shifted and the fire stream is untouched. The existing check is exact and already
passes on seven arms: the per-step fire digest must be identical to the baseline
on all 240 steps of every seed. A two-depot spawn that changed UAV count or agent
creation order would break it immediately.

3. THE DISTANCE-BASED THRESHOLD AND THE MARGIN

3.1  The battery model, the ordering, and the measured drain
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
agents.py:509-517   advance() = _apply_return_to_base(); moved = move();
                    _update_battery_after_step(moved)
so the trigger reads the battery BEFORE that step's drain and the position BEFORE
that step's move.
agents.py:289-296   -0.1 unconditionally, -0.2 additionally ONLY IF THE MOVE
                    HAPPENED, then (mode 3) +recharge, then clamp to [0,100].
                    Maximum single-step drop 0.3.
A move fails - costing 0.1 with no progress - in exactly four ways: the docked
hold, the pipeline hold, out-of-bounds, and another UAV on the target cell. On a
mechanism-2 return leg only the last can fire.

Measured on the mode-0 canonical sample, 52 UAV-runs:
  move fraction        mean 0.9869   min 0.8577   max 1.0000
  drain rate           0.2983 per step
  final battery at 240 mean 28.71    min 28.00    max 34.80
  crosses 60 at step   mean 135.8    min 134   max 156   (52 of 52)
  crosses 30 at step   mean 235.1    min 234   max 240   (48 of 52; 4 never do)

This confirms the brief's premise to the step - 30% is reached at 234-240 of 240 -
and it validates the shipped derivation's conservatism: it assumed a move fraction
of 5/6 = 0.8333 and the measured MINIMUM is 0.8577, so the assumed drain is
genuinely a lower bound on the real one and 5/6 is the safe number to keep using.

3.2  What the battery actually gates
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For MOVEMENT: nothing. Reaching zero does not stop, kill, slow or reroute a UAV.
For PLANNING: three things, and only one of them is currently live.
  - src_extension/analysis/global_analyzer.py:43-44,679-720 raises a LOW_BATTERY
    StructuredTrigger at <= 30.0 and CRITICAL_BATTERY at <= 15.0.
  - src_extension/dashboard/alert_manager.py:236-245 raises a low_battery alert at
    cfv.LOW_BATTERY_THRESHOLD = 30.0.
  - INTRODUCED BY FEATURE 3 ITSELF: the mechanism-1 return option carries its own
    battery_level, and the utility layer marks any option with
    battery_level <= 15.0 infeasible. Feature 3 gave a previously dead planning
    gate a key to read.
LOW (30) already fires in the baseline - a mode-0 run ends at 28.7 - so it is an
exercised path. CRITICAL (15) has NEVER fired: the minimum battery ever recorded
across every arm of every base-station run is 28.00. That asymmetry is what sets
the arrival buffer in 3.4: 15, not 30.

3.3  What a return leg actually costs - measured on 208 recorded trips
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
outputs/_dc_trips.py, over the previous round's rtb_log:

  MECHANISM 2 (hardcoded, fire-blind - THE SHIPPED MECHANISM, used by all four
  arms), pooled bsret + bsfull, 137 arrived trips:
     battery spent vs the 0.3*d prediction: median 0.00  p95 0.10  p99 0.10  MAX 1.10
     realised steps minus Manhattan d:      median 0     p95 1              MAX 11
  MECHANISM 1 (planner, fire-aware), not used by any arm here:
     battery spent vs 0.3*d:  mean 2.11-2.91  p95 10.20-12.60  MAX 19.30
     steps minus d:           mean 6.1-10.3   p95 24-44        MAX 65

Mechanism 2 flies the Manhattan path essentially exactly; its whole overhead is
BLOCKED steps at 0.1 each, and the worst case observed (11 blocked steps = 1.10
points) is ONE CONTIGUOUS STANDOFF against a UAV parked in the approach corridor,
not blocking proportional to leg length. That is the same corridor that produces
the deadlock in 0.4.

3.4  THE FUEL CONSTRAINT, and why it is not sufficient
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Trigger, evaluated live from the UAV's own position:
      return when  battery <= 0.3 * d_nearest + MARGIN
0.3 = battery_drain_per_step + battery_drain_per_move, the cost of one moving
step; d_nearest is the Manhattan distance to the nearest of this UAV's berths.

The fuel-only margin, every term measured or a constant of the source:
  blocked-step allowance   1.10   measured MAXIMUM over 137 mechanism-2 trips
                                  (11 blocked steps x 0.1). p99 is 0.10; the

3.5  THE HORIZON CONSTRAINT, UNFROZEN - and this is the margin
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The shipped reserve is max(fuel, horizon) evaluated at the WORST CASE D = 90; the
horizon term is what selected 60. Unfreezing d - the same "computed live from the
UAV's actual position" move the brief asks for, applied to the other constraint -
is what makes the trigger genuinely distance-based AND keeps the trip completing.

  trigger at step t where battery first <= 0.3d + M, outbound drain e per step:
      t = (100 - 0.3d - M) / e
  the return takes d steps plus its blocked steps, and must finish by H:
      t + d + blocked <= H
  =>  M  >=  [100 - e*H + e*blocked]  -  d*(0.3 - e)
  worst at d = 0, with e = 0.1 + 0.2*(5/6) = 0.26667 (3.1) and blocked = 11 (3.3):
      M  >=  100 - 64.00 + 2.9333            =  38.9333
  plus the trigger latency of 3.4                 +  0.30
                                             ------------
      BASE_STATION_RETURN_MARGIN             =    39.23

  with  UAV_RETURN_TO_BASE_RESERVE = 0.0 for arms B, C and D. A reserve of 0 is
  not a special case: the trigger is a max() over battery levels, which are
  non-negative, so 0 is the identity element and "reserve 0" means exactly "no
  flat floor", with no code change.

6.6  INSTRUMENT DRIFT - the plan, decided before the wave

The hazard, recorded in memory as baseline-instrument-drift and hit for real in the

it is cheap, and the merge round is why this round does not rely on that
reasoning holding.

One restartable queue through outputs/_dim_pool.sh, which is memory-aware: the
ceiling on this box is COMMIT CHARGE, not RAM or cores, and only ~13-15 concurrent
simulation processes fit at ~0.85-0.9 GB each. MAXPAR=10 MINFREE_KB=3000000 is what
the previous round's 144-run queue used. Measured effective parallelism on this box
is ~7 rather than MAXPAR, because the binding constraints are free commit and the
25 s launch gap - so budget on ~7, i.e. roughly 5 hours for the harness runs, with
the rbgate shards after. The pool skips any run whose output JSON already exists
and is non-empty, so the wave is restartable. Queue files must be LF; a CR in the
last field reaches argparse as a stray argument.
