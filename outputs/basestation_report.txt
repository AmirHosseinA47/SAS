================================================================================
1. WHAT WAS BUILT
================================================================================

1.1  The depot
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A model-level region, `WildFireModel.base_station`, built in reset() from
configuration constants before any agent is placed. It holds the origin, the
size, the frozen cell set, the ranked berth list and the per-unit assignment.

It adds no agent, consumes no RNG, shifts no unique_agents_id and does not touch
the fire model - set_fire_agents, new_fire_agent and the Fire class are
untouched. That choice is vindicated by the measurement: burnt_cells is identical
across every arm (1.0 above).

Fire spreads into the depot exactly as into any other cell and nothing models the
depot being destroyed. UAVs are fire-immune - _check_fire_casualties
(wildfire_model.py:4679-4851) kills victims and firefighters and contains no UAV
branch at all - so a hardened pad would buy no behaviour while inserting a 5x5
firebreak straight into burnt_cells.

Corner: NW, x in [0,4] and y in [WIDTH-5, WIDTH-1], origin (0,45) at 50x50.

1.2  Spawn
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Berths are ranked by DESCENDING distance from the nearest grid edge - deliberately
the same key the executor's own margin-3 edge filter uses - so the units handed
berths first have the most legal moves out of the block. UAVs take the front of
the ranking and firefighters the next slice: a contiguous prefix, so the two sets
cannot collide and neither is pushed onto the outer ring, where a firefighter's
4-connected retreat options would be halved by two walls.

At 4 UAVs and 2 firefighters:
  UAV berths          (4,45) (3,45) (3,46) (4,46)
  firefighter berths  (2,45) (2,46)
A pure function of (HEIGHT, WIDTH, origin, size, NUM_AGENTS, NUM_FIREFIGHTERS),
drawing from no RNG. Overflow raises rather than wrapping, because wrapping would
stack two UAVs on one cell and cost them a blocked first move.

1.3  Return-to-base, both mechanisms
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The trigger, the docking, the recharge and the re-launch are shared, so the two
arms differ ONLY in the outbound routing and the comparison is clean.

  TRIGGER   battery <= max(RESERVE, 0.3 * manhattan_to_berth + MARGIN)
  DOCKING   at the assigned berth, or on any depot cell already reached if some
            other UAV is standing on that berth
  HOLD      UAV.move() returns False for a docked UAV, above the pipeline check,
            because there is no "stay" action anywhere in the model
  RECHARGE  applied in _update_battery_after_step before the existing clamp
  RELAUNCH  at full charge

  MECHANISM 2, hardcoded (the shipped default). _apply_return_to_base runs as the
  first statement of advance(), the last-writer-wins slot for selected_dir, and
  sets all three fields the executor would set. Setting only selected_dir would
  produce a silent permanent hold. FIRE-BLIND by construction.

  MECHANISM 1, planner. A per-UAV local option carries the berth as a waypoint
  through PathDecision to the executor. Retains the hard-safety filter and the
  fire, smoke and fire-probability penalties, so it is FIRE-AWARE.

Mutually exclusive by selector: mechanism 2's override is the last writer of
selected_dir before move(), so a "both" arm would measure only one of them while
appearing to measure both.

1.4  The charging state
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On the grid, parked on a berth, KEEPING ITS ROLE, with no filter added to any
roster builder. Measured consequence in 3.5: zero reshuffles.

At mode 2 a returned UAV parks and nothing recharges it, so it is labelled
"docked", not "charging" - calling it charging would hide the cost the
return-only increment exists to measure.

1.5  Rendering
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The depot is an OVERLAY on both surfaces, never a cell colour: all 2500 cells
carry a Fire agent and both colour deciders test smoke/burning/burnt/scorched
first, so a depot painted into the cell colours would vanish exactly when the
corner burns.

  dashboard  one rect descriptor in the frame payload, stroked in drawMap between
             the gridline stroke and the trails loop - immediate-mode canvas, so
             statement position is z-order: above the ground, below every marker.
  mesa       perimeter bars at LAYER 2 in CanvasGrid.render, the same mechanism as
             the existing UAV observation frame. Verified: 20 portrayals, Layer
             exactly int 2 (a fractional layer would draw OVER the victims,
             because CanvasModule.js enumerates non-index keys last), interior
             cells unpainted so the ground stays readable, nothing emitted when
             the feature is off.

Colour #770099, an inline literal in both renderers exactly as #2b2b2b, #895e00
and #2f4a1a already are. A CIEDE2000 sweep over the 39 co-occurring map colours
puts it at a minimum separation of 28.76 dE against 27.34 for the already-shipped
scorched #895e00 on the identical set.

Also added, display only: battery_status (computed every step on the agent since
before this round and never published) and base_state ("" / returning / docked /
charging), with the serve_dashboard._slim_panel whitelist updated - that
whitelist is a silent-break site where a forgotten key is dropped with no error
and no test failure. The legend gains a base-station swatch and LOSES the
"spared veg" swatch, which advertised a colour the canvas has never painted
(0 of 102,500 emitted cells, re-measured at HEAD).

The observation-radius frame is NOT in this round, per the maintainer.

1.6  The kill switch
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BASE_STATION_MODE, an ordinal ladder 0/1/2/3, read at CALL TIME through the
common_fixed_variables MODULE by accessors in agents.py - never through the
star-imported names and never at import time. Five entry points each carry a
top-of-function guard: depot construction, spawn placement, return trigger,
docked hold, recharge tick.

2. WHAT THE IMPLEMENTATION FOUND - the mechanism (a) diagnosis
================================================================================
The largest finding of Part 2. The design predicted mechanism (a) was feasible
through the per-UAV local path channel. It is - and the chain up to the planner
worked first time: the option was generated on 26 of 26 eligible steps and
SELECTED on 26 of 26. The UAVs nonetheless flew AWAY from the depot on every
step. Three further gates sat downstream, and each refuted a different
assumption.

  GATE 1 - the action token is rewritten.
  local_uav_path_planner.py:149-157 replaces next_action with a CARDINAL word
  whenever the option carries a target_position and is not the wind-aware search.
  "return_to_base" reached the executor as "west", so any guard keyed on the
  action alone is dead. FIX: key the executor guard on the option id as well,
  which survives the rewrite and is the same marker the dispatcher's own
  _path_consistent_with_return_to_base test uses.

  GATE 2 - two geometry filters override the chosen direction.
  For searchers the interior-hazard gate (shipped at 16b2da8, always on at range
  99) replaces the direction with a hazard-scored retreat; for trackers
  _apply_final_direction_safety pushes a UAV near a boundary back toward the
  interior. A depot berth is by construction near a boundary. Measured: searchers
  oscillated one cell short of the berth indefinitely, trackers walked away from
  it. FIX: exempt a return leg from both, exactly as the executor already exempts
  its own deliberately pathfinding-routed escapes. Both are GEOMETRY filters, not
  fire filters - leaving them in place does not make the return safer, it makes
  it impossible.

  GATE 3 - the direction chooser is a DISPERSION function.
  _choose_best_direction subtracts 6.0 * (1 - d/8) for every OTHER UAV within the
  observation radius, plus a visit penalty, against a distance term of 0.1 per
  cell. With four UAVs converging on one 5x5 block the separation term alone
  reaches -18. These are coverage heuristics that exist precisely to stop UAVs
  clustering; a depot is the one target in this model they are supposed to
  converge on.

  THE CONTROL THAT PROVED IT. With a SINGLE UAV and no other change, mechanism 1
  works perfectly: trigger at step 3, converge (7,45) -> (6,45) -> (5,45) ->
  (4,45), dock at step 7, recharge to 100, re-launch at step 8, second trip at
  step 10. With four UAVs, zero arrivals. The cause is the inter-UAV term, not
  the planner chain.
  FIX: skip the two dispersion terms for a return leg and let progress dominate,
  as it already does for a victim approach. The fire, smoke, fire-probability and
  hard-safety filters are NOT skipped, which is what keeps mechanism 1 genuinely
  fire-aware.

Every one of the three changes is guarded so it cannot fire unless a base-return
path decision exists - which is why the kill-switch arm is still byte-identical.

WHAT IT MEANS. Mechanism 1 is not "the existing machinery used unchanged".
Reaching a corner depot required relaxing three behaviours that exist to spread
UAVs out and keep them off edges. That is a finding about the architecture, not a
defect in the feature: the executor has no concept of a destination the fleet is
supposed to SHARE.

A CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF'S PREMISE, carried over from Part 1 and confirmed here:
the frozen 12-field MissionDecision was never the blocker, and the "0.01 loss on
1,280 emergency decisions" was measured on the forced crit12 battery arms, not on
a stock run - a stock 240-step canonical run enters EMERGENCY zero times. The
one-line tuple reorder in fail_safe_planner.py was therefore diagnosed and
DECLINED rather than shipped as a tenth measured-inert change.

3.6  THE KEY QUESTION - does recharging cost more coverage than it saves?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEITHER. It is outcome-neutral, and the cost belongs entirely to the return leg.

  bsfull - bsret  =  0 on rescued, dead, never_detected, unreachable,
                     geographically_isolated, horizon_unresolved, candidate,
                     firefighter_deaths and burnt_cells, across all 13 runs.

  bsret - bsspawn =  never_detected +3, unreachable +3, candidate -3,
                     with rescued, dead and firefighter_deaths unchanged.

So the whole coverage loss - three victims moving from "unresolved" to "never
detected" - is bought by flying home, not by charging.

The step budget says the same thing. Of 12480 UAV-steps in the canonical sample:
  return legs   2182 steps   17.5%
  charging       584 steps    4.7%
The design predicted the return leg would dominate the charge roughly four to
one. Measured it is 3.7 to 1 on steps, and infinite on outcomes, because the
charge contributes no outcome change at all.

WHY, mechanically: at a reserve of 60 there is one cycle per UAV per run. The
trigger fires near step 136, arrival near step 178, the charge takes about
11 steps, and the re-launch lands near step 190 - leaving roughly 50 steps of
re-flight, too late to change any victim's fate at this horizon. Recharging would
only start paying at a longer horizon or a higher reserve, and section 5.4 of the
design already declined to raise the reserve to force more cycles.

WHAT RECHARGING DOES BUY, even while outcome-neutral:
  - every return completes. bsfull is the only arm with 52/52 arrivals; bsret
    leaves 5 UAVs still flying home at the horizon.
  - it halves the fire exposure of the return-only arm (3.06% of UAV-steps on a
    burning cell against 4.40%), because UAVs do not sit parked in the corner for
    the last 60 steps of the run.

3.8  Behavioural costs of the corner spawn, measured
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first full-suite run gave 11 failed / 520 passed. The totals reconcile - 508
baseline tests plus 23 new ones is 531, and 520 + 11 = 531 - so three
previously-passing tests broke. All three had one root cause, and it is a real
property of the feature rather than a defect:

A corner spawn puts every UAV at boundary distance <= 4 from step 0, so
_victim_near_edge_escape_required (<= 5.0) is true immediately.

  test_wind_aware_victim_search::..._when_downwind_exists
      asserts the bare label "victim_search_wind_aware"; it becomes
      "victim_search_wind_aware_retarget_to_interior".
  test_victim_searcher_scenario_matrix::test_no_crash_zero_victims
      asserts strict_fire_smoke_steps <= 2; it becomes 3.
  test_wind_aware_trajectory_regression::test_all_cardinals_...
      asserts the four winds drive the searcher into distinct regions; every run
      now starts in the same corner, which compresses the early trajectories.

Each was pinned to BASE_STATION_MODE=0 with monkeypatch, so nothing leaks to the
next test, and each pin carries a comment naming this report. The pins are
defensible - their subject is the wind-aware searcher, not the depot, and they
already pin NUM_AGENTS and wind the same way - but the behaviour behind them is
NOT waved through. Measured across the whole canonical sample:

  UAV-steps on a BURNING cell   bsbase 1.44%   bsspawn 3.07%   (+1.63 points)
  UAV-steps in SMOKE            bsbase 1.50%   bsspawn 2.78%   (+1.28 points)
  mean distance to nearest fire bsbase 8.88    bsspawn 9.96

Corner-spawned UAVs sit FURTHER from the fire on average yet cross it more than
twice as often - the trajectory from the corner runs through the front instead of
skirting it. This is the same metric family the interior-hazard round tuned
(searcher steps on a burning cell, 2.1% -> 3.2%), so it is worth stating plainly.
It has no mechanical consequence, because UAVs are fire-immune; it is a realism
and behaviour signal, not an outcome cost.

Full exposure table, canonical:
  arm       burning   smoke   mean dist to fire
  bsbase     1.44%    1.50%        8.88
  bsspawn    3.07%    2.78%        9.96
  bsret      4.40%    4.62%       11.48
  bsretp     4.04%    3.97%       11.19
  bsfull     3.06%    2.80%       10.37
  bsfullp    3.30%    3.01%       10.22

Note that the fire-aware route is NOT reliably safer on this measure: it is
0.36 points better at mode 2 and 0.24 points worse at mode 3. The fire-awareness
of mechanism 1 shows up as a slower, longer route (3.4), not as measurably less
time in fire.

two units. It is a concrete defect signal rather than a threshold judgement, and
it is NOT explained by the never_detected story: both units are firefighters and
their positions ([0,24] and [30,6]) are nowhere near the depot, so the mechanism
is indirect and is NOT yet diagnosed.

The rescued result also directly contradicts the canonical sample. Rescued across
every seed-run measured this round:
  canonical 13    35 -> 38   (+3)
  fresh 10        29 -> 29   ( 0)
  rbgate 18       53 -> 48   (-5)
  TOTAL 41       117 -> 115  (-2)
The canonical +3 was sample luck. The honest summary is that rescued is flat to
slightly negative, and the variance between samples is larger than the effect.

4.2  What this does and does not mean
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It does NOT mean the implementation is wrong. Everything checkable for
correctness checks out: the kill switch is byte-identical on 23 runs, the fire
model is provably untouched (burnt_cells identical on every arm), no UAV is ever
stranded, the partition never reshuffles, the design's arithmetic matches the
measured arrival battery to one decimal place, and the failing test name set is
exactly the baseline's.

What fails is the DEFAULT. Turning the feature on moves 17.5% of UAV-steps from
searching to flying home, and the mission metrics move with it. That is the
feature working as specified; the specification costs coverage.

4.3  Options, for the maintainer
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (a) SHIP WITH BASE_STATION_MODE = 0. The depot, both return mechanisms, the
      recharge and both renderers all ship and are covered by 23 unit tests, but
      the model's default behaviour is unchanged and every gate passes by
      construction. The supervisor's requirement is met in code and demonstrable
      by flipping one constant; it is not met in the default run.
  (b) SHIP MODE 1 - spawn from a visible depot, no return leg. Best measured
      profile of any armed arm on the canonical sample (+3 rescued, -2 dead,
      -4 firefighter deaths, never_detected +1) with no return leg to pay for.
      It does NOT satisfy the return-to-base and recharge requirements, and it
      has NOT been through the route_blocked gate - that is another 18-run wave
      before it could be claimed.
  (c) SHIP MODE 3 AND ACCEPT THE TRADE, on the argument that firefighter deaths
      fall by two thirds (15 -> 5 over 23 runs) while rescued is flat. This means
      overriding two gate items and reopening the 70e1b33 latch category, and I
      do not recommend it without first diagnosing the seed-808 latch.
  (d) DIAGNOSE THE LATCH FIRST. Two firefighters permanently route_blocked on one
      seed is the only finding here that looks like a defect rather than a
      trade-off, and it should be understood before any of (a)-(c) is chosen.

CHOSEN: (a) now, (d) next. BASE_STATION_MODE ships at 0. The feature stays
complete and killable in the tree while the latch is understood, rather than
shipping a default that reopens a closed defect category.

4.4  A pattern this round is the third instance of
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A gain measured on the canonical 13 evaporated under wider measurement:

  rescued   canonical 13   35 -> 38   (+3)
            fresh 10       29 -> 29   ( 0)
            rbgate 18      53 -> 48   (-5)
            all 41        117 -> 115  (-2)

The +3 was reported as a result before the 18-seed sample existed, and it was
sample luck: the between-sample variance is larger than the effect. This is the
THIRD time in this campaign that a canonical-sample gain has failed to survive a
wider one - _reset_idle_retreat_state and the dimension fix were the first two.
The canonical 13 is a fast screen, not evidence of an effect, and a result should
not be stated as one until it has been seen on a second, independent seed set.
