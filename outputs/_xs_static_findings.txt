  (1) THE RULE. Each carrying advance goes straight to agents.py:1819-1854 and calls
  `_move_toward(exit_target)` (2369-2464):
  - **Neighbours.** It takes the 4 in-bounds neighbours in a fixed order: +x, -x, +y, -y
  (1958-1965). Burning cells are dropped (2395).
  - **Flags per remaining cell.** improving or maintaining; adjacent_fire (any 4-neighbour
  of that cell is burning; out-of-bounds counts as not burning, 1913); smoke (the cell's own
  Fire agent's smoke); preferred (the larger-axis step toward the target, x winning ties);
  risk = 100*adjacent + 10*smoke.
  - **No neighbour left.** It marks route_blocked and returns without moving (2411-2413).
  This is the only way the unit holds still.
  - **Tiers.** Tier 1 is improving and clean. Tier 2 is maintaining and clean, and it is
  always empty: every step changes Manhattan distance by exactly +/-1. Tier 3 is any clean
  cell, which in practice means a clean cell that moves away. The first non-empty tier wins,
  with min by (dist_after, not-preferred) and then list order. Because tier 3 never contains
  the preferred cell, its choice is pure neighbour order. Tier 4 is min over all non-burning
  cells by (risk, dist_after, not-preferred).
  - **No memory, no hold, no timeout.** The step is a pure function of (pos, exit_target,
  burning set, smoke set, bounds). The reachability search is off for carrying units
  (2387-2391).
  - **Checked against recorded data.** I re-derived the rule from the recorded neighbour
  flags for all 210 carrying `_move_toward` calls in the three traces. It matched the actual
  cell and tier 210/210. No call had a maintaining neighbour.

  (2) HOW IT OSCILLATES. Take p at distance k from the exit and q next to it at k+1.
  - At p every improving neighbour is burning, fire-adjacent or smoky, and q is the first
  clean worsening neighbour in list order. So tier 3 moves p->q.
  - At q, p is a clean improving cell that wins the tie-break. So tier 1 moves q->p.
  - The tier sequence is 3,1,3,1... It is stable for as long as the board around p and q
  does not change.
  - There is also a tier-4 version (4,4). It happens when no clean neighbour exists and the
  exit has a higher risk than a neighbour that moves away.

  At the exit itself: from distance 1, the only improving cell is the exit cell E. So E is
  refused whenever:
  - E is burning, or
  - any of its 3 in-grid neighbours is burning (this includes the unit's own cell), or
  - E is smoky.

  A smoky-only E has risk 10. It is entered in tier 4 when every alternative is fire-
  adjacent, but refused as soon as a single clean distance-2 cell exists. In other words,
  the exit is refused exactly when the unit's surroundings are safe.

  How long it lasts depends on smoke timing (Smoke class 172-220, Fire.step 89-115):
  - Smoke switches on at the 3rd smoke_step call after the cell is first seen burning
  (SMOKE_PRE_DISPELLING_COUNTER=2).
  - It stays on for F+1 more calls, where F is the cell's initial fuel, 7-10.
  - Calls happen every fire tick (every 3 steps) while the cell is not burnt, and every step
  once it is burnt.
  - So a cell that burns briefly and is left scorched with fuel remaining smokes for about
  3(F+1) = 24-33 steps, all after its fire has gone out. A cell that burns out continuously
  smokes for about 16-25 steps, mostly while still burning.
  - Burning itself is not permanent. It is re-drawn every fire tick from the radius-3
  neighbourhood while fuel remains, so it can go out and restart. That corrects
  moveoscillation_part1.txt section 6.2, which says a cell burns at most once. Smoke really
  is one-shot.

  - Mode 1 is F1: a carrying unit completes the rescue on any boundary cell.
  - Mode 2 is F5 plus F1. F5 is a new candidate: each carrying step, the unit takes the
  first step of a stateless breadth-first search over clean cells to the nearest boundary
  cell. A clean cell here is one that is not burning, not next to fire and not smoky, which
  is the same filter _move_toward uses for its tiers 1-3. If no clean route exists, the unit
  falls back to the existing _move_toward(exit_target).

  F1 is the smallest change and has no downside anywhere in the replays: no leg got longer
  and exposure did not rise. But it only removes the stall where the unit is already on the
  boundary. That is the maintainer's example: uhD east/half/1433805104 is on the boundary at
  step 218 and would complete at 219, so the leg takes 13 steps instead of 76. It fixes only
  1 of the 7 ungated stalled legs.

  F5+F1 is the smallest change that removes the actual mechanism. The carrying leg uses a
  greedy one-step rule with no memory. It prefers any clean cell over progress. When the
  cell that would improve distance is next to fire or smoky, tier 3 takes a worsening clean
  cell. For an exit on x=0 or x=49 that is the straight reverse step, because of the
  neighbour order at agents.py:1961. From there the cell it just left is clean and improving
  again, so the unit steps back. The result is a two-cell back-and-forth that can last
  dozens of steps.
