1.3 Which choices actually matter (SCALING vs HOLDING give materially different runs)

  MATERIAL:
    - the never-detected timeout (E): 210 vs budget-30 decides whether victims found late
      are rescued or written off - Part 2.6;
    - the SCALE group A/B taken together: corridor camping detectors that fire on the
      wrong half of the grid, and a coverage mode that starts a traverse too early or
      too late - Part 2 arm A vs arm B is exactly this comparison;
    - ignition margin 10 (fraction vs absolute) - changes the fire/victim geometry;
    - COVERAGE_SWEEP_BAND_MARGIN / WIND_EDGE_MARGIN: material in the WRONG direction if
      scaled (unswept edge strips); the correct choice is to hold.
  COSMETIC (hold or scale, no measurable difference expected):
    - COVERAGE_INTERIOR_X_MIN/MAX (inert fallbacks), WIND_INTERIOR_MARGIN band share,
      list truncations (global_monitor.py:263 gaps[:50], wildfire_model.py:3639
      audit[-50:], serve_dashboard.py timeline[-25:]), knowledge_manager % 25 cadence.
  NOT A CHOICE (physics; changing them changes the simulator, not the grid):
    - everything in D.



PART 2 - THE STEP BUDGET, MEASURED
----------------------------------
2.0 Method
  - Probe: outputs/_gs_probe.py, a copy of the evaluate_scenarios._run_seed sequence with
    read-only observers and runtime monkeypatches (no source edits). The grid is set on
    common_fixed_variables BEFORE the first simulation import, which is what a WIDTH/
    HEIGHT source edit does for every star-import and for global_monitor's import-time
    binding. The model's dimensions are exposed on the class (--expose-dims) in arms A/B/C,
    because without it the 100x100 managing system is pinned to 50 (Part 3 W1, raw100).
  - Canonical mix: scenario D, the campaign's 13-run sample (D/east half-roles x5, D/south
    half-roles x5, D/east default-roles x3; seeds 101..505), BATCH_SIZE = 960 (W2).
  - Budgets 240/500/960 are in-run checkpoints of ONE 960-step run per seed. The model has
    no knowledge of the budget (BATCH_SIZE only arms the step-302 exit guard), so its
    state at step S is the state of a run with budget S. Validated: the probe at 50x50/240
    reproduces all 13 recorded _ffr_latbase_* runs exactly (evaluation, fire digest at
    step 240, stdout sha256).
  - Performance patch: --fast-nsb, an exact re-implementation of _never_seen_proximity_
    bonus (2.7). It caches, once per (map, step), the dict position of every never-seen /
    stale cell and accumulates the same terms in the same order, so the float result is
    bit-identical; --verify-nsb re-runs the original on every 50th call at 50x50 (13 runs)
    and every 200th call at 100x100: 418,575 verified calls (of 66.7M) across all 71
    fast-path runs, 0 mismatches.
  - Coverage definition: a cell counts as covered once it has been inside a UAV's 17x17
    observation square (UAV_OBSERVATION_RADIUS 8, clipped to the grid; initial position
    included). "searcher-only" = UAVs whose role is victim_searcher, "all-UAV" = every
    UAV. The model's own visibility_model.visible_cells fraction is reported alongside.
    Under this definition the 50x50 reference at step 240 is 0.84 searcher-only (0.60-
    0.76 with one searcher, 0.77-0.99 with two) and 0.93 all-UAV - higher than the ~56%
    the brief quotes, which came from a different measure (one searcher's own footprint
    in an earlier round); both role groups are reported separately below.
  - Detection is Euclidean distance <= 8 from ANY UAV (wildfire_model._detect_victims_in_
    uav_radius); "first detection" below means the victim's first confirmed/assigned/
    rescued status - a write-off (candidate -> unreachable) is not a detection.
  - Runs per arm: 13 (A held, B scaled, C timeout-off) at 100x100/960 steps; 13 val50
    and 13 shim50 at 50x50/240; 2 raw100 at 100x100/300 without the shim; 2 single-group
    attribution runs at 100x100/300. Waves ran 12-13 processes at a time (the box is
    commit-limited, 3.6).

2.6 UNDETECTED_STREAK_STEPS = 210 - reachable-but-fair or guillotine, per budget
  Arm A (held) and arm C (off) together answer this exactly:
  - 240: 21 of 52 victims are still undetected at step 210 (1 of 52 at 50x50). The
    timeout fires with 30 steps to spare, as designed for 50x50, but at 100x100 the
    two-searcher sweep is nowhere near complete at 210 (searcher coverage 0.45): 13 of
    those 21 are detected later when the search is allowed to go on, 12 of them by step
    638. Guillotine.
  - 500: the timeout fires at 210, after 42% of the mission, and because the write-offs
    zero the unresolved count the searchers stop searching (arm A coverage flat from 240
    on). With the timeout off, 31 victims are rescued by 500 instead of 26 and 42 of 52
    are detected. Guillotine, with the extra cost that the remaining 58% of the budget
    buys nothing.
  - 960: same mechanism; 37 vs 26 rescues, 44 vs 35 detections. Guillotine.
  - A timeout that is derived from the budget (steps - 30, the rule its own comment
    states) is fair at every budget tested: with the timeout off nothing is written off
    that a firefighter could still reach (0 write-offs, 15 deaths in 960 steps versus 18
    with it held), and the five victims that are never found are found by nobody in 960
    steps. Keep the constant, derive it.

2.7 Runtime and memory (13 processes concurrent on a 32-core box, commit-limited)
  - Per-step wall (mean over 13 runs): steps 1-240 15.3 s (worst run 23.6 s), 241-500
    7.0 s, 501-960 4.4 s. The early phase is expensive because every searcher retarget
    scores the whole candidate grid while victims are unresolved; once the guillotine
    idles the searchers the step cost falls to the fire-physics floor (~4 s for 10,000
    Fire agents plus the dashboard-state poll).
  - Per run: mean 7,522 s (2.1 h), median 8,000 s, max 9,502 s (2.6 h) for 960 steps.
    Prorated: a 240-step run is ~1.0 h (3,677 s mean at the 240 checkpoint), a 500-step
    run ~1.5 h. At 50x50 the same 13 runs take 297-509 s (mean 433 s) for 240 steps.
  - These numbers are WITH the measurement-time fast path. Without it the stock
    _never_seen_proximity_bonus makes the early phase 78-155 s/step (measured on seed
    101: steps 2-5 took 78, 155, 153 and 112 s; the fast path gives 7, 14, 12 and 10 s
    for the identical state), so a stock 960-step run would take roughly 12-25 h and a
    240-step run 5-10 h. The function is O(cells) per candidate and is called for every
    candidate cell of _pick_global_coverage_escape_target (all interior cells), i.e.
    O(cells^2) per retarget - 16x the 50x50 cost. It must be fixed in source before a
    100x100 baseline is affordable; the probe's version is an exact, order-preserving
    drop-in (418,575 verified calls, 0 mismatches, across 71 runs at 50x50 and 100x100).
  - Memory: 0.85-0.96 GB commit per process (peak 957 MB at step 960), ~230 MB working
    set; identical to 50x50 within 10% because it is the library footprint. Thirteen
    concurrent runs need ~12.5 GB of commit; this box has ~14 GB of headroom once other
    applications are counted, which is why the waves were capped at 13.

PART 3 - WHAT BREAKS
--------------------
(what gives WRONG results, as opposed to what merely performs differently)

3.1 WRONG RESULTS - things that give incorrect answers on a 100x100 grid

  W1. The managing system never learns the grid size: every instance lookup defaults to 50.
      WildFireModel builds mesa.space.MultiGrid(HEIGHT, WIDTH) (wildfire_model.py:132) but
      never stores HEIGHT/WIDTH (or height/width/grid_height/grid_width) on itself. Every
      consumer that asks the MODEL for its size falls back to the literal 50:
        - agents.py:888-889  Firefighter exit target. The exit cell is the nearest of
          (0,y), (H-1,y), (x,0), (x,W-1) with H = W = 50. For a victim at x > 49 or y > 49
          the "distance" to (49,y) is negative, so it always wins: the unit walks INTO the
          grid to column/row 49, `pos == exit_target` fires there, the rescue is declared
          complete and the victim is finalised in the middle of the forest
          (agents.py:924-936), and the Feature 1 hand-over removes the unit from an
          interior cell. Rescue counts are inflated by rescues that never reached an edge.
          [raw100 evidence: see 3.3]
        - local_adaptation_generator.py:3369-3377 _grid_bounds -> (0, 49, 0, 49). Used by
          _compute_wind_aware_search_target (:3740) - the searchers' target generator
          scans candidates in range(x_min+1, x_max) x range(y_min+1, y_max) (:3462-3463),
          i.e. cells 1..48 only, and the cross-wind lanes (:332-362) split the 0..49 span,
          so both searchers are steered inside the south-west quarter of the grid; and by
          _resolve_fire_tracker_target_cells / _fire_tracker_path_parameters /
          _sanitize_fire_tracker_path_parameters (:2892, :2955, :3030), which clamp tracker
          holds to the same quarter. Three quarters of the grid are never targeted.
        - uav_executor.py:1877-1878 _bfs_escape_max_depth = max(100, 50+50+5) = 105 on a
          grid whose diameter is 198; :2050-2051 _victim_edge_blocked_direction treats
          x_max = 49 as the edge; :3774-3775 fire-tracker flank geometry; :1176-1183 sweep
          H/W (harmless in the canonical mix because the sector bounds override them);
          local_uav_path_planner.py:503-533 _grid_height/_grid_width/_uav_near_boundary.
      A one-line fix (self.HEIGHT = HEIGHT; self.WIDTH = WIDTH in reset()) resolves all of
      them. Part 2's arms A/B/C run with exactly that shim as a runtime monkeypatch
      (--expose-dims); the raw100 arm runs without it to show the damage.
      Side effect of the fix to know about: _grid_dimension (uav_executor.py:4613) starts
      returning a value, which brings _position_at_boundary (:540) and
      _distance_from_boundary (:552) to life. They are dead today at 50x50 (always
      False / 0.0), so the shim is a behaviour change at 50x50 too - see 3.4 shim50.

  W2. Any step budget above 301 silently truncates the run.
      WildFireModel.step() (wildfire_model.py:5269-5274) calls sys.exit(0) when
      BATCH_SIZE == evaluation_timesteps_counter - 1, i.e. on the 302nd step of a run
      configured with BATCH_SIZE=300. evaluate_scenarios.py passes --batch-size (default
      300) independently of --steps, and _run_seed's `except Exception` does not catch
      SystemExit. Demonstrated with the maintainer's own tool:
        python evaluate_scenarios.py --scenario D --wind east --n 1 --steps 320 --seeds 101
      -> exit code 0, stdout is the header line and the rule, nothing else: no per-seed
      row, no summary, no REPRODUCE line (the MR1/MR2 prints go into the redirected
      stdout buffer and are lost). A 500- or 960-step campaign needs --batch-size >=
      steps on every invocation, or the guard removed / BATCH_SIZE derived from --steps.
      The probe passes BATCH_SIZE = max(300, steps) for that reason.

  W3. global_monitor.py binds HEIGHT/WIDTH at import (:11-13; _grid_cell_total :62).
      Correct after a source edit; wrong (2500 cells) under any runtime override such as
      apply_scenario_config(cfv, wf, HEIGHT=100, WIDTH=100) - coverage_observed_fraction
      would exceed 1.0 and coverage_unobserved_fraction would be clamped at 0 from the
      first steps. Relevant to every harness that parametrises the grid without editing
      the source (the probe patches cfv BEFORE the first import to stay equivalent to an
      edit).

  W4. The never-detected timeout is a step-budget constant with a 240-step value.
      UNDETECTED_STREAK_STEPS = 210 (rescue_planner.py:52) is derived in its own comment
      as 240 - 30. Under a 500- or 960-step budget it writes off every still-undetected
      victim at step 210 regardless of how much mission remains, and because it is the
      kwarg default of unreachable_escape_victims (:463-464) it cannot even be changed by
      rebinding the module constant. Not wrong at 240; wrong by construction at any
      other budget. Quantified in Part 2.6.

  W5. Rectangular grids are not supported by the code as written (informational).
      MultiGrid(HEIGHT, WIDTH) uses HEIGHT as the x extent (wildfire_model.py:130-132 says
      so) while fire_runtime_model/visibility_model.initialize_grid(width=HEIGHT,
      height=WIDTH) (:183-184) swap the names again, and the harnesses' boundary tests
      assume x < HEIGHT, y < WIDTH. Square grids hide it; 100x100 is square, so this is
      a note, not a blocker.

4.3 Step budget - recommendation with the measurement behind it
  With the timeout HELD at 210 (arm A) no budget above 240 buys anything: 22 rescues at
  240, 26 at 500, 26 at 960; the timeout, not the budget, ends the mission (all 13 runs
  all-terminal by 374, median 210, by write-off). With the timeout DERIVED (arm C) the
  rescue count grows with the budget and saturates: 22@240, 25@300, 28@400, 31@500,
  34@600, 34@700, 36@800, 37@960 of 52; detections stop at step 638 (44 of 52 ever).
  RECOMMENDATION: 600 steps, UNDETECTED_STREAK_STEPS = 570, BATCH_SIZE >= 600, battery
  drain rescaled. 600 gives 92% of the 960-step rescues, matches the 50x50/240 absolute
  count (34 vs 35), keeps all but two detections, and costs 1.85 h per run (2.7 h wave)
  instead of 2.27 h (3.2 h). Choose 800 (timeout 770) for >= 97% of achievable rescues.
  240 at 100x100 is not a mission: 20 victims are still undetected at the horizon.

4.4 Cost of the new baseline
  - 13-run canonical sample at 100x100, 960 steps, 13 concurrent, WITH the fast path:
    2.6 h wall for the wave (per run 1.3-2.6 h, mean 2.1 h); 240 steps: ~1.0 h; 500: ~1.5 h.
    WITHOUT the fast path (stock source): ~12-25 h per 960-step run, so a wave is a day+.
  - Gates to regenerate: the 13-run canonical sample (outputs/_ffr_latbase_* and every
    arm derived from it), the route_blocked gate shards (_rblatch_camp2_*), the fixNN
    matrices (all 50x50, all with the boundary helpers dead), the Feature 1/2 numbers
    (9c3eac6, 5000f70), the lane-matrix baselines (already non-comparable), and the
    pytest expectations that embed 50-grid cells (3.2).
  - If the W1 fix is applied, the 50x50 gates need regenerating too (3.4), which is the
    cheap part: 13 runs x 240 steps = ~10 min per wave.
