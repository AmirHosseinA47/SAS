================================================================================
1. WHAT WAS BUILT
================================================================================
ONE TRIGGER, TWO BEHAVIOURS, TWO SWITCHES.

THE TRIGGER. `rtb_stall_steps` - CONSECUTIVE refused steps on a return leg, reset
by any successful move. Threshold BASE_STATION_RETURN_STALL_LIMIT = 3, DERIVED:
over every armed run on disk before this round, runs of consecutive refused steps
are 1 step (184 episodes) or 2 (5), then NOTHING AT ALL between 3 and 8, then 13
episodes of 9 to 65 steps - every one a stall the UAV never escapes. 3 sits inside
that empty gap, and a one-step transit produces at most ONE refused step, so it
cannot reach the limit BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by measured frequency.

MECHANISM 1 - the docking fallback is RE-KEYED, not debounced, not removed.
Its trigger changes from "is my berth occupied AT THIS INSTANT" to "has this leg
stopped moving". The old trigger fired on a ONE-STEP TRANSIT - mesa mutates the
grid during the advance() sweep, so a later-ordered UAV sees it - and permanently
re-assigned a berth. It was also SILENT where it was needed most, because it asked
about the BERTH: in all five mode-2 strandings the berth is FREE and stays free.
A debounce of that predicate would have asked the wrong question more slowly.
The dock CELL is unchanged: inside the latched depot. Section 5b says why.

MECHANISM 2 - a latched recovery. A leg that has stalled OUTSIDE its depot latches
the nearest free cell of that depot and steers to it ON EVERY STEP until inside,
where the ordinary steer and the re-keyed terminator take over. _rtb_direction is
NOT modified except for its comment: its distance-monotonicity is what bounds the
moves on a leg and makes a consecutive-stall counter a valid termination variant.

THE WAYPOINT FIX - a separate defect, a separate switch, measured apart.
BASE_STATION_WAYPOINT_FIX = 1. The mechanism-1 planner waypoint published the UAV's
HOME berth rather than the berth LATCHED for the trip. Section 4.10.

NO NEW RECORDED FIELD. NO HARNESS CHANGE. The "before" side of every comparison in
this report was already on disk and cost zero runs.

NO DOCK CELL IS OUTSIDE A DEPOT FOOTPRINT, before or after, in any arm. That is

BUT DOCKS ONTO ANOTHER UAV'S BERTH RISE FROM 7 TO 28, almost all of it in arm C
(1 -> 18). This is a genuine behavioural change and it is the one thing in this
round that looks worse on its face, so it is worth being plain about.

WHY IT HAPPENS. The old trigger fired the INSTANT a berth was occupied, wherever
the returning UAV happened to be standing - usually a plain cell it was passing
through, which is why 21 of arm C's 26 docks landed on plain cells. The re-keyed
trigger fires only after the leg has stopped moving for three steps, and a leg
stops moving where something is in its way - which, inside a depot, is
disproportionately a berth. The dock lands closer to the contention because that
is now what the trigger is measuring.

WHY IT IS BENIGN HERE, AND WHAT WOULD MAKE IT NOT. Displacing a UAV matters only
if the displaced UAV is then stranded. It is not: stuck is ZERO in every arm on
both samples (4.2), unarrived is unchanged, every trip that used to arrive still
arrives, arm B's arrivals go 89 -> 90 of 90, and rescued/dead/ff_deaths/
never_detected are identical to the unit (4.5). The dcB/808 trace shows the shape
of it directly - 2501 and 2502 swap berths and BOTH terminate, recharge and
relaunch. A berth swap is a resolution, not a collision.

What would make it harmful is a displaced UAV with no recovery, which is precisely
the hole mechanism 2 closes: a displaced unit can now reach the depot from
outside. The two halves are load-bearing together, and this table is the clearest
evidence for that - mechanism 1 alone would have moved MORE docks onto berths
while leaving the displaced units no way back.

5. WHAT THE ROUND EARNED - three things that are not footnotes
================================================================================
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. THE RECOVERY BUG THE ROUND CAUGHT IN ITS OWN DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first written design engaged the recovery ONLY ON STEPS WHERE THE UAV WAS
STALLED, and let the shipped greedy steer run otherwise. THAT IS PING-PONG WITH
EXTRA STEPS: the escape step MOVES the UAV, so on the next step it is not stalled,
the memoryless greedy toward the berth runs, and it walks straight back.

It was invisible because THE PROTOTYPE AND THE PROSE DISAGREED. The sandbox rule
steered to the latched cell on every step while outside the depot; the code
written up in the design document gated the same idea on `stalled`. The sandbox
therefore passed while the design was wrong, and only an adversarial read of the
prose found it. The lesson is specific: a prototype validates the RULE, not the
transcription of it, and those are different artifacts.

A SECOND BUG FOLLOWED IT, found by replay rather than by reading. Having latched a
new target, the first steer toward it was still being handed `stalled=True` - a
stall recorded against the BERTH. The shipped swap exists to break a deadlock
against the cell the UAV was refused moving toward; applied to a target it has
never been refused against, it swaps to the wrong axis, and at (4,44) that axis is
the blocked one. The stall is relative to a target. `rtb_recovery_steered` records
which target the previous steer was for, and the stall is carried over only when
the target is unchanged.

Both are pinned by tests: test_the_recovery_is_sticky_not_stall_gated and
test_the_recovery_cannot_livelock.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5b. WHY THE REVIEW'S OWN RECOMMENDATION WAS REJECTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The adversarial review recommended leaving the steer alone and letting the
terminator dock the UAV on the depot's one-cell DOORSTEP after L stalls. It
terminates, with a shorter proof, and touches less code. It was rejected on this:

    _update_battery_after_step (agents.py:312) gates the recharge on
    self.rtb_docked ALONE, with NO POSITIONAL TEST. The harness derives
    base_state from the same flag, also with no positional test.

So a doorstep dock at mode 3 REFUELS A UAV ON OPEN GROUND, one cell outside the
depot, and flips its recorded base_state from "returning" to "charging". The
round's stranding gate counts only UAVs whose final state is "returning".
A doorstep dock therefore CLEARS THE GATE WITHOUT THE UAV EVER COMING HOME: it
would have converted TWELVE flagged stalls into twelve unflagged open-ground
recharges and read as a clean sweep. Replayed, it docks at exactly (4,44) and
(3,44) - the two cells the gate currently flags.

The half of that recommendation that was RIGHT is adopted and is load-bearing: the
consecutive-stall trigger, and leaving _rtb_direction distance-monotone.

The premise is pinned by test_recharge_still_has_no_positional_test, so that if a
positional test is ever added to the recharge, the rejection can be revisited
rather than silently outliving its reason.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5c. THE THIRD TIME A GATE ASKED THE WRONG QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
After seed 909 counted as a latch when it was a horizon artifact, and after the
old zero-battery stranding gate scored the dcB deadlock as 0 because the UAV ended
with 35.4 battery in hand, this round found the third instance BEFORE spending the
wave rather than after:

    A UAV that MOVES every step while getting nowhere has an unchanged-position
    count of ZERO. The positional stranding gate - the redefined one, the one
    that CAUGHT the previous two failures - scores a livelock clean.

This is not hypothetical. The obvious minimal fix for the pure-axis hole ("when
stalled, do not re-pick a direction the grid already refused") livelocks in
exactly this way: replayed on the recorded mode-2 configuration it cycles
(3,44) -> (4,44) -> (3,44) forever, 20 revisits in 40 steps. It is also MORE
expensive than the freeze it replaces - 2 moves and 1 stall per 3 steps is 0.233
battery/step against 0.100 - and it puts a MOVING UAV onto cells a frozen one
never touches. A fix that turned the twelve freezes into twelve livelocks would
have passed every gate this round started with.

So cycle and oscillation detection are MANDATORY, not optional, and they are
defined carefully:
  CYCLE        the leg returns to a cell it had LEFT. Consecutive repeats are
               collapsed first, because standing still while a stall counter runs
               is not a cycle - that distinction is the whole test, and getting it
               wrong is what made the first version of this gate fail its own
               passing run.
  OSCILLATION  a 10-frame window inside a leg spent on at most 2 distinct cells
               while changing cell at least twice.

  RESULT, mechanism-2 arms:        cycles   oscillations
    before (dcA..dcD, bsret)          0          0
    after  (dfA..dfD)                 0          0
    controls (dfzero, dfkill)         0          0
    mode 2 (dfm2ref, dfm2fix)         0          0

At HEAD the failures are freezes and never cycles, so any non-zero result would
have been caused by the fix. There is none.

A LIMIT OF THIS GATE, stated rather than buried: it is valid for MECHANISM 2 and
NOT for mechanism 1. The mechanism-1 arms show 8-11 cycles each, most of them with
byte-identical traces in BOTH the fixed and unfixed arm - a fire-aware planner
legitimately backtracks, so a revisit there is not a livelock. That is why 4.10
counts only oscillations INSIDE A DEPOT, where the planner has no reason to
backtrack and the signal is attributable.

6. WHAT IS NOT FIXED
================================================================================
1. A RELEASED UAV IS NOT OBLIGED TO LEAVE ITS BERTH. In the dcB/808 trace at HEAD,
   2502 released at step 216 and never left (4,45), head-on with 2503, each holding
   what the other wanted - in the ORDINARY movement layer, not the RTB code. This
   fix removes the usual CAUSE of that pileup (the misplaced dock) without touching
   not_UAV_adjacent or the planner. It is a different blast radius and a different
   round. Nothing in this wave shows it persisting, but nothing here forecloses it.

2. THE RECOVERY IS NOT A PATHFINDER. Termination is guaranteed while the latched
   cell stays free and reachable by monotone greedy steps. If the approach to the
   LATCHED cell is itself pure-axis and blocked, the UAV re-latches to a different
   free cell; with at most NUM_AGENTS-1 other UAVs and 25 depot cells there is
   always another, but that is a property of this configuration, not a theorem. A
   depot packed to capacity is the case to watch, and the livelock gate is what
   would catch it going wrong.

3. L = 3 IS DERIVED FROM ONE GEOMETRY over 154 runs. The 3-to-8 gap is clean but it
   is one campaign. BASE_STATION_RETURN_STALL_LIMIT is a constant precisely so this
   is adjustable, and the termination guarantee holds for ANY finite L - a wrong L
   costs latency, not correctness.

4. THE BRIEF EXPECTED dcC TO BE THE STRESS CASE. It was right about contention and
   wrong about risk. dcC has 26 of the 34 fallback docks but 21 land on plain depot
   cells; its central depot absorbs contention. The arm that STRANDS is dcB, whose
   6 docks include 5 onto another UAV's berth. Dock count is not the risk measure -
   where the dock LANDS is.

5. THE REJECTED ALTERNATIVE REMAINS THE FALLBACK POSITION. If a future round finds
   the recovery causing exposure or livelock regressions, the doorstep terminator
   with its dock scoped to the depot INTERIOR is where to go - but it does not
   terminate on its own and would need the recovery back, so it is a trade to
   re-open then, not a simplification available now.
