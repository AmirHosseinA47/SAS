P.1 EVERY CITATION USED FOR FRONT PRIORITY (SEARCHER_TARGETING 5), AND WHAT EACH SUPPORTS
  Van Wagner 1969, "A simple fire-growth model", Forestry Chronicle 45:103-104
      - the elliptical shape of a free-burning, wind-driven fire;
      - the ignition point at the rear focus.
  Catchpole, de Mestre & Gill 1982, "Intensity of fire at its perimeter", Australian Forest Research 12:47-54
      - the polar form of the spread rate at angle theta from the head, R(theta) = R_H (1 - e) / (1 - e cos theta),
        with the ignition at the rear focus;
      - T(c) = |v| / R(theta) is this formula.
  Anderson, Catchpole, de Mestre & Parkes 1982, "Modelling the spread of grass fires", J. Austral. Math. Soc. B
  23:451-466; Richards 1990, Int. J. Numer. Methods Eng. 30:1163-1179
      - Huygens' principle: each point of the front spreads as an elliptical wavelet;
      - under homogeneous conditions the arrival time is the minimum over the front, the envelope.
  Cruz & Alexander 2019, "The 10% wind speed rule of thumb for estimating a wildfire's forward rate of spread in
  forests and shrublands", Annals of Forest Science 76:44
      - the head-fire rate R_H = 0.1 x U10 (10-m open wind) for forests and shrublands in dry conditions;
      - 2.0 km/h at U10 20 km/h;
      - the authors exclude grasslands.
  Andrews 2018, "The Rothermel surface fire spread model and associated developments", USDA FS RMRS-GTR-371,
  following Anderson 1983, USDA FS INT-305
      - the length-to-width ratio z = 1 + 0.25 U_mf (U_mf = effective midflame wind, mi/h), capped at 8;
      - the eccentricity e = sqrt(z^2 - 1) / z.
  Albini & Baughman 1979, USDA FS INT-221; Andrews 2012, USDA FS RMRS-GTR-266
      - the wind adjustment factor WAF = 0.4 from 20-ft to midflame wind for unsheltered surface fuels.
  Turner & Lawson 1978, Canadian Forestry Service BC-X-177
      - the 10-m to 20-ft wind conversion, U10 = 1.15 U_20ft.
  Weidmann 1993, Transporttechnik der Fussgaenger, ETH Zurich IVT Schriftenreihe 90
      - the mean free walking speed 1.34 m/s, which converts the fire's rate to cells / step. One cell per step is
        the fleeing person's movement quantum in the simulator (A6).
  Cova, Dennison, Kim & Moritz 2005, "Setting wildfire evacuation trigger points using fire spread modeling and
  GIS", Transactions in GIS 9(4):603-617; Dennison, Cova & Moritz 2007, Natural Hazards 41:181-199 (WUIVAC)
      - the idea of ranking space by modelled fire travel time (evacuation trigger buffers), which the urgency weight
        implements as a smooth function of the estimated arrival time.
  WMO Beaufort scale
      - U10 = 20 km/h is the lower bound of "moderate breeze" (force 4). An assumption: the simulator has no wind
        speed.
  NOT literature (stated as such):
      - kappa = 1, the operator's value judgement: a person the fire is about to reach counts up to twice a person
        not under threat;
      - tau = 16 steps = 2 x sensor radius / UAV speed, the time to cross one detection footprint (the sensor
        specification K5 and the simulator's movement quantum).
  WHAT THE CITED SHAPE IMPLIES (ruling D-2, recorded plainly):
      - At U10 = 20 km/h: R_H = 0.415 cells / step, z = 2.08, e = 0.877, head : back = 15.3.
      - The flanks spread at about 0.05 cells / step at every U10 in {10, 20, 40} km/h.
      - So the weight falls mostly AHEAD OF THE FIRE HEAD: w = 1.55 / 1.30 / 1.09 at 4 / 8 / 16 cells ahead.
      - The flanks count more only within a cell or two (w = 1.29 / 1.09 at 1 / 2 cells).
      - The shape was not tuned to give the flanks more weight.
  THE ESTIMATE IS NOT THE SIMULATOR'S RULE.
      - The simulator spreads fire stochastically per cell over 28 Euclidean-3 offsets weighted d^-2. It boosts
        only the three sources directly upwind on the same row or column (a line, not an ellipse). It ticks every
        3 steps, at most 1 cell per step.
      - The estimate is deterministic and elliptical, and its rate comes from the literature. It reads no simulator
        fire constant.
      - It is an operator model, so the inverse crime is avoided.

P.2 THE ITEM-1 EDGE DEFECTS, FIXED BEFORE ANY MEASUREMENT RUN (switch SEARCHER_TARGETING_FIX, ships ON)
  Verdict on the edge band:
    - The sensing arithmetic is exact at the edge (zero padding, clipping, prior support).
    - The band was under-observed by four defects of the method's implementation against its own design:
  (a) F1-a THE MOTION MODEL DID NOT REFLECT.
      - The diffusion walk and the flee model's calm component moved q/k to each available neighbour, so the
        stationary density was proportional to k.
      - Edge rows fell to 0.79x and corners to 0.55x of uniform after 360 steps, and mass was pushed off cells next
        to burning ones.
      - Design 2.4: "the grid edge reflects"; diffusion "is fire-agnostic in direction".
      - Fixed: q/4 per direction; a blocked move stays.
  (b) F1-b THE SCORE CREDITED A FOOTPRINT THAT WAS NEVER FLOWN.
      - The gain counted the sensor disc AT the target, but the leg was released 2 cells short of it.
      - At band-boundary targets the never-flown rim was the band itself. The searcher kept returning to the edge:
        outer-ring targets were 0.41-0.50 of issues against 18% of the lattice.
      - Fixed: the leg ends on the target when the target is admissible, and the gain and the coordination claims
        credit only the discs actually flown.
  (c) F1-c THE SWEPT RULE COUNTED THE HOLDER'S OWN APPROACH.
      - Design 3.5(d): a target is swept when "another UAV or the fire took its mass".
      - Fixed: the rule ignores what the holder itself has observed since issue.
  (d) F1-d THE TARGET LATTICE WAS COMPASS-ASYMMETRIC.
      - Edge distance 4 on the west / south sides, 5 on the east / north sides.
      - Fixed: a mirror-symmetric lattice with edge distance 4 on every side.
  Also fixed under the same switch:
    - F1-f: no strategy steers a searcher on the step its recall fires. Movement was already identical; now the
      management state is identical too.
    - F1-g: the planner's gain uses the belief's own P_d at the PD_SMOKE sensitivity point.
    - F1-h: the launch "boxed by UAVs" test uses the candidate filter's own rule.
  The fix3b screens and re-screen (and every Bayes number quoted before this round) PREDATE these fixes.

P.3 ITEM 5's GUARDS (switch UAV_DOCKED_NOT_OBSTACLE, ships ON)
  The move rule tests the DESTINATION cell only, an occupancy test. The earlier records' "adjacent" wording was wrong.
  (a) A UAV docked in a depot is on the ground: it does not block the cell for a mover that is not on a return leg.
      Return legs keep today's rule, so docking is unchanged by construction.
  The three guards that make (a) safe:
  (b) NO CO-DOCKING: a UAV never docks on a cell where another UAV is docked. Its own cell is not "free" then, and
      the leg steers to a free cell.
  (c) NO RELEASE UNDER ANOTHER UAV: a docked UAV is not released while another UAV stands on its cell. It relaunches
      the step after. Without this, a refused first move would leave two airborne UAVs on one cell.
  (d) THE GLOBAL COLLISION CHECK COUNTS ONLY AIRBORNE UAVs, like session 2's local collision alarm. Otherwise a UAV
      over a docked one would raise a CRITICAL COLLISION_RISK and the fail-safe.
  Firefighters, the dispatcher's yield rule (already airborne-only) and the planners' UAV-free route sets are
  unchanged.
