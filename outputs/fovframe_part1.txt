EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The feature is small and the dashboard half is genuinely ~10 lines, as the prior
round estimated. But four of the brief's premises are wrong, and one of them
would have silently produced a validation that proved nothing.

1. THE RING IS NOT AN OFF-BY-ONE, AND IT IS NOT A TIGHT BOUND EITHER. The mesa
   ring sits at Chebyshev exactly 9; the observed set is Chebyshev <= 8; the
   intersection is EMPTY (verified at all 2500 grid positions). Its interior is
   exactly the 289-cell FOV. So it is a border drawn strictly OUTSIDE the
   observed area - deliberate in effect, and forced given centred cell rects.
   But the bar is drawn at the ring cell's CENTRE, so the visible line lands 0.5
   cell beyond the true edge: an 18.0-cell span for a 17-cell footprint, a 12.1%
   area overstatement, with four OPEN corners. Section 1.

2. THE SQUARE IS EXACT, NOT AN APPROXIMATION - the brief has this backwards.
   Cell/fire observation is Chebyshev (moore=True), returning precisely the
   17x17 = 289-cell square. The brief's "detection is Euclidean, so a square is
   an approximation of a circular region" describes only VICTIM detection, a
   second and narrower consumer: Euclidean dist <= 8 over 197 cells. The square
   is the EXACT footprint for fire observation AND the EXACT bounding box of the
   victim disc. It overstates victim detection by 92 cells (31.83%), all of them
   in the four corners. Section 2.

3. BUILD NOTHING FOR THE FIREFIGHTER; FOR THE VICTIM, NOT A SQUARE. Rescue is
   SAME-CELL (radius 0), dispatch is unbounded, and the one real firefighter
   range is state-dependent (3 idle / 1 assigned) and Manhattan. The victim has
   exactly one real radius, VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE = 3 - but it is a
   MANHATTAN DIAMOND, and the square the brief asks for would put 24 of its 49
   cells (49%) outside the actual range. Section 3.

4. BLACK FAILS, AND SO DOES EVERY OTHER FLAT COLOUR. Black is dE2000 = 0.0000
   against five distinct semantic marks at six code sites, and leaves 63.40% of
   real frame cells below WCAG 3:1. An exhaustive sweep of 140,608 sRGB values
   puts a hard ceiling of 2.73 dE2000 on ANY single opaque colour - below the
   just-noticeable difference. The palette contains three full luminance ramps
   and has no free luminance slot. The answer is a TWO-TONE casing (white core,
   black casing): 38.37 dE2000 worst-case, 14x the single-colour ceiling.
   Section 4.

5. THE BRIEF'S POSITIVE CONTROL IS THE WRONG INSTRUMENT, AND WOULD HAVE PASSED
   BY LOOKING LIKE A FAILURE. `cells` is built inside a Fire-only loop; no UAV
   can contribute a byte. A cell-colour digest over a dashboard FOV overlay is
   guaranteed IDENTICAL - which is precisely the "the change was never
   exercised" reading the brief wants to rule out. cellcolor must be DEMOTED to
   a negative control and replaced by a three-layer instrument ending in a

1.2  IS THE RING ONE CELL TOO LARGE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured, exhaustively:

  UAV_OBSERVATION_RADIUS      = 8              (common_fixed_variables.py:475)
  limit                       = 9              (:112)
  observed set                = Chebyshev <= 8 = 289 cells (17x17)
  ring set                    = Chebyshev == 9 =  72 cells (19x19 perimeter)
  ring INTERSECT observed     = 0 cells        <-- verified at all 2500 positions
  interior of the ring        = 289 cells      = exactly the observed set
  ring at limit = 8 instead   = 64 cells, ALL 64 inside the observed set

So the ring does NOT paint over wrong cells. It is a border drawn on the cells
immediately outside the footprint, and what it encloses is exactly the FOV. In
CELL terms it is correct, not off-by-one.

In PIXEL terms it overstates. GridDraw.js defaults xAlign/yAlign to 0.5
(/usr/local/lib/python3.11/dist-packages/mesa/visualization/templates/js/
GridDraw.js:81-82), so each bar is centred in its ring cell - half a cell beyond
the FOV boundary. At the mesa surface's 10 px cells (main.py:115: 10*WIDTH over
WIDTH cells):

  true FOV block, x            170.0 .. 340.0 px
  right vertical bar           344.0 .. 346.0 px      -> 4.0 px of daylight
  left/right bar centrelines   165.0 and 345.0        -> 180.0 px = 18.0 cells

An 18.0-cell span for a 17-cell footprint: 324 vs 289 cell-areas, a 12.1%
overstatement for any viewer who reads the drawn line as the bound.

1.3  DELIBERATE, OR AN OFF-BY-ONE? - VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT: INTENTIONAL-BY-CONSTRAINT in its radius choice, CARELESS in execution.
It is not a bug to be "fixed" by moving the ring inward, and the dashboard
should match its INTENT (a border that occludes nothing) rather than its
PIXELS (a border 0.5 cell out with open corners).

    for fire observation it is the true shape (Section 2).
  - README.md:28-30 describes the file as "a Mesa class, modified for making UAV
    observation areas visible on the graphical web interface."

For careless:
  - Lines 111-127 carry no comment at all (measured: zero '#' in that range).
  - The only commit touching them is a4a01b4, a squashed whole-file import whose
    message is just "Add core project files and update README" - it states no

2.3  THE EXACT GEOMETRY - WHAT THE FRAME DOES AND DOES NOT REPRESENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Chebyshev square  (fire/cell observation)   289 cells   17x17
  Euclidean disc    (victim detection)        197 cells   bounding box 17x17
  square - disc                                92 cells
  overstatement of victim detection            92/289 = 31.83% of the frame
  disc as a fraction of the square            197/289 = 68.17%

The 92 mismatched cells lie entirely in the outer three Chebyshev rings and in
the corner regions: 4 at ring 6, 28 at ring 7, 60 at ring 8. Rings 0-5 are
identical under both metrics.

CORRECTING THE BRIEF. The brief says: "Detection is Euclidean (dist <= radius)
and iterates EVERY UAV with no role filter - so a square frame is an
approximation of a circular region. State that explicitly: the frame shows the
bounding box, not the true footprint."

That is right about victim detection and wrong as a general statement. The
honest sentence, which this design adopts:

    The square frame is the EXACT footprint of the UAV's cell/fire observation
    (Chebyshev radius 8, 289 cells - the same 289 that is the DQN's input
    width). It is ALSO the exact bounding box of the separate, narrower victim
    detection test, which is Euclidean over 197 cells; for that one purpose the
    frame overstates by 92 corner cells, 31.83%.

So the brief's instruction to say "bounding box, not true footprint" would
itself be inaccurate if applied to the frame as a whole. It is a true footprint
for the primary consumer and a bounding box for the secondary one.

Is the exemplar's box a UI convention rather than the real bound? NO - and this
is worth stating plainly because the brief allows for it. README.md:139 says
"technically it is not a radius, since observed areas have square shapes". The
square is the model's real observation shape. The only convention in the mesa
rendering is the +1 offset and the centred bar (Section 1.2).

apply_scenario_config setattrs only the cfv and wf modules

MAX_STEPS are durations in steps, not cell radii; IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS = 6 is
a leash on a remembered origin cell, not a radius around the unit.

VERDICT (b): no radius exists that a frame could honestly mean. A frame here
would be a pure MARKER - and it would be the FOURTH marker this entity already
carries (0.7-cell status rect, dashed assignment line, walked trail).

IF the maintainer wants firefighter information on the map, the honest artefact
is not geometry. `nearest_fire_dist` is already computed every step and thrown

existing firefighter table (serve_dashboard.py:746-747): one payload field, zero

3.2  VICTIM - ONE REAL RADIUS, BUT NOT A SQUARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE = 3 (common_fixed_variables.py:142) IS a genuine
radius centred on the victim. Spec at :122-126: "A victim holds its cell until
the nearest ACTIVELY BURNING cell is within VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE
(manhattan)". Live at agents.py:1070+. It fires on a measured 16.1% of victim
steps. A region around a victim at r=3 really does mean "the distance at which
it flees from fire" - the brief's reading is right on meaning.

BUT IT IS A MANHATTAN DIAMOND. A square of half-width 3 has 49 cells; the
diamond has 25. 24 of the square's 49 cells (49%) are outside the actual range,
and its corners sit at Manhattan 6 - double the trigger. Drawing the requested
square here would misstate the rule by a factor of two at the corners.

NOT candidates, for the record:
  - VICTIM_FLEE_MAX_DISPLACEMENT = 6 is a leash on `leash_anchor`, which
    measurably diverges from the victim's own cell (27.6% of advance calls, by
    up to 15 cells) precisely when the leash binds. A frame around the victim
    marker cannot mean it.
  - The detection radius is centred on the UAV and Euclidean; drawing it around
    a victim would duplicate the UAV frame in the wrong shape and place.

VERDICT (a) with a shape correction. If geometry is wanted, it must be a
Manhattan diamond at r=3, read through the accessor at draw time (an accessor
already exists) so the kill switch stays live, and suppressed for terminal
victims and victims in firefighter custody, for whom the rule does not run.

MY RECOMMENDATION: do not draw the diamond. At 5 victims it is five 25-cell
overlays on a map that already carries five full-cell status circles - and the
victim circle's diameter is exactly one cell (serve_dashboard.py:716), so
anything at r<=1 fuses with it. The same information at a fraction of the ink:
add a thin ring or colour shift to the EXISTING victim circle whenever the
trigger condition holds. That encodes "in danger / fleeing", a boolean the
current five-status colour map genuinely does not carry - and which is what an
operator scanning the map actually needs. Offer both; recommend the ring.

3.3  WHY THE UAV PRECEDENT DOES NOT TRANSFER

The UAV frame is a square because the UAV's observation set IS a Chebyshev

4.4  THE RECOMMENDATION: A TWO-TONE CASING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMEND: a 1 px #FFFFFF core inside a #000000 casing - drawn as two strokes of
the same rectangle path, lineWidth 3 in #000000 then lineWidth 1 in #FFFFFF, on
the Math.round(k*cs)+0.5 half-pixel convention the gridlines already use at
serve_dashboard.py:683-684.

The figure of merit for a two-tone mark is: for every background b, the line is
visible if EITHER tone separates, so score(b) = max(dE(t1,b), dE(t2,b)), and the
result is the minimum over b, taken at the worst vision condition.

  worst-case vs the 50 nominal colours      38.37 dE2000   (14.0x the flat ceiling)
  worst-case vs the 239 effective colours   36.55 dE2000   (35.5x)
  worst-case in probability mode            40.32 dE2000
  internal contrast (core vs casing)       100.00 dE2000 / 21.00:1 / dL* = 100
  min best-tone WCAG over real frame cells   4.79:1
  frame cells below WCAG 3:1                 0.00%   (against 63.40% for black)

My own independent computation of the same scheme against the 50-colour union
gives 40.32 worst-case across colour, greyscale and all three CVD types, versus
1.74 for the best flat colour - agreeing with the above to within the difference
in background sets.

AND IT CARRIES A GUARANTEE NO PALETTE-DERIVED COLOUR CAN. For ANY background
lightness in [0,100], the better of black and white is at least 50.0 L* away -
minimum best-tone contrast 4.61:1 over the entire grey axis. This holds no matter
what colours the palette gains later. That is why it survives the dead #2f4a1a
mute being repaired, a new scenario palette, or another ground state being added.

REJECTED, WITH NUMBERS:
  1. BLACK (the exemplar)      - 0.0000 against five semantic marks; 63.40% of
                                 frame cells below 3:1.
  2. WHITE                     - 0.0000 against BW[0] and the probMode canvas;
                                 1.11:1 against #fefa01.
  3. EVERY high-chroma hue     - ceiling 2.73 dE. The normal-vision winner
                                 #990033 scores 25.97 and collapses to 0.34
                                 achromatic. This is precisely the failure for
                                 which the scorched round rejected #66301c.
  4. ROLE-COLOURED CORE        - collapses to 0.00 internal contrast for the
                                 "Black" role fallback (the one role for which
                                 the operator has no other cue), and is weak at
                                 17.56 for relay #0066cc. See also 5.3.
  5. DASHING AS THE FIX        - zero visibility gain: a dash alternates the same

                                 (setLineDash([5,4]) at :712).

TWO IMPLEMENTATION CONSTRAINTS, both load-bearing:
  - PIXEL ALIGNMENT IS NOT COSMETIC. At an unaligned alpha=0.5 pixel straddle,

5.2  PER-UAV, NOT MERGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: draw one outline PER UAV and let them overlap. Reject the merged union.

  - A merged union buys only 26% less line (measured: 150.8 vs a constant 204
    cell-edge units) while destroying per-UAV identity, because the union is a
    SINGLE CONNECTED BLOB on 62% of steps (37/60; 2 components on 23/60).
    At spawn, three UAVs would produce a 72-edge outline essentially
    indistinguishable from one UAV's 68-edge frame.
  - It costs a marching-squares pass over ~870 cells per animation frame instead
    of three strokeRect calls.
  - Per-UAV frames preserve the one thing an operator cannot recover any other
    way: HOW MANY sensors cover a given cell. That is the actual operational
    question a coverage overlay answers.

5.3  BUT NOT PER-ROLE COLOUR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Colouring each frame by its UAV's role is tempting - it is even what mesa ships
by accident (1.5) - and it is measurably wrong. Each role's colour has its WORST
contrast on exactly the terrain that role operates over:

  fire_tracker      #FF00FF  on hot fire #fe0101       contrast 1.08  (1.00 = invisible)
  victim_searcher   #00FFFF  on light vegetation       contrast 1.04
  relay             #0066CC  worst-case dE             17.56 (weak)
  role fallback     #000000  internal contrast          0.00 / 1.00:1

A role-coloured frame would disappear precisely when it matters. Identity is
already carried by the UAV's own role-coloured marker at the centre of its frame
(serve_dashboard.py:717). If per-frame identity is ever genuinely needed, a small
role-coloured tick at one corner costs no legibility - but I do not recommend
building it now.

5.4  DOCKED UAVs - NO SUPPRESSION RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The prior round asked whether the frame should be suppressed for a docked UAV.
DECISION: NO, on three independent grounds.

  - It would be WRONG: a docked UAV measurably still detects (2.4).
  - It would be DEAD CODE: BASE_STATION_MODE = 0 ships as the default, no depot
    is built, and even forced to mode 3 no UAV docks within 120 steps against an
    80-step default horizon.
  - It would need new plumbing: fr.uavs carries only {id, x, y, color}
    (serve_dashboard.py:161-163); the docked flag would have to be added or
    joined from the panel by id, for zero measured benefit.

The clutter that rule was meant to fix IS real, but its cause is the mode-0 SPAWN
CLUSTER, not docking. If suppression is ever wanted, key it on CO-LOCATION (skip
a frame whose centre is within Chebyshev 2 of an already-drawn one) - the
condition that actually fires - and docking falls out of it for free. I recommend
accepting the launch cluster instead: it is self-resolving within ~8 steps and
LAUNCH_GRACE_STEPS already tells the operator that phase is special.

5.5  OCCLUSION - WHAT A FRAME HIDES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MESA (measured with the real CanvasGrid.render). Layer keys returned are
[0, 1, 3] - ground 0, UAV + frame bars 1, victims/firefighters 3 (layer 2 is the
depot, empty at BASE_STATION_MODE 0). CanvasModule.js iterates layers in
ascending numeric order. So:
  - a frame bar DOES cover ground (layer 0);
  - it does NOT cover victims or firefighters (layer 3, drawn above);
  - it DOES cover another UAV's marker (same layer 1) - the winner within a
    layer is insertion order, i.e. the UAV later in (x, then y) ascending order,
    because render() iterates x outer, y inner.
  - mesa additionally repaints #eee gridlines over the finished frame.

DASHBOARD. Immediate-mode canvas, so statement order IS z-order. drawMap
(serve_dashboard.py:675-719) runs: ground fill :676-680, gridlines :682-685,
depot overlay :698-703, px/py helpers :704, trails :706-709, assignment lines
:711-714, firefighters :715, victims :716, UAVs :717-718.

INSERT THE FRAME AT :704 - after the depot loop closes, before the trails loop.
That puts it above ground, gridlines and the depot (so it is visible, and its
colour is not tinted 22% violet by the depot fill) and below every trail,
assignment line and unit marker (so it can occlude no unit and no assignment
relationship).

WHAT IT CAN THEN OBSCURE: a 0.24-0.30% sliver of ground per UAV, and nothing
else. A cell-BOUNDARY stroke occludes no marker even at exact coincidence,
because every marker is inset from its cell edge: UAV 0.8 cell at +0.1
(:717), firefighter 0.7 at +0.15 (:715), victim a circle inscribed exactly in
the cell (:716). For contrast, any FILLED/translucent treatment would cover
11.6% of the canvas per UAV. NEVER FILL.

        x0 = Math.round((u.x - R)     * cs) + 0.5
        x1 = Math.round((u.x + R + 1) * cs) + 0.5
        y0 = Math.round((H - 1 - u.y - R)     * cs) + 0.5
        y1 = Math.round((H - 1 - u.y + R + 1) * cs) + 0.5
    each clamped to [0, W*cs] / [0, H*cs] because the grid is non-torus
    (MultiGrid(HEIGHT, WIDTH, False), wildfire_model.py:169). Clamping is
    load-bearing, not defensive: it fires on 31.7-39.6% of steps (1.6).

    Note the y mirror follows drawMap's existing convention. The pre-existing
    x/y axis swap between the model and the renderer (documented in the comment
    at :689-696) is inert while the grid is square and is not touched here.

(c) COLOUR - two strokes of the same path: lineWidth 3 #000000, then lineWidth 1
    #FFFFFF (Section 4.4).

(d) LEGEND - a two-tone chip at :657-659, or the legend misdescribes the mark.

This is the ~10 lines the prior round estimated, and the dashboard gets a strictly
BETTER frame than mesa's: exact 17x17, closed corners, zero occlusion - because
canvas can stroke on a cell boundary, which a mesa cell rect cannot.
