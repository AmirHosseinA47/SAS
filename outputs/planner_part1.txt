THE DESIGN IN ONE PARAGRAPH (sections 2-6). With GLOBAL_PLANNER_MODE = 1 the role
family is generated PER UAV: one option "switch this UAV to the other live role"
(fire_tracker <-> victim_searcher) for every UAV that is available (not returning, not
docked, more than 10 moving steps of battery above its return trigger when a base station
returns UAVs), is not the last
available UAV of its role, and is not flagged unstable by the analyzer. Each option
carries the nine values, from_role/to_role, the UAV it targets and confidence 1.0 (D-2).
The unchanged scorer ranks them against the unchanged do-nothing baseline; the planner
reader is fixed to turn "role + named UAV" into a MissionDecision; the unchanged
GlobalExecutor writes the role and, on a real change only, lets the model reset the
switched UAV's stale per-UAV state. With GLOBAL_PLANNER_MODE = 0 (shipped) none of these
paths is entered or can fire: value-identical to dfbfbe7 by construction, confirmed by
measurement on C13 (7.1).

THE NINE VALUES (section 3; one sentence each for the paper). Every contribution term
uses ONE rule - "the share of a demand the UAV would carry in its new role, minus the
share it carries now, each role's demand split evenly among the UAVs holding it" - which
makes a switch and its immediate reversal exact opposites (3.0).
  fire_contribution          demand = believed-burning area not stale, in UAV view areas (<=1)
  victim_contribution        demand = share of the mission's victims not yet detected
  communication_contribution EXPLICIT ZERO: no communication model exists (D-3)
  uncertainty_reduction      demand = share of map cells never seen
  information_recovery       demand = believed-burning area gone stale, in UAV view areas (<=1)
  collision_risk             share of the other UAVs working in the joined role within 10 cells
  battery_cost               share of the battery margin above the return trigger that
                             reaching the new role's nearest work cell uses up
  drift_risk                 1 if the UAV's last move was refused or redirected, else 0
  switching_cost             0.15 + 0.85 x (1 - role-stability timer / 30)+, the timer being
                             steps since the UAV's last role assignment (launch included) - 1

THE BASELINE CONSTANTS STAY (section 4). They become the margin a switch must clear over
"change nothing". Real options DO clear it: open loop, in the states the local strategy
actually passes through on C13, the best switch beats the baseline on 8.24% of planner
steps (257 of 3,120), in 11 of 13 runs (the final design, E3x, confidence 1.0). With the
shipped trigger-mean confidence kept instead, 2.40% of steps (75), in 6 of 13 runs (D-2,
ruled 1.0; the trigger mean is reported as a sensitivity result).

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER. Today the planning step of the loop is a constant: it
cannot select anything but "change nothing", and could not act if it did. After this
round, with the switch on, it chooses each step between "change nothing" and moving one
UAV between fire tracking and victim search on stated world quantities, and its choices
reach the UAVs. Open loop its preferences are coherent: move a TRACKER TO SEARCH early,
when victims are undetected and most of the map is unseen (first at step 3 in all 3
default-roles runs, step 9 in four half-roles runs and step 14 in a fifth), and a
SEARCHER TO TRACKING late, after every victim has been detected and the map is explored,
when the fire is large and part of its picture has gone stale (first at step 88, 95, 98,
124, 143 and 237 in six half-roles runs). The split is clean: all 136 would-win
tracker-to-search steps had an undetected victim, all 121 searcher-to-tracking steps had
none. The paper must also say that the confidence discount was switched off for role
options (D-2), that the fire inputs read a monitor-fed belief (N4), and that
communication plays no part. Rescuing FEWER than the local strategy remains possible and
is not a gate failure.

2. THE DESIGN
================================================================================

2.1 WHAT THE PLANNER DECIDES
  Every step: "change nothing", or "move ONE UAV to the other live role" (fire_tracker,
  victim_searcher - the only roles the model writes and fully implements). At most one role
  change per step. Invariants, enforced by what the generator emits (never by a scorer
  rule):
    I1 AVAILABLE UAVs only: rtb_active False, rtb_docked False, and - when a base station
       returns UAVs (base_station_mode() >= 2) - battery_level above its return trigger at
       the nearest own berth by more than 10 moving steps (3.0 battery). A UAV about to turn
       home cannot take up a new role, and the trigger is tested later in the same step
       (agents.py:687-691), so the plain flags alone would be one step stale.
    I2 never switch a UAV out of a role unless ANOTHER AVAILABLE UAV remains in it: no live
       role is ever emptied (resolve_victim_searcher_uav_ids silently falls back to "the last
       UAV by index" when no searcher exists, local_adaptation_generator.py:306-316), and a
       role never loses its last working member to a switch.
    I3 REAL changes only: an option exists only for from_role != to_role, so the executor
       never re-asserts a role (N5: re-assertion freezes the timers).
    I4 LIVE ids only, read from uav_resource_model.by_uav_id (a wrong key crashes, N5).
    I5 never re-task a UAV that a current OSCILLATION_RISK or INSTABILITY_DETECTED trigger
       names (read from the generator's own first argument, global_analysis_result
       ['trigger_list'] / trigger_batch). The analyzer's instability signal thus reaches the
       planner: a UAV is not switched within 10 steps of its 2nd+ switch, and at most 3
       times per mission. Without I5 the delay_change option the analyzer causes to be
       generated is a no-op that can never win - nothing would slow further switching.

2.2 THE ROLE FAMILY WITH THE SWITCH ON (GLOBAL_PLANNER_MODE = 1)
  For each UAV u (by_uav_id keys sorted by int) passing I1, I2, I5; a = its role (managed
  store first, resource model second - the order of wildfire_model._uav_assignment_role -
  with "victim_search" read as victim_searcher); b = the other live role:
      option_id       global_role_assignment_<b>_<u>   e.g. global_role_assignment_fire_tracker_2503
      option_type     "role_assignment"               unchanged: keeps evaluator routing
                                                      and every type test
      target_entity   <u>
      parameters      the five base parameters exactly as today (all None, see 3.10)
                      assigned_role <b>   - a STRING (hashable: N2)
                      target_uav_id <u>   - NEW: the UAV the option moves
                      from_role <a>, to_role <b>   - the scorer's own change reader (5.2)
                      battery_level <u's battery>  - feeds the scorer's existing gate (inert
                                           at base-station mode >= 2, see N4)
                      the nine values, finite floats (section 3)
      cost_estimate 1.0, risk_estimate 0.2     unchanged (cost_estimate is never consulted,
                      3.9; risk_estimate is checked by the filter against >= 0.35, never binds)
      confidence      1.0                      D-2, ruled (4.3)
  Kept as today, plus the nine values as EXPLICIT 0.0: global_role_assignment_maintain_
  current (its id is pinned by tests/test_global_adaptation.py:43) and, when the triggers
  of N5 fire, global_role_assignment_delay_change. Both change nothing, still score 0.0 and
  cannot beat the baseline.
  NOT generated with the switch on: the five generator-vocabulary options (fire_tracking and
  victim_search are replaced by the per-UAV options in the live words; victim_confirmation,
  victim_tracking, communication_relay have no behaviour, 1.4 d). Every other family and
  the baseline are generated exactly as today.

2.3 WHY PER-UAV SINGLE SWITCHES
  A role option must say WHICH UAV (N3). Battery, position, drift, recency and crowding are
  per-UAV facts. One change per step keeps every decision attributable to one UAV, one step
  and nine numbers - what section 7 must prove. The forced arm's "all four become X" is not
  in the action space (I2).

2.4 WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
  The scorer (not one line), the weight profiles, the baseline and both constants, the
  GlobalExecutor's role writes (it stays role-agnostic, as tests/test_execution_core.py
  :96-110 and test_global_executor_role_signature.py require), MissionDecision (frozen, 12
  fields pinned by tests/test_planner_core.py:51), OptionEvaluation (pinned by
  tests/test_utility_core.py:46), the dispatcher and its result keys, the pipeline stages
  (pinned by tests/test_adaptation_manager_cycle.py:205), the other four option families.

3. THE NINE VALUES (design E3)
================================================================================

3.0 NOTATION, THE SHARE RULE, AND WHY A SWITCH CANNOT BE UNDONE ON UNCHANGED DEMANDS
  An option moves UAV u from role a to role b. Read at generation time (pre-move of step t):
  knowledge models as of the end of step t-1; managed_victims[*].confirmed possibly updated
  by this step's pre-move detection (adaptation_manager.py:101 runs before :111); agent rtb
  flags, berths and trigger level live. FT/VS = fire_tracker / victim_searcher.
    n_r  = number of UAVs holding role r (ALL of them, returning and docked included - the
           way the simulator itself divides lanes and flanks; availability decides only who
           may be switched, I1-I2).
  Four demands, each in [0, 1], none depending on who holds which role:
    B        = fire_runtime_model.belief.estimated_burning_cells (probability >= 0.7)
    S        = {c in B : observation status STALE_INFORMATION}       (visibility model)
    D_fire   = min(1, |B \ S| / 289)      289 = one UAV's observation area, (2 x 8 + 1)^2
    D_stale  = min(1, |S| / 289)          the same unit; each believed cell counted once
    D_vict   = (N_v - N_det) / N_v, 0 if N_v = 0, clamped to [0, 1];
               N_v = len(managed_victims) - the mission's victim count, not their positions;
               N_det = number with confirmed True (the flag only)
    D_unc    = |{cells with status NEVER_SEEN}| / |cells|   (observation_status_map, 2500)
  THE SHARE RULE, for a demand D belonging to role r:
      share(D, r) = [b = r] x D / (n_r + 1)  -  [a = r] x D / n_r
  (the part of r's demand u would carry after joining r, minus the part it carries now).
  PROPERTY. Let F be an option's value and R the value of the reverse move evaluated right
  after it (n_a -> n_a - 1, n_b -> n_b + 1): R = D_a/((n_a - 1) + 1) - D_b/(n_b + 1) = -F.
  A move and its reversal are exact opposites WHEN THE DEMANDS AND THE MODE ARE UNCHANGED
  (the weights come from the mode); n_r no longer changes with returns and releases (E3),
  so the battery schedule cannot break it. Only a change in demands or mode can make the
  planner want to undo a move - which is then a decision, not an oscillation. (With D/n_b
  instead of D/(n_b + 1), the reverse of a +0.5 move is again +0.5.) It does not prevent a
  cascade in one direction; I2 and the share rule's diminishing gains bound that.
  RANGE. I2 gives n_a >= 2 (u plus another available holder), so a loss is at most D/2.
  Both live roles are held at launch in every configuration this round runs (half 2+2,
  default 3+1), and I2 applied to every switch keeps each role held, so n_b >= 1 and a gain
  is at most D/2. Each contribution term lies in [-0.5, 0.5]. (A configuration that
  launched with a role unheld would allow a gain up to D; none is run.)

3.1 fire_contribution
  MEASURES  the fire-tracking demand the switch moves onto (+) or off (-) the UAV.
  READS     fire belief B and the stale set S; roles.
  FORMULA   share(D_fire, FT).          RANGE [-0.5, 0.5]; 0 when no fresh belief of fire.
  WHY       the tracker's work is the fire; its demand grows with the (non-stale) believed
            fire, and one UAV's view area is the natural unit - a fire that fills one area
            can occupy one tracker. The belief is monitor-fed (N4): stated, not changed.
  PAPER     "The share of the believed-burning area (in UAV view areas, at most one) that
            the UAV would carry as a fire tracker after the switch, minus the share it
            carries now, each role's demand split evenly among the UAVs holding it."

3.2 victim_contribution
  MEASURES  the part of the outstanding victim search the switch moves onto/off the UAV.
  READS     len(managed_victims) and their confirmed flags; roles.
  FORMULA   share(D_vict, VS).          RANGE [-0.5, 0.5]; 0 once every victim is detected.
  WHY       searchers exist to find victims. Using the COUNT of victims is a mission-
            briefing prior (the local generator's _count_unresolved_victims and mission_goals
            .alive_victims_remaining already use it). A DELIBERATE CHOICE: the planner reads
            only the detection flag, so a victim that died undetected keeps counting as
            outstanding - the managed side knows it is dead (mission_goals and
            _count_unresolved_victims read that), the planner does not look. Detection is
            role-agnostic (any UAV within radius 8, wildfire_model.py:1355) but only
            searchers sweep the far field, which is why losing searchers cost rescues in the
            forced arm. It moves together with uncertainty_reduction until all victims are
            found (both favour search); they diverge after that.
  PAPER     "The share of the mission's victims not yet detected that the UAV would carry as
            a victim searcher after the switch, minus the share it carries now." Also: the
            planner may keep valuing search after every undetected victim has died.

3.3 communication_contribution - EXPLICIT, DOCUMENTED ZERO
  MEASURES  nothing: no role switch has a communication effect in this simulator.
  READS     nothing. Written as 0.0 on every switch option (present, not absent).
  WHY       no link model exists (N4). The only per-UAV "communication" signal is a refused-
            move proxy, which drift_risk already carries; fleet delivery_confidence is an
            artifact. A proxy invented here (e.g. distance to a depot) would put a number on
            something the simulator does not model. (D-3.)
  PAPER     "Zero for every role switch: the simulator has no communication model in which
            a UAV's role or position changes message delivery."

3.4 uncertainty_reduction
  MEASURES  the part of the unexplored map the switch moves onto/off the UAV.
  READS     visibility_model.state.observation_status_map (NEVER_SEEN); roles.
  FORMULA   share(D_unc, VS).           RANGE [-0.5, 0.5]; 0 once every cell has been seen.
  WHY       the demand is the unexplored map; the searcher is the role that sweeps the far
            field (cross-wind lanes), while trackers stay on the fire perimeter and never
            search (uav_executor.py:3115-3124). NOT "searchers alone clear it": NEVER_SEEN is
            also cleared by any UAV's 17x17 view and by the monitor's feed of fire and smoke
            cells (wildfire_model.py:1290-1324), so D_unc falls with fire spread and tracker
            motion too. It is monotone non-increasing.
  PAPER     "The share of the never-seen map cells that the UAV would carry as a victim
            searcher after the switch, minus the share it carries now." Also: the map
            knowledge it reads is partly fed by the environment monitor.

3.5 information_recovery
  MEASURES  the stale fire belief the switch moves onto/off the UAV.
  READS     B and S (as 3.0).
  FORMULA   share(D_stale, FT).         RANGE [-0.5, 0.5]; 0 when nothing believed is stale.
  WHY       because truly burning cells are re-reported every step (unless under smoke),
            a stale believed-burning cell is almost always one that went out while the
            belief still says it burns. Re-observing it recovers information that was known
            and went bad - the literal meaning of the term - and the tracker, patrolling the
            fire area, is the role that does it. Disjoint from fire_contribution: a believed
            cell is counted in exactly one of D_fire and D_stale.
  PAPER     "The share of the believed-burning area not re-observed for more than 10 steps
            (in UAV view areas, at most one) that the UAV would carry as a fire tracker after
            the switch, minus the share it carries now."

3.6 collision_risk
  MEASURES  crowding in the role the UAV would join.
  READS     positions from uav_resource_model (equal to the agents' at generation); roles;
            rtb flags; SECURITY_DISTANCE = 10 (cfv:739).
  FORMULA   |{w != u : role(w) = b, w not returning/docked, euclid(u, w) <= 10}| / (N - 1).
  RANGE     [0, (N - 2)/(N - 1)] = [0, 2/3] for 4 UAVs (I2 leaves another member of a, which
            is not in b). It cannot reach the scorer's hard-collision threshold 0.95
            (utility_evaluation.py:258/:273-275) for N <= 20 UAVs: no switch is ever made
            infeasible by it here.
  WHY       joining a role whose working members are already close means working the same
            area (trackers converge on the fire's flanks). Existing collision signals cannot
            be used: local congestion is always 1.0 (no distance filter, wildfire_model.py
            :1799-1802) and the global COLLISION_RISK fires only below distance 1.0
            (global_analyzer.py:860). Docked/returning UAVs are excluded: at a depot they
            are parked, not working.
  PAPER     "The share of the other UAVs that are working in the role this UAV would join
            and within the security distance of it."

3.7 battery_cost
  MEASURES  how much of the UAV's usable battery the switch commits before it can work.
  READS     u's position and battery (uav_resource_model); its own berths (agent.rtb_berths,
            else (rtb_berth,)); drain per moving step 0.3 (agents.py:284-285); the return
            trigger at a position via the same formula as agent._rtb_trigger_level (agents.py
            :418-437: max(reserve, 0.3 x Manhattan distance to the nearest own berth +
            margin)) - Part 2 adds a PURE helper rtb_trigger_level_at(uav, cell) used only
            by the planner, pinned by a test to equal _rtb_trigger_level at the UAV's own
            cell, so there is no second copy that can drift; NOT _rtb_select_berth (it
            writes rtb_target_*, agents.py:557-558).
  FORMULA   c   = the nearest work cell of b (FT: nearest non-stale believed-burning cell;
                  VS: nearest never-seen cell; Manhattan; ties to the lowest (x, y));
            d   = Manhattan distance u -> c (0 if no such cell exists);
            tau_u, tau_c = the trigger level at u's cell and at c (at base_station_mode() < 2:
                  BATTERY_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD 15 at both);
            m   = battery - tau_u;
            battery_cost = 1 if m <= 0, else min(1, (0.3 x d + max(0, tau_c - tau_u)) / m).
  RANGE     [0, 1]. The numerator lies between 0.3 d (flight only) and 0.6 d (flight plus
            a trigger that rises 0.3 per cell away from the berth).
  WHY       what a UAV can spend on work is its battery above its own return trigger;
            flying d cells both costs 0.3 d and, away from the berth, raises the trigger.
            It prices distance to the role's NEAREST work cell, not to the lane or flank the
            local strategy will then assign - an approximation, stated (see 10).
  PAPER     "The share of the UAV's battery margin above its return-to-base trigger that
            flying to the nearest work cell of the new role would use up, counting both the
            flight and the higher trigger there."

3.8 drift_risk
  MEASURES  whether the UAV is currently failing to execute its moves.
  READS     uav_resource_model.by_uav_id[u].drift_level (the monitor's last-move drift).
  FORMULA   0 if drift_level is None (before the first move), else min(1, drift_level).
  RANGE     {0, 1} in practice (raw values 0, 1, sqrt(2), 2; clamped).
  WHY       a UAV whose last move was refused or redirected (hazard gate, occupied cell) is a
            poor moment to re-task: its new path starts from a blocked state. It delays
            rather than vetoes: the planner re-decides next step. NOT local_risk_status,
            which sticks at high_drift after one refusal (uav_resource_model.py:217-222; a
            defect, cleanup list). Open loop it was non-zero on 1.0% of scorings and never
            decided anything - it is pre-registered as expected NOT to move a decision.
  PAPER     "1 if the UAV's last move was refused or redirected, else 0: the risk of
            re-tasking a UAV that is not currently executing its moves."

3.9 switching_cost
  MEASURES  the cost of changing a role, higher right after this UAV's last assignment.
  READS     uav_resource_model.by_uav_id[u].role_stability_timer: steps since u's last role
            ASSIGNMENT - including the launch assignment (scenario_init sets it to 0 at step
            0) - minus one, because it is refreshed post-move (uav_resource_model.py:97-100,
            :161-165).
  FORMULA   0.15 + 0.85 x max(0, 1 - timer / 30).
  RANGE     [0.15, 1.0] - never 0.0, so the scorer's fallbacks (cost_estimate 1.0, then 0.15,
            utility_evaluation.py:862-865) never fire, and Cost_switch equals this value (the
            policy term stays 0 because role_change / task_change / recent_switch_count are
            absent and the base role_switch_count stays None, 3.10).
  WHY       0.15 is the scorer's own default cost of a role change (:865). The recency term
            is the second anti-oscillation device, for the case the share rule cannot cover
            (demands changed). It also brakes the start: at launch every UAV pays 1.0,
            still 0.72 at step 11 - a partial early blackout (open loop the first wins came
            at step 3 with 3+1 roles and step 9 with 2+2, where demand outweighed it).
            30 steps is a design constant (D-6), not tuned; the analyzer's own instability
            window is 10 steps after a 2nd+ switch (N5), which I5 honours separately.
  PAPER     "0.15 for any role change, plus up to 0.85 more that decays linearly to zero over
            the 31 steps after the UAV's previous role assignment (launch included)."

3.10 WHERE A VALUE HAS NO MEANING, AND WHAT STAYS None
  - communication_contribution: 0.0 on every switch option (3.3).
  - maintain_current and delay_change: all nine written as 0.0 - they change nothing.
  - The five legacy base parameters stay None as today. Filling role_switch_count would add
    0.15 x 0.2 x count (x a timer damp) through _compute_switching_cost (:311-316) - a
    second, hidden switching cost no sentence above states; the 0.3 policy term keys on
    role_change / task_change (:310), which are never written.
  - The do-nothing baseline is not a role option and is not touched: its nine values are
    zero by definition (it changes nothing) and absent in code, exactly as today.

3.11 PURITY
  Reads only: knowledge models (fire belief, visibility status map, uav_resource_model
  via by_uav_id.get - never _get_or_create or update_*), managed_victims[*].confirmed and
  its length, cfv constants at call time, the analyzer's trigger list, and from the agents
  only rtb_active, rtb_docked, rtb_berths / rtb_berth and the pure trigger formula (the
  only sources of those facts - PAPER: the planner reads these managed-entity fields
  directly, as the local generator already does). Never calls the helpers that look like
  reads but write: _wind_search_state, UAV._rtb_select_berth,
  VisibilityModel.get_uncertain_regions. Draws no RNG, prints nothing. Nothing at all is
  computed with the switch off.

4.1 DECISION: BOTH CONSTANTS STAY EXACTLY AS THEY ARE
  S_hysteresis_bonus (0.12 x sw) and S_stability_idle_floor (0.05 x sw) are not changed and
  not made world-dependent:
  (1) once the options carry real values, "the baseline wins because every alternative is
      0.0" is gone; what remains is a fixed margin 0.17 x sw a switch must clear over
      "change nothing" - a hysteresis threshold, which is a legitimate role for a constant;
      the world-dependence belongs to the options, where it now is;
  (2) the baseline is shared by all five families, three of them out of scope;
  (3) switch-off identity then needs no baseline argument.
  Your condition - show that real options can beat them - is 4.2.

4.2 REAL OPTIONS CAN BEAT THEM
  WORKED EXAMPLES, scored by the REAL scorer against the baseline's real score in every
  profile, confidence 1.0 (outputs/_pl_verify/_pl_analytic.py/.txt; the draft's hand sums
  were wrong - 0.44/0.33 should have read 0.473/0.345 - and are replaced):
    A tracker to search, 2+2 roles, step >= 31, all 4 victims undetected, half the map
    unseen, D_fire 0.1: normal 0.283 WIN, information_recovery 0.431 WIN, victim_support

4.3 THE CONFIDENCE DISCOUNT (D-2) - RULED 2026-09-27: 1.0 FOR SWITCH OPTIONS
  PROPOSED: switch options carry confidence 1.0, the baseline's value. The multiplier (N1)
  weighs a switch by how sure the analyzer is of its TRIGGERS while the baseline is weighed
  as certain; the options' own evidence is the nine values, read directly from the
  knowledge models. Weighting one side only is a bias, not a judgment, and it makes step 1
  unselectable by construction. Other families keep their confidence (untouched).
  THIS IS A CHANGE TO THE SCORING COMPARISON THAT THE BRIEF DID NOT ASK FOR. It touches
  only the new options, but "the constants stay" rests on it in part. If you keep the
  shipped discount, the design is otherwise unchanged; open loop it still wins on 2.40%
  of steps in 6 of 13 runs, so P1 (>= 15 of 43 runs) might or might not pass - I no longer
  "expect it to fail P1" as the draft said. PAPER: with 1.0, the paper must say the
  confidence discount was switched off for role options; "the constants were left
  unchanged" alone would mislead.
  RULING (maintainer): 1.0 for switch options. Reason given: the discount weights role
  options by the analyzer's trigger confidence, and the cleanup list shows those triggers
  are largely artifacts (collision every step, drift latched; 11A (c)), while do-nothing
  counts as fully certain - a bias, not a margin. planner_report.txt reports the
  trigger-mean variant AS A SENSITIVITY RESULT: open loop on C13, 2.40% of planner steps
  (75 of 3,120) in 6 of 13 runs, against 8.24% (257) in 11 of 13 at 1.0 (4.2; Linux,
