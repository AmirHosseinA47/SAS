THE "UNSAFE" PREDICTION, CHECKED AGAINST THE DATA
==============================================================================

PREDICTED IN ADVANCE (outputs/fix14_part1.txt section 4, written before any
Part 3 code existed):

  (a) UNSAFE would NOT be as rare as the 1.3% hazard-target share the hazard
      re-validation fix achieved at proposal time, because that fix only ever
      runs at proposal time while a hold exposes a fixed cell to a moving
      hazard field for 12+ steps.
  (b) Sharper, falsifiable form: UNSAFE would be rare on D/north and A/north
      (~15% peak hazard share of the grid) and NOT rare on D/south (~50%).

MEASURED - AND THE PREDICTION IS WRONG. BOTH PARTS.

The release counts alone appear to support (a): 2 unsafe releases out of 16
(12.5%), or 2 out of 20 holds issued (10%), against 1.3% at proposal time.
That reading is an artifact of the denominator. A per-RELEASE rate cannot be
compared with a per-PROPOSAL rate - there are 16 releases against 940 held
steps, so any per-release percentage is inflated by roughly the mean hold
length.

The probe records the right denominator on every step. The base probe at HEAD
already carried tgt_in_hazard (is the issued target in fire or smoke NOW), and
this round added held_in_hazard for the cell currently under hold. P2ONLY is
the control: same tree, same hazard fix, no hold.

  side     issued target in hazard      held target in hazard
  -------------------------------------------------------------
  P2ONLY         8 / 956 = 0.8%              n/a (no holds)
  FIX14          2 / 956 = 0.2%              2 / 940 = 0.2%

  (a) WRONG. The held target sits in hazard on 0.2% of held steps - BELOW the
      1.3% proposal-time figure, not above it, and below P2ONLY's own 0.8%
      issued-in-hazard rate on the same tree.

  (b) WRONG, AND EXACTLY INVERTED. D/south - the run predicted to be worst,
      with smoke covering up to half the grid - had ZERO unsafe releases and
      0.0% held-in-hazard steps. D/north and A/north, predicted to be rare,
      had one release each and 0.4% of held steps.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT WHETHER THE HAZARD FIX GENERALISES - the question the
round asked to be answered explicitly either way:

  IT LARGELY DOES GENERALISE, and the reason is visible in the numbers. There
  were exactly 2 held-in-hazard steps and exactly 2 unsafe releases. One
  hazardous step per release means the UNSAFE condition fires on the FIRST
  step the held cell becomes unsafe, with zero lag. A target is validated safe
  at proposal by the hazard fix and re-checked every step by the hold, so the
  window in which a held target can be hazardous is one step wide by
  construction.

  A second, unpredicted result points the same way: holding REDUCED total
  hazard exposure rather than increasing it. FIX14's issued-in-hazard rate is
  0.2% against P2ONLY's 0.8% on the identical tree. Re-choosing a target every
  four steps means four times as many chances to issue into a cell that has
  just become hazardous; holding one already-validated cell means fewer. The
  churn was itself a source of hazard exposure.

  The two unsafe releases were both long holds that had closed most of their
  gap - D/north [45, 45], initial 68, best 15, held 53 steps; A/north
  [42, 45], initial 65, best 13, held 52 - and both were eastward traverses
  into the downwind side where fire concentrates. Neither was issued into
  hazard; both ignited during approach. That failure mode is real and
  proposal-time validation cannot see it. But at 2 occurrences in 940 held
  steps it is a rare event the hold already handles, not the systematic
  exposure the prediction claimed.

  The prediction was wrong because it reasoned from how much of the grid is
  hazardous (up to 50% on D/south) without accounting for the fact that the
  scorer's candidate filter and the hazard fix keep targets away from the
  hazard field in the first place, so the relevant probability is not the
  grid-wide share.
