1.1  A HYPOTHESIS THIS ROUND REFUTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Going in, the natural explanation for "the counter never leaves 0" was that the
mode is always NORMAL, so line 140 resets it every step. MEASURED FALSE.

Mode is non-NORMAL at 6,214 of 6,240 counter updates - 99.58%:

    mode at counter update        observations     branch taken
    normal ....................        26          normal_reset
    information_recovery ......     4,764          unsatisfied_reset
    safety_first ..............     1,446          unsatisfied_reset
    degraded ..................         4          unsatisfied_reset
    emergency .................         0          -
                                                   satisfied_increment: 0

The 26 normal observations are exactly 2 per run: the two update() calls of
step 1. The system enters information_recovery at step 2 of EVERY run and never
returns to normal in ANY of the 13 - transitions_to_normal is empty everywhere.

So the counter is pinned at 0 by the UNSATISFIED-RESET branch, not the
normal-reset branch. _recovery_conditions_satisfied ran 6,214 times and
returned False 6,214 times.

This was independently corroborated during Part 3 by a completely separate
instrument: the control harness's mode_hist column, which records the mode after
every step with no patching of any kind, shows norm:1 in all 13 runs (section 3.3).

I called line 92 a tautology in early analysis. THAT WAS TOO STRONG AND IS

tautology, and the distinction matters because it means the drop does lose

What it actually pinned: an already-NORMAL, reason-free manager must not report
should_return_to_normal() == True. A line tracer over the test shows it reaches

negating == to != - survives.

THE JUSTIFICATION, STATED NARROWLY: the assertion's subject is itself being
deleted, so its one bit of coverage becomes UNCONSTRUCTIBLE rather than lost.
You cannot mutate a function that no longer exists. If the campaign ever
