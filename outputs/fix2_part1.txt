2. ITEM 2 - "HOLD" THAT DOES NOT HOLD - RE-VERIFIED, HOLDS, AND SMALLER THAN IT LOOKED
================================================================================
RE-VERIFIED  UAV.move has no stay action (agents.py:819-856): only rtb_docked and a missing managed
  direction stop it; the executor's 'hold' re-commits the previous heading (_execute_hold,
  uav_executor.py:539-545) and skips both post-filters (:419, :428). c08456b rows (16 runs): 'hold'
  1,472 steps, moved 1,465 (99.5%); 'fire_flank_hold' 1,278, moved 1,274 (99.7%); 'hold_escape' 377,
  moved 377 (an escape by design).
WHERE THE HOLDS COME FROM (mf2D, 4 runs, value-identical to their twins)
  - Every executor 'hold' is the FAIL-SAFE safe_hold: 167/167 in SAFETY_FIRST steps, 0 planner holds,
    trackers only. In all 16 runs 'hold' occurs only in safety_first steps (1,472/1,472) - so item 1
    removes almost every 'hold'. Searchers never hold: under SAFETY_FIRST the executor receives no
    fail-safe decision (decision_dispatcher.py:519-535), so a searcher's rewritten hold looks like a
    planner hold and is re-routed to the wind-aware search (uav_executor.py:346-363).
  - 'fire_flank_hold' is NOT a hold in 652 of 653 cases: the flank layer aims at a LATERAL neighbour -
    a tangential patrol step at standoff distance (_fire_tracker_lateral_hold_target,
    uav_executor.py:3526-3568, scores sideways steps up and radial steps down). Only once did it
    target the tracker's own cell. It occurs in information_recovery steps (1,278/1,278), i.e. it is
    the trackers' ordinary flank behaviour, not a fail-safe response.
DESIGN (UAV_HOLD_STATIONARY)
  (a) A STAY ACTION. The executor commits a stay flag with the direction (agent.execution_stay) for
      'hold' and for a 'fire_flank_hold' whose target is the tracker's own cell; UAV.move returns
      without moving when it is set (after the docked and no-direction checks, which already stay).
      'hold_escape' and the lateral flank step keep moving.
  (b) A STAY IS INTENDED: the step's intended delta is (0, 0), so the drift measure scores it 0 - owned
      by this switch, because a stationary hold would otherwise create a new drift sample (1.0) with
      item 1 off.
  (c) RETURN LEGS WIN: _apply_return_to_base runs first in advance() (agents.py:858); when it steers a
      leg or docks it clears the stay flag. A docked UAV already stays (agents.py:830-832).
  (d) BATTERY: a staying UAV drains 0.1 per step - the model's existing rule for a UAV that did not
      move (agents.py:371-386), as a refused move does today. Reported, not changed.
  (e) THE FAIL-SAFE HOLD SCOPE - DESIGNED WITH ITEM 1. Today SAFETY_FIRST rewrites EVERY non-exempt
      path to 'hold' (decision_dispatcher.py:483-516). With a stationary hold that would (i) stop every
      tracker whenever two UAVs anywhere are within 2 cells - 15.6% of steps under item 1's real
      condition - and (ii) hold BOTH members of a close pair, so the pair stays close, the alarm never
      clears and the fleet freezes for good. So under the switch the dispatcher rewrites to 'hold' only
      a UAV that must YIELD:
        airborne, not on a return leg, named by its own COLLISION_RISK (the local trigger names the UAV
        itself), and another airborne UAV with a LOWER unique_id within Manhattan 2 (right of way to
        the lower id);
        and not after 3 consecutive yield steps - a UAV that has yielded 3 steps in a row proceeds on
        the next step, so no pair can freeze (3 = BASE_STATION_RETURN_STALL_LIMIT and the analyzer's
        UAV_STUCK threshold).
      Every other UAV keeps its own path under SAFETY_FIRST (the mode still sets the mission phase and
      the utility profile). The yield applies to searchers too - a collision risk is role-blind: the
      dispatcher marks the rewritten path as a fail-safe yield, so the executor holds it instead of

6. HOW THE FIVE ITEMS INTERACT
================================================================================
  1 x 2  THE CENTRAL ONE. Item 1 makes SAFETY_FIRST rare but REAL (a close pair 15.6% of steps, a
         blocked UAV 2.2% of UAV-steps); item 2 makes the resulting hold REAL (stationary). A fleet-wide
         stationary hold would then freeze every tracker for a proximity event anywhere and never clear
         for the pair itself - hence the scoped yield with right of way and a 3-step cap (2e). Item 2
         also owns the (0,0) intended delta, so its stays cannot re-create item 1's drift artifact.
         Order: with item 2 on and item 1 off the artifact SAFETY_FIRST (14.9%) still exists, but the
         yield test is geometric (a lower-id airborne UAV within 2), so only real close pairs hold.
  1 x 4  staggering lowers some launch charges: B's 28.0 raises LOW_BATTERY on the berth at step 2 (no
         consumer); no stagger value reaches CRITICAL (<= 15, would need launch <= 10.1), so the
         EMERGENCY path stays unreachable.
  1 x 5  a standoff is exactly what the fixed drift alarm names (a UAV refused on >= 2 of 5 steps):
         with item 1 on, a standoff puts the fleet in SAFETY_FIRST; with item 2's scope that holds
         nobody extra (a returning UAV is never held; the blocker is only held if it yields to a
         lower-id airborne UAV within 2 - and its cap releases it).
  2 x 5  a yielding (stationary) UAV next to a depot approach could block a return leg for up to 3
         steps; 5b gives the return leg right of way over non-returning UAVs.
  3b x 5 the always-retreat gate pushed searchers east whenever no fire was near; near the NW depot
         that pushes them across the depot approach (the dcd4 round's head-on standoffs with a flying
         searcher). 3b removes that push; item 5 is measured with 3b on in the all-together arm.
  3 x 4  with staggering one of C/D's two searchers is out for ~46 steps early (D ~63-109, C ~85-131)
         while the other flies; cross-wind lanes (two searchers) leave the out searcher's lane
         uncovered for that window. Reported in P3-5 / P3-6.
  1 x 3  under information_recovery searchers are exempt from the override (unchanged); item 1 makes
         the mode normal most of the time, which switches victim-search priority ON in the utility
         profile - searcher option ranking can change (measured).
  4 x 5  item 4 is expected to remove the synchronised convoys that produce most standoffs; item 5 is
         built only if the checkpoint says so.
  1B x 1 FLEET_FIRE_LOST reads the fire belief directly (its own path), so item 1 does not depend on
         the fire-source switch, and the fire-source switch does not change the fail-safe reasons.
