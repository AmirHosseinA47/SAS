2. THE DELAY-PATH CHECK YOU ASKED FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The concern: the _DELAY_CANCEL_BONUS round found "delay" structurally
unselectable through _is_delay_or_cancel_option, and after the predicate fix
at 0e9c912 a second dead gate downstream kept it inert.

That predicate belongs to the planner-UTILITY path (adaptation options scored
by RescuePlanner). The delay this feature relies on is the PHYSICAL PAIRING
path: select_rescue_assignment's empty-pool branch returns a RescueDecision
with rescue_action "delay", and RescueExecutor.apply_physical_pairing_decision
returns {"message": "delayed"} without mutating anything. Three checks that it
fires, none of them assumed:

  (a) code, downstream of the decision: _handle_rescue_incident (wildfire_
      model.py:2898-2930) applies the decision and, for casualty / route_blocked
      incidents whose result is not a success, only prints "[Rescue Failed]"
      when the action was mark_unreachable, then syncs knowledge. There is no
      second gate: a delayed victim keeps status "confirmed" (the casualty path
      unassigns it with reset_victim_pending=True first) and remains needy, and
      the only thing that ever re-dispatches such a victim is
      _try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims, which runs after every
      recycle - and therefore after every return.
  (b) baseline data, 1511ada, 13 runs: 9 empty-pool "delay" decisions were
      taken; 8 of the 9 victims were dispatched later, every one of them by the
      post-recycle pass (same step as a recycle). The ninth (D/south/half 404,
      step 57, replacement_after_blocked) was overtaken by the victim's death.
  (c) end to end on the real model, tests/test_firefighter_rescue_absence.py::
      test_planner_delays_casualty_replacement_while_a_unit_is_returning:
      unit B exits with a victim and goes off-grid for 3 steps; unit A,
      assigned to another victim, is burned by _check_fire_casualties; the
      real replacement path runs; the victim is NOT marked unreachable, stays
      "confirmed", no rescue_failed event; the re-derived decision is "delay"
      with returning_firefighters == ["ff_unit_1"]; on B's return step the
      drain re-places B and the post-drain pass assigns it to that victim.
      The all-dead control (test_planner_still_gives_up_when_every_unit_is_dead)
      still yields mark_unreachable.
In the canonical sample the new branch changed no outcome: the 7 empty-pool
decisions taken while a unit was off-grid were all "initial" reasons, which
delayed before the change too, and no casualty fell inside a window (sec 7).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. VERDICT AND WHAT TO WATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The feature does what the requirement says: on every rescue completion the
firefighter leaves the grid for 3-5 steps, comes back at the cell it left
from (or the nearest safe cell within the idle-retreat leash when fire has
taken that cell), and is immediately available to assist - the same
post-drain dispatch pass that served a recycled unit before now serves a
returned one. On the canonical sample it costs nothing measurable: every
outcome metric is identical on every seed, and the only movement is one
rescue completing 3 steps later. The cost it CAN incur is the one Part 1
predicted - a waiting victim starts 3-5 steps later, cumulatively for a unit
that rescues back to back - and the 8 traced cases show that in this sample
those victims were either lost regardless or rescued regardless.

Where to look first if rescued ever drops under this feature: the empty-pool
"initial -> delay" decisions taken while a unit is off-grid (the harness
records n_offgrid_alive per planner decision), and the +d / +2d shifts on the
same-drain re-dispatches; a casualty inside a window now delays instead of
marking the victim unreachable, and that branch is covered by the end-to-end
test rather than by the sample, which contains no such case.
