2.1  THE FORM, CHOSEN BY MEASUREMENT: A BLIND LEGIBILITY PANEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Reads as a marker at the zoom the dashboard actually uses" is a gate item, so it
was measured rather than judged from a zoomed crop.

PROTOCOL. Each reader is a fresh agent that opens exactly ONE 560x560 PNG - the
full map as a user sees it at 100% zoom - rendered by the REAL drawMap on a REAL
captured frame with the candidate injected into it as text. File names are
neutral (m01..m12, n01..n06, k01..k06). Readers are told nothing about bases,
flags or what is being tested; they may not run code, zoom, crop, enhance or open
any other file. They describe both map corners, say what each REPRESENTS with a
0-100 confidence, and answer "where do the vehicles return to?". Rounds 1-2 use
three frames: quiet (east/101 step 0), NW depot burnt (west/101 step 105), SE
depot in smoke and fire (south/202 step 99). In every candidate variant the old
text label is removed, so the candidate is tested alone. All 24 images regenerate
bit-for-bit from committed code (outputs/_bm_blind_prep.py).

                       n   TL conf  BR conf  mentions a   reads BASE   hedges toward
                           (mean)   (mean)   flag / pole  both ends    goal/destination
   status quo          3    50.0     55.0       0/3          0/3          1/3
   pennant 22x30 px    6    68.2     58.3       6/6          0/6          2/6
   pennant x1.4        6    71.5     60.0       6/6          0/6        2-3/6
   sign "BASE" plate   6    84.2     77.8       3/6          6/6          0/6
   BANNER              6    84.7     76.7       2/6          6/6          1/6

(TL = top-left = NW depot; BR = bottom-right = SE depot, empty of units in all
three frames and so the harder, cleaner test. Units are parked in the NW block
only in the step-0 frame; in the other two, readers lean on the trails converging
there. The hedge column is a reading of each response; a bare keyword count gives
1/3, 2/6, 3/6, 1/6, 1/6.)

A third round (5.4) showed the banner alone on three OTHER frames to six more
readers: 84.7 / 78.3. It replicates.

WHAT THE PANEL SAYS.
  - The status quo is a guess: 40-45 on the empty SE corner when its label sits on
    vegetation. It reads 80 on the one frame where the old label happens to sit on
    smoke and becomes legible - the word, not the block, is what identifies it.
  - A pennant IS a flag at 1:1 - 12 of 12 name one - and lifts the corners to
    58-72. But 4 or 5 of the 12 hedge (one wrote "probably another base/home or a
    destination/safe zone"), and at 22x30 px 4 of 6 readers describe the pennant
    itself as WHITE, 5 if one ambiguous "white outlined" is counted (2 of 6 at
    x1.4). Its #770099 field, 97 px and without luminance contrast (1.3), barely
    registers; the white keyline does.
  - Scaling the pennant x1.4 makes the field register and buys +3 / +2. Size is
    not the lever.
  - The word is the lever. Both text variants read BASE at both corners 12 of 12.
    Against the status quo the gap is 22-35 points; against the pennants, 13-19.
    One reader: "The word BASE on the purple tags is the only explicit cue ...
    Without the text I would not know."
  - Sign and banner are indistinguishable (84.2/77.8 vs 84.7/76.7) - AND THE PANEL
    GIVES NO EVIDENCE THE BANNER IS SEEN AS A FLAG. Two of six banner readers
    mention a pole "like a flag"; four call it a "label box". The sign does no
    worse (3 of 6). Both are read as a labelled tag.

LIMITS, stated plainly. The readers are vision-language models, not people; they
read small text well, and a person at arm's length may find 9 px bold harder.
n = 3 to 6 per variant, so differences of a few points mean nothing; the finding
is the 13-35 point step between "no legible word" and "legible word". The images
are the 560x560 backing store at device scale 1: at Windows 125% / 150% display
scaling the browser resamples the bitmap and a 1 px keyline or 9 px glyph is
softened (as the FOV frames' 1 px core already is). That is measured in Part 3
(8.7), not here.

DECISION. The banner. The panel cannot separate it from the sign, so the choice
is an engineering one: its geometry is FIXED (the sign prototype sized its plate
with measureText, which depends on the platform's font), its pole stands on the
block's map-edge column, clear of every preset's berths (2.3), and its glyphs are
CLIPPED to the cloth, so the set of pixels that may change is exact and
font-independent (8.3). If the maintainer specifically wants a mark that reads as
a FLAG, the panel says only a pennant does that - and a pennant without the word
loses 13-19 points. A pennant WITH the word beside it was not tested.

2.2  SHAPE AND SIZE AT CELL SCALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At the shipped grid (cs = 11.2, 56 px block, scale s = 1), all integer pixels,
inclusive ranges:

   pole    3 x 30 px : 1 px #FFFFFF core inside a #000000 casing (the same
                       two-tone idiom as the FOV frames), left edge 4 px in from
                       the block's MAP-EDGE side, top 3 px below the block top
   cloth  28 x 15 px : attached at the pole top, flying toward the block interior,
                       sharing the pole's inner casing column; 1 px #000000
                       casing, 1 px #FFFFFF keyline, 24 x 11 px #770099 field
   text              : 'BASE', bold 9 px monospace, #FFFFFF, centred, maxWidth
                       22 px, CLIPPED to the 24 x 11 field

   NW banner   pole x 4-6,     y 3-32        cloth x 6-33,    y 3-17
   SE banner   pole x 553-555, y 507-536     cloth x 526-553, y 507-521

   bounding box 30 x 30 px = 2.7 x 2.7 cells
   ink 495 px per banner (pole 90 + cloth 420 - 15 shared) = 15.8% of the block:
   129 black + 102 white hard-tone px, and 264 px of cloth interior
   (measured: 112 of those 264 carry glyph ink in this Chrome / font)

For scale: a UAV marker is 9 x 9 px, a firefighter 8 x 8, a victim an 11 px disc.
The banner is the largest glyph on the map and the only one carrying text, which
is the point - and at 0.16% of the canvas it costs nothing the FOV round's "never
fill" rule was protecting (that rule was about 11.6% per UAV).

Everything is fillRect on integer pixels EXCEPT the glyphs, so nothing but the
text is anti-aliased. Dimensions scale with the block (s = clamp(size*cs/56, 0.6,
1.5), each rounded to whole pixels), so the mark is pixel-exact at the shipped
grid and stays proportionate if the grid or BASE_STATION_SIZE changes. Only
s = 1 has been rendered and measured; other scales are arithmetic, not evidence.

2.3  REPLACE THE BLOCK, OR SIT ON TOP OF IT?  ON TOP - ARGUED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief says a flag plus the block is "probably clearer" and asks for the
argument rather than the assumption. Three arguments, two of them measured.

  (i)  THE BLOCK IS REAL GEOMETRY, NOT DECORATION. _build_base_station
       (wildfire_model.py:601-699) defines each depot as "a 5x5 model-level
       region": the frozenset of cells units berth on, recharge on, and return to.
       It is the base-station analogue of the FOV square - the exact footprint of
       a rule. Removing it to make room for a label would delete true information
       to add a caption.
  (ii) THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS. The block says WHERE and HOW BIG; the
       banner says WHAT. The panel shows the block alone cannot say what (50/55),
       and no reader of a text variant lost the footprint: all 12 (6 sign, 6
       banner) still describe a purple-outlined square, 9 sizing it at about
       55 px and 3 at about 50.
  (iii) THE BLOCK GIVES THE BANNER SOMEWHERE SAFE TO STAND. Berths are ranked by
       DESCENDING distance from the nearest grid edge (wildfire_model.py:647-651):
       units fill each block from its INNER corner outward, so the two map-edge
       lines of cells fill last. The pole stands in the map-edge column. The cloth
       is at the pole's TOP, because a flag is upright - and that is where the two
       shipped depots differ:

          NW banner  covers cells (0..2,49) (0..2,48) (0,47)
                     berth indices    20,21,22 / 19,12,13 / 18      -> lowest 12
                     This IS the block's outer (map) corner.
          SE banner  covers cells (47..49,4) (47..49,3) (49,2)
                     berth indices    8,15,24 / 7,14,23 / 22        -> lowest 7
                     plus a 1 px casing column in (46,3) / (46,4), 6 and 9 px.
                     Map-edge side horizontally, but the block's TOP is its INNER
                     edge vertically - the SE block's map corner is bottom-right.

       The 12-vs-7 difference is that inner-edge row (it reaches ring-2 cells),
       not the ranking's tie-break. A point-mirrored SE banner in the true outer
       corner would have a lowest index of 11 - but it would hang its cloth BELOW
       the pole, or need a stub pole, and would sit on the canvas's clipped corner
       (6.2). Kept upright, deliberately.

       WHAT THIS BUYS. The four presets use 6 / 5 / 8 / 6 units, i.e. berth
       indices 0..7. NW: nothing below 12. SE: index 7 exists only in preset C
       (5 UAV + 3 FF), where index 7 is a FIREFIGHTER berth, and firefighters home
       at depot 0 = NW. So no preset ASSIGNS a home berth under either shipped
       banner. Two honest limits: (1) all 25 cells are berths in principle, and
       the form can reach 24 units; (2) the dock fallback (agents.py:736-776,
       shipped at DOCK_FIX 2) docks a stalled UAV on whatever depot cell it is
       standing on. Neither was observed over a banner in the six runs (UAVs only
       TRANSIT (47,4) and (47,3), one step each), and neither matters, because of
       2.5: whatever stands there is drawn over the banner.

2.4  BOTH DEPOTS, SAME MARK, POLE ON THE MAP-EDGE SIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both. As docks the two are identical by construction: every unit has a berth in
EVERY depot (uav_berths_by_depot / firefighter_berths_by_depot,
wildfire_model.py:675-680), and an operator watching a UAV head for the SE corner
needs to know that corner is a base.

They are NOT identical in use, and the design deliberately does not encode that.
At default roles every unit launches from NW - trackers and firefighters always
home at depot 0, and a lone searcher falls back to it (wildfire_model.py:709-715;
common_fixed_variables.py:425-441). Measured: a unit is inside the NW block on
24.9-100.0% of steps, inside the SE block on 0.0-10.8%. But "home" is a per-UNIT
property once there are two searchers (each lane-matched to its own depot), so a
"primary" flag would be wrong for some unit in some configuration. One mark, the
same on both.

THE POLE SIDE IS MIRRORED: east edge if the block touches it, else west; the cloth
flies inward. Measured benefit: with an un-mirrored (always top-right) banner the
NW banner's lowest berth index would be 8; mirrored it is 12. Mirroring is an
improvement in margin, not the only way to keep presets clear.

OTHER ANCHORS. The pole-side rule generalises; the berth clearance does NOT, and
that should be known. Lowest berth index under the banner: NW 12, NE 11, SE 7,
CENTRAL 6, SW 5. A configuration with only a SW or CENTRAL depot would park a
preset unit under the cloth - harmless by 2.5 (the unit is drawn over it), but not
the clean separation NW enjoys. All five are fully on-canvas: the banner is INSIDE
the block and the block is on-grid by construction (0 cells of ink outside it).

2.5  Z-ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Drawn AFTER the sensing overlays (UAV frames :806-812, victim diamonds :828-847)
and AFTER the walked trails (:849-853), and BEFORE assignment lines (:854-858) and
every unit marker (:859-862) - i.e. immediately before the `// assignment lines
(A)` comment at :854. Immediate-mode canvas: the statement's position IS the
z-order.

  - ABOVE THE TRAILS - a refinement made on measurement. My first slot was just
    before the trails. Then I measured: a translucent trail (alpha 0.30 / 0.35,
    60-point history, and it PERSISTS while a unit stands still) would cross
    banner ink on 26.4% of steps (0.0-78.8% per run), tinting up to 167 of its
    990 px. A landmark should not change tint with traffic, and an untinted
    banner keeps the pixel audit's tones exact. The cost is that same ink hidden
    instead: up to ~167 px of a faint history line, about 30 px of length per
    crossing trail. Trails are neither units nor assignment relationships, so the
    FOV round's rule (below) is untouched. The banner images the panel saw are
    PIXEL-IDENTICAL under the two slots (sha256; no trail touches banner ink in
    those frames), so 2.1 carries over exactly.
  - NOT in the depot slot (:762-767), under the overlays. The banner shares two of
    its three tones with the frames, so a frame stroke crossing the cloth would
    fuse with its keyline - and it would cross on 12.3% of steps (6.2).
  - NOT on top of the units. The FOV round's rule - an overlay may occlude no unit
    and no assignment relationship - is kept. Whatever stands on the banner's
    cells is drawn over it.
  - A technical trap the depot slot hides: twoTone and snap are `const`s declared
    at :790-795, BELOW the depot block. Calling them from the depot slot is a
    temporal-dead-zone ReferenceError. The banner uses only fillRect and sits
    after :795 anyway.

3.1  METHOD, AND REPRODUCING THE FOV ROUND FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same method as the FOV round (fovframe_part1.txt section 4): CIEDE2000 from sRGB
(D65), five vision conditions - normal, protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia,
achromatic - and for a multi-tone mark the figure of merit
     score(b) = max over the mark's tones of dE2000(tone, b)
     result   = min over every background b, at the WORST vision condition.
No colour library is installed, so the maths was written from scratch, TWICE, by
two agents who did not see each other's work and were told to use different CVD
models: instrument A (outputs/_bm_colour_a.py) Machado et al. 2009; instrument B
(outputs/_bm_colour_b.py) Vienot 1999 + Brettel 1997. A cross-checker then
reviewed both CIEDE2000 implementations and both sRGB->Lab paths line by line
(no bug in either), compared 245 normal / achromatic cells (maximum |A - B| 0.01,
none over 0.05 - B does not force white exactly neutral), and wrote a THIRD
instrument with no dichromat model at all (outputs/_bm_colour_x_check.py), which
agrees with both to 4 dp on all six headline numbers.

Each instrument reproduces all 34 Sharma, Wu & Dalal pairs to 1e-4 (worst error
4.95e-05). Provenance limit: only 9 of the 34 exist as a file in this repo
(outputs/_sc_deltae.py:52-60); the other 25 were typed from recall by both agents
- the two tables are identical and all three implementations hit them, which a
mis-typed row could not do.

The FOV round's probe lived in that session's scratchpad and was never committed,
so comparability is established by REPRODUCTION:

5.1  THE ONLY HONEST MEANING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    "The Manhattan distance at which a burning cell makes THIS unit drop what it
     is doing and retreat, as the model applies it on this step."
Real, live, evaluated on every advance. It is the SAME KIND of thing as the victim
flee diamond that shipped - a fire tripwire, not a sensor - which is the honest
argument FOR drawing it, and 5.3 (b) takes that argument seriously. No radius is
invented anywhere below.

5.2  WHAT THE SIX RUNS SAY  (4,338 unit-steps, shipped mode 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATE OCCUPANCY (the state that selects the range):
        idle 62.9%   assigned 26.5%   exiting 5.6%   dead 3.1%   off-grid 1.8%
        per run: idle 38.9-81.9%, assigned 12.7-48.3%, exiting 1.1-14.4%

IS IT A FLICKER?  NO.
   90 state transitions in all (2.07 per unit per 100 steps): idle->assigned
   (dispatch) 25, assigned->exiting (pickup) 20, exiting->off-grid (hand-over) 19,
   off-grid->idle (respawn) 19, assigned->idle (recall) 5, deaths 2. Under a
   three-state frame 20 of those change nothing visible (exiting -> off-grid or
   dead is "no frame" to "no frame"), so
        VISIBLE shape changes      70 = 1.61 per unit per 100 steps
                                   = one per 62 steps = 12.4 s at 200 ms/step
        mean dwell before a change idle 16.8-105.0 steps, assigned 30.7-58.2,
                                   exiting 4.0-26.0
   and every one is a real event. The brief asks whether the flicker is
   informative or distracting: it is not a flicker, and in principle it IS

VERIFICATION, IN THREE LAYERS.
 1. Three code audits (firefighter ranges, depot rendering, palette), each
    followed by an adversarial refuter: 4 audit claims refuted and corrected - the
    count of documents stating the "original simulator unchanged" rule is three,
    not four; the frame payload CAN already distinguish the firefighter states;
    drawMap's alpha overlays have five distinct RGB bases, not four; and
    base_station_contains DOES have a caller (the UAV dock test), though Fire code
    never reads the station. Two independent colour instruments and a cross-check
    with a third: 41 quantities, 1 refuted - the WCAG "4.87" had been generalised
    beyond the 15 grounds it covers.
 2. This document's first draft, against five verifiers re-deriving every number
    and citation from raw files: 64 findings, all folded in. The ones that
    changed CONTENT rather than digits: deleting "lines 766-767" would have
    deleted the loop's closing brace; the SE banner is not in its block's outer
    corner; the panel gives no evidence the banner is seen as a flag; the 88.2%
    clipping is mostly boundary re-entry, not berths; dispatches do not all start
    at the depot, and all six runs share one victim layout; "uncoloured while
    exiting" would have hidden the cue before a death; "would damage Item A's
    gate" was a prediction - so it was measured (blind round 3) and withdrawn; my
    own pre-registered legibility gate was already failed by design-time data;
    the FOV payload digest does not cover fr.depots; and "independent of the JS"
    overstated a predictor that is independent of the browser.
 3. Where my own first design was wrong - the pennant - the correction is the
    subject of 2.1.
One slip of mine with no effect on any file: `chrome.exe --version` does not print
on Windows; it opened a window in the maintainer's existing Chrome session. The
version was then read from file metadata.
