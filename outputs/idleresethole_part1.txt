2. WHAT IS THE RESET FOR?  (asked before proposing to change it)
================================================================================
Same question that saved the `last_cell` guard itself and the `unassign()`
route_blocked preservation from a naive fix.  It has a clear answer, and the
answer separates three of the four fields from the fourth.

2.1  THE MANOEUVRE, AND WHY THREE CLEARS ARE LOAD-BEARING
-----------------------------------------------------------
  `_survival_move` performs a BOUNDED, ANCHORED relocation: an idle firefighter
  that finds fire within IDLE_RETREAT_SAFETY_BUFFER walks away from it, one cell
  per step, leashed to within IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS of where the walk began, on
  a step budget, until it reaches an ideal standoff.  Three fields are that
  manoeuvre's bookkeeping:

    _idle_retreat_origin   the leash anchor; every candidate is rejected if its
                           Manhattan distance from it exceeds 6 (885-886).
    _idle_retreat_steps    the step budget; at `steps >= 6` the scan switches
                           from "only genuinely improving steps" to "take any
                           required-safe cell, else the least-bad one".
    _idle_retreat_stalled  the verdict "nothing nearby was worth stepping to",
                           latched in one scan and read at the top of the next.

  Clearing each is essential.  A stale origin leashes a fresh retreat to a cell
  the unit left long ago - exactly the defect 62b4fbe's part 4b fixed.  A stale
  step count starts a fresh manoeuvre at or past cap and skips the
  improving-only tier entirely.  A stale stall verdict was reached under the old
  leash and must not bind the new one.  So the reset has a real and correct
  purpose, and `origin = None` is its operative sentinel: it is what makes the
  next `_survival_move` anchor afresh.

2.2  THE FOURTH FIELD IS NOT MANOEUVRE BOOKKEEPING
----------------------------------------------------
  `_idle_retreat_last_cell` is of a different kind: a ONE-MOVE-DEEP PHYSICAL
  MEMORY - the cell being vacated, written on the line immediately before the
  grid move (854, 994) - whose sole consumer is the equality filter at 883.
  Nothing about "this manoeuvre is over" makes the cell the unit occupied one
  tick ago stop being the cell it occupied one tick ago.

  FOUR INDEPENDENT PIECES OF EVIDENCE THAT CLEARING IT IS INCIDENTAL:

  (i)   NOTHING RELIES ON IT BEING None.  There is no liveness sentinel on this
        field.  The only None-test is at 902, the c4d5a25 fallback, whose
        purpose is to RE-ADMIT the cell - so a preserved value widens that
        escape hatch rather than endangering it.

  (ii)  THE COMMENTS JUSTIFY THE OTHER FIELDS AND NOT THIS ONE.  The 20-line
        comment at 770-782 explaining the re-anchor argues explicitly for
        re-anchoring the origin ("The recorded origin then belongs to a
        manoeuvre that is over ... Anchor a fresh manoeuvre here instead") and
        explicitly for the stall flag ("The stall flag goes with it: that
        verdict was reached through the old leash").  It never mentions
        `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, which line 786 clears three lines later.

  (iii) THE MOST RECENT AUTHOR HERE TREATED IT AS AN UNWANTED SIDE EFFECT AND
        REFUSED THE HELPER FOR THAT REASON.  agents.py:997-1000, added by
        62b4fbe:

            # Only the stall flag is cleared. `_reset_idle_retreat_state` would
            # also drop `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, the anti-oscillation memory, on
            # the one path being added here. The existing reset sites still fire
            # normally once the unit is genuinely safe.

        Direct evidence of intent: dropping the memory was recognised as
        undesirable and avoided wherever the author had the choice.

        AND THAT COMMENT'S SECOND SENTENCE IS FALSE FOR SITE 863.  "Once the
        unit is genuinely safe" holds for 859 (>= 4).  It does not hold for 863,
        which fires at min_fire_distance 2 or 3 - a cell the code's own
        predicate says still needs retreating from.  The author's stated model
        of when the reset fires does not match what 863 does.

  (iv)  THERE IS NO DESIGN COMMIT.  `git log -S` shows the helper, all four
        fields, the inline re-anchor and four of the five call sites arriving
        together in a4a01b4 ("Add core project files and update README"), a bulk
        import with no rationale.  The "zero everything" shape has never been
        argued anywhere.

2.3  HONEST SUMMARY OF THE PURPOSE QUESTION
---------------------------------------------
  The reset has a legitimate purpose and three of its four clears are essential
  to it.  The fourth clears a co-located spatial memory the manoeuvre does not
  own.  So this is NOT the unassign()/route_blocked situation, where a naive fix
  would have broken a real reason: there is no reason on record, in comment or
  in code, for clearing this particular field.

  What IS like those rounds is section 6: preserving it has its own hazards,
  and they are structural, not hypothetical.

================================================================================
4. THE OBVIOUS FIX IS A NO-OP.  PROVED, THEN MEASURED.
================================================================================
Both earlier rounds wrote that "the reset clears last_cell and permits a
reversal".  Taken at face value that says: stop the reset clearing it.  That
change cannot alter a single simulation step.

4.1  THE STRUCTURAL PROOF
--------------------------
  1. `_reset_idle_retreat_state` sets `_idle_retreat_origin = None` (749).
  2. `_idle_retreat_origin` has exactly three writers: 394 (__init__), 749 (the
     reset), 783 (the re-anchor).  Verified by grep.
  3. So after any reset the origin stays None until 783 next runs.
  4. Line 783 is inside the block guarded at 764-765 by
     `if origin is None or (...)`.  With origin None that guard is TRUE, so the
     NEXT `_survival_move` for that unit enters the block and runs 783-786 - and
     786 is `self._idle_retreat_last_cell = None`.
  5. The field's only two reads, 800 and 981, are both downstream of 786 in that
     same call (981 via the call to `_revalidate_idle_retreat_stall` at 793,
     which is after 786).
  =>  A value preserved past line 752 is erased at 786 before anything can read
      it.  It is unobservable.

4.2  THE MEASUREMENT
---------------------
  Arm `keeplc`: the reset clears origin, steps and stalled but PRESERVES
  `_idle_retreat_last_cell`.  Monkeypatch; nothing written to source.

    13 of 13 runs TRAJECTORY-IDENTICAL to stock - full per-step position and
    death trace, not merely equal totals.  r29/d23/ff6 both sides.  Tight
    reversals 186 -> 186.  Hole reversals 7 -> 7, at the same steps.

  The write log shows it directly.  ed_202, arm keeplc, step 207:
      step 207  site 786  in_reset=False   [29, 49] -> None
  The reset kept the memory; the re-anchor threw it away one step later, because
  the same reset had nulled the origin.

4.3  CONSEQUENCE FOR ANY FIX
------------------------------
  A change confined to `_reset_idle_retreat_state`, or to call sites 859/863,
  CANNOT change behaviour.  To close the hole a change must also stop line 786
  clearing the field - and 786 fires on the first `_survival_move` of every
  fresh manoeuvre (169 times in 13 runs), not only after a reset.  That is a
  materially wider change than the earlier rounds' framing implies.

6. RISKS OF THE ONLY FIX THAT CAN WORK
================================================================================
Section 4 shows a fix must stop line 786 clearing the field.  That has a wider
blast radius than "change the reset", and real hazards.  The last_cell round
built three arms because a change to anti-oscillation state can push units into
worse behaviour, and its arm A measured actively harmful.

6.1  THE STRUCTURAL HAZARDS  (from code reading, not measured to occur)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (a) EMPTY CANDIDATE SET AT THE FIRST SCAN OF A FRESH MANOEUVRE.  At a
      re-anchor the origin is set to the current cell, so every neighbour is at
      Manhattan 1 and the leash excludes nothing.  The only exclusions there are
      out-of-bounds, active fire, and last_cell.  A preserved last_cell can
      therefore empty the candidate set where it currently cannot - and for an
      idle unit an empty set means lines 806-810 latch `stalled` and return
      WITHOUT MOVING.
  (b) THAT LATCH IS CLOSE TO ABSORBING.  A latched idle unit is routed into
      `_revalidate_idle_retreat_stall`, which re-reads the SAME preserved
      last_cell and re-applies the same exclusion, and which deliberately has no
      tier-3 fallback.
  (c) THE c4d5a25 FALLBACK DOES NOT COVER (a).  It is gated on
      `current_dist == 0`; a unit merely adjacent to fire is at 1 and gets no
      second chance.  A no-move step next to fire is not benign - the model
      kills a firefighter outright as soon as its cell is in fire_cells.
  (d) STALENESS.  `_move_toward` and `_assigned_one_step_retreat` never write
      the field (verified: neither contains any `_idle_retreat_*` reference).  A
      unit that retreated at X, was then walked 50 cells by `_move_toward`, and
      later went idle again would still carry last_cell == X - exactly the class
      of staleness the re-anchor comment complains about for the origin.

6.2  WHAT THE MEASUREMENT SAYS ABOUT THOSE HAZARDS
----------------------------------------------------
  On the canonical sample they did not materialise.  hardlc vs none, 13 runs:

    empty candidate sets                72  ->  67      (fewer, not more)
    stalled share of live agent-steps   4.61% -> 4.43%  (fewer, not more)
    survival moves / survival calls     78.5% -> 77.1%
    live agent-steps                    5316 -> 5419    (+103, one fewer death)
    scans where the guard was armed      293 -> 429     (+46%)
    scans where it excluded a neighbour  251 -> 377     (+50%)

  The guard is armed at ~46% more scans and excludes a neighbour ~50% more
  often, and nothing was stranded.  Reassuring - but 13 runs, and the hazards
  above are structural, so a larger sample could still find them.

6.3  NOTHING IN THE TEST SUITE WOULD CATCH EITHER VARIANT
-----------------------------------------------------------
  No test in tests/ exercises `_survival_move`, `_reset_idle_retreat_state`, the
  stall latch, the leash, or the last_cell guard.  Every Firefighter test
  constructs a fresh agent whose `_idle_retreat_last_cell` is None from
  __init__.  tests/_run_standoff_validation.py concerns UAV fire-tracker
  standoff, not firefighters, and is not collected by pytest (no `test_`
  prefix).  Baseline remains 8 failed / 427 passed.  A fix here would have to be
  validated by simulation, not by the suite.

8. VERDICT
================================================================================

  (D) ALREADY RESOLVED - RULED OUT.  agents.py is byte-identical between
      c4d5a25 and HEAD (blob 399fa7f3...); no later commit touched it.

  (C) NOT A DEFECT - REJECTED, though it has a real case.  Clearing the memory
      at a genuine standoff (site 859, min_fire_distance >= 4) is defensible,
      and section 5.1 shows the resulting reversals are the code's own best
      move in 6 of 7 cases.  But site 863 - which opened 6 of the 7 - fires on a
      cell at min_fire_distance 2 or 3, which the code's own hazard predicate
      still classifies as needing retreat, and the unit re-enters the retreat
      scan on the very next step 100% of the time.  Declaring the manoeuvre over
      there is inconsistent with the code's own definition of safe, and with the
      author's stated model of these sites at agents.py:999-1000.  So there IS
      something wrong here, and (C) would overstate the case.

  (A) REAL DEFECT, ACTIONABLE - NOT SUPPORTED AT THIS SAMPLE SIZE.  One
      firefighter death (sh_101 ff_unit_1) is traceably attributable to a hole
      reversal, and blocking it saves that unit.  That single case is real and I
      am not discounting it.  But on 10 independent seeds where the fix fires 8
      times, the outcome delta is exactly zero, and the aggregate across 23 runs
      is +1/-1/-1 concentrated in the characterisation sample.  Recommending a
      change to anti-oscillation state on that evidence would be tuning to this
      round's own numbers - the exact thing the idle-retreat round refused to do
      with its section 4.4 proposal.

  >>> (B) REAL BUT INERT.  The reversals are real, they fire regularly (15 in 23
      runs), they are correctly attributed to this code, and they have NO
      consequence that survives an independent sample.  RECOMMEND LEAVING IT IN
      PLACE, DOCUMENTED.

  THE ONE THING THAT QUALIFIES (B), STATED PLAINLY RATHER THAN BURIED: this is
  not literally "no observable consequence".  There is one observable
  consequence in 23 runs - sh_101 ff_unit_1, a firefighter that lives under the
  counterfactual and dies under stock, by a legible mechanism (greedy re-entry
  into a corner pocket).  What the evidence does not support is that this
  generalises: 8 further firings on fresh seeds produced nothing.  A reader who
  weighs that one traceable death more heavily than the fresh-seed null could
  reasonably call this (A); I do not, for the reasons above, and the numbers to
  make that judgement are all in sections 5 and 7.

  WHAT THE EARLIER ROUNDS GOT RIGHT AND WRONG
    RIGHT  the reversals exist, are on the idle/unlatched survival path, number
           2 on the changed seed of the last_cell round, and 27 of that seed's
           30 tight reversals are `_move_toward` pairs.  All four re-verified
           exactly.
    WRONG  "the pre-existing reset at agents.py:859/863 ... clears last_cell and
           permits a reversal" names half the mechanism.  The reset's own clear
           is REDUNDANT: the inline re-anchor at 786 clears the field again one
           step later, because the same reset nulled the origin.  A fix aimed at
           the reset alone is provably and measurably a no-op.  Neither round
           was in a position to notice this, since neither built the arm.

9. IF IT WERE EVER TO BE FIXED - SCOPE, FOR THE RECORD, NOT RECOMMENDED NOW
================================================================================
Recorded so a later round does not have to re-derive it.  NOT proposed.

  WHAT WILL NOT WORK
    * Preserving last_cell inside `_reset_idle_retreat_state`.  Provable no-op
      (section 4).
    * Removing the reset calls at 859/863, or gating them.  Same reason: 786
      still clears the field on the next scan.

  THE MINIMUM CHANGE THAT CHANGES ANYTHING
    Stop line 786 clearing `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, i.e. re-anchor the leash
    (origin, steps, stall verdict) without discarding the one-move-deep spatial
    memory - which is precisely the distinction agents.py:997-1000 already draws
    on the revalidation path.  Removing the clear at 752 as well is optional and
    free (it is dominated by 786), and is worth doing for consistency so the two
    code paths agree about what "end the manoeuvre" means.

  A NARROWER VARIANT WORTH MEASURING FIRST, IF ANYONE PICKS THIS UP
    `hardlc` keeps the memory alive everywhere, which is why it perturbs 9 of 13
    canonical and 3 of 10 fresh trajectories - far more than the 15 reversals it
    removes.  A tighter arm would suppress the 786 clear ONLY when the re-anchor
    was triggered by `origin is None` set by a reset in the IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING
    step, leaving the genuine 62b4fbe staleness re-anchor (Manhattan > 6)
    untouched.  That targets exactly the 15 reversals and should be close to
    trajectory-neutral elsewhere.  Alternatively, address site 863 directly:
    require `_cell_is_ideal_idle_standoff` there as 859 already does, so the
    manoeuvre is only declared over when the unit is actually safe.  That is a
    smaller, better-motivated change and it addresses the inconsistency
    identified in section 2.2(iii) rather than the symptom.

  HOW TO VALIDATE IF IT IS EVER BUILT
    Both variants are monkeypatchable - `hardlc` already is, via a property
    interceptor on the field keyed on caller line, with no source edit - so A/B
    them as arms first, exactly as the last_cell round did with its three.
    Gates: the canonical 13 AND at least 10 fresh seeds (the fresh set is what
    would have stopped this round over-claiming); the route_blocked gate, which
    is required because this changes simulation source; no unit left stranded
    (watch empty-candidate scans and stalled share, section 6.2); and
    8 failed / 427 passed with an identical failure set.
