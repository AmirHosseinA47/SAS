2.3  WHY THIS HUE AND NOT A DARK RED OR A BROWN

Four colours were proposed under four independent lenses (collision analysis,
wildfire-cartography semantics, colour-vision accessibility, minimal-change
conservatism) and scored by three independent judges (an adversarial one that
re-derived every number from scratch, an operator one, an implementer one).
#895e00 won all three, unanimously. The conventional answers were tried and
lost on measurement, not on taste:

  #66301c  "ember char", dark burnt-sienna. BEST SEMANTIC READ of the four -
           the colour you would reach for picking by intuition. Rejected: its
           separation from burnt collapses when chroma does. Two independent
           re-implementations measured 13.88 dE protanopia and 6.45 dE
           achromatic against #2b2b2b. The proposal claim of 23.78 protan did
           not survive re-checking, and was backwards on its face - a warm
           brown against neutral charcoal must LOSE separation as chroma
           collapses, not gain it. On a greyscale print or a washed-out
           projector, ember char re-merges with burnt. That is this defect
           returning.

  #4d3020  "smouldering brown". Same failure, worse: 3.84 dE achromatic vs
           burnt. This lens did the best source analysis of the four, and its
           own neutral-grey sweep is what proves a lightness-only answer is
           impossible.

  #9a6960  "scorched terracotta". Wins the narrow contest on burnt separation
           under colour-vision deficiency (24.65 worst case). Rejected because
           it bought that margin by moving the ground to L*49, which parks it
           19.34 dE from #888888, the return_to_base UAV drawn ON TOP of it,
           at a WCAG contrast of 1.30:1 - the marker disappears into the
           ground. Its submitted table omitted all 11 overlay colours, so this
           was concealed rather than weighed. Visible in the rendered
           comparison: the terracotta panel also blurs into the smoke grey.

  #895e00  holds its separation from burnt in EVERY condition tested -
           31.3 normal, 32.0 deuteranopia, 29.4 protanopia, 34.6 tritanopia,
           19.9 achromatic - and stays clear of every overlay marker (nearest
           is #FF8C00 at 27.54). It is warm (h=78.2 deg), so it reads as
           fire-adjacent ground rather than as safe ground, but it sits 14-41
           L* BELOW the entire fire ramp, so it cannot be mistaken for an
           active burn - and it is never drawn on a burning or smoking cell
           anyway, by branch order.

I also looked at the rendered result rather than trusting the arithmetic. All
six candidates were painted onto the same real run at step 60
(outputs/_sc_candidates.png). The verdict from the image matches the numbers:
#9a6960 blurs into the smoke grey; #66301c and #4d3020 are legible but
noticeably quieter than the fire they sit behind; #895e00 separates cleanly
from both the charcoal and the greens at 7px cells.

HONEST WEAKNESS, recorded so it is not discovered later. At h=78.2 deg this is
a yellow-bronze, not an ember red. Out of context it could read as dry
stubble. In context it will always be surrounded by burnt, fire and smoke, and
the legend will name it - but if the priority is that the colour be
self-evidently "ember" to someone who never reads a legend, #7a3b12 is the
nearest defensible fallback (still 25.4 dE from burnt normal-vision, 20.1
protan), at the cost of ~4 dE of separation and of the achromatic margin.
I recommend #895e00 because a colour that fails on a greyscale projector fails
in exactly the conditions where an operator is most likely to be misreading
the map already.

A SECOND HONEST NOTE, on the whole approach. Under protanopia #895e00 lands
1.02 dE from #fe2301. That is not a regression this change introduces: the
shipped palette already puts the canvas background #1c630b and the hottest
fire #fe0101 at 0.40 dE under protanopia, so a protanope already cannot
separate the fire front from unburned ground; only 1 of the 666 existing pairs
is tighter than the one I add. The critical scorched/burnt split survives at
29.4 dE. The real conclusion is that colour alone should not be the only
hazard channel on this display - a hatch or stipple on scorched cells would be
robust to every deficiency at once. That is a follow-up, not a blocker, and I
am not doing it in a round scoped to a colour literal.

2.4  CONSTANT OR INLINE LITERAL

Inline literal, matching the file existing convention: #2b2b2b is inline twice
and #2f4a1a is inline once; common_fixed_variables.py holds ramps
(VEGETATION_COLORS, FIRE_COLORS, SMOKE_COLORS), not single-state colours.

Recorded as a known trade, not hidden.

3.3  WHY (a) IS NOT A JUDGEMENT CALL

Both sides of the argument agreed on this one, from opposite directions. The
muting line at :145 is an UNCONDITIONAL overwrite that runs AFTER _cell_color
has already assigned the scorched colour. The self-guard is the only thing
standing between the new scorched colour and being painted over.

Scorched cells have fuel > 0 and are by definition not burning and not
smoking, so the self-guard is the ONLY test they would fail. Drop them from it
and they are all eligible for muting. A cell that re-ignites 96.6% of the time
and carries a ~15x per-step firefighter death rate would then be painted
#2f4a1a and labelled in the legend as "spared veg".

That is strictly worse than the defect being fixed. Today bug says "this
dangerous ground is safe burnt ground". That bug would say "this dangerous
ground is vegetation the fire never reached" - we would have built a colour to
mark the hazard and then overwritten it, on its own footprint, with the map
affirmative safety colour.

The structural rule that prevents it: the self-guard must remain LITERALLY the
same expression as _cell_color burned-family test at :63. Both new colours
still live under that same has_burned umbrella. Keep the two expressions
identical and the guard is correct by construction; derive "darkness" a second
way and they can desynchronise.

3.4  WHY (b) - THE ARGUABLE HALF - ALSO STAYS

The case for removing scorched from the neighbour count is real and I want it
on the record, because it is the intuitive answer: a green cell with two
scorched neighbours is arguably the most dangerous unburnt cell on the map -
intact fuel against ground that relights 96.6% of the time - and the pass
specifically selects it for dimming. Under a burnt-only count, a mute would
mean at least two neighbours in a genuinely absorbing state (0 re-ignitions in
23,126 opportunities), which is a claim the model can actually back.

Three things defeat it.

  1. THE PASS CANNOT MAKE A SAFETY CLAIM AT ALL. It only ever recolours cells
     that are unburnt, and it recolours them green -> green. Its whole output
     vocabulary is one flat "muted or not" on honestly-green cells. It cannot
     make any cell look safer or more dangerous than it is; the entire hazard
     encoding is carried by the fire / scorched / burnt colours and the
     legend. This is a SHAPE heuristic - "is this pocket embedded in the burn
     scar" - and "scar" means ground the fire has been through, which is
     exactly has_burned. Fuel remaining is a claim about the FUTURE; the
     neighbour count asks a question about the PAST.

  2. REMOVING IT WOULD CREATE A PHANTOM EVENT AND A STROBE. has_burned is
     monotone; burning is not. At step 60 scorched outnumbers burnt, so a
     pocket showing 4 dark neighbours today would show ~1.5 and fall under the
     >= 2 threshold - the muting would switch off through the middle of a run
     and back on late as scorched drains into burnt. An operator would watch
     pockets that never change state sit bright green and then silently darken
     around step 130. Worse, burning cells are dark under any variant, so a
     scorched-but-quiet neighbour that is not dark while scorched-and-aflame
     IS dark would flip on every re-ignition cycle - and 96.6% of them
     re-ignite, repeatedly. A pocket resting on two such neighbours would
     flicker between #2f4a1a and bright green every few frames, tracking
     nothing in the world.

  3. dark_n >= 2 IS A TUNED CONSTANT, TUNED AGAINST A DARK SET THAT HAS ALWAYS
     INCLUDED SCORCHED. Shrinking the set silently tightens the threshold.
     Recovering the coverage would mean >= 1, which over-mutes.

And the decisive one, given 3.1: THE PASS IS DEAD. Counting scorched is not
"keeping the current behaviour" - there is no current behaviour. But by the
same token, NOT counting it is not a fix either; it is shipping a new,
unobservable, unvalidatable effect into a dead branch, in a round whose gate
is "simulation provably unchanged, colour visibly split". The conservative
choice is to leave the semantics exactly as the pass author tuned them and
change only what is demonstrably wrong - the name.

THE CRITICAL FINDING, and it changes the answer: has_burned_cells IS NOT THE
SCORCHED COUNT. From agents.py:26, :105-106, :109-110 - has_burned is set the
first spread-tick a cell is burning and is NEVER cleared, and burnt implies
has_burned. So

    has_burned = burnt  UNION  scorched  UNION  (burning cells past one tick)

It is a cumulative "ever ignited" figure that double-counts cells the map
paints orange and grey, sitting in the ground-state column directly beneath
"burnt", where the pair reads as two disjoint buckets. They are nested: burnt
is a SUBSET of charred.

AND SCORCHED IS NOT RECOVERABLE BY SUBTRACTION IN THE BROWSER.
FIRE_SPREAD_SPEED is 3 (common_fixed_variables.py:23). Ignition happens in
