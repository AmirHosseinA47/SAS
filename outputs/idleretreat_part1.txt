VERDICT ON THE BRIEF'S DESCRIPTION: ACCURATE. Three refinements follow, none
of which overturn it.

  CONFIRMED. The three candidate filters are exactly as described - fire
  (:785-786), anti-oscillation `last_cell` (:787-788), and the
  IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS=6 leash from `_idle_retreat_origin` (:789-791) - and
  the hard early return at :772-775 sits above all of them. For an idle unit
  `self.target_pos` is falsy, so :773 short-circuits and :775 is a bare
  `return`: no candidate scan, no hazard re-evaluation, no move.

  REFINEMENT 1 - the latch is usually set by a call that DID move.
  Two of the three live idle set-sites (:846 and :862) fire on a call that
  goes on to move the unit at :878. Their meaning is not "I could not move"
  but "nothing I could reach was any better, so I took the least-bad step and
  I am marking that stepping again is pointless". Only :810 means "no
  candidates at all". This matters for the fix: the latch's legitimate content
  is anti-drift, not anti-null-result.

  REFINEMENT 2 - there are five `_reset_idle_retreat_state()` call sites, not
  two: :597 (the standby branch of `advance`), :761, :855, :882 and :886. The
  investigation named only :597 and :761. Its conclusion is nevertheless
  correct: :855, :882 and :886 all sit BELOW the early return at :772-775, so
  once the flag is set control never reaches them again for an idle unit.
  Three more reset sites than stated; exactly zero of them reachable while
  latched.

  REFINEMENT 3 - :864-868 and :871-875 are unreachable. `chosen` is assigned
  `max(...)` over `candidates` in every branch, and `candidates` is non-empty
  at that point (:807 returned otherwise), so `chosen is None` at :864 cannot
  happen. `target` comes from `_neighbor_cells()`, which only emits
  `(cx +/- 1, cy)` or `(cx, cy +/- 1)`, so `target == cell` at :871 cannot
  happen. Both are harmless and are NOT touched. The live idle latch sites are
  :810, :846 and :862.

WHY THE LATCH IS PERMANENT FOR AN IDLE UNIT - re-derived from source:
  * `_survival_move` is reached for an idle unit only from `advance():460` ->
    `_needs_immediate_survival_retreat()` true, which for an idle unit
    (agents.py:684-702) means: on fire, OR smoke on the cell, OR orthogonally
    adjacent to fire, OR min-Manhattan fire distance <= 3.
  * `_cell_is_ideal_idle_standoff` (:738-746) requires: not on fire, not
    adjacent to fire, no smoke, AND min fire distance >= 4. That is the exact
    logical complement, so the reset at :761 can never fire on the call path
    that reaches it.
  * The standby branch at :589-603 is an `elif` in a chain whose first arm
    (:460) already returned at :497 whenever the unit is threatened, so :597
    is unreachable while fire is near.
  * The unit does not move, so it cannot change its own distance to the fire.
  The only exits are: the fire burns out and recedes, the unit is reassigned
  (which masks but does not clear the flag), or it dies. CONFIRMED.

        investigation's own tracing of the idle population to
        `unassign / replacement_after_blocked` churn: stale origins need a
        unit to be stranded far from where its retreat began, and these seeds
        produced less of that.
      * 4b is a no-op when the condition is absent, so carrying it costs
        nothing. But it should NOT be sold as a general win. Part 4 should be
        read expecting 4a to do most of the visible work.

PART 2 - DESIGN

2.0  WHAT THE LATCH IS FOR - STATED BEFORE ANYTHING IS REMOVED
---------------------------------------------------------------
There is no commit that explains it. `git log -S "_idle_retreat_stalled"` and
`-S "IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS"` over agents.py each return exactly one commit,
a4a01b4 "Add core project files and update README" - the bulk initial import.
Nothing in docs/ or README.md mentions it. So the intent has to be read off
the three live set sites, and here is that reading:

  `_idle_retreat_stalled` is a FUTILITY marker on a retreat manoeuvre:
    :810  "the filter chain gave me nothing to step to."
    :846  "I could step somewhere, but nothing I can reach improves my
           position. I took the least-bad step and I am marking that stepping
           again is pointless."
    :862  "I have spent my six-cell retreat budget and still cannot reach a
           safe cell."
  Together with `_idle_retreat_last_cell` (block the immediate reversal) and
  IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS (bound the total excursion), the design intent is: a
  retreat is a bounded, monotonically-improving manoeuvre, and when it stops
  improving it should stop, rather than let an idle unit drift or ping-pong
  across the map making moves that achieve nothing.

  THAT INTENT IS LEGITIMATE AND IS KEPT. What is defective is not the futility
  verdict but its LIFETIME. The verdict is computed against one fire geometry
  at one step and then read as permanent. Fire spreads every step; at :846 and
  :862 the unit's own position changed as well. And 1.1 proves no reset is
  reachable while the unit is still threatened. So a statement about one step
  silently becomes a statement about the rest of the run.

  This is the same defect shape as the route_blocked consumer latch fixed in
  70e1b33: a flag raised on a specific failure, consumed as a permanent
  property, clearable only by the very action it suppresses.

2.1  (4a) THE LATCH - REPLACE THE EARLY RETURN WITH A REAL RECHECK
-------------------------------------------------------------------
PROPOSED, agents.py only. Replacing :772-775:

      if bool(getattr(self, "_idle_retreat_stalled", False)):
          if self.target_pos:
              self._assigned_one_step_retreat(fire_cells)
              return
          self._revalidate_idle_retreat_stall(cell, origin, fire_cells)
          return

  ASSIGNED UNITS ARE BYTE-IDENTICAL. The existing code is
  `if self.target_pos and self._assigned_one_step_retreat(...): return` /
  `return`; because `and` short-circuits and both arms return, that is exactly
  "if assigned, try a one-step retreat; return either way". The rewrite is the
  same statement, restructured only so the idle arm can be given a body. No
  assigned-unit behaviour changes this round.

  THE NEW METHOD:

      def _revalidate_idle_retreat_stall(self, cell, origin, fire_cells):
          last_cell = getattr(self, "_idle_retreat_last_cell", None)
          current_dist = self._min_fire_distance(cell, fire_cells)
          current_risk = self._firefighter_cell_risk(cell)
          chosen = self._pick_improving_retreat(
              self._retreat_candidates(
                  cell, origin, last_cell, fire_cells, current_dist),
              current_dist, current_risk)
          if chosen is None:
              return                    # nothing changed: stay put, stay latched
          steps = int(getattr(self, "_idle_retreat_steps", 0) or 0)
          self._idle_retreat_last_cell = cell
          self.model.grid.move_agent(self, chosen["cell"])
          self._idle_retreat_steps = steps + 1
          self._idle_retreat_stalled = False

  SAME FILTER CHAIN BY CONSTRUCTION, NOT BY COPY. The loop at :783-805 is
  extracted verbatim into `_retreat_candidates(cell, origin, last_cell,
  fire_cells, current_dist)` and the main path rewritten to call it. Both
  paths then share one definition of "no candidates" and cannot drift apart in
  a future edit. Same iteration order, same three filters, same dict keys, so
  the unlatched path stays bit-identical.

  SELECTION IS SHARED TOO, AND THAT IS THE HEART OF THE DESIGN.
  `_pick_improving_retreat(candidates, current_dist, current_risk)` is tiers 1
  and 2 of the existing not-at-cap choice, extracted verbatim:
      tier 1  an `ideal` standoff, best by (dist, -risk)          :815-820
      tier 2  else a strictly `improving` cell, best by
              (improvement, dist, -risk)                          :821-839
      else    None
  The existing tier 3 - `max(candidates)` taken even though nothing improves,
  then latch (:841-846) - is deliberately NOT in the helper. That tier is
  precisely the move the latch exists to stop repeating. The main path keeps
  it (calls the helper, falls back to tier 3 on None); revalidation does not.

  So the rule is exactly: WHEN LATCHED, RE-RUN THE SAME SCAN; MOVE ONLY ON A
  GENUINE IMPROVEMENT; OTHERWISE STAY PUT AND STAY LATCHED. When nothing has
  changed, the observable behaviour is identical to today - same position,
  same flag - which is what keeps this from being a behavioural rewrite.

  WHY "IDEAL" IS TAKEN EVEN WHEN IT IS NOT DISTANCE-IMPROVING. An ideal
  standoff is the terminal state of the manoeuvre - it is what triggers the
  resets at :761 and :881-882. Reaching one ends the retreat and puts the unit
  outside the condition that calls `_survival_move` at all, so it cannot feed
  an oscillation. A unit standing in smoke at fire-distance 5 should take a
  smoke-free neighbour at distance 4; tier 1 allows that and tier 2 alone
  would not.

  WHY at_cap IS NOT CONSULTED ON THE REVALIDATION PATH. `at_cap` gates a step
  COUNT; the leash already bounds the spatial excursion, which is the real
  containment. An at_cap arm on revalidation would add a second way to become
  permanently stuck - the exact bug class being removed. The at_cap arm on the
  main path is untouched.

  WHY THE TAIL CLEARS ONLY THE STALL FLAG, RATHER THAN CALLING
  `_reset_idle_retreat_state()`. The main path's post-move rule (:881-886)
  resets when the landing cell is ideal, or required-safe with
  `(at_cap or stalled)`. Mirroring that here was implemented and MEASURED, and
  rejected: `_reset_idle_retreat_state` also clears `_idle_retreat_last_cell`,
  the anti-oscillation memory, on the very path being added. Clearing only the
  stall flag keeps the leash anchor, the step budget and the last_cell guard
  intact, and the existing reset sites still fire normally once the unit is
  genuinely safe (the standby branch at :597 handles exactly that). Both
  variants were run on D/south seed 101: deaths identical, movelog identical,
  and the full 480-row firefighter trace differed in 2 rows, in the `origin`
  bookkeeping field only - positions and death flags identical. So this is a
  free choice on the evidence, taken in the direction that preserves more
  anti-oscillation state.

2.2  (4a) RECHECK FREQUENCY - EVERY STEP, AND WHY THAT IS NOT ARBITRARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * The rechecked condition is "is a neighbour strictly better", a function of
    the fire geometry. Fire spreads once per model step, so the condition
    changes at exactly step granularity; a lower rate would sample below the
    rate of the thing being measured.
  * It is not a background poll. `_survival_move` is entered only when the
    unit is on fire, in smoke, adjacent to fire, or within 3 cells of it, so
    "every step" already means "every step in which this unit is in danger".
  * Cost is negligible against work the same call already does.
    `_survival_move` calls `_fire_cells()` unconditionally at :758, sweeping
    every agent in the schedule. The added work is at most four
    `_firefighter_cell_risk` + `_min_fire_distance` evaluations.
  * The measured windows are short. Idle-to-death in the investigation's
    sample is [1,2,3,4,8,8,8,9,36,47,77] - median 8, and four of eleven at 4
    steps or fewer. Rechecking every N>1 steps would miss (1 - 1/N) of the
    windows in which escape was available, and would miss the short mode
    almost entirely.

  RELATIONSHIP TO THE 70e1b33 TEMPLATE. That fix added an out-of-band
  model-level pass because the stale flag's owner - an unassigned unit - had
  no per-step code path of its own that could re-test it. Here the opposite is
  true: `_survival_move` is ALREADY called every step for exactly the
  population that needs rechecking. The pattern is the same (re-test the real
  condition instead of trusting a flag set under an older world state); the
  placement is in-band because the natural hook already exists. A second
  model-level pass would duplicate a call that already happens.

2.3  (4a) ANTI-OSCILLATION - WHY THIS CANNOT PING-PONG
-------------------------------------------------------
The investigation warns that removing the latch without a genuine
anti-ping-pong term risks a unit alternating forever, which "would look like a
fix (no more stalls) while still never escaping". Four independent reasons
this design cannot do that:

  1. `last_cell` STAYS IN THE CHAIN. `_retreat_candidates` keeps the
     `ncell == last_cell` filter verbatim, and every revalidation move sets
     `_idle_retreat_last_cell = cell` before moving. The immediate A->B->A
     reversal is blocked exactly as today. The tail deliberately does not
     clear it (2.1).
  2. EVERY REVALIDATION MOVE IS STRICTLY IMPROVING. Tier 2 requires
     `improvement > 0`, or strictly lower risk at not-lower distance. Within a
     fixed fire geometry the key (dist, -risk) strictly increases on every
     such move, so no cycle is possible within a step's geometry. Tier 1
     terminates the manoeuvre outright.
  3. THE LEASH STILL BOUNDS THE EXCURSION. Revalidation moves go through the
     unchanged `from_origin > IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS` filter, so the unit
     stays inside a 6-cell Manhattan diamond around its (re-anchored) origin.
     Worst case is a bounded walk driven by real fire movement, not drift.
  4. REVALIDATION IS STRICTLY MORE CONSERVATIVE ABOUT MOVING THAN THE CODE
     ALREADY IS. The existing unlatched path at :841-846 WILL take a
     non-improving move; revalidation refuses to. On the "moves when it
     shouldn't" axis the new path is a subset of behaviour the unlatched path
     already exhibits, so the fix cannot introduce a class of movement the
     current code would not already make.

  MEASURED IN THE WHAT-IF RUNS (2.6): across both applied seeds, zero
  reversals originated from a latched escape. The two reversals observed are
  both on paths this round does not change - see the two notes below.

  NOTED, NOT FIXED (1): `_assigned_one_step_retreat` (:888-916) has NO
  anti-oscillation term at all - no last_cell, no leash. The recorded stock
  traces already show it ping-ponging: D/south seed 101 ff_unit_0 moves
  (45,29)->(46,29) at step 99 AND again at step 101, so it moved back at step
  100. Pre-existing, on the assigned path, out of scope. Recorded so that a
  Part 4 reversal is not misattributed to this change.

  NOTED, NOT FIXED (2): on the EXISTING path, a tier-3 move that lands on a
  required-safe cell triggers `_reset_idle_retreat_state()` at :883-886, which
  clears `last_cell` and so permits an immediate reversal on the next step.
  This was observed directly in the applied run (D/south 101 ff_unit_1,
  (0,3)->(1,3) at step 127 and back at step 128, both moves unlatched). It is
  entirely pre-existing code; the revalidation tail avoids adding new
  instances of it by not resetting (2.1).

2.4  (4b) THE LEASH
--------------------
THE DEFECT. `from_origin` at :789 is measured against `_idle_retreat_origin`,
set only at :766 and cleared only by `_reset_idle_retreat_state()`. A unit
that was assigned, walked across the map, and was then unassigned or recycled
is still leashed to wherever it stood when its ORIGINAL retreat began.
Measured: 17 of 17 leash-caused zero-candidate verdicts on idle units are
this (1.4); observed staleness distances of 7, 9 and 40 cells.

TWO DESIGNS WERE OFFERED IN THE BRIEF. Neither was chosen; a third was, and
the reasoning below includes the part where the first attempt at it was WRONG
and the check caught it.

  (i) RESET THE ORIGIN AT unassign (wildfire_model.py:3520-3524) AND recycle
      (2077-2101). Correct as far as it goes, but it enumerates transitions,
      and 1.2 found a third drift source those two do not cover:
      `_assigned_one_step_retreat` moves the unit with no leash test and never
      touches the origin, entirely inside agents.py. Any future mover would
      need adding to the list too. It also requires wildfire_model.py to know
      about firefighter retreat bookkeeping it has no other reason to know
      about, and puts an edit inside the unassign path the previous two rounds
      worked in.

  (ii) COMPARE AGAINST A "CURRENT ASSIGNMENT-START POSITION". This needs a new
      persisted field recording the assignment-start cell - the same class of
      object with the same staleness exposure - and a never-assigned unit has
      no assignment start at all.

  (iii) CHOSEN: RE-ANCHOR A PROVABLY STALE ORIGIN, IN agents.py, AT :764-770.

      origin = getattr(self, "_idle_retreat_origin", None)
      if origin is None or (
          not self.target_pos
          and abs(cell[0] - origin[0]) + abs(cell[1] - origin[1])
          > IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS
      ):
          self._idle_retreat_origin = cell
          self._idle_retreat_steps = 0
          self._idle_retreat_stalled = False
          self._idle_retreat_last_cell = None
          origin = cell

      This is not a heuristic. It is the negation of an invariant the code
      itself maintains, so "the origin is stale" is DECIDED, not guessed.

      THE INVARIANT, AND THE CORRECTION THE CHECK FORCED. The first form of
      the claim was: "_survival_move alone can never leave a unit more than
      IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS from its origin, because the origin is set to the
      unit's own cell (d=0) and every move it makes passed the
      `from_origin > 6 -> continue` filter (d<=6)."

      That claim was tested against the recorded traces BEFORE any code was
      written (outputs/_ir_invariant.py) and it was REFUTED: 10 of 143 moves
      landed at d = 7 to 10. The reason is that `_survival_move` has TWO
      movers, not one - the leashed candidate move at :878, and
      `_assigned_one_step_retreat`, which has no leash test. All 10 violations
      were the second mover.

      CORRECTED INVARIANT, which the design now rests on: all four call sites
      of `_assigned_one_step_retreat` (:773, :808, :865, :872) are guarded by
      `self.target_pos`, so it can only ever run for an ASSIGNED unit. For an
      IDLE unit the leashed move at :878 is the only mover inside
      `_survival_move`. Re-tested on the same traces, split by engagement:

          idle moves with an origin        64      landings beyond leash:  0
          assigned moves with an origin    79      landings beyond leash: 10

      So for an IDLE unit, d > IDLE_RETREAT_MAX_CELLS is sound proof that its
      position or origin changed during an earlier assigned/exiting spell -
      the origin belongs to a manoeuvre that is over.

      THE `not self.target_pos` GUARD IS LOAD-BEARING, not decoration. Without
      it the re-anchor would fire on assigned units, where d>6 is normal and
      proves nothing, and would change assigned-unit behaviour - out of scope,
      and squarely in the population entangled with the route_blocked rounds.

      WHY THE STALL FLAG IS CLEARED TOO. The stall verdict in these cases was
      REACHED THROUGH the stale leash: the unit latched at :810 because
      `n_candidates == 0`, and the candidates were empty because every
      neighbour was leash-excluded. Correcting the leash invalidates the
      premise the verdict was derived from, so keeping the verdict would be
      exactly the bug being fixed - trusting a conclusion drawn from input now
      known to be wrong. The conservative variant (re-anchor, keep the latch,
      let revalidation decide) was considered and is measurably worse: see
      2.6, where the re-anchor's fresh-manoeuvre step is what moves D/south
      101 ff_unit_0 off the cell that later burns.

      THIS CANNOT SELF-SUSTAIN. After a re-anchor, d = 0, and the unit's own
      leashed moves keep d <= 6. So it cannot re-anchor itself; another
      re-anchor requires another external relocation, which requires a real
      dispatch event.

      WHY THIS IS BETTER THAN (i):
        * exact, not enumerated: it fires on staleness however it arose -
          unassign, recycle, `_move_toward` drift, `_assigned_one_step_retreat`
          drift, or any mover added later;
        * it cannot loosen the leash. After re-anchoring the unit is bounded
          to six cells from its CURRENT cell, a tighter and correctly-centred
          constraint. It never gets a larger per-manoeuvre excursion;
        * it stays in agents.py, next to the field it owns.

      COVERAGE, MEASURED: 17 of 17 leash-caused zero-candidate verdicts on
      idle units have d>6 (1.4). 100% of the measured idle leash harm.

      ACCEPTED RESIDUAL 1: an origin can be stale while d<=6 - a unit
      assigned, walked three cells, then unassigned. The re-anchor will not
      fire and the unit keeps a leash centred three cells away. The sample
      contains no idle zero-candidate event of this shape, so it is not a
      measured harm; and with 4a in place such a unit is no longer frozen - it
      rescans every step within its narrower diamond. Detecting it would need
      new per-unit state to spot the engaged->idle edge, which nothing in the
      data justifies.

      ACCEPTED RESIDUAL 2, stated because it is a real hole in "escape when
      escape was possible": a unit standing at exactly d=6 from a LEGITIMATE
      origin, whose only free neighbours are the outward ones at d=7, is still
      leash-blocked - even when standing in fire. This is pre-existing, is not
      made worse by this change, and did not occur on any idle unit in the
      sample. Part 4 instruments for it explicitly rather than assuming it
      away; if it appears it will be reported, not silently patched.

SCOPE DECISION, STATED EXPLICITLY AS THE BRIEF REQUIRES:
  The brief pre-authorised touching wildfire_model.py at the two named reset
  points if 4b required it. IT DOES NOT. Design (iii) covers every case (i)
  would, plus the `_assigned_one_step_retreat` drift source (i) would miss,
  and does so inside agents.py. THIS ROUND TOUCHES agents.py ONLY; the offered
  scope expansion is declined rather than taken and left unused.

2.5  INTERACTION WITH THE 70e1b33 route_blocked RECOVERY PASS
--------------------------------------------------------------
The brief asks whether the two mechanisms can conflict - both acting on the
same unit in the same step - and how ordering resolves it. Answer: they are
strictly sequenced within a step and cannot race; the coupling through
position is real and is measured in Part 4, not assumed away.

  THE ORDERING, TRACED FROM SOURCE.
    WildFireModel.step()                              wildfire_model.py:4523
      ...
      self.schedule.step()                            :4564
          mesa SimultaneousActivation (:127): every agent's step(), then every
          agent's advance(). Firefighter.step() is a no-op (agents.py:453);
          all movement, `_survival_move` included, happens in advance().
      self._run_post_move_decision_cycle(...)          :4565
        -> AdaptationManager._run_post_move_cycle   adaptation_manager.py:130
             _process_pending_agent_removals          :144  (recycle here)
             ...
             _check_fire_casualties                   :170  (deaths decided here)
             _process_rescue_incidents                :171
             _sync_firefighter_marker_status          :172
               -> _revalidate_route_blocked_firefighters  wildfire_model.py:2139

  So within one step: FIREFIGHTERS MOVE FIRST, CASUALTIES ARE DECIDED SECOND,
  THE ROUTE_BLOCKED PASS RUNS THIRD. The pass reads `ff_marker.pos` live
  (:2199), so it always sees post-move positions. No torn state, no ordering
  ambiguity to resolve.

  A DIRECT CONSEQUENCE OF THAT ORDERING, worth stating because it is where the
  lives are saved: a unit that steps off a burning cell during advance() is
  no longer on a fire cell when `_check_fire_casualties` runs. In the applied
  what-if runs this is exactly what happens - see 2.6.

  DISJOINT WRITE SETS.
    `_survival_move` writes `self.pos` (via grid.move_agent) and the four
      `_idle_retreat_*` fields. It never reads or writes `status`.
    `_revalidate_route_blocked_firefighters` reads `status`, `dead`,
      `assigned`, `exiting`, `pos`; writes `status` ("route_blocked" ->
      "available") and may call `_try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims()`,
      which sets `assigned`/`target_pos`. It never touches `_idle_retreat_*`
      and never moves anything.
  The ONLY coupling is position. They cannot contend for the same unit in any
  meaningful sense: one moves and does not judge, the other judges and does
  not move.

  CONSEQUENCES THAT ARE REAL, AND THEIR DIRECTION.
    a) The BFS verdict changes. `_path_exists_avoiding_fire` runs from the
       unit's current cell to live victim cells, so a unit that escaped
       presents a different source cell. The pass can clear a unit it
       previously would not, or fail to clear one it previously would. This is
       the specific risk the investigation flagged, and it is why the 18-run
       route_blocked campaign is re-run seed-matched in Part 4 rather than
       assumed unaffected.
    b) The "trapped" guard at :2196-2198 (`all neighbours burning -> skip`)
       becomes MORE permissive, not less: a unit that escapes is by definition
       no longer surrounded, so it moves from "skipped as trapped" to
       "eligible to be tested". The reverse - moving INTO a cell whose
       neighbours all burn - requires a destination at fire-distance 1, which
       revalidation can only choose from fire-distance 0, i.e. from standing
       inside the fire. That is still an improvement, and it is a move the
       existing unlatched path would also make.
    c) Dispatch churn, second-order. If the pass clears a unit and dispatch
       follows, `target_pos` is set; on the NEXT step `_survival_move` sees
       `self.target_pos` truthy and takes the assigned arm, so this fix stops
       applying to that unit - correct, it is no longer idle. Separately,
       `select_rescue_assignment` sorts candidates on Manhattan distance with
       no reachability test, so moving idle units changes who is nearest.
       Part 4 therefore reports `rescued` and victim deaths alongside
       `firefighter_deaths`, never deaths alone.

2.6  WHAT-IF / COUNTERFACTUAL CHECK, RUN BEFORE ANY SOURCE EDIT
----------------------------------------------------------------
agents.py is UNCHANGED. The proposed logic lives in outputs/_ir_whatif.py as a
monkeypatch, in two modes:
  observe - stock behaviour runs; at every latched idle `_survival_move` call
            the proposed decision is additionally computed and recorded but
            NOT applied, so the trajectory stays identical to baseline;
  apply   - the proposed `_survival_move` is installed and the run diverges.
Two cases from the 13-run sample were used: D/south seed 101 (the
investigation's own "single clearest instance") and D/east seed 101 (the most
extreme stale origin in the sample, d=40).

2.6.1  IT ALREADY CAUGHT ONE DESIGN BUG - which is the point of doing it
  The leash invariant as first stated was WRONG, and outputs/_ir_invariant.py
  refuted it on recorded data before any code was written (2.4). The corrected
  form forces the `not self.target_pos` guard on the re-anchor. Without that
  check the fix would have silently changed assigned-unit behaviour on the
  basis of a false premise - the direct analogue of the trapped-guard need
  discovered before route_blocked's Part 3.

2.6.5  DESIGN VARIANTS TESTED RATHER THAN ARGUED
  (a) REVALIDATION TAIL. Both candidate tails were run on D/south 101:
      mirroring the main path's `_reset_idle_retreat_state()` versus clearing
      only the stall flag. Deaths identical, movelog identical, and the full
      480-row firefighter trace differed in 2 rows, in the `origin`
      bookkeeping field only. The same pair was run on D/east 101 with
      identical results. So the choice made in 2.1 is free on the evidence and
      was taken in the direction that keeps more anti-oscillation state.

  (b) SEPARABILITY - each half run ALONE on the same seed, D/south 101.
      The brief asks for two separable pieces. Rather than assert it, the
      what-if probe takes a `--pieces` flag and each arm was run:

        arm                     deaths   what happens to ff_unit_0
        ---------------------   ------   --------------------------------------
        stock                     2      never moves; dies step 111 at (46,30)
        4a only (latch recheck)   2      STILL dies step 111 at (46,30). The
                                         stale leash empties the candidate set,
                                         so the recheck has nothing to find.
        4b only (leash re-anchor) 2      moves once at step 103 to (46,31), then
                                         re-latches and freezes; dies step 111
                                         at (46,31). Fire distance 2,3,3,3 then
                                         1,1,1,0.
        both                      1      escapes at 103 and 104; fire distance
                                         2 -> sustained 3-4; SURVIVES

      NEITHER HALF ALONE SAVES THE UNIT. 4a cannot act because 4b has not
      corrected the leash; 4b gets one move and then hits the very early
      return 4a removes. This is the clearest possible demonstration that the
      two changes are separable AND that both are required for this case.

      Side note from the 4a-only arm, worth recording because it illustrates
      the investigation's "necessary but not sufficient" caveat exactly:
      ff_unit_1 there escapes the fire cell three times (steps 138, 141, 144,
      each 0 -> 1) and is caught again each time, dying at 147 instead of 138.
      It bought nine steps in a position it could not win. Retreat is reactive
      and greedy; against a front that keeps coming it only buys time.

  (c) REFACTOR EQUIVALENCE - the control that makes the above readable.
      The proposed `_survival_move` is not only two behaviour changes; it also
      extracts the candidate scan and the tier-1/tier-2 selection into shared
      helpers and restructures the assigned arm of the latch check. Running it
      with BOTH pieces disabled (`--pieces none`) on D/south 101 reproduces
      the stock run exactly:

          deaths            (111, ff_unit_0, (46,30)), (138, ff_unit_1, (0,5))
                            - identical to stock, step for step and cell for cell
          firefighter trace 480 rows, 480 matching keys, and ZERO rows differing
                            in (position, dead, stalled)

      So the refactor introduces no behaviour of its own; every difference
      observed anywhere above is attributable to 4a or 4b and nothing else.
