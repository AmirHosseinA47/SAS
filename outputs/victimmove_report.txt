1. WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED
================================================================================

1.1  The rule (agents.py, Victim._flee_approaching_fire)
---------------------------------------------------------
A victim holds until the nearest ACTIVELY BURNING cell is within
VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE (3) manhattan, then steps to the orthogonal
neighbour that maximises distance from the nearest burning cell, subject to:

  * in bounds, and containing no actively burning Fire agent;
  * within VICTIM_FLEE_MAX_DISPLACEMENT (6) manhattan of the LEASH ANCHOR;
  * the step must strictly increase distance from fire, or the victim holds.

Ties break by distance to the anchor, then by a fixed offset order. A total
order over a deterministic candidate set: IT DRAWS FROM NO RNG AT ALL, so no
dedicated stream was created and none was needed.

Five guards, each a hard return: feature disabled; no grid position; status in
("dead", "rescued") - the SAME skip set `_check_fire_casualties` uses, so the
set of victims that can move is exactly the set that can die, and mandatory
because that sweep leaves dead victims on the grid and in the scheduler; a
firefighter has custody; nothing is burning.

1.2  Why a victim can escape its own igniting cell
---------------------------------------------------
mesa advances agents in insertion order - Fire, UAV, Victim, Firefighter - and
`_check_fire_casualties` runs later still, in the post-move cycle. So when a
victim advances, this step's fire has committed and the casualty sweep has not
yet looked. Verified on a live model. Deaths then occur only when a victim is
surrounded, which is why section 7 is the accounting this makes necessary.

1.3  Guard 1 - dead-end avoidance
----------------------------------
PREFER a destination that still has an onward exit: at least one orthogonal
neighbour that is in bounds and not burning, NOT COUNTING the cell being
vacated. Excluding the vacated cell is what gives the test content; counting it
would make the answer True for every candidate whenever the victim is not
already standing in fire.

A preference, not a filter: when every reachable cell is a dead end the victim
still takes the best of them rather than freezing. Same shape as the trapped
guard added in 70e1b33, which skips a route_blocked clear when every in-bounds
neighbour burns.

GEOMETRIC LIMIT, verified exhaustively on a small grid and confirmed by every
firing in the sample: a cell is a dead end only if a neighbour burns, so a dead
end ALWAYS sits at fire distance 1. It can therefore never be strictly further
from fire than an open cell, and the guard can only ever BREAK A TIE among
distance-1 cells. Combined with the strict-improvement rule that makes it live
exactly when the victim is standing IN fire choosing an escape - the
enclosure-shaped case. Correctly aimed, narrower than "prefer" suggests.

1.4  Guard 2 - leash re-anchoring
----------------------------------
The leash was measured against `spawn_cell`, which has exactly ONE writer in the
tree and is never reassigned, so a victim was confined to a permanent diamond
around where it spawned while the front, which has no leash, kept coming. When
the front arrives FROM the spawn side the leash stopped the victim escaping
rather than bounding a manoeuvre. The firefighter's `_idle_retreat_origin` never
had that problem: it re-anchors on reaching safety, making its budget renewable.

`leash_anchor` now re-anchors to the current cell whenever the victim is safe -
fire beyond the trigger radius, or nothing burning at all. Same threshold as the
trigger, so no new constant enters the model, and the number coincides with the
firefighter's ideal standoff of BUFFER + 1 = 4.

THE 93f23b7 RESET HOLE IS NOT INHERITED. The firefighter's two re-anchor sites
also null `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, its anti-oscillation memory - diagnosed in
93f23b7 as real but inert. A leash re-anchor has no business discarding
oscillation history, so the INTENDED behaviour is mirrored: re-anchoring writes
exactly one field. The victim rule keeps no such memory today, and a test
asserts unrelated state survives a re-anchor so a future one cannot be silently
wiped.

`spawn_cell` stays IMMUTABLE, so every report still means the true spawn by
"displacement from spawn".

1.5  Custody, and the stale target_pos
---------------------------------------
A victim holds when a firefighter is bound to it, is not dead, and either is
`exiting` OR is standing on its cell. The co-location clause closes a one-step
hole: victims advance BEFORE firefighters, so on the step a unit sets `exiting`
the flag is still False while the unit is already co-located. A dead carrier is
skipped explicitly - a corpse must not pin a live victim.

`target_pos` was written once at assign (wildfire_model.py:3822) and never
refreshed. `Firefighter._refresh_target_from_victim` re-reads the bound victim's
live cell at the top of `advance()`. It never changes WHICH victim the unit is
bound to, only where that victim now is; the executor keeps sole authority over
`assigned`, `rescued_victim` and `status`.

1.6  Kill switch
-----------------
VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE <= 0 disables every touched path: the flee rule
returns at its first line, both guards are unreachable behind it, the
firefighter's re-target is skipped so the approach path is the pre-feature code
literally, and last_known_position stops being updated. Read at call time
through the `cfv` module so a per-run override is actually seen.

9. MECHANISM - WHAT THE MOVEMENT ACTUALLY DID (guard 1 + 2)
================================================================================
  TOTAL   victims 52, victims that moved 26, moves 413, own-cell escapes 47,
          moves into ground that burned later 334, leash re-anchors 192

Every step is one cell, so TOTAL DISPLACEMENT equals the move count: 413.

  final displacement from the TRUE spawn, victims that moved:
      n=17   min 1   median 10   max 32      (raw leash 6; guard 2 renews it)

  ground a moved victim ends the run standing on:
      burnt 9, scorched 4, green 4, off-grid because rescued 9

A STATIC VICTIM COULD NEVER STAND ON BURNT OR SCORCHED GROUND. The old invariant
"a live victim can never stand on a burnt cell" is broken benignly: burnt ground
is fuel-exhausted and permanently safe, so fleeing onto it is the best available
outcome. Scorched ground - has_burned with fuel remaining - is the dangerous
state, and 4 victims end there.

OWN-CELL ESCAPES: 47 of the 413 moves were made while STANDING IN FIRE, which
the scheduler order permits. Those are moves that would have been deaths in the
baseline.

10. GATE 5 - NO FABRICATED RESCUE.  ONE FAILURE, PROVED PRE-EXISTING
================================================================================
  baseline    exit transitions 29   without contact 0
  guard 1     exit transitions 33   without contact 0
  guard 1+2   exit transitions 36   without contact 1

The one failure is D/east/half 303 step 148: ff_unit_1 started exiting at
(49,24) with its bound victim_0 already rescued and off the grid.

IT IS NOT VICTIM MOVEMENT. outputs/_vm_phantom_probe.py drives the model
directly with NO VICTIM EVER MOVING and reproduces it identically on untouched
9c3eac6:

  Q1  one victim assigned to two firefighters      : YES on both checkouts
  Q2  second unit still bound after the first
      completes the rescue                          : YES on both checkouts
      it then walks to the stale target and starts
      exiting against empty ground                  : YES on both checkouts

Mechanism: the executor's assign guard only blocks giving a DIFFERENT victim to
an already-assigned unit, so one victim can be claimed by two firefighters; and
`_handle_rescue_incident` unassigns on victim_dead but NOT on rescue_complete,
so the loser is never released. Guard 2 changed which unit survived long enough
to reach the stale target - in the pre-guard arms the rival died first, so the
sample never exposed it.

It does NOT inflate the rescue count: `_finalize_rescued_victim` returns early
for an already-rescued victim (3 completions, 2 rescued in that seed). The cost
is wasted capacity - 88 steps bound to a victim someone else had carried out,
then a rescue absence it had not earned.

OUT OF SCOPE and NOT FIXED HERE: this is rescue-assignment authority, not victim
movement. Recommended for its own round.

The live re-target itself is heavily exercised and correct: it fired 141 times
across 10 of 13 runs in the pre-guard arm, against 0 in the baseline, and no
rescue in any arm began against a LIVE victim standing elsewhere.

11. LATERAL-WHEN-PINNED, MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSERTED
================================================================================
Raised as a possible third guard and deliberately kept separate. An earlier
claim that verification had shown it blocks escape INDEPENDENTLY of the anchor
was an overstatement: the verification read code, ran nothing, and framed it as
an aggravating detail OF the leash pinning. It is now instrumented.

  no_candidate                            37
  no_candidate.leash_removed               6
  no_improvement                         231
  no_improvement.lateral_available       175
  no_improvement.leash_removed            17

Of 231 holds where a triggered victim found no strictly-improving step, 175 had
a lateral (equal-distance) step available and only 17 involved the leash
discarding any candidate. So roughly 158 lateral-blocked holds are
ANCHOR-INDEPENDENT, and re-anchoring does not address them. The strict
improvement rule at agents.py refuses a lateral step that merely maintains fire
distance, which is what a victim needs to slide around an approaching front.

This IS a real separate consideration, now with a number rather than an
inference. It is not proposed as part of this feature.

12. PREMISE CORRECTIONS
================================================================================
Four premises behind the guard request were re-verified against the tree rather
than trusted. Three needed correction.

  (a) TRAPPED GUARD (70e1b33) - CONFIRMED, with a refinement. It exists and is
      written as "skip if ALL neighbours burn", equivalent to requiring >= 1
      fire-free neighbour. But it is NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT: a live BFS
      reachability test runs after it. Also "fire-free" means only "no burning
      Fire agent" - smoke and scorched ground still count as free - and
      out-of-bounds neighbours are dropped from the list rather than treated as
      blocked.

  (b) "3 OF 6 FIREFIGHTER DEATHS WERE GENUINE ENCLOSURE" - the figure is 3 of
      SEVEN, not 3 of six. It is bucket C1 of the idle-retreat residual, defined
      as every neighbour burning. A separate "3 of the 6 remaining" figure does
      exist but counts deaths on SCORCHED ground re-igniting under a stationary
      unit - a different mechanism, and not the same three units.

  (c) "THE last_cell ROUND'S C4 RESIDUAL" - the description is accurate (a unit
      on a burning cell with exactly one free neighbour) but C4 is the case that
      round CLOSED, not one it left open, and the load-bearing cause was that
      the single free neighbour WAS `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, so the
      anti-oscillation filter removed it. Without that clause C4 is
      indistinguishable from C3, which was excluded by the leash instead.

  (d) LEASH ASYMMETRY - CONFIRMED AND TOTAL, and it is what guard 2 fixes. The
      firefighter re-anchors via one explicit branch plus five reset sites; the
      victim's `spawn_cell` had exactly one writer and never re-anchored.

16. LIMITATIONS AND WHAT I WOULD NOT CLAIM
================================================================================
  * 13 seeds of one scenario. Every per-seed effect below about 2 counts is
    inside the noise this sample can resolve.
  * Guard 1 is inert on outcomes here. That is a statement about this sample,
    not a proof that dead-end avoidance never matters; its reach is provably
    narrow (section 1.3) but its firing rate on other scenarios is unmeasured.
  * The +2 firefighter deaths under guard 2 are not diagnosed to a mechanism.
    The chase dynamic in section 8.2 is the leading candidate and is confirmed
    in one seed, but it was not traced in the other.
  * Two runs no longer reach an all-victims-terminal state inside 240 steps, so
    their final counts are horizon-truncated rather than settled.
  * The phantom-rescue defect is reported, not fixed, and it remains live.
