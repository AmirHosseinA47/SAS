WHY NEITHER SIDE YIELDS
  The returning UAV (agents.py):
  - After 3 stalled steps outside the depot, _rtb_recovery_berth latches the
    nearest cell of the target depot that has no UAV on it (_rtb_cell_free).
      d4D: (4,45).
      d4A: (3,45) while (4,45) held a charging UAV, then (4,45) from about step
        190.
    Every latched cell is on the blocked row.
  - The latch is re-chosen only when it is reached or becomes occupied. A
    blocked INTERMEDIATE cell never triggers a re-latch.
  - _rtb_direction is distance-monotone by design (its comment forbids detours)
    and has no second axis on a pure-axis approach, so the refused -x move is
    re-issued every step.
  - The in-depot docking fallback does not apply at (7,45) or (11,45).
  - dockfix_part1.txt 10.1 names this as the recovery's "honest limit" but
    assumes a blocked approach to the latched cell "stalls again and re-latches
    to a different free cell". The code does not do that. This is the prototype
    validating the rule rather than its transcription, again.
  The blocking searcher (src_extension/execution/uav_executor.py):
  - With VICTIM_SEARCHER_HAZARD_RETREAT_RANGE = 99 (cfv:165), the hazard gate
    replaces the chosen direction with _retreat_to_safe_interior_direction on
    every gated step (:2340-2363).
  - That scoring uses hazard distance only and has no UAV-occupancy term
    (:2246-2290).
  - Both blockers carry the label victim_search_wind_aware_retarget_to_interior
    on every frame of their standoff.
  - The executor's stuck escape sits in the known-victim-target branch
    (:1071-1079) and did not engage.
  - So the standoff ends only when the searcher's battery trigger fires (d4D) or
    the fire moves its retreat argmax (d4A).
  A fix could sit on either side; this round does not propose one.
