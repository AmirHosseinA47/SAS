  N+ has 164 entries whose greyscale luminances tile the whole range, so some entry is always
  within ~1 dE of any candidate once hue is discarded. That is not a property of the candidate -
  EVERY colour this repo already ships fails it on the identical instrument (section 7: scorched
  0.13, depot/BASE 0.15, burnt 0.29, spared 0.46). Making achromatic a veto would set a standard
  nothing in the repo meets, and would drive the choice to near-black - which is exactly the

[5a] THE SWEEP RESTRICTED TO L* 22..82 - AND WHAT THAT CONSTRAINT REALLY IS
  Stated honestly, because the first draft justified it with a danger this instrument says
  does not exist: the unconstrained chromatic winner #00006e (L* 10.0) is 27.98 dE from burnt
  and 89.58 from white, so the objective was NOT failing to separate it from either. The L*
  floor is not catching a confusion the sweep missed.
  What it IS: a proxy for the ACHROMATIC condition this script deliberately does not veto on
  ([4a]). A ground at L* 10 sits in the same luminance band as burnt (16.0) and black (0),
  so on a greyscale display, a monochrome print, or for a viewer with achromatopsia, ground
  that CANNOT burn would be the same tone as ground that has burned to nothing - which is
  the exact confusion this colour exists to remove. Keeping L* well clear of both ends is
  the only part of the achromatic condition that can be honoured at all.
  IT IS NOT FREE: it cuts the chromatic ceiling from 8.65 (#00006e) to 6.82, a 21%% cut in
  the deciding quantity. That is the price, and it is a judgement, not a measurement.
