1d.2 THE STRANDING GUARD (brief item 1)
================================================================================
1d.2.1 PRINCIPLE
    A fix step never moves a unit onto a route the fire is expected to close
    before the unit passes.
    - "A fix step" = a decision at which fix (a) or fix (b) ACTS: its pure choice
      n is a cell other than today's choice (Part 1 22.2.1, 22.3.1).
    - "Expected to close before the unit passes" = by the frozen operator-side
      fire-arrival estimate (FAE), the fire reaches the way from n to the
      victim, or makes it fire-adjacent, before the unit (one cell per step) has
      walked it and picked her up.
    - READING OF "THE ROUTE", for ruling W-1: the guard admits the step to n if
      ANY time-safe clean route from n reaches her in time. The path fix (a)
      will actually walk - a shortest clean path - may itself be expected to
      close: on this round's runs 71 of 393 admitted steps had only a LONGER
      time-safe route (1d.2.6). The stricter reading ("the fix's own shortest
      path must be time-safe") is offered as W-1 (c).
    This is the property U1's safe-in-time route was built to express (Part 1
    8.2: "the parameter-free way to require 'a route the fire is not expected to
    close before the unit has passed'"). The guard reuses that test unchanged
    and applies it at the cell the fix would step to.

1d.2.2 RULE (SG-T). At a decision where (a) or (b) would act, with the unit's
    cell u, the fix's cell n (a 4-neighbour of u), the bound victim's current
    cell v, the true burning set B and active-smoke set S (agents.fire_board_sets,
    the units' own predicates), and the wind:
      T        = FAE arrival_time(grid.width, grid.height, B, wind,
                 FrontPriorityParams()), exactly as U1's view (WM:3111-3112;
                 the grid is 50 x 50):
                 - the published-rate estimate at its defaults, as U1 (ruling
                   U-6: never the SEARCHER_FP_* knobs);
                 - wind = cfv.wind_vector_from_direction(the model's wind label),
                   as U1's view (WM:3106-3110).
      unclean  = burning, smoky, or 4-adjacent to a burning cell (U1's
                 unclean_cells; the exact negation of the survival trigger).
      T*(x)    = min of T over x and its in-grid 4-neighbours (U1's t_star).
      c_n      = the earliest hop at which v is entered on a TIME-EXPANDED CLEAN
                 route whose first step is u -> n:
                   - n is entered at hop 1 iff n is clean now and T*(n) > 1;
                   - then a cell x is entered at hop k iff x is clean now and
                     T*(x) > k (U1's safe_route_hops, 8.2);
                   - u is not re-entered;
                   - c_n = infinity if v is never entered.
      ADMIT(n) iff c_n is finite AND c_n + 1 <= T(v)   (U1's test 8.3; "+1" is
                 the pickup step, as M8 and U1).
    This is, term for term, the function guard() in outputs/_mvg_guard_diag.py
    (frozen, 1d.2.6), which calls U1's unclean_cells, t_star and
    safe_route_hops unchanged (safe_route_hops from n with T* - 1, u added to
    the blocked set, c_n = 1 + hops[v]).
    ADMITTED: the fix's step runs, as in the urgency round.
    VETOED: the fix does not act at this decision. TODAY's step runs verbatim:
      - for (a), _move_toward(target) (AG:3115);
      - for (b), _survival_move() and its fallback (_move_toward when the unit
        did not move), with today's _idle_retreat_* state writes and
        _firefight_after_retreat (AG:3047-3079).
    Nothing is stored between decisions; the guard is re-evaluated at every one.
    SWITCH: FF_FIX_STRANDING_GUARD ships 1 (ON); OFF only on an exact 0 (the
    housekeeping rule for a switch that ships ON). It is evaluated only where
    (a) or (b) would act; with both fixes at 0 it is never evaluated (identity).
    SETTINGS, all inherited, none tuned:
      - FAE FrontPriorityParams() defaults: U10 20 km/h, 10 % rule, walking
        speed 1.34 m/s, WAF 0.4, 10 m to 20 ft 1.15, 0.25 per mi/h, LW max 8
        (Part 1 4.1 and 7.1; bayesprep_part1.txt 2.3);
      - one hop per step (the units' movement quantum);
      - "+1" for the pickup (M8, U1);
      - CLEAN and unclean as the survival predicate (Part 1 8.2, 22.2.1).
    PAPER: "a movement fix may step a firefighter toward a victim only if, by the
    operator's fire-arrival estimate, a clean route from that step reaches her
    before the fire closes it; otherwise the baseline's step is taken".

1d.2.3 WHY THIS FORM
    (1) It is the brief's principle stated by an existing, frozen estimate: the
        time-expanded clean route is U1's (8.2), and T is the FAE every rule of
        this project reads (bayesprep, U1). No new estimate, constant or
        threshold.
    (2) VETO TO TODAY'S STEP, not a re-route. The guard adds no movement rule of
        its own: a vetoed decision is exactly today's code path. That keeps the
        guard a pure filter with a clean identity (arm G = arm N up to the first
        veto, Zg-3) - but it is NOT exposure-neutral (1d.2.4 (f)). A re-route
        ("walk the shortest time-safe path") or a non-approaching veto action
        would each be a new movement rule with its own proof (W-2).
    (3) The APPROACH route only. (a) and (b) change only the way TO the victim.
        The way out after pickup is MODE 2's carry leg, untouched here; G-ESC
        (Part 1 22.2.1) requires a clean way out of her region NOW, which is a
        static test (1d.2.4 (c)).
    (4) The operator's information only: the burning set, the smoke and the
        wind (assumptions A3, A4). The simulator's spread rule (stochastic,
        radius-3, a tick every 3 steps) is NOT used: the managing system does not
        know it (Part 1 4.3(d)).
    PAPER: "the guard is the urgency rule's own route-safety test, reused".

1d.2.4 WHAT IT DOES NOT DO - THE STATED LIMITS
    (a) It inherits FAE's clock (Part 1 4.2): late in 100 of 101 burned
        detection cells by a median of 74-91 steps, and sometimes early. At the
        frozen parameters FAE predicts about 2.4 steps per cell downwind, 19.6 on
        the flank and 36.8 upwind (Part 1 4.1), while the simulator can spread
        up to 3 cells every 3 steps in any direction. Cell by cell, on the flank
        and upwind, "T*(x) > k" is therefore PERMISSIVE. It guards against
        EXPECTED closure only, as the brief states; unexpected closure remains.
        Measured after the freeze (1d.2.6), this does NOT make the guard rare:
        the test must hold along the whole route, (a)'s vetoed legs are long,
        and on this round's runs it vetoes about two thirds of (a)'s steps.
    (b) It does not touch today's mover. A vetoed decision is today's step, which
        may itself walk into a closing route (as it does in arm 0).
    (c) It does not guard the carry leg, idle units, or the exit: G-ESC is a
        static test made now; cells flicker, smoke changes every step and
        victims flee (Part 1 22.2.3, 7.2).
    (d) It does not restore (a)'s no-circling property under vetoes: on a static
        board an ADMITTED run of (a) steps still lowers D by exactly 1 per step
        (Zm-a, restated in 1d.6.2), but a vetoed decision is today's greedy step,
        which can leave the shortest path, and an admit-veto 2-cycle is possible
        on an unchanged board (1d.2.5 G4).
    (e) It does not use the victim's flight. T(v) is at her current cell, as U1.
    (f) A VETO IS NOT EXPOSURE-NEUTRAL. Every veto on this round's runs was
        c = infinity (no time-safe route from n at all), and fix (a) acts only
        when today's step is off every shortest clean path. A veto therefore
        replaces a step the estimate judges unsafe by today's greedy step,
        which is not judged at all and keeps approaching - often from a cell
        that earlier admitted fix steps reached and arm 0 never reaches (e.g.
        set3/ring/C_W: 32-47 admitted along the edge detour, 48-50 vetoed from
        (15,47)). Arm G can thus follow trajectories neither arm 0 nor arm N
        has, and can raise firefighter exposure relative to N. 11 of the 29
        vetoed legs were vetoed on every fix step (today's behaviour on those
        legs); 5 of the 10 closed vetoed legs had admitted steps before the
        first veto. Reported: 1d.6.5 (the post-veto DIAG).
    (g) Cells unclean NOW (smoke lasts about 2 steps) are impassable at every
        hop, while future spread is FAE-optimistic on the flank and upwind; and
        FAE ignores fireproof depots (Part 1 4.3(d)).
    (h) The reading of "the route" (1d.2.1): any time-safe route from n, not the
        one (a) will walk.
    PAPER: "the guard withholds fix steps onto routes the operator's estimate
    expects the fire to close; a withheld step is the baseline's own step, which
    the guard does not judge, and closures the estimate does not foresee
    remain".

1d.2.5 PROPERTIES, SAFETY, INTERACTIONS
    - G1 SUBSET. At every decision the guard either keeps the fix's step or
      replaces it by today's. Under CRN, arm G equals the guard-less fixes
      (arm N) up to the first veto, and a cell with no veto is identical end to
      end (Zg-3). After a veto the trajectories may differ arbitrarily.
    - G2 ADMITTED STEPS ARE TIME-SAFE by construction (Zg-1).
    - G3 A VETO IS TODAY'S STEP (Zg-2): the cell taken, the raise, the tier where
      today's path writes one (_move_toward; _survival_move writes none, Part 1
      24.1(b)), and for (b) the _idle_retreat_* writes equal today's shadow at
      that decision.
    - G4 THE GUARD WRITES NOTHING; each verdict is a function of the current
      board and (u, n, v). It can alternate admit / veto across steps, also on an
      unchanged board: an admit to u1 can rest on a longer time-safe route (71 of
      393 admits), at u1 the fix's shortest-path step can be vetoed, and today's
      greedy step can return to u0. No zero catches such a cycle (Zm-a skips
      non-adjacent admitted pairs); it is reported (1d.6.5) and covered by the
      sign-tested oscillation counts (F5). On this round's trajectories an admit
      was followed by a veto on the next step 5 times and admit-veto-admit never
      (descriptive; G's trajectories differ).
    - SAFETY. Every safety statement of Part 1 22.2.4 and 22.3.4 holds for every
      admitted step. The guard adds no command (Z2-M), no raise and no write. It
      does NOT make a vetoed decision safer than today's (1d.2.4 (f)).
    - route_blocked: unchanged as a function of (position, target, fire); on an
      admitted (a) step today's raise predicate is false (Z-RB, as before). A
      vetoed (a) decision cannot raise either: (a) acts only with a clean, hence
      fire-free, path.
    - (a) / (b) interplay: Part 1 22.3.6's caveat ("with today's greedy
      approach, the next approach step can still leave the route") now applies
      at every veto. G-ESC, tie-breaks and tier labels 7 and 10: unchanged.
    - U1 and fix (c) are NOT in this round (rulings, report 11).
    - COST (written after the diagnostic). One FAE arrival_time
      (O(|B| x W x H), chunked numpy) and one BFS per decision at which a fix
      would act: about 24 per run on this round's arm M (1 019 in 43 runs); in G
      the count lies between that and arm 0's would-act shadows (1 955 in 64
      runs, at most 156 in one run), since a vetoed unit stays on today's mover
      and the fixes keep wanting to act. Offline wall time on the 1 019 recorded
      decisions (outputs/_mvg_guard_timing.py, output in
      outputs/_ud_replay/_mvg_guard_timing.txt; one process, no contention):
      median 11.3 ms, p99 21.7 ms, max 28.7 ms (198 burning cells at the median,
      485 at most; a first, unrecorded run gave 11.4 / 22.5 / 30.2 ms). In-run,
      U1's identical computation measured p99 34.6-43.0 ms (report 4.1). The
      guard is timed as its OWN fix-code call, separate from the pure choice
      (p99 10.4 ms, report 4.1); S6 applies to each. S6's 50 ms is expected to
      hold for both, with a narrow margin for the guard; R stays far below 2 %.
      The implementation may memoize T per (step, board digest) - value-
      identical, pinned by a test. The smoke measures it in-run (1d.8 (9)); an
      S6 failure is NOT READY (1d.6.3).
    PAPER: "the guard can only withhold a fix step, and a withheld step is the
    baseline's".

1d.13.2 THE CAUSAL SAFETY CLAUSE (L4) - frozen now
    It applies review 1.3's knockout evidence (the rule 1d.6.5 names) in both
    directions, at the step of each death, so that a death moved earlier counts
    as caused and a death postponed counts as prevented.
    CANDIDATES, per fresh cell (sets 5-6), arm X in {G, N} against arm 0 (CRN;
    the unit ids are the same in every arm):
      (A) X-DEATH: unit U dies at step t in X and is ALIVE at step t in arm 0
          (it survives the run there, or dies later).
      (B) 0-DEATH: unit U dies at step t in arm 0 and is ALIVE at step t in X.
      A death at the same step in X and in arm 0 is neither; it is reported with
      review 1.3's C-NONE check.
    REPLAYS (wave W3; outputs/_mvg_replay.py, the port of _ud_replay.py, on X's
    own committed run line). A knockout acts on the GUARDED fix decision: the
    fix's pure choice returns None, so today's step runs and the guard is not
    evaluated; in G a vetoed decision is already today's step.
      - R0 for every cell with a candidate (boards on): it must reproduce X's
        record exactly (every per-step row, the commands, the movement events,
        eval, stdout); otherwise the cell's candidates are UNRESOLVED.
      - (A): KO-OWN (U's own fix decisions knocked out at every step <= t),
        KO-LAST (only U's last live fix decision before t), KO-OTHERS (every
        other unit's, at every step <= t); in G also KO-GUARD (U's vetoed
        decisions at steps <= t taken as fix steps: the guard off for U).
      - (B): KO-OWN and KO-OTHERS, with t = U's arm-0 death step.
      - A knockout whose decision set is empty (no live fix decision of U, or of
        any other unit, at steps <= t; for KO-GUARD no veto of U) is not run: it
        equals R0, and R0's outcome is used.
    COUNTS for arm X, pooled over sets 5 and 6, and per set:
      CAUSED(X)    = the (A) candidates for which U is ALIVE at step t in KO-OWN
                     or in KO-OTHERS - the unit's own, or the other units', fix
                     steps are but-for for the death at t. (Together: review
                     1.3's C-PROX and C-BUTFOR, its C-DOWN "via others' fix
                     steps", and its C-DOWN with an own earlier-leg but-for.)
      PREVENTED(X) = the (B) candidates for which U is DEAD by step t in KO-OWN
                     or in KO-OTHERS - the unit's own, or the other units', fix
                     steps are but-for for its survival at t.
      - An UNRESOLVED (A) candidate counts as CAUSED; an UNRESOLVED (B) candidate
        does not count as PREVENTED: a hard clause never passes on missing
        evidence.
      - Reported, never counted: candidates no knockout reverses (downstream
        coupling); KO-GUARD (a death a VETO caused - a vetoed step is today's,
        not a guarded fix step); KO-LAST (single step); review 1.3's class of
        every (A) candidate, with condition (iii) on the R0 boards; and the
        RUN-LEVEL variant (a unit counts only if it dies somewhere in one run
        and nowhere in the other, and a knockout reverses that).
    THE CLAUSE (L4, literal, HARD, part of S5 of FULL(0 -> G)):
        FAIL iff CAUSED(G) > PREVENTED(G).
      Recorded both ways - CAUSED and PREVENTED, pooled and per set - for G
      (gating) and for N (reported, never gating). A tie passes.
    CHANCE RATE: not computable in advance - the clause is mechanistic (1d.3.2

      round's in-sample arm M, CAUSED would be 7 by this definition (review 2:
