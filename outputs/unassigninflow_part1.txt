-----------------------------------------

SO: "where does the reassign get decided" has two distinct answers, and the
distinction matters for the design.
  * The DECISION TO RELEASE is taken at wildfire_model.py:2809-2829, in
    `_handle_rescue_incident`. It is unconditional: if the victim still needs
    rescue and this (ff, victim) pair has not been tried, the unit is released.
    Nothing about the unit's own safety is consulted.
  * The CHOICE OF REPLACEMENT is taken afterwards at 2831-2840 by
    `select_rescue_assignment`, and may well come back "delay" (no available
    firefighter). The release is NOT contingent on a replacement being found.

2.3  TWO STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT RELEASES, NOT ONE
---------------------------------------------------
This is the single most important thing the static read adds to the framing in
the brief, and it changes what "the unit is left standing there" means.

  (i)  `not scored` - agents.py:1110.  Every in-bounds neighbour is burning.
       `_mark_route_blocked()` runs, the unit is released, and `_move_toward`
       RETURNS. The unit does not move. This is genuine full enclosure and is
       exactly the C1 bucket of the idle-retreat round.
       Reachable from all three advance() call sites, including the carry-out
       branch, so an EXITING unit carrying a victim can be released this way -
       and the release clears `exiting`, `exit_target` and `rescued_victim`,
       i.e. it drops the victim it was carrying. `reset_victim_pending` then
       hands that victim back to the dispatcher as "confirmed".

  (ii) `route_blocked_now` - agents.py:1114.  BFS says there is no fire-free
       path to the target, but free neighbours remain. `_mark_route_blocked()`
       runs, the unit is released - and then the call KEEPS GOING and moves it.
       It is NOT left standing there. It takes one more step, chosen by tiers
       scored against `dist_before` to the target it no longer has:
         tier 1  closer to the target, not adjacent to fire, no smoke
         tier 2  same distance, not adjacent to fire, no smoke
         tier 3  any cell not adjacent to fire and no smoke
         tier 4  lowest `_firefighter_cell_risk`, tie-broken by CLOSER to target
       Only the `not self.exiting` guard at agents.py:1086 scopes this branch
       to the approach phase, so case (ii) never applies to a carrying unit.

  The brief's premise - "since it was route_blocked, 'where it was' is, by
  definition, next to fire, the unit is left standing there" - is precisely
  true for (i) and is wrong in two ways for (ii): the unit is not necessarily
  next to fire (BFS blockage is a property of the corridor, not of the unit's
  own cell), and it does not stand still.

2.4  WHEN CAN EACH FIRE, RELATIVE TO THE RETREAT LOGIC?
---------------------------------------------------------
`advance()` runs `_survival_move()` at most once per step, and reaches
`_move_toward` from the immediate-danger branch ONLY when that `_survival_move`
failed to move the unit (agents.py:460-471). That yields a complete, closed
taxonomy of the state a unit is in at the instant it is released:

  (i)   ENCLOSED.  `_needs_immediate_survival_retreat()` is necessarily True
        (a neighbour burning implies `_cell_adjacent_to_fire`), so
        `_survival_move()` HAS ALREADY RUN THIS VERY STEP, on this exact
        state, and returned without moving. The retreat logic has already been
        asked and has already answered "nowhere".

  (ii)  FREE NEIGHBOUR, IN IMMEDIATE DANGER (on fire / in smoke / adjacent to
        fire / min-fire-distance <= 1). Same as above: `_survival_move()` ran
        first this step and failed to move the unit, otherwise `_move_toward`
        would never have been reached.

  (iii) FREE NEIGHBOUR, NOT IN IMMEDIATE DANGER (>= 2 cells from fire, no
        smoke, not adjacent). `_move_toward` is reached directly from the
        approach branch. `_survival_move()` did NOT run this step. This is the
        only category in which an extra retreat call at the release point
        could do anything that is not already being done.

  Section 3 measures how the real releases distribute across (i)/(ii)/(iii).

2.5  A HARD IMPLEMENTATION CONSTRAINT ON THE STEP-5 PROPOSAL
--------------------------------------------------------------
Found while reading, and it constrains any version of "check for immediate
danger and move now at the unassign site":

  `_move_toward` builds `scored` from `self._neighbor_cells()` at agents.py:1093
  - BEFORE `_mark_route_blocked()` is called at 1117. If the release handler
  moves the unit, `chosen` at 1157 is still a neighbour of the cell the unit
  has just LEFT, and the trailing `grid.move_agent(self, chosen)` at 1163 will
  place it there: a teleport of up to two cells, straight back toward the
  fire it just retreated from.

  So the step-5 proposal cannot be implemented as an isolated added call. It
  necessarily also requires ending the remainder of that `_move_toward` call.
  The design in section 5 tests those two pieces separately for exactly this
  reason.

================================================================================
4. CAN THE MODEL TELL WHETHER THE RELEASE IS SAFE, AND WHAT CAN BE DONE
   FOR A UNIT WITH ZERO ESCAPE?   (Part 2 step 4)
================================================================================

4.1  CAN IT TELL? YES, TRIVIALLY.
-----------------------------------
Section 2.2 establishes this from the source: `ff_marker` at
wildfire_model.py:3516 is an `agents.Firefighter`, so the enclosure test the
route_blocked trigger itself uses is one line away:

    all(ff_marker._cell_contains_active_fire(c)
        for c in ff_marker._neighbor_cells())

together with `_min_fire_distance`, `_cell_meets_required_idle_safety`,
`_cell_is_ideal_idle_standoff` and the same `_path_exists_avoiding_fire` BFS.
There is no information barrier at the release point. The premise of the
brief's step 4 holds completely.

The conditional it proposes - "when releasing a unit whose current position
has zero legal fire-free neighbours, do NOT simply leave it standing there
passively" - is therefore implementable. The question is only what the
alternative branch would DO.

4.2  WHAT CAN BE DONE FOR A FULLY-ENCLOSED UNIT: NOTHING. STATED PLAINLY.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
The honest answer the brief asks for is the negative one, and it is not a
judgement call - it is a property of the model's action space, checked rather
than assumed. For a unit whose four in-bounds neighbours are all burning:

  (a) MOVE. There is nowhere to move to. `_cell_contains_active_fire` is a
      hard filter in EVERY firefighter movement path without exception -
      `_move_toward`'s `scored` (agents.py:1094), `_retreat_candidates`
      (agents.py:881), `_assigned_one_step_retreat` (agents.py:1002). A cell
      that is burning is not a legal destination anywhere in the code. There
      is no damage model, no "push through the flames" tier, and adding one
      would be inventing a new physical mechanic, not fixing a defect.

  (b) SUPPRESS THE FIRE. Not available. Checked exhaustively: `Fire.burning`
      and `Fire.fuel` are written in exactly three places, all of them inside
      `Fire.step()` / `Fire.advance()` (agents.py:108, 111, 123, 125). No
      firefighter, no UAV, no model method and nothing in src_extension ever
      extinguishes a cell or removes fuel. Despite the name, the Firefighter
      agent in this model rescues victims; it does not fight fire. "Clear a
      cell to escape through" is not a capability that exists to be invoked.

  (c) CALL THE RETREAT LOGIC. Already done, in the same step, before the
      release, in 4 of 4 enclosed cases (section 3.4). `_survival_move()`
      builds `_retreat_candidates` from non-burning neighbours; with none, the
      candidate list is empty by construction and the function returns without
      moving. Calling it a second time re-derives the empty list from the same
      grid state. This is a no-op by construction, not by measurement.

  (d) WAIT. This is the only thing that ever works, and it is not an action.
      A cell stops being an obstacle when its fuel runs out (agents.py:110-112,
      `burnt = True; burning = False`), and burnt cells are passable - the
      movement filters test `is_burning()`, never `is_burnt()`. That is
      precisely what saved the one enclosed release that survived: E/h seed
      202, released enclosed at [39,25] on step 57, regained a free neighbour
      on step 63 and a genuinely safe cell on step 65, purely because the fire
      around it burned out. Nothing the unit or the model did caused that.

  This matches, from a completely different direction, what the idle-retreat
  round already found in its synthetic labs (standby_investigation.txt Part
  1.4): suppressing the stall latch every step did NOT save a unit against a
  solid front, and against a front that crosses the grid every starting
  position dies. The conclusion there was "removing the latch is necessary,
  not sufficient". The conclusion here is stronger, because it is about the
  action space rather than about one filter: for genuine full enclosure there
  is no sufficient action, because there is no action.

4.3  SO THE C1 CASE HAS NO FIX, AND THE ROUND'S TARGET IS NARROWER THAN THE
     FRAMING SUGGESTS - BUT NOT IN THE DIRECTION THE FRAMING EXPECTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief anticipated that genuine enclosure might be unfixable and asked for
that to be said plainly if so. It is: 4 of the 8 releases are into genuine
enclosure, 2 of those die in the same step already standing in fire, and no
release-point branch can change any of them.

What the brief did NOT anticipate is that the other half is not fixable
either, and for the opposite reason: those 4 units were NOT abandoned
passively. Each had exactly one legal cell, and the existing code already
moved them onto it in the same step (section 3.3). There is no idle interval
during which nothing prompts them to act, because there is no idle interval
at all - the release and the move happen inside one `_move_toward` call.


================================================================================
5. DOES AN EXPLICIT "CHECK DANGER AND MOVE NOW" CALL AT THE UNASSIGN SITE
   HELP?  MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED.   (Part 2 step 5)
================================================================================

5.1  HOW IT WAS TESTED
------------------------
Nothing was written to source. Each variant was applied as a runtime
monkeypatch on top of 62b4fbe (outputs/_ui_whatif.py), the same
design-before-implementation discipline as outputs/_ir_whatif.py in the
previous round. Three arms:

  none     control.
  abort    end the remainder of the `_move_toward` call at the release, i.e.
           stop stepping toward the target the unit no longer has.
  retreat  call the already-fixed `Firefighter._survival_move()` from the
           unassign site - the literal step-5 proposal, reusing the retreat
           logic rather than duplicating it. IMPLIES `abort`.

  `retreat` cannot be tested (or shipped) without `abort`, for the reason in
  section 2.5: `_move_toward` builds `scored` from `_neighbor_cells()` at
  agents.py:1093, BEFORE the release at 1117. If the release handler moves the
  unit, the trailing `grid.move_agent(self, chosen)` at 1163 places it on a
  neighbour of the cell it has just left - a teleport of up to two cells back
  toward the fire. That is a property of the code, not of the arm.

  IMPLEMENTATION NOTE, since it constrains any real fix too: `abort` cannot be
  done by raising out of `apply_physical_rescue_command`.
  `_process_rescue_incidents` wraps the handler in a bare
  `except Exception: continue` (wildfire_model.py:2869-2875), which swallows
  it; and even re-raised, it would skip the replacement dispatch at
  wildfire_model.py:2831-2840, which is not part of what the arm changes. The
  probe instead flags the released agent and suppresses that one trailing
  `grid.move_agent`.

  Run over the 8 seeds that actually produce a release - the other 5 never
  execute the arm code path at all, so they cannot differ. Control arm checked
  for neutrality first:

      arm=none, E/h 404 -> MATCH stock;  arm=none, E/d 101 -> MATCH stock

5.2  THE ANSWER: THE EXTRA CALL IS A MEASURED NO-OP, 9 TIMES OUT OF 9
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
    combo seed unit        step  cell      enclosed n_free | ran   MOVED
    E/d   101  ff_unit_0     38  [42,41]      -        1   | yes    NO
    E/d   303  ff_unit_0     55  [41,23]      -        1   | yes    NO
    E/d   303  ff_unit_0     68  [37,26]      -        2   | yes    NO
    E/h   101  ff_unit_0     30  [44,40]      Y        0   | yes    NO
    E/h   202  ff_unit_0     57  [39,25]      Y        0   | yes    NO
    E/h   303  ff_unit_0     55  [41,23]      -        1   | yes    NO
    E/h   404  ff_unit_0     29  [31,38]      -        1   | yes    NO
    E/h   505  ff_unit_0     49  [45,23]      Y        0   | yes    NO
    S/h   404  ff_unit_1     57  [ 3,20]      Y        0   | yes    NO

  `_survival_move()` was invoked at every single release and moved the unit
  ZERO times. This is a direct measurement of the proposed mechanism, not a
  sampling result: the same function had already run that step and latched
  (section 3.4), and re-running it re-derives the same verdict from the same
  grid state.

  Note the arms produce 9 releases where stock produces 8 - E/d 303 acquires a
  second one at step 68 once its trajectory diverges. That extra case is
  useful rather than noise: it had TWO free neighbours and was still refused,

  `_pick_improving_retreat` requires a genuine improvement and neither cell
  was one.

  Consequently `retreat` and `abort` are IDENTICAL on every metric, on every
  seed, and at every trajectory divergence step. The step-5 proposal
  contributes exactly nothing on top of the abort it is forced to carry.

5.3  AND THE ABORT IT REQUIRES COSTS TWO FIREFIGHTERS
-------------------------------------------------------
Seed-matched against stock over the 8 release-bearing seeds
(outputs/_ui_wicompare_out.txt):

    combo seed   ff_deaths        rescued   victims dead   diverges at step
    E/d   101      1 -> 1          2 -> 2      2 -> 2        38
    E/d   303      0 -> 1  <--     2 -> 2      2 -> 2        55
    E/h   101      1 -> 1          2 -> 2      2 -> 2        never
    E/h   202      0 -> 0          2 -> 2      2 -> 2        never
    E/h   303      0 -> 1  <--     1 -> 1      3 -> 3        55
    E/h   404      1 -> 1          2 -> 2      2 -> 2        29
    E/h   505      1 -> 1          2 -> 2      2 -> 2        never

    ---------------------------------------------------------------------
    TOTAL          5 -> 7         15 -> 15    17 -> 17

  Identical for both `abort` and `retreat`. Two seeds get worse, none gets

  are unchanged, so the whole effect falls on firefighter survival.

  WHAT THE MECHANISM IS, AND HOW FAR I WILL PUSH THE CAUSAL CLAIM.
  The suppressed step is direct and visible - E/h seed 303 ff_unit_0 at step
  55 goes [41,23] -> [42,23] in stock and stays at [41,23] under the arm. What
  follows is trajectory divergence, and I am not going to dress that up as a
  clean causal chain:

      step   STOCK                      ARM
       54    [41,23]                    [41,23]
       55    [42,23]  <- its only       [41,23]  <- suppressed
                          free cell
       57    [42,23]                    [40,23]
       60    [42,23]                    [39,24]
       66    [41,22]                    [38,26]
       69    [41,22]                    [36,26]
       75    [43,22]  mfd 2, ESCAPES    [36,26]  DEAD
       77    [43,20]  mfd 4, alive      -

  The unit does not freeze and burn where it stood; it takes a different route
  westward and dies 20 steps later somewhere else. With two divergences in a
  chaotic spread model, "-2 deaths" is a real measured outcome on this sample
  but is NOT proof that aborting is lethal per se.

  What it does prove is narrower and sufficient: the change has no measured
  upside anywhere in the sample, 2 of 8 seeds regress and 0 improve, and the
  one thing it definitely removes is the continued `_move_toward` step - which
  section 3.3 showed is the ONLY thing moving these units, onto the only free
  cell they have, after the retreat logic declined it. Removing a compensating
  behaviour while leaving the thing it compensates for untouched is not a fix.

5.4  WHY THE PROPOSAL FAILS, IN ONE SENTENCE
----------------------------------------------
There is no window to act in: the release, the retreat attempt and the move
all happen inside a single `advance()` of the released unit, in that order,
and the retreat attempt has already failed before the release is even issued -
so "act now rather than wait for the next natural step-cycle" describes a wait
that does not exist.

================================================================================
7. CONCLUSION FOR THIS ROUND
================================================================================

  NO FURTHER FIX IS INDICATED AT THIS LAYER. Part 3 should not be run.

This is the answer, not a failure to find one, and each of the three candidate
levers is closed for a different, independently checked reason:

  STEP 4 - the fully-enclosed release (4 of 8). The model CAN tell that the
  release is unsafe (section 4.1) and there is nothing for it to do (section
  4.2). Burning cells are a hard filter in every movement path; no agent in
  this simulation can extinguish fire at all - `Fire.burning` and `Fire.fuel`
  are written only inside `Fire.step()`/`advance()`; and the retreat logic has
  already been called and returned empty in the same step. Two of these four
  units are already standing IN fire when released and die in that same step.
  Waiting for burnout is the only escape, and it is not an action. This is the
  irreducible floor, and it is the same floor the previous round's synthetic
  labs found from the other direction.

  STEP 5 - the proactive retreat call, which would apply to the other 4.
  Measured rather than assumed: `_survival_move()` fired at 9 of 9 releases
  and moved the unit 0 times, so the proposal is a no-op by measurement as
  well as by construction. It cannot be applied without also aborting the rest
  of `_move_toward`, and that abort removes the one behaviour that is actually
  moving these units - costing 2 firefighters over 8 seeds, with 0 improved
  and no change in victims rescued or lost.

  STEP 6 - the upstream dispatch decision. 8 of 8 commitments had an open BFS
  route, the corridor closed 12-37 steps into transit, and every alternative
  that existed was equally open. There is nothing to flag.

WHAT I AM CORRECTING IN THE FRAMING, RATHER THAN QUIETLY WORKING AROUND IT
  The brief's premise - "that branch clears assigned/target_pos/rescued_victim
  but does NOT relocate the released unit; the unit is left standing there" -
  is exactly right about the CODE and half wrong about the CONSEQUENCE. For
  the 4 enclosed releases the unit is indeed left standing, and nothing could
  be otherwise. For the other 4 it is not left standing: the same
  `_move_toward` call continues past the release and steps it onto its only
  free cell - the best cell available, and the one the retreat logic had just
  refused. The "downstream half" that 62b4fbe fixed and the "upstream half"
  this round was asked to fix turn out not to be two halves of one problem.

  It follows that the deferred option (d) from standby_investigation.txt -
  "pair the replacement with an explicit retreat/withdraw for the released
  unit" - is, on measurement, already happening, and doing better than the
  explicit version would.

THE ONE THING WORTH RECORDING FOR A SEPARATE DECISION - NOT IMPLEMENTED HERE
  In all 4 non-enclosed releases, the single free cell that `_survival_move`
  refused was `_idle_retreat_last_cell`, excluded by the anti-oscillation
  guard at agents.py:882-883. That is the same C4 shape the idle-retreat
  report named in its section 4.4 and deliberately left alone, alongside the
  C3 leash case, with the evidenced proposal "suspend the leash and the
  last_cell exclusion specifically when the unit's own cell is on fire". This
  round adds four more instances to that file's evidence, seen from the
  release side rather than the death side.

  I am NOT implementing it and NOT proposing it as this round's fix. It lives
  in the agents.py retreat logic that 62b4fbe just changed and that this round
  is explicitly forbidden to reopen; it is a retreat-logic question, not an
  unassign-inflow question; and doing it here would be exactly the "manufacture
  a fix to have something to ship" the brief warns against. It is recorded so
  that the next decision has the evidence in front of it.

HONEST BOUNDS ON ALL OF THIS
  * 8 releases and 7 deaths over 13 runs is a small sample and every count
    above should be read as one. The two firmest results are the ones that are
    NOT sampling results: the 9-of-9 no-op is a direct measurement of the
    proposed mechanism, and "nothing can be done for an enclosed unit" is a
    property of the model's action space, established by reading every write
    site of `Fire.burning` / `Fire.fuel`.
  * The -2 deaths under `abort` is the weakest number here and is reported as
    weak: two divergences in a chaotic model. It is not used to claim aborting
    is lethal, only that the proposal has a measured downside and no measured
    upside.
  * Only scenario D, winds east and south, 240 steps, was examined. The
    route_blocked release population is small enough that another scenario
    could show a different mix.
