2.2 STALL DETECTION - A FALSE STALL IS NOW POSSIBLE
    Round 1's progress metric (6.3) is d, the BFS distance over non-burning
    cells. Under fix (a) a bound unit on a clean cell walks a shortest CLEAN
    path. On a static board along that path the clean distance D_c falls by
    exactly 1 per step (the movement round's Zm-a), but d need not fall at all.
    WORKED EXAMPLE (hand-derived from the code's tie-breaks, not simulated;
    investigator report "move" Q3):
      - Board: a burning wall at x = 10, y = 10..20, with a non-burning gap at
        (10,15); victim v = (20,15); unit A bound at (8,15).
      - Distances: d(A,v) = 12 through the gap. The gap and its row are unclean,
        so D_c(A,v) = 26 around the wall's north end.
      - A's d over steps 0-15: 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 17, 18, 17, 16, 15,
        14, 13, 12, 11.
      - k reaches 10 at step 10, while A, at (11,22), has 16 clean steps left on
        a path it is walking without error.
      - The stall rule then accepts any fresh spare with a clean approach and
        d(B) < 16 + 10 = 26. Example: B at (5,15), d = 15, D_c = 29, 13 steps
        worse.
    BOUND. After a progress event the no-progress span is at most
    eps = D_c - d at that event. eps is limited only by geometry (the length of
    a detour around a wall, a burnt band or a smoke patch). Board changes can
    re-open the gap: smoke changes every step, fire adjacency at every tick.
    Round 1's 6.4 lower-bound argument for S = 10 ("normal movement produces at
    most about 5 no-progress steps in a row") therefore no longer holds under
    fix (a).
    Guard vetoes, by contrast, produce TRUE stalls. A veto runs today's greedy
    step, and on an unchanged board admit/veto 2-cycles occur: the movement
    screen's G arm had 36 static-board revisits holding a veto and 12 admit ->
    veto alternations (mvg_report 4.2). The stall rule is still needed for them.
    PAPER: with the shipped movement, "no progress in fire-free distance" is not
    evidence of lost time; the stall rule must measure progress on a distance
    the unit can actually walk.

2.8 THE TWO CONSISTENCY AMENDMENTS (rulings X-5, X-6; no constant)
    R-1 ONE ROUTE METRIC, AND PROGRESS AGAINST THE UNIT'S OWN HISTORY.
      D_c(u, v) = the clean distance: a BFS from v's cell over CLEAN cells (in
          bounds, not burning, not smoky, no burning 4-neighbour; J's existing
          clean map, the same predicate as the fixes' _clean_predicate). It is
          infinite if v's cell is unclean, and uses round 1's unit-cell
          exemption: an unclean unit cell is 1 + the best clean neighbour.
      d*(u, v) = D_c(u, v) if finite, else d(u, v).
      USED FOR: the fill's length keys (L2' / L3', 4.2), margin comparisons and
      the stall rule's lost-time charge. FEASIBILITY IS UNCHANGED: D_c finite
      implies d finite (clean cells are non-burning, and a living unit stands on
      a non-burning cell at a J point), so d* is finite iff d is finite iff the
      route test passes. J's set of allowed pairs for W is exactly round 1's.
      d* is ADMISSIBLE for units on clean cells (2.4). It is not a strict lower
      bound for units on unclean cells: survival and T4 steps can cross unclean
      cells. That is a stated limit.
      d* is the movement round's ROUTE distance (urgency_part1.txt 22.8.3 F5)
      without its Manhattan fallback.
      PROGRESS (replaces 6.3's best / e-shift). Per binding (A, v), J keeps
      H = the cells A has stood on at J-post evaluations since its last
      progress (H = {A's cell} at the bind). At each J-post evaluation with A's
      route OPEN (d(A_t, v) finite):
        - PROGRESS iff A reached v, or
              d(A_t) < min { d(h) : h in H, d(h) finite }, or
              D_c(A_t) < min { D_c(h) : h in H, D_c(h) finite }
          (D_c(A_t) finite; a minimum over an empty set is infinite). Every
          distance is computed on the CURRENT board, to v's CURRENT cell.
        - On progress: H <- {A_t}, k <- 0. Otherwise: H <- H + {A_t},
          k <- k + 1 unless FROZEN.
      A J-post at which A's route is CLOSED is C2's closed-route step (5.2):
      H <- H + {A_t}, k unchanged.
      WHY THIS FORM.
        - Fire changes and victim motion are factored out by construction: every
          comparison is made on one board to one victim cell. Round 1's e-shift
          needed an anchor cell.
        - Nothing is ever re-based, so a spell in which D_c or d is undefined
          (smoke on v's cell, a closed route) cannot hand a loop a false
          progress event (review finding design B2).
        - A cell revisited by a loop never beats H's minimum.
        - An admitted clean-path step lowers D_c by exactly 1 below every
          earlier cell of the path, so correct fix movement never adds to k.
        - Two-cell loops, veto cycles and pocket livelocks beat neither minimum
          and still count.
      CONSEQUENCE FOR S = 10. 6.4's argument holds again for admitted movement.
      The residual no-progress spans of correct movement are:
        - one survival out-and-back (2);
        - one fix-(b) on-route retreat (at most +1 in d);
        - the pickup step (1).
      A fire change that lengthens the route from A's cell but not from H's
      cells is no progress, correctly: A is not closer than it was. One that
      shortens it from A's cell alone counts as progress: an exogenous credit,
      stated as a limit.
      M = 5 (6.5) is restated. One step of motion moves the gap
      d*(A) - d*(B) by at most 4 only while both units stand on clean cells
      and neither crosses the clean-field boundary. The unit-cell exemption and
      a unit's own move between the clean and fallback regimes can move it by
      more in one step. P = 3 filters only jumps that do not persist. The
      instrument records how many margin decisions had either unit on an
      unclean cell.
      COST AND INFORMATION:
        - one clean BFS per victim in W or in a contest at every J point where
          PRE holds, so smoke is read at every such J point (round 1: only when
          a contest existed);
        - |H| map look-ups per contest per J-post;
        - 9.1 / 9.2's information rule is amended accordingly, still within the
          ruled assumption D-12 (fire perimeter AND smoke known to the team in
          real time).
    R-2 CLEAN APPROACH FIRST.
      CLEAN APPROACH(u, v): D_c(u, v) is finite (J's existing clean_ok, with the
      unit-cell exemption).

5. CORRECTION C2 - A LATCHED UNIT IS AN INCUMBENT (cause 2)

5.1 PRINCIPLE. A bound unit whose route is closed is still the victim's unit.
    - It is credited with the fastest it could possibly arrive: its grid
      distance, for a stationary victim.
    - The time it spends blocked is counted apart from its own movement.
    - It is replaced only on the evidence that replaces any incumbent: a unit
      nearer by the margin M, held for P evaluations; or S counted steps
      without progress, with the lost time charged.
    Round 1 treated a latched unit as worthless: it was latch-filled at the
    first J point at which any free unit had a finite route, at any distance.

5.2 THE RULE (under Limit 3, i.e. DISPATCH_JOINT = DISPATCH_REASSIGN = 1)
    - CONTESTS (6.2 amended): a binding (A, v) is contestable at J-post iff
        - v is detected and needy and A is v's only living binder;
        - A is alive, on grid, not exiting, not rescue_completed and not on v's
          cell;
        - A's status is en_route / assigned (ACTIVE) or route_blocked (LATCHED).
      A bound unit's route_blocked label is written only by its own raise
      (_mark_route_blocked, AG:3712-3719; called only from _move_toward's two
      raise branches, AG:4079 / 4085), and a first raise unbinds
      (WM:3974-3987).
    - W_L victims leave the fill under Limit 3: steps 2 and 4 serve W only, and
      a latched-held victim is a contest. Under Limit 2 alone (REASSIGN 0) there
      are no contests, and ruling D-7 stands unchanged: the fill offers a W_L
      victim a second claimant.
    - OPEN OR CLOSED, read from the route, not the label: A's route is OPEN at a
      J point iff d(A, v) is finite there.
      - A latched unit whose label lags an open route (2.6) is treated as open.
      - An ACTIVE unit whose route is closed - for example during a survival
        excursion that postpones its raise (AG:3081-3087) - is treated as
        closed. This replaces round 1's 6.3 "not evaluated this frame".
    - THE INCUMBENT'S DISTANCE delta(A, v) at an evaluation:
        - d*(A, v) if A's route is open;
        - G(A, v) = |dx| + |dy| if it is closed. This is the grid distance, the
          length of a shortest path through any cells (the grid has no
          impassable cell other than burning ones and the bounds). For a
          stationary victim it is the fastest A could arrive if the fire opened
          now: an admissible lower bound. A victim fleeing toward A can be met
          sooner, as for d.
    - TWO COUNTS per binding, both reset at the bind; neither advances at a
      FROZEN J-post (no unit - the incumbent or any free unit - has a clean
      approach to v; nobody could pick v up then):
        k   = J-posts with A's route OPEN and no progress (R-1's history rule,

                read at each evaluation).

6.3 THE RULE (recommended, X-4 (a)): NO NEW BEHAVIOUR.

        unserved victim in W form an allowed pair with a finite route. So the

11. THE CAUSAL CLAUSE L4 FOR DISPATCH (brief; frozen now)
================================================================================
11.1 CANDIDATES (as 1d.13.2), per fresh cell; the unit ids are the same in both
    arms:
      (A) dq1-DEATH: unit U dies at step t in dq1 and is ALIVE at t in dq0.
      (B) dq0-DEATH: unit U dies at step t in dq0 and is ALIVE at t in dq1.
    A death at the same step in both arms is neither; it is reported with the
    C-NONE check. Every unit qualifies, whatever its state when it died: bound,
    latched, unbound or carrying.

11.2 WHAT A DISPATCH KNOCKOUT IS - OVERRIDE SEMANTICS (frozen)
    THE COUNTERFACTUAL ("today's choice") at a J point, on J's own snapshot
    (round 1's M4 counterfactual, legacy_pairs):
      - for each detected needy victim without an active binder (W, and W_L as
        legacy treats it, i.e. a second claimant), in victim-index order, the
        Manhattan-nearest unchosen free unit, whatever its route (closed
        included);
      - no replacement of any kind.
    It matches select_rescue_assignment's rule (Manhattan-nearest available
    unit, ties to the smaller id; investigator and review checks).
    A KNOCKOUT OF A UNIT SET K, at every J point <= t:
      (0) J RUNS EXACTLY AS IN R0 on the knockout run's own state, with every
          unit of K present in every candidate set: the fill, the spare lists,
          the nearest-qualifying computation and the contest order.
      (1) Every bind J made of a unit in K is dropped. Every release J made of
          a unit in K is dropped, and so is the replacement it belonged to.
      (2) A contest whose selected challenger is in K gets no replacement, as
          when C1 finds every nearest spare barred.
      (3) Each unit of K that the counterfactual binds is bound to its
          counterfactual victim, before J's own binds are applied (reason
          "ko_today"; distance = Manhattan, as the executor's fill path needs an
          integer). Any J bind on that victim is dropped, and its unit stays
          free until the next J point.
      (4) Every other J decision is applied unchanged.
    - KO-OWN(U, t): K = {U}.
    - KO-OTHERS(U, t): K = every unit except U. No replacement can then occur
      (every replacement involves a unit of K as challenger or as incumbent),
      so U keeps only its FILL decisions. A death U met as a challenger is
      reported with that label.
    - KO-LAST(U, t) (reported): only U's last J decision before t that differs
      from the counterfactual, under the same override.
    - EMPTY KNOCKOUT. A knockout's decision set is empty when, at every J point
      <= t, (1)-(3) change nothing: no unit of K was bound, released or
      selected by J where the counterfactual differs, and no unit of K is bound
      by the counterfactual where J did not bind it. By (0) and (4) an empty
      knockout reproduces R0 exactly. It is not run, and R0's outcome stands.
      Part 2's W3 tooling check runs one empty knockout anyway and requires
      exact reproduction (13 (9)).
    - R0: dq1's own run line through the replay tool. It must reproduce dq1's
      record EXACTLY - every per-step row, the commands, the J events, the
      movement events, eval and stdout. Otherwise the cell's candidates are
      UNRESOLVED.
    - UNRESOLVED also: a knockout replay that is missing, INVALID, or crashed or
      stopped before t; and a knockout whose first changed J decision is absent
      from its knockout log (the KO-evidence check).
    - DISCLOSED: a dispatch knockout is today's CHOICE at J's decision times,
      not today's code path. Legacy pairs mid-advance at events; J at J-pre /
      J-post. The counterfactual can also create states J never produces (a
      second claimant on a latched-held victim). The knockout is a hybrid.
      Reported per candidate: whether the knockout's rows equal dq0's up to t.

11.3 COUNTS for dq1, pooled over sets 7-8, and per set
    CAUSED    = the (A) candidates for which U is ALIVE at t in KO-OWN or in
                KO-OTHERS, plus every UNRESOLVED (A) candidate.
    PREVENTED = the RESOLVED (B) candidates for which U is DEAD by t in KO-OWN
                or in KO-OTHERS.
    THE CLAUSE: FAIL iff CAUSED > PREVENTED. A tie passes. An uncomputable cell
    counts against: its (A) candidates are CAUSED, its (B) candidates not
    PREVENTED.
    - Same-step deaths, the run-level variant, KO-LAST and every candidate's
      class are reported, never counted.
    - The chance rate is not computable in advance (a mechanistic clause, as the
      movement round said).
    - W3 runs after W2 and BEFORE the verdict. Its queue is generated
      mechanically from the W2 records by a frozen script; the analyzer prints
      no verdict until W3 is complete and every R0 is checked.
    PAPER: "every firefighter death that differed between today's and the
    corrected dispatch was attributed with knockout replays, fixed before the
    screen and run before the verdict. A knockout replaces the dispatcher's
    choices for the unit, or for all the other units, with today's choice at
    the same moments. The corrected dispatcher fails if its decisions caused
    more such deaths than they prevented. Both counts are reported."
