THE FOUR VERDICTS OF DEFECT #9 - ALL NOW CLOSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The original audit item was "charred (burnt-out) cells are treated as
safe/passable by movement and hazard logic, when they arguably should not be."
It named one population but described two, and it splits into four threads. All
four are now resolved; the item should not be reopened for a fifth pass.

(C) NOT A DEFECT - `burnt` cells (has_burned and fuel <= 0).
    Treating them as ordinary passable ground is CORRECT. Absorbing state,
    0 re-ignitions measured, cannot enter the casualty set, 759 firefighter-
    steps with 0 deaths, permanent firebreak above a measured critical width
    of 3. The audit premise is simply inverted here.  [burntcells_part1.txt]

(D) INERT - the inversion, "burnt cells should be PREFERRED as retreat targets".
    Mechanically real but without opportunity to act: burnt ground is adjacent
    in 2.8% of danger-steps, 7.1 cells away on average, and was available in
    0 of 6 deaths.  [burntcells_part1.txt]

(A1) REAL DEFECT, FIX MEASURED HARMFUL - the hazard model does not distinguish
    scorched ground from virgin ground. Diagnosis correct; remedy refuted at
    every magnitude tested. Two structural reasons, both measured this round:
    scorched ground is not more ignitable (only better positioned), and it is
    the mandatory boundary layer of the burn scar, so penalising it evicts units
    from the safest ground in the model.  [scorched_part1.txt]

(A2) DELIBERATE DESIGN CHOICE, OUT OF SCOPE, FLAGGED FOR THE MAINTAINER -
    main.py:77 and serve_dashboard.py:63 paint burnt and scorched the identical
    charcoal #2b2b2b, merging the safest ground in the model with the most
    dangerous into one colour an operator cannot tell apart. This is specified
    and locked by tests/test_executor_routing_and_burnt.py:202, so changing it
    is a design decision and a test change, not a bug fix. NOT touched this
    round by instruction. The data is already on hand if a third colour is ever
    wanted: dashboard_state_builder.py:296-301 publishes burnt_cells and
    has_burned_cells separately. The call is the maintainer's.
    NOTE: A1 being harmful does NOT settle A2. A1 is about what the MODEL should
    do with scorched ground - and the answer turned out to be "nothing". A2 is
    about what the OPERATOR should be able to see, which is a different
    question and is untouched by this round's result.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDINGS WORTH CARRYING FORWARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. _move_toward tiers 1-3 never read `risk`.  (agents.py:1081-1176)
   The tier chain that moves an assigned firefighter toward a victim filters on
   adjacent_fire/smoke and ranks on distance-to-target. `risk` is consulted only
   in the tier-4 fallback, reached only when every free neighbour is already
   fire-adjacent or smoky. So _firefighter_cell_risk CANNOT INFLUENCE APPROACH
   MOVEMENT AT ALL, at any magnitude - it influences retreat only. This is why
   none of the three named deaths could move, and it is the single most
   important thing for anyone who later wants approach-time hazard aversion:
   that work belongs in the tier filters, not in the risk score.

2. The 96% re-ignition figure was uncompared, and the comparison changes it.
   Part 2 reported 96.6% of scorched cells re-ignite vs 0 for burnt cells. Both
   halves are correct, but the virgin-ground comparator was never measured. Over
   the same runs virgin ground ignites at 63.5% (D/east 101) and 95.7%
   (D/south 404), because by step 240 the fire has reached most of the grid.
   The 96% figure is therefore mostly "the fire eventually gets nearly
   everywhere", not evidence that scorched ground is a distinct hazard class.
   This CORRECTS the previous round's headline number - it should not be quoted
   again as a hazard measurement without its comparator.

3. probability_of_fire never reads has_burned.  (agents.py:53-84)
   It gates on the cell own fuel and sums over BURNING neighbours in a Moore
   radius-3 disc. Measured confirmation: at matched cell_prob, scorched and
   virgin ground ignite at indistinguishable rates in every probability band in
   both seeds (7.7% vs 8.1%, 16.0% vs 14.6%, 69.0% vs 68.6%, ...). The whole
   difference is exposure - mean cell_prob per exposure 0.21 scorched vs 0.012
   virgin. `has_burned` is a lagging POSITIONAL PROXY for "near the fire", and
   _cell_adjacent_to_fire is already a leading positional term for the same
   thing. The one real physical difference runs the wrong way for the fix:
   scorched ground has partially depleted fuel, so it burns for FEWER steps if
   it relights.

4. Scorched ground is the only door into the black.
   Firefighter step transitions, 23 runs: green->burnt 0, burnt->green 0, and
   all 32 first entries onto burnt ground came off a scorched cell (100%). Grid
   geometry, 3 seeds x 4 snapshots: 1 of 10,933 burnt-cell observations has a
   green 4-neighbour (0.009%). This is structural - a cell burns for 21-30 steps
   before going out, and spread is Moore radius-3, so every 4-neighbour has
   itself ignited long before that cell burns out. Consequence: a penalty on
   scorched ground is a toll on the only route into and out of the safest ground
   in the model. That is the mechanism behind the displacement failure.

5. Arms A and B were trajectory-identical, so a third arm was required.
   +1 and +10 produced identical per-step signatures on all 23 runs. Two arms
   that identical have tested one magnitude twice, not two. Arm C at +100 - the
   next EXISTING rung on the model 1_000_000 / 100 / 10 ladder, not a swept
   parameter - moved exactly one run (south_half_101) and changed no outcome.
   A hundredfold change in the penalty is therefore measured to be immaterial:
   every decision the term touched was a tie between cells of equal stock risk.

6. A hypothesis tested and NOT supported, recorded so it is not re-proposed:
   that the harm/benefit split tracked "unit sheltering inside the scar" versus
   "unit arriving at its rim". Measuring ground over the 10 steps before each
   divergence refutes it - east_half_505 was 0/10 on scar ground and harmful,
   south_half_606 was 10/10 and neutral. The pattern that DOES hold is the
   burnt-occupancy signature: all four runs that got worse show burnt-ground
   occupancy collapsing, and the single run that improved is the only one where
   it rose.
