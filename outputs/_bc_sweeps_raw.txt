SUMMARY:
The UAV/fire-tracker stack has NO concept of burnt-ness anywhere in targeting, belief, or coverage scoring. The role split is `fire_tracker` vs `victim_searcher`, assigned by index in `wildfire_model.py:_assign_uav_roles` (line 641-660). A fire tracker's target pipeline is: read the belief `fire_probability_map` -> keep cells with prob >= 0.3 -> classify "front" cells (a >=0.3 cell with fewer than 4 neighbours also >=0.3) -> drop smoke-obscured cells -> order them by a lateral/upwind flank score -> split north/south between trackers -> convert each to a smoke-free standoff hold 1-2 cells lateral/upwind. Every stage is driven by belief probability, wind geometry, smoke sets and distance. The CRUCIAL ANSWER: burnt-ness never enters any utility function. It is not a term, not a filter, not a penalty, and no layer stores it. The only way a permanently-dead cell loses attractiveness is indirect and generic: a UAV flying over it sees no fire, emits a NEGATIVE observation, and `update_no_fire_observation` multiplies its probability down; separately `apply_time_decay` bleeds all probabilities toward zero with age. A burnt cell is thus treated identically to a never-ignited green cell — the model has no way to distinguish "this cell is cold because it never burned" from "this cell is cold because it is permanently over." I also found that the one place that tries to read ground-truth fire status, `_collect_active_fire_cells`, reads a non-existent attribute `burnt_status` and is dead, and that it would classify the string "burned" as ACTIVE fire if that attribute ever existed. The belief/visibility layers do decay and expire (probability, confidence, staleness, negative observations), but only on time, never on terminal fire state.
