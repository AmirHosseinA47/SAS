EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The feature is buildable, and the piece the brief flagged as the likely blocker
is not the blocker. Five findings reshape the design.

1. THE FROZEN DATACLASS DOES NOT BIND. MissionDecision is frozen with exactly
   12 fields and carries no destination (decision_objects.py:19-34) - confirmed,
   but it is not on the required path. PathDecision (decision_objects.py:37-50)
   already carries waypoints_by_uav and path_segment; the dispatcher's
   return-to-base test reads exactly those (decision_dispatcher.py:538-547); and
   the executor already turns waypoints[0] into a direction
   (uav_executor.py:1445-1447). Mechanism (a) needs NO change to any frozen type.
   What IS dead is the fleet-wide fail-safe channel, which cannot address a
   single UAV at all. Section 3.

2. THE FEATURE IS INERT-BY-DEFAULT AT A 30% THRESHOLD, AND NOT MARGINALLY SO.
   Measured on seed 101 at canonical defaults, the three UAVs cross 30% at steps
   234, 238 and 239 of 240, against a worst-case return of 90 moves. ZERO returns
   can complete. 30% is thin geometrically too: the worst-case return costs 27.0
   points, leaving 3.0. The reserve must be >= 55 for a worst-cell return to fit
   inside 240 at all. Design default: 60. Section 5.

3. A CHARGING UAV MUST STAY ON THE GRID. Feature 1's off-grid pattern does not
   port. An off-grid UAV kills the run on its FIRST step (TypeError out of
   global_monitor.py:194, verified in-process); there are 12 unguarded UAV
   position sites in 6 files, not the 7 the prior round listed; and two of the
   required guards are not simple skips - guarding state() permanently truncates
   MR1_LIST and thereafter credits the wrong UAV. On-grid parking was verified to
   cost nothing. Section 4.

4. THE BRIEF'S LANE/SECTOR EXCLUSION REQUIREMENT CANNOT BE SATISFIED AS WRITTEN,
   AND DOES NOT NEED TO BE. Both rosters are position-blind and role-based, so a
   parked UAV that KEEPS its role causes NO reshuffle - the partition is frozen
   for the charge duration. Filtering it out is worse in every configuration:
   with one searcher the legacy fallback re-elects it
   (local_adaptation_generator.py:306-316); with two searchers the lane goes None
   for BOTH, because n and idx come from the same filtered list (:333-350). This
   is a brief requirement I am declining on evidence. It needs a maintainer
   ruling, not silent adoption. Section 4.4.

5. THE OBSERVATION-RADIUS FRAME IS HALF-BUILT ALREADY. It ships on the mesa
   surface today (Canvas_Grid_Visualization.py:111-127) - as a 19x19 ring at
   radius+1 around a true 17x17 field of view. Only the dashboard lacks it, where
   it is roughly ten lines. Section 6.5.

ONE THEORY-VS-MEASUREMENT CONFLICT, RESOLVED BY PROBE RATHER THAN LEFT AS A RISK.
The executor's margin-3 edge filter (uav_executor.py:2061-2075) leaves 0 legal
moves on the corner block diagonal and at most 1 elsewhere, which reads as an
egress trap that would sink a corner depot outright. It is not one: the filter is
reached only from victim-searcher code, and the searcher's dominant path skips
the hazard gate entirely (uav_executor.py:404-411). Probed in ALL FOUR corners by
placing every UAV on the WORST cells of the block - boundary distance 0, the
zero-legal-move block corner included - and stepping 40 steps: 16 of 16 UAVs
cleared the depot, within 1 to 9 steps, moving on 37-40 of 40 steps against a
centre-spawn baseline of 34-38. Section 1.4.

1. THE DEPOT
================================================================================

1.1  WHAT IT IS - a model-level region, not an agent and not terrain
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: the depot is a MODEL-LEVEL REGION. `self.base_station` is built in
reset() from configuration constants and holds the origin, the size, the frozen
cell set, and the per-unit berth assignment. Nothing is added to the grid,
nothing is added to the scheduler, and set_fire_agents / new_fire_agent / the
Fire class are not touched.

Three candidate representations were measured against seed reproducibility, the
constraint that governs everything downstream:

  OPTION-1  skip creating Fire agents on the 25 depot cells      REJECTED
  OPTION-2  create Fire everywhere, flag 25 as non-flammable     DEFERRED
  OPTION-3  model-level coordinates only, fire untouched         CHOSEN

OPTION-1 is baseline-breaking three ways at once, measured A/B at seed 101:
construction draws fall 5002 -> 4952; every later unique_agents_id shifts by -25
(UAVs 2500-2502 -> 2475-2477); per-spread-step draws fall 2500 -> 2475. The draw
VALUE sequence stays positionally identical for 4952 entries, which makes an
RNG-stream hash look clean, but the values land on different cells: only 615 of
2475 cells keep their baseline fuel, and burning cells at step 30 were 150
against 214. It also leaves a permanent 25-cell hole in the belief layer
(fire_runtime_model.initialize_grid seeds all HEIGHT*WIDTH cells, but
_belief_confirmation_flags skips cells with no Fire agent), renders the depot as
ordinary vegetation, and silently moves the burnt_cells population from 2500 to
2475 while outputs/_bc_analyze.py:63-67 hard-codes 2500. Reject outright; do not
prototype. Note for anyone tempted to validate a depot change by hashing the RNG
draw log: under OPTION-1 that hash agrees for 4952 entries while the fuel field
has already moved. Validate against the fuel/burn MAP, never the stream.

OPTION-2 is bit-exact at construction, and was verified so: baseline versus an
in-memory emulation over 30 steps at seed 101 reproduced the full 30001-element
draw log, the fuel-field hash, the unique ids and the burning-cell count exactly.
That holds only if the no-ignite flag is honoured at the ASSIGNMENT site
(agents.py:104) with the draw at agents.py:103 kept. Placing the guard as an
early return before line 103 silently deletes one draw per depot cell per spread
step and desynchronises the stream from the first spread step onward. That is the
single easiest way to get OPTION-2 wrong. Deferred, not rejected - see 1.3.

OPTION-3 is trivially bit-identical: no draw added, no id shifted, no Fire agent
changed, both renderers' contracts untouched. It is the only option under which a
base-station arm stays directly seed-comparable with the ihrest99 baselines, so a
behaviour delta is attributable to spawn / return / recharge alone rather than to
a perturbed fire map.

The order in reset() that makes this true (wildfire_model.py:121-177):
_make_ff_absence_rng (getstate, consumes nothing) -> set_fire_agents (2 randint,
then 2500 x (random, randint)) -> UAVs -> _init_runtime_knowledge -> victims ->
firefighters. Fire agents are created FIRST, so every later unique_agents_id is a
direct function of the fire-agent count. Touch the fire count and everything
moves; leave it alone and nothing does.

1.2  WHERE IT IS - the NW block, x in [0,4], y in [45,49]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: origin (0, 45), size 5. The 25 cells are x in [0,4], y in [45,49].

Grid x is the HEIGHT axis and grid y is the WIDTH axis - the grid is built
MultiGrid(HEIGHT, WIDTH, False) with an explicit comment about the inversion
(wildfire_model.py:129-142). Every depot coordinate must derive from model.HEIGHT
for x and model.WIDTH for y, never from literals and never swapped. That mix-up
is the exact defect class this repo has hit nine times, and two live helpers
already disagree about it (uav_executor.py:4712-4716 compares nx against WIDTH
while :541-551 uses the opposite mapping; both inert only because HEIGHT==WIDTH).

All four corners are provably identical on every static collision, so the choice
turns on wind and distance. Three independent criteria all point the same way:

  criterion                       NW(0-4,45-49)  NE(45-49,45-49)  SW(0-4,0-4)  SE(45-49,0-4)
  -----------------------------   -------------  ---------------  -----------  -------------
  mean victim distance, 4 scen.       39.65           40.88           41.55        42.63
  summed over the 4 scenarios        157.2           163.5           166.2        172.5
  upwind of east (fire -> +x)          yes              no             yes           no
  upwind of south (fire -> -y)         yes             yes              no           no
  corridor target blocked, east         no             yes              no          yes
  corridor target blocked, south        no              no             yes          yes
  spawn-cell collisions                zero            zero            zero         zero
  can be the ignition cell               no              no              no           no

NW is the only corner upwind of BOTH canonical winds, and it also has the
shortest mean distance to the victim ring, hence the shortest return leg. SE is
downwind of both and worst on every metric that separates the four.

Wind semantics were verified against the actual spread test in
agents.Wind.is_on_wind_direction (agents.py:209-223), not merely the vector
helper: east pushes fire toward +x, south toward -y.

STATED ASYMMETRY, so it is not discovered later. common_fixed_variables.py:36
sets WIND_DIRECTION = 'west'; evaluate_scenarios.py:192 and
serve_dashboard.py:350 default to 'east'; main.py contains no wind reference at
all. A bare `python main.py` / Canvas run therefore inherits 'west', under which
the NW corner is DOWNWIND and burns. The gated wave is east and south, so the
depot is sited for the gated wave. This goes in the commit message.

1.3  DOES FIRE SPREAD INTO THE DEPOT?  YES - and that is a decision
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: the depot is BURNABLE. Fire spreads into it exactly as into any other
cell, the 25 cells keep their Fire agents, and NOTHING models the depot being
destroyed. It is a service location, not a structure.

Three reasons, in order of weight:

  (i)  IT COSTS NOTHING MECHANICALLY. _check_fire_casualties
       (wildfire_model.py:4679-4851) kills victims and firefighters and contains
       no UAV branch - UAVs are fire-immune in this model. A charred depot cell
       therefore has zero consequence for spawning, returning or recharging
       unless new code invents one. Making the depot non-flammable buys no
       behaviour at all.

  (ii) IT WOULD CONTAMINATE THE MEASUREMENT. A non-flammable depot is a permanent
       5x5 firebreak. The corners do burn: at the default west wind the SW corner
       ended 25/25 ever-burned in all five seeds tested and NW in four of five.
       The firebreak would remove real burnt cells AND suppress downstream
       spread - a confound sitting directly inside burnt_cells, one of the
       metrics this round is judged on - and would force its own re-baseline,
       exactly as the dimension fix did.

  (iii) SEPARABILITY DEMANDS IT. The brief requires a delta to be attributable to
       spawn, return or recharge. Any change to the fire model injects a fourth,
       un-separable effect into every armed run.

If the maintainer later wants a hardened pad, the correct upgrade is OPTION-2 as
its own arm with its own re-baseline, never bundled here. Two caveats recorded
now so that round need not re-derive them: OPTION-2 is identical to baseline only
UNTIL fire first reaches the depot, because a cell that never burns never takes
the burnt early-return at agents.py:91-95 that skips the draw (measured step
~150-180 for the SW corner at seed 101) - advertise it as "identical
construction, identical until the fire arrives", not "baseline-identical"; and a
non-burnable depot becomes a 25-cell survival sanctuary for firefighters, which
moves firefighter_deaths.

1.4  CAN AGENTS MOVE THROUGH IT?  YES - it is not an obstacle
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The depot imposes no passability rule of its own. Victims, firefighters and UAVs
cross it freely. The only constraint is pre-existing: not_UAV_adjacent
(agents.py:285-291) is misnamed - it tests only the TARGET cell - so two UAVs may
not occupy the same cell, while a UAV may share a cell with a Fire agent, a
victim or a firefighter. mesa MultiGrid stores a list per cell (the third arg is
torus=False, not single-occupancy); 11 of 2500 cells already hold two agents at
t=0.

THE EGRESS QUESTION, SETTLED BY PROBE.
_victim_edge_blocked_direction (uav_executor.py:2061-2075) rejects any move that
does not strictly increase min-boundary-distance once that distance is <= 3
(margin = 3). Inside a 5x5 corner block that leaves exactly one cell with all
four moves legal (the inner corner, (4,45) here), at most one legal move for
twenty cells, and ZERO legal moves for the five cells on the block diagonal.
Taken alone that reads as a trap - and it IS the reason to reject a
parity/checkerboard spawn layout, which would place UAVs on precisely the
zero-move cells.

It does not bind in practice, for two verified reasons (and note the filter's
geometry is identical in all four corners, so this is not a property of the
corner chosen):
  - the filter is reached only from victim-searcher code. Call sites are
    uav_executor.py:2002, 2081, 2192, 2219, 2314, 2325, 2342, 4218 - all searcher
    machinery. Fire trackers never consult it.
  - the searcher's dominant path skips the hazard gate altogether: at
    uav_executor.py:404-411 the gate is bypassed when the action label contains
    "retarget_to_interior" and _last_escape_method is non-empty. On a stock
    centre-spawn run that is 37-39 of 40 steps for both searchers. It is the
    normal mode, not an edge case.

all four UAVs placed on the WORST cells of each block - the outer ring, boundary

2.1  What exists, and what actually changes behaviour
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UAVs spawn in a hardcoded 2x2 block at the grid centre - (25,25),(26,25),(25,26),
(26,26) from base_x = HEIGHT//2, base_y = WIDTH//2 (wildfire_model.py:152-172).
Victims and firefighters spawn on the two deterministic trig rings above.

Placement is already 100% deterministic. The ONLY RNG in reset() is
set_fire_agents(), and it runs BEFORE any UAV, victim or firefighter is created
(wildfire_model.py:145 versus 161/183/187). Moving those agents therefore shifts
not a single fire draw, provided no new RNG call is introduced. Introducing one -
for instance shuffling depot cells - would consume from SYSTEM_RANDOM after
set_fire_agents and desynchronise every seed-matched comparison against the
ihrest99 baselines. The depot placement draws nothing.

warmup_dirs = (0,1,3) assigned to selected_dir at spawn is MEASURED-INERT: the
extension pipeline is always active (decision_dispatcher is constructed
unconditionally at wildfire_model.py:272), the pre-move MAPE cycle runs before
_prepare_uav_directions_for_step, the executor overwrites selected_dir in
_commit_execution_direction (uav_executor.py:141-147), and UAV.move() refuses to
move at all unless execution_direction_applied is True (agents.py:323-325).
Forcing every UAV's selected_dir to 0 right after construction reproduced the base
trace EXACTLY - positions, selected_dir and execution_action - for 40 steps on
seed 101 and 30 steps on seeds 202 and 303. KEEP the assignment anyway, unchanged
and with the same cycle, so the OFF arm is the pre-feature code literally. It is
inert on three seeds of one scenario, not proven inert structurally (the executor
still reads selected_dir as a fallback at uav_executor.py:3462, 4379, 4625).

Spawn POSITION, by contrast, changes behaviour at step 1. Measured: moving the
same four UAVs to a corner block flips the searchers' action label and changes
their chosen directions on the very first step. This is expected and is the whole
point of the spawn arm.

2.2  The berth assignment
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: every unit gets a DEDICATED berth cell, assigned deterministically by
index. This is the same set used for spawn and for docking - they must not be two
different sets, and pre-assignment is what removes the return-leg deadlock (3.5).

Ordering rule. Rank the 25 depot cells by DESCENDING min-boundary-distance, then
ascending (x, y) as a stable tie-break. For the NW block that distance is
min(i, 4-j) for cell (i, 45+j), so the ranking starts at (4,45) - the single cell
with all four moves legal under the margin-3 filter - and works outward through
the distance-3 ring, then 2, then 1, then the nine outer-ring cells.

  UAV k          -> ranked_cells[k]          (front of the list)
  firefighter j  -> ranked_cells[24 - j]     (back of the list)

Canonical maxima are 5 UAVs and 3 firefighters, so the two sets never meet: 25
cells against 8 units. This is a pure function of (origin, size, N, M) and draws
nothing.

REJECTED: a parity/checkerboard layout. It reads well but places UAV #0 on the
block corner and later UAVs on the diagonal - exactly the cells with zero legal
moves under the margin-3 filter (1.4). Descending boundary distance is the right
key precisely because it is the key the filter itself uses.

If N + M ever exceeded 25, RAISE - do not wrap with modulo. Wrapping stacks two
UAVs on one cell, which MultiGrid permits at placement (it already happens today:
NUM_AGENTS=5 puts two UAVs on (25,25) via `a % len(base_cluster)`) but which then
costs a blocked first move through not_UAV_adjacent. An explicit error is better
than a silent quirk. Note that removing that existing 5-UAV stacking quirk is
itself a behaviour change for scenario C, and belongs to the spawn arm's account.

Placement must be done INSIDE the existing spawn loop, not as a fix-up after
_init_runtime_knowledge: _init_managed_uav_states snapshots agent.pos
(wildfire_model.py:704-720), so a later move leaves managed_uav_states[uid].position
stale for one step.

2.3  Firefighters at the depot - the part that is not free
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The requirement says the drones AND firefighters start from the base station, so
both are moved. Nothing BREAKS: the rescue planner reads live marker positions,
managed_firefighters[...].position is overwritten from the marker every step, and
the post-rescue exit target is computed from the victim's cell. But two behaviours
change materially, and both land on `rescued`, which the gate protects:

  (a) REACHABILITY CLIFF. _update_unreachable_victims seeds ONE BFS flood-fill
      from the live firefighter cells (wildfire_model.py:4430-4456, 4468-4486).
      Ring-spread firefighters give that fill three separated seeds; a corner
      depot gives it one. A fire arc that separates the corner from the interior
      flips EVERY victim to geographically unreachable in the same step. That is
      a cliff, not a gradual degradation.
  (b) DISPATCH DEGENERACY. "Assign the closest available firefighter" compares
      Manhattan distances (rescue_planner.py:646-652). With all units inside a
      5x5 block those distances differ by at most 8 while typical victim
      distances are 30-60, so the lexicographic tie-break decides most dispatches
      and ff_unit_0 becomes the de facto sole responder at t=0.
  Plus roughly 45-65 Manhattan cells of extra transit to the first rescue, and
  halved retreat options in the extreme corner cells (_neighbor_cells is
  bounds-filtered and 4-connected; the idle-retreat leash disc is clipped by two
  walls), which feeds ff_deaths.

DESIGN RESPONSE: make the firefighter half separately attributable rather than
arguing about it in advance. BASE_STATION_SPAWN_FIREFIGHTERS (default 1, per the
requirement) gives a diagnostic arm with UAVs-only at the depot, so if `rescued`
drops the report can say which half caused it. This directly serves the gate item
"rescued does not decrease without an explained reason". One extra constant, one
extra arm.

2.4  Determinism statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under a fixed seed the depot, the berth ranking, and every unit's spawn cell are
pure functions of (HEIGHT, WIDTH, origin, size, NUM_AGENTS, NUM_FIREFIGHTERS).
No RNG is consumed by any of it, the SYSTEM_RANDOM stream position at the end of
reset() is unchanged, and unique_agents_id numbering is unchanged because the
creation ORDER in reset() is untouched. If a randomised element is ever wanted,
the only acceptable pattern is Feature 1's dedicated random.Random seeded from
sha256(SYSTEM_RANDOM.getstate()) (wildfire_model.py:2519-2528), since getstate()
consumes nothing.

3.1  Correcting the premise the brief inherited
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two facts from the battery round were re-checked and one does not survive.

CONFIRMED: the 0.01 is real, and it is exactly where the code says.
_MODE_PREFERRED_ACTIONS[EMERGENCY] = ("safe_hold", "return_to_base")
(fail_safe_planner.py:50) is scored by _mode_preference_bonus as
2.0 + (len(preferred) - index) * 0.01 (:615), giving safe_hold 2.02 and
return_to_base 2.01. That bonus is the FIRST key of the sort tuple in
_select_fail_safe_option_mode_override (:574-581), so it decides before the
utility score and before the +0.75 critical bonus that return_to_base alone
earns. Reordering the tuple is a one-line, 0.02-swing fix.

NOT CONFIRMED, and materially wrong: "return_to_base loses to safe_hold on all
1,280 emergency decisions" implies 1,280 emergency decisions exist. A stock
240-step canonical run (seed 101, scenario D, east, default roles) produced ZERO
emergency decisions - 158 information_recovery, 81 safety_first, and one
degenerate step-1 return_to_base. The battery never reaches critical in a stock
run (floor 29.2 against a threshold of 15.0), so critical_battery is never a live
reason. The 1,280 almost certainly came from the forced crit12/low25 battery arms
in outputs/_bat_*, where outputs/_bat_analysis.txt:321 records CRITICAL_BATTERY
firing 1,912 times. Flagged as UNVERIFIED rather than re-run; it does not change
the design, but it does change what "fixing the 0.01" would buy.

CONSEQUENCE: reordering _MODE_PREFERRED_ACTIONS alone would be the tenth
measured-inert change in this campaign's dead-input family. It is diagnosed and
DECLINED here, with the reason, rather than shipped as a cosmetic win.

3.2  Mechanism (a) - through the planner. FEASIBLE, via the LOCAL channel
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

and _apply_final_direction_safety entirely. That is the deliberate, declared
difference between the arms, not a confound to be discovered in the results.

3.4  How the two coexist:  THEY DO NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: mutually exclusive, selected by BASE_STATION_RETURN_MECHANISM
(0 = none, 1 = planner, 2 = hardcoded). A "both on" configuration is UNDEFINED and
the implementation must reject it rather than silently pick one.

The reason is that mechanism (b)'s override at the top of advance() is the last
writer of selected_dir before move(), so a planner-routed waypoint committed by
the executor earlier in the same step would be overwritten with no trace. A
"both" arm would measure only (b) while appearing to measure both.

COMPARABILITY CAVEAT, declared in advance rather than discovered: arm (a) is
safety-filtered and arm (b) is not, so an (a)-vs-(b) difference is a difference in
routing AND in hazard filtering together. Rather than add a third sub-arm, the
report will carry the per-step fire-exposure columns the harness already records
from the interior-hazard round (burning / smoke / distance-to-nearest-fire under
each UAV), so the filter's contribution is directly visible instead of inferred.

3.5  Arrival, docking and re-launch - shared by both mechanisms
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deliberately ONE implementation, so the two arms differ only in the outbound
routing and the comparison is clean:

  - a UAV is DOCKED when its position equals its assigned berth and rtb_active is
    set. Because berths are pre-assigned and distinct, two UAVs never contend for
    a cell and the greedy-steer deadlock (a memoryless steer re-picking the same
    blocked direction against not_UAV_adjacent, forever) cannot arise.
  - while docked, move() returns False via the branch in 3.3(2). The UAV holds its
    cell exactly. Verified behaviour, not assumed: a UAV whose executor returns
    without applying a direction held its cell for four consecutive steps with
    execution_action == "no_managed_direction_hold" and battery draining exactly
    0.1/step, with no consumer raising and no other UAV affected.
  - re-launch at full charge (5.4). rtb_active and rtb_docked clear together, and
    the UAV re-enters normal execution the same step.

ONE THING THE FOUR-STEP OBSERVATION ABOVE DOES NOT ESTABLISH, and which Part 3
must: stuck_count reaches 3 on the third motionless step, at which point
_hold_needs_escape flips true for every role (wildfire_model.py:2736-2743,
uav_executor.py:567-571) and _hold_escape_direction begins scoring moves by
boundary distance, pushing toward the interior. Under the docked branch the UAV
does not move regardless of what the executor commits, so this should be cosmetic -
it pollutes the action label and the stuck-based analytics, not the position. A
four-step observation cannot distinguish "stable" from "stable just long enough",
and a charge dwell is tens of steps. Part 3 runs a >= 40-step dwell probe before
the wave.

4.1  On the grid, not off it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: a charging UAV stays ON the grid, parked on its berth. Feature 1's
off-grid pattern is the obvious template and it does not port.

Feature 1 was cheap for firefighters because Firefighter.advance already carried
`if self.pos is None ... return` (agents.py:841) and exactly one consumer needed
patching. For UAVs neither holds:

  - UAV.advance has NO pos guard (agents.py:339-346). Adding one silently stops
    the battery drain, because _update_battery_after_step is reached only from
    advance():346.
  - An off-grid UAV KILLS THE RUN ON ITS FIRST STEP. Verified in-process:
    model.step() raises TypeError out of the pre-move cycle at
    global_monitor.py:194 before anything else executes. Guarding sites one at a
    time walks a crash chain: global_monitor:194 -> wildfire_model:985 ->
    uav_executor:3502 (tracker) or :4194 (searcher) -> wildfire_model:2806 (MR2)
    -> agents:298 (surrounding_states).
  - The unguarded-consumer count is 12 line-level sites across 6 files, not the 7
    the prior round listed, and one of its citations is wrong: global_monitor.py:58
    is `self.uavs = uavs`, an assignment; the real read in that file is only :194.
  - Two of the required guards are not simple skips. Guarding state() PERMANENTLY
    TRUNCATES MR1_LIST - MR1 does `self.MR1_LIST = [a + b for a, b in
    zip(self.MR1_LIST, reward)]`, so a shorter reward list makes zip truncate and
    the assignment makes the shrink permanent; worse, index i then refers to a
    DIFFERENT UAV and every subsequent step credits the wrong accumulator
    (wildfire_model.py:2785-2791). Guarding surrounding_states instead makes the
    zero-padding loop at :2823-2829 produce an all-zero observation of full
    length, which MR1 scores as "no burning cells observed" - a false reading
    rather than a missing one.
  - As a trap for reviewers: uav_executor._position_at_boundary returns False on
    pos None (:544), so the `or` at :369 short-circuits straight into an
    unguarded int(agent.pos[0]) at :370. A guard is visible; the crash is one line
    later.

On-grid parking needs none of this. It uses vocabulary the model already has.

4.2  How long, and what is the UAV doing
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GRADUAL, not instant. BASE_STATION_RECHARGE_PER_STEP, default 5.0/step, applied
in _update_battery_after_step (agents.py:270-281) when docked, BEFORE the existing
clamp at :275 which already bounds at 100. The -0.1/step drain continues, so the
net rate is +4.9.

Gradual because instant would make "total steps spent charging" identically zero,
and the brief asks for exactly that number. At the default reserve of 60 a UAV
docks at roughly 60 minus 0.3 x (distance travelled) and refills to 100 in about
8-10 steps.

While charging the UAV holds its berth, contributes nothing to coverage, keeps
its role, and keeps its lane/sector slot (4.4).

4.3  Latch and release
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Latch on trigger; release at BASE_STATION_RECHARGE_RELEASE_LEVEL, default 100.0.
Full-charge release, not a partial level, because a partial release chatters
around the threshold and because it makes charge duration a clean function of the
depth of discharge - which is what makes the "does recharging cost more coverage
than it saves" question answerable.

4.4  Lanes and sectors:  KEEP THE ROLE, ADD NO FILTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is where I decline a brief requirement, so the argument is given in full.

The brief requires that a charging UAV "must be excluded from lane and sector
partitioning". I recommend the opposite, on measured evidence.

BOTH ROSTERS ARE POSITION-BLIND AND ROLE-BASED, rebuilt live on every call with
no memo:
  - resolve_victim_searcher_uav_ids (local_adaptation_generator.py:288-307)
  - _update_fire_tracker_sector_assignments (wildfire_model.py:440-446, 491-532),
    called once per step at :5289
Neither consults pos. So a parked UAV that KEEPS its role produces NO RESHUFFLE
AT ALL: no other UAV's lane or sector moves, nothing changes mid-traverse, and
the returning UAV lands back in the lane it left. The partition is frozen for the
charge duration. Verified: taking a searcher off the grid changed neither the
roster nor either lane.

EXCLUDING IT IS WORSE IN EVERY CONFIGURATION:
  - With ONE searcher (the legacy default split, n-1 trackers + 1 searcher):
    filtering empties the list and the legacy fallback at
    local_adaptation_generator.py:306-316 RE-ELECTS the excluded UAV by
    unique_id regardless of role. Verified: flipping the only searcher to role
    'charging' left the roster unchanged. The exclusion is a no-op.
  - With TWO searchers (the 3T+2S / half-roles override used by 10 of the 13
    canonical runs): removing one makes _searcher_crosswind_lane return None for
    BOTH, because n and idx are derived from the SAME filtered list (:333-350).
    The survivor does not inherit a wider lane - it loses lane discipline
    entirely and reverts to the whole grid. At two searchers, "exclusion" means
    "lane feature off".
  - On the tracker side a role flip moved both surviving trackers' sectors and
    left the departed tracker's STALE sector dict in place, because
    _update_fire_tracker_sector_assignments never deletes entries and
    _fire_tracker_flank_bounds reads that dict by id - so a returning charger
    would be steered by a box computed for a roster size that no longer exists.
  - It is not one edit. It needs a skip in the searcher loop, a guard on the
    legacy fallback, a skip in the sector loop, explicit disposal of the stale
    sector entry, and the same treatment in a THIRD builder,
    _fire_tracker_uav_ids (local_adaptation_generator.py:2409-2435), whose result
    is index-searched at :2453. Three functions, two files, five or six insertion
    points - and the roster is read from two sources that can disagree
    (managed_uav_states[uid].role first, then uav_resource_model.by_uav_id
    current_role), with _fire_tracker_uav_ids reading by_uav_id FIRST, so a writer
    that updates only one moves the trackers and not the searchers.

WHAT THE BRIEF IS PROTECTING AGAINST IS REAL: a lane assigned to a UAV that is
not there is a silent coverage hole. My answer is that it is the same hole the
already-accepted Feature 1 takes for firefighters, it is bounded by the charge
duration, and - unlike today - it becomes MEASURABLE. Trade an unmeasured hole
for a measured one; do not trade it for a reshuffle that is worse in both
configurations.

MAINTAINER RULING REQUESTED. If exclusion is required anyway, it is buildable at
the cost above and should be its own arm, not the default.

SCOPE NOTE for the gate: at the default role split the searcher lane is already
inert (n = 1 gives lane None), so this decision only bites at the half-roles
override. The tracker sector is the live surface at default roles; the searcher
lane at 3T+2S.

4.5  Instrumentation the brief's reporting requires
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"How often lanes reshuffled due to a charging transition, and whether any
searcher's lane changed mid-traverse" has an expected answer of ZERO under 4.4,
and the report must PROVE that rather than assert it. Record per step, per UAV:
  - the searcher roster (the ordered id list) and each searcher's (axis, lo, hi)
  - the tracker roster and each tracker's sector bounds
  - the docked/returning flag and the battery level
  - the current search target, so "mid-traverse" is well defined
A reshuffle is then any step at which a UAV's lane or sector tuple differs from
the previous step while that UAV was not itself transitioning. Mid-traverse is a
reshuffle on a step where the UAV had an unreached target.

REQUIRED HARNESS EDIT, BEFORE THE FIRST WAVE. outputs/_ffr_harness.py records NO
battery at all (zero grep hits) and no docked state. Add both columns to the
uav_steps row (outputs/_ffr_harness.py:498-509), plus the roster/lane/sector
capture above, and then FREEZE the harness. Adding a field changes the recorded
JSON shape, so arms recorded before and after the edit are not comparable on the
identity fields - the interior-hazard round added --uav-actions to EVERY arm in
its queue, including the baseline checkout arm, for exactly this reason.

5.3  Is 30% enough?  NO, on two independent grounds
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GEOMETRY. Worst-case return is the far corner to the nearest depot cell:
90 Manhattan cells under 4-direction movement, identical for all four corners.
Every returning step is a moving step, so the cost is 90 x 0.3 = 27.0 points.
A 30% reserve arrives with 3.0 - below the 5-point margin the brief asks for,
which needs 32.0. It also has no slack against a congested return: the true cost
is (90/f) x 0.1 + 18 for move-success fraction f, which reaches exactly 30.00 at
f = 0.75. Measured f today is 0.95-1.00, but a depot with UAVs queueing to enter
is precisely the situation that lowers it.

TIME, and this is decisive. With a moving step costing 0.3, level(t) = 100 - 0.3t,
so 30% is first reachable at step 233.3 of a 240-step run:

  reserve R    earliest step reached (moving every step)    steps left of 240
  ---------    -----------------------------------------    -----------------
     15                     283.3                            NEVER REACHED
     20                     266.7                            NEVER REACHED
     25                     250.0                            NEVER REACHED
     30                     233.3                                 6.7
     40                     200.0                                40.0
     50                     166.7                                73.3
     55                     150.0                                90.0   <- floor
     60                     133.3                               106.7
     70                     100.0                               140.0
     80                      66.7                               173.3

MEASURED, seed 101, canonical defaults: the three UAVs cross 30% at steps 234,
238 and 239, finishing at 28.4 / 29.2 / 29.6 with effective drain 0.2933-0.2983
per step. One to six steps of runway against a 90-step return. ZERO returns can
complete. The 15% gate needs step 288 and is never reached at all.

So at the stock threshold the feature would be INERT BY DEFAULT and every
seed-matched A/B would show an exact tie - the same signature as the drain-zeroed
negative control from the battery round. A base-station round shipped at 30 would
prove nothing.

HARD FLOOR: 55. Below it a worst-cell return provably cannot finish inside 240.

5.4  The chosen rule
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: trigger when

    battery_level <= max( UAV_RETURN_TO_BASE_RESERVE ,
                          0.3 * manhattan_to_berth + BASE_STATION_RETURN_MARGIN )

with UAV_RETURN_TO_BASE_RESERVE = 60.0 and BASE_STATION_RETURN_MARGIN = 5.0.

DERIVATION OF 60. The reserve is the larger of two independent constraints. The
first is the one the brief names; the second is the one that actually binds, and
neither is "30 did not work".

Common terms. The worst-case return distance is the farthest grid cell from the
depot: (49,0) to the nearest depot cell (4,45) = 45 + 45 = D = 90 Manhattan cells
under the 4-direction action space. A moving step costs 0.3 (0.1 per step + 0.2
per move); a BLOCKED step still costs 0.1. Let f be the move-success fraction on
the return leg and m the move fraction on the outbound leg, so the effective
outbound drain is e = 0.1 + 0.2m.

  (A) FUEL - can the UAV physically reach the depot?
      cost(f) = (D/f) * 0.1 + D * 0.2
        f = 1.00 -> 90.0 steps -> 27.00      f = 0.90 -> 100.0 steps -> 28.00
        f = 0.95 -> 94.7 steps -> 27.47      f = 0.75 -> 120.0 steps -> 30.00
      Add one step of trigger latency (the analyzers read a one-step-stale copy
      of the battery, so under mechanism (a) the reaction lands one step after
      the crossing): +0.3. Add the 5-point margin the brief asks for.
          R_fuel = 27.0 + 0.3 + 5.0 = 32.3

  (B) TIME - can it reach the depot before the run ends?
      The return needs D/f steps and must finish by step 240, and the UAV reaches
      reserve R at t_trig = (100 - R)/e:
          (100 - R)/e + D/f <= 240        =>        R >= 100 - e*(240 - D/f)
      At f = 1 and D = 90 that is R >= 100 - 150e:
        m = 1.0000  e = 0.3000  moves every step                  R >= 55.00
        m = 0.9777  e = 0.2955  measured, seed 101, slowest UAV    R >= 55.67
        m = 0.9000  e = 0.2800  holds 1 step in 10                 R >= 58.00
        m = 0.8333  e = 0.2667  holds 1 step in 6                  R >= 60.00
        m = 0.7500  e = 0.2500  holds 1 step in 4                  R >= 62.50

          R_time = 60.0

  R = max(R_fuel, R_time) = max(32.3, 60.0) = 60.

WHAT 60 BUYS, stated as a guarantee rather than a hope. Read constraint (B)
backwards - R = 60 requires e >= 0.2667, i.e. m >= 5/6 - so:

    A reserve of 60 guarantees that a UAV at the WORST cell on the grid completes
    its return inside the 240-step horizon, provided it moved on at least five of
    every six steps beforehand.

Measured outbound move fractions today are 0.95-1.00, so 5/6 = 0.833 is a
comfortable envelope rather than a tight one. For comparison, R = 55 - the naive
floor from assuming the UAV moves on every single step - tolerates m >= 1.0000
exactly, i.e. it has no slack at all and fails on the very first held step; R = 56
tolerates one held step in 30; R = 58 one in 10.

The guarantee also absorbs an obstructed return leg. At m = 1 outbound:
    f = 1.00 -> 133.3 + 90.0  = 223.3      f = 0.90 -> 133.3 + 100.0 = 233.3
    f = 0.95 -> 133.3 + 94.7  = 228.1      f = 0.85 -> 133.3 + 105.9 = 239.2
so the worst-cell return still completes down to f ~ 0.85. Pre-assigned berths
(2.2) are what keep f high: no two UAVs ever contend for the same target cell,
which is the only UAV-vs-UAV blocking this model has.

WHY BOTH TERMS ARE IN THE RULE, and not just the constant 60:
  - the DISTANCE term is the correct engineering formulation of constraint (A).
    It yields 32.0 at the worst cell and about 5 next to the depot, and it is
    what guarantees the UAV gets home from wherever it actually is. Alone it
    fires too late to be observed inside 240.
  - the FLAT RESERVE carries constraint (B), which the distance term cannot see
    because it has no notion of the horizon.
Taking the max means the UAV turns back at 60%, and if it is ever somehow
further out than 60% covers, the distance term raises the trigger above it.

CYCLE ARITHMETIC (work = (100-R)/0.3, return = d moves, charge = (100 - R + 0.3d)
/ rate):

  R = 60, d = 25, rate = 5   ->  133.3 + 25 + 9.5   = 167.8   1.43 cycles / 240
  R = 70, d = 25, rate = 5   ->  100.0 + 25 + 7.5   = 132.5   1.81 cycles / 240
  R = 75, d = 25, rate = 5   ->   83.3 + 25 + 6.5   = 114.8   2.09 cycles / 240
  R = 80, d = 25, rate = 5   ->   66.7 + 25 + 5.5   =  97.2   2.47 cycles / 240

DO NOT chase two or three cycles at 240 steps. Two need R >= 70.65 at mean return
distance and 77.83 at worst; three need 84.51 / 94.09. A UAV that turns for home
three-quarters charged is not a battery model, it is a metronome. And raising the
DRAIN instead is arithmetically self-defeating: two cycles at R = 30 would need
0.9333/step, which burns 84 points over the 90-cell return and strands the UAV at
-54. If multiple cycles are genuinely required, extend the horizon (two cycles
need about 509 steps at mean return distance) rather than distorting either
constant - and that is a separate decision, because it re-bases everything.

A NOTE THE ARITHMETIC MAKES VISIBLE, and which section 6 of the brief's Part 3
question turns on: the CHARGE is not the expensive part. At rate 5 a full refill
costs 8-10 steps; the RETURN LEG costs d steps of not searching, and d is 15-45.
The return leg dominates the coverage cost by roughly four to one. Expect the
"does recharging cost more than it saves" answer to be governed by travel, not by
charging.

6.2  The z-order trap - the depot must be an OVERLAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: draw the depot as an OVERLAY on both surfaces, never as a cell colour.

The reason is measured, not stylistic. All 2500 cells carry a Fire agent
(DENSITY_PROB = 1), and on BOTH surfaces the colour decider tests
smoke / burning / burnt / scorched before anything else (serve_dashboard.py:58-67,
main.py:66-83). A depot expressed as a cell background is preempted exactly when a
depot cell smokes or burns - that is, at the moment an operator most needs to see
where the base is. Since the depot is burnable (1.3), that moment will arrive.

  DASHBOARD. Publish the footprint once in _capture_frame's return dict
  (serve_dashboard.py:199-202) as a single rect descriptor, and stroke it in
  drawMap between the gridline stroke at :657 and the trails loop at :660. The
  canvas is immediate-mode, so statement position IS z-order: that slot puts the
  depot above the ground and below every unit marker.

  MESA. Synthesise per-cell portrayals in CanvasGrid.render at LAYER 2 - currently
  free (ground 0; UAV, PathMarker and the FOV frame 1; Victim and Firefighter 3) -
  following the precedent of the existing UAV frame at
  Canvas_Grid_Visualization.py:111-127. A multi-cell outline cannot be one shape:
  GridDraw.js:223-224 clamps every rect inside a single cell.

  THE LAYER MUST BE AN INTEGER. CanvasModule.js:63 does `for (const layer in
  data)`, which enumerates array-index-like keys in ascending numeric order but
  enumerates any non-index key such as '0.5' afterwards, in insertion order. A
  fractional layer chosen to "slot between ground and markers" would silently draw
  OVER the victims and firefighters.

NEVER put the depot (or the FOV frame) into the dashboard `cells` dict. That
destroys ground truth: the live payload is 2500 entries / 49,000 bytes, and
overwriting depot cells there would hide their real fire state.

7.1  The recipe, taken from the three shipped features
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9c3eac6 (FF_RESCUE_ABSENCE_MAX_STEPS <= 0), 5000f70 (VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE
<= 0) and 16b2da8 (VICTIM_SEARCHER_HAZARD_RETREAT_RANGE 0/K/99) all use one
identical recipe:
  - a single constant in common_fixed_variables.py with a prose block above it
    stating the kill value;
  - a tiny accessor that reads it AT CALL TIME through the cfv MODULE handle,
    with a try/except and a clamp;
  - one early-return guard at the TOP of each entry point, so the OFF path is the
    pre-feature code literally.

Three ways to get it silently wrong, all of which have happened here before:
  - reading via the star-import (`from common_fixed_variables import *`) - the
    local name keeps its import-time value forever, because apply_scenario_config
    setattrs only the cfv and wf modules. agents.py has BOTH import forms at
    :9-10, and agents.py is where UAV logic lives.
  - reading at IMPORT time (`RANGE = SOME_CONSTANT` at module level) - same
    failure even with the module handle.
  - guarding the visible EFFECT rather than returning before any state WRITE.
    Feature 2 needed guards at three separate entry points; a missed one leaves a
    residual write that breaks byte identity on a field like eval or the planner
    log.

7.2  The entry points - count them first

  2. the return trigger in UAV.advance()
  3. the docked hold in UAV.move()
  4. the recharge tick in _update_battery_after_step()
  5. the depot region construction itself (self.base_station must not even be
     built when off, so no consumer can see it)
Plus two display sites, which are outside the identity argument but inside the
commit: the depot overlay on each renderer.

BASE_STATION_MODE is an ORDINAL LADDER, not a set of booleans, and each higher
mode is a STRICT SUPERSET of the lower - so a mode-N minus mode-(N-1) diff
attributes exactly one increment. This matters: the interior-hazard round got
three arms out of one queue with one ordinal constant, while Feature 2's two
booleans could not be separated by config and cost two extra 13-run waves as
staged code arms.

BASE_STATION_CORNER is numeric, not a string, deliberately - it sidesteps the
question of how the harness's --set parsing and the pool script's CR-stripping
handle a string value.

Note the mechanism selector is genuinely orthogonal to the ladder, so it is its
own constant rather than folded into BASE_STATION_MODE. Folding them would break
the superset property for the increment the brief cares most about.
