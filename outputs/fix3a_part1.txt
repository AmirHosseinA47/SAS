3.1 A1-R  SEARCHER_ROUTE_FIRE_FIELD - THE ROUTE PLANS WITHIN ITS OWN GUARD (class R)
    Scope: _attempt_pathfinding_toward_target at the shipped 3b setting (VICTIM_SEARCHER_HAZARD_RETREAT_
    RANGE >= 99 and SEARCHER_GATE_NEAR_FIELD on). Any other setting, or the switch at 0: fix2 3b(ii)
    exactly. The searcher hazard GATE (3b(i), the other steps) is not changed.
    Rule, per routed step, d0 = the searcher's Manhattan distance to the nearest BURNING cell:
      (i)   THE NEAR FIELD IS THE FIRE'S. d0 counts burning cells only. Smoke stays IMPASSABLE (the
            planner never steps into a fire or smoke cell - unchanged) but no longer vetoes a step at
            range. Reason: 52% of the vetoes were smoke alone (2.2); smoke does not spread onto a UAV,
            it is left behind a front, and it outlives the fire by its fuel count.
      (ii)  OUTSIDE THE NEAR FIELD (d0 > SEARCHER_GATE_NEAR_RANGE = 6): the existing planner (greedy,
            then BFS over non-fire / non-smoke cells), no veto.
      (iii) INSIDE IT: the planner itself keeps a CLEARANCE T = min(d0, K) - every greedy candidate and
            every BFS cell must have burning distance >= T (K is ruling D-2; K = 6 is exactly the
            veto's "never closer while within 6", K = 3 lets the route pass the front at 3 cells).
            The plan can then never be vetoed, so the veto disappears and the route cannot alternate
            with a retreat.
      (iv)  NO ADMISSIBLE STEP toward the target: the legal neighbour (in bounds, not edge-blocked, not
            fire / smoke, no UAV on it) with the largest burning distance, IF that is strictly larger
            than d0 ("fire_retreat"); else the searcher WAITS on its cell ("fire_wait": a stay, like the
            fail-safe hold; it intends its own cell, so no drift sample). Both labels are routed steps,
            exempt from the gate as every routed step already is.
    WHY IT CANNOT OSCILLATE: in a static fire every routed step keeps the burning distance >= T and
    every fallback step strictly increases it or stays - the burning distance of the searcher's cell is
    non-decreasing while it is within the near field (K = 6) or never below K (K < 6). A two-cell cycle
    needs the fire itself to move. The honest cost: a searcher that is enclosed (no admissible step,
    no strictly safer neighbour) WAITS until the fire moves - a deliberate, stationary wait the report
    counts separately from a pocket (section 7).

3.2 A1-S  SEARCHER_SWEEP_IN_BOUNDS - NO TARGET THE EDGE FILTER FORBIDS (class S)
    A victim searcher's sector bounds - the region its sweep, sector filters and sector-biased
    targets range over (_uav_sector_bounds; the full grid today) - exclude the edge band it may not
    enter: edge distance >= 4 (the edge filter's margin 3, + 1), i.e. x, y in [4, 45] on 50 x 50; and
    the lawnmower sweep state (sweep_x, sweep_y) is clamped into the same box wherever it is read
    (_init_wind_aware_sweep_state, the inline advance, _safe_victim_sweep_target), so the at-target
    and end-of-lane tests compare against a cell the searcher can reach. Detection is Euclidean <= 8, so
    a searcher at edge distance 4 still sees the whole band. (The prototype did exactly this.)

3.3 A1-D  SEARCHER_CORNER_ESCAPE - A CORNER HAS A WAY OUT, AND AN ESCAPE DOES NOT RUN THROUGH A UAV (class D)
    (i)  The edge filter measures PENETRATION of the band per axis: p(c) = max(0, 4 - dx) + max(0, 4 - dy),
         dx / dy = the distance to the nearer x / y edge. Outside the band (p = 0) nothing is blocked -
         identical to today's filter, which never blocks at edge distance > 3. Inside the band a move is
         legal iff it strictly reduces p. Along one edge this is today's rule exactly (p = 4 - d);
         it differs ONLY at corner cells, where today every move is refused (min over axes) and now
         the inward move on either axis is legal. Every footprint cell of a corner depot therefore has
         an exit (p > 0 always has a strictly-reducing neighbour).
    (ii) The pocket escape's BFS and greedy step treat every other UAV's cell as blocked (added to
         _pocket_blocked_cells_for_escape), so two searchers can no longer plan through each other.
    Expected test change: tests/test_victim_searcher_offgrid_guard.py::test_it_cannot_be_starved_
    where_the_margin_three_filter_is pins "at a corner all four directions are edge-blocked" - the
    very trap (i) removes; Part 2 pins it to A1-D = 0 and adds the corner-exit test for A1-D = 1.

4. A2 - FREE-CELL DOCKING (the maintainer's decision), DESIGNED WITH A1
================================================================================
4.1 THE RULE (switch FREE_CELL_DOCKING, ships 1; at 0 the berth docking of a5a496db exactly):
    - TRIGGER: battery <= max(reserve, 0.3 * d + BASE_STATION_RETURN_MARGIN) with d = the Manhattan
      distance to the NEAREST FREE CELL of either depot footprint (free = no other UAV on it); ties
      (distance, the first greedy step blocked, depot index, x, y). The leg LATCHES that cell and its
      depot (rtb_target_berth / rtb_target_depot keep their names, so every reader keeps working).
    - STEER: along the first step of the SHORTEST PATH that avoids every other UAV's cell (BFS on the
      4-neighbour grid) to the latched cell. RE-PICK, within the latched depot, when another UAV takes
      the latched cell or no UAV-free path to it remains: the free cell with the shortest UAV-free
      path (ties: Manhattan, x, y). No UAV-free path to any free cell: the UAV makes no move that step
      (a stalled step, counted; it intends its own cell).
      WHY A PATH, NOT MANHATTAN + THE GREEDY STEER: the first prototype (variant 1: Manhattan-nearest,
      _rtb_direction, re-pick on taken or 3 stalls) FAILED C/W - two released trackers parked on the NW
      doorstep at (4,45) and (3,44), every free cell's greedy approach ran through one of them, the
      re-pick cycled among blocked cells, and searcher 2504 waited 33 steps at (4,44). A memoryless
      greedy steer cannot detour (the dock-fix round's own finding); a shortest path can, and it is
      distance-monotone in a static field (path length drops by one per move), which keeps the
      termination argument the berth code relied on.
    - DOCK: the moment the UAV stands on ANY footprint cell of any depot. A footprint cell is free by
      construction (the move rule never puts two UAVs on one cell), so there is no berth to wait for.
    - CHARGE: only while docked AND inside a depot footprint - the positional test
      _update_battery_after_step never had. With A2 on, docking cannot happen outside a footprint, so
      the test is an invariant guard (Part 3 checks it reads 0); tests/test_base_station.py::
      test_recharge_still_has_no_positional_test changes with it (pinned to A2 = 0; a new test pins
      the positional guard for A2 = 1).
    - RELEASE: unchanged (BASE_STATION_RECHARGE_RELEASE_LEVEL); the UAV relaunches from its dock cell.
    - "DOCKED" MEANS INSIDE THE FOOTPRINT, and "no two UAVs on one cell" holds by the move rule and the
      distinct spawn cells (Part 3 checks both on every step).

5.3 B3  SCENARIO_B_RETURN_DELAY (ships 1) - THE EXACT RULE AND THE MARGIN
    At the return-trigger test of a victim searcher S (UAV._apply_return_to_base; S not returning, not
    docked; BATTERY_SCENARIO = 1), with d the distance the trigger uses (A2 on: to the nearest free
    footprint cell; A2 off: to the nearest own berth) and T(d) = max(reserve, 0.3 d + 39.23):
      if battery <= T(d)                                   (the return would start now)
      and at least one other victim searcher exists and EVERY other victim searcher is away
          (rtb_active or rtb_docked)                       (S is the only searcher flying)
      and battery > F(d) = 0.3 d + A + 1.70 + 0.60         (the floor)
      then S DELAYS: no return starts this step; S flies its normal search step.
      Otherwise the return starts exactly as today. The test repeats every step, so the delay ends the
      step the other searcher relaunches or the floor is reached. Nothing else changes (no target, no
      steering); a delayed searcher is an ordinary airborne searcher.
    THE MARGIN - A RULING IS NEEDED (D-6). "Enough battery to reach the nearest depot with the existing
    safety margin" has three readings:
      (a) the margin the code calls the margin, BASE_STATION_RETURN_MARGIN 39.23: then F(d) >= T(d) and
          the rule can NEVER fire - the trigger already is "enough to get home with 39.23 to spare".
          39.23 is a HORIZON margin (a return from full charge must finish inside 240 steps, cfv), not a
          fuel margin. VACUOUS - not recommended.
      (b) RECOMMENDED - A = LOW_BATTERY_THRESHOLD (30): F(d) = 0.3 d + 32.30. It preserves the
          existing SAFETY PROPERTY: no UAV reaches LOW in flight (fix1's finding that the battery safety
          is the return rule and no battery trigger fires in flight stays true), CRITICAL_BATTERY (a live
          fail-safe reason that reaches EMERGENCY with a real collision alarm, and R5 - the EMERGENCY
          freeze of the trackers - is unreached) stays unreachable. Delay capacity from the trigger:
          39.23 - 32.30 = 6.93 of battery, i.e. ~23 steps of flight at a constant distance from the
          depot, ~12 flying straight away from it.
      (c) A = BATTERY_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD (15), the arrival buffer of the FUEL-only margin recorded in cfv
          (16.40): F(d) = 0.3 d + 17.30; ~73 / ~37 steps; the delayed searcher flies below LOW
          (LOW_BATTERY fires - no consumer) and arrives at >= 15.
      The terms: 1.70 = 17 blocked steps x 0.1 (the recorded maximum return-leg standoff; the derivation
      used 11); 0.60 = one more delayed step moving AWAY (battery -0.3, threshold +0.3).
    THE GUARANTEE (never delays past the point): if the delay is allowed at step t, then at t + 1
    battery >= 0.3 d(t+1) + A + 1.70, so the return that starts at t + 1 at the latest arrives with
    >= A after up to 17 blocked steps. Stranding (battery 0) would need ~100 steps beyond that on the leg;
    with A2 no leg can wait for a berth, so the premise is the path length. Part 3 measures the lowest
    battery on every return and every stranding.

  - Rule 3 (NO YIELD AT A DEPOT - either UAV returning, docked or within Manhattan 3 of a depot cell):
    unchanged and still sufficient. With A2 every dock cell is a footprint cell, inside the area; the
    launch leg from an edge dock cell is shorter but starts inside the area; docked UAVs remain outside

10. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER
================================================================================
  - A1: the depot pockets were a CONTROLLER defect, not a property of the depot: a safety veto on the
    searcher's escape route (session 2's near-field rule) disagreed with the planner it guarded, and a
    memoryless retreat between them produced a livelock wherever the target lay beyond a fire front or
    a smoke field. It cost three of 16 runs their completion and two victims their detection at the
    shipped defaults. The fireproof depot made the refuge last longer; it did not create it (a burnable
    depot pocketed too). Two further pre-existing geometry defects (a sweep target the edge filter
    forbids; a corner cell with no legal move) complete the class. Lesson for the paper's method
    section: a guard must be planned within, not appended after, or it becomes a livelock generator -
    and a safety rule's range should match the hazard it models (smoke does not spread onto a UAV).
    Any result published at the fix2 defaults includes these livelocks (the three runs that never finish).
  - A2: docking no longer depends on a reserved cell. The "docking standoff" limitation of sessions 1-2
    and the earlier docking deadlock are removed by construction, and charging is now provably
    positional. Found on the way: at the fix2 defaults a searcher pocketed on a berth can strand a
    returning UAV at the doorstep (B/W/9608, 107 steps, LOW reached in flight) - the only in-flight
    battery trigger on record, and the reason A1 and A2 ship together.
  - B: with the new team (2 searchers) and the staggered launch (85/65 searchers, 78/72 trackers) the
    no-searcher gap of scenario B disappears on the prototype screen without extra return trips - the
    first stagger in this project that pays for itself, because every launch value sits above the
    return point (session 2's staggers failed because B's recharge-in-place erased them and because in
    C/D the extra trips fell before the terminal step). The return-delay rule is a backstop that did not
    fire once the launches were staggered; alone it closed part of the gap. An 8-run screen per arm, on
    a5a496db code with the rules monkeypatched - Part 3 and the measurement round decide.
  - Every number measured before this session at the fix2 defaults (pocket counts, completion, never-
    detected, the B scenario with 3 UAVs) describes the pre-fix3a system.
