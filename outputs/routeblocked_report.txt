HEADLINE
================================================================================
The trigger fix works and is measurably better than what it replaced:

  * route_blocked firing rate DOUBLED: 7 -> 14 firings, 0.33% -> 0.69% of
    _move_toward calls over the same 18 runs.
  * The signal now precedes 13 of 14 firefighter deaths, up from 7 of 14.
  * It fires on the "moving to victim" call site for the first time - that site
    was unreachable by construction before.
  * 5 of the 14 firings happen while the unit is standing in fire; every one of
    those was silently suppressed by the old guard.
  * No new test failures (8 failed / 427 passed, before and after, identical
    failure lists).
  * Cost is 0.03% of run wall time, measured.

But the honest reading of the outcome is more restrained, and two things must be
said plainly:

  1. THE EXTRA LEAD TIME IS SMALL. Median lead is 3 steps; the maximum is 8.
    The improvement is almost entirely in COVERAGE (7/14 -> 13/14 deaths get any
    warning at all), not in EARLINESS. A 3-step warning is not enough time for a
    replacement unit to cross the map. As a predictor of "this specific unit is
    about to die", the signal is now reliable; as an early warning with room to
    act, it is still late.

  2. DOWNSTREAM, THE LIVE SIGNAL IS CURRENTLY A NET NEGATIVE. Outcomes did not
    improve, and moved slightly the wrong way: over the 18-run campaign,
    rescued 44 -> 42, victims dead 25 -> 26, firefighter deaths 14 -> 14. Two
    seeds account for all of it, and both are explained by consumer defects
    (section 6), not by the trigger misfiring. Every one of the 14 firings was
    a correct identification of a genuinely unreachable target.

  So the real fix is now in the CONSUMERS and in the retreat/standby posture,
  not in the trigger condition. That is the next round's scope. Details and
  evidence below; nothing here was tuned to produce a pass.

1. WHAT WAS CHANGED (agents.py only, +64/-9 lines)
================================================================================

  (a) NEW HELPER `Firefighter._path_exists_avoiding_fire(src, dst, fire_cells)`
      4-connected BFS, active fire impassable, early exit on reaching dst.
      Semantics copied from the already-validated
      outputs/_ffdeath_probe._bfs_path_len rather than reinvented:
        - bounds via the existing self.model.grid.out_of_bounds
        - impassable via set membership on one self._fire_cells() result
        - the DESTINATION counts as reachable even when it is itself burning
          (a victim's cell can burn transiently; calling that "no route" would
          hand a still-rescuable victim to a replacement for nothing)
        - the SOURCE is never tested, which is what makes the standing-in-fire
          case answerable at all.
      This is the same notion of reachability wildfire_model already uses to
      decide whether a VICTIM is unreachable (`_safe_path_reachable_cells`,
      :3685). The change applies the model's own definition to the firefighter's
      own route rather than introducing a second, inconsistent one.

  (b) NEW TRIGGER in `_move_toward`, computed before `scored` is built:
          route_blocked_now = (not self.exiting) and not path_exists(pos, target)
      Raised via `_mark_route_blocked()` AFTER the `if not scored:` early return,
      then execution FALLS THROUGH to the normal tier selection and the unit
      still moves. Raising the flag deliberately does not stop the unit -
      choosing the best available step is the retreat logic's job and retreat
      logic is out of scope.

  (c) THE OLD CONDITION IS KEPT UNCHANGED. `if not scored: _mark_route_blocked()`
      still fires on every call site exactly as before. The two conditions are
      not strictly nested (if the target IS one of the four burning neighbours,
      `scored` is empty but BFS reports reachable), so keeping both removes no
      existing behaviour. Confirmed in the data: the 1 exiting-site firing in the
      AFTER run is an old-condition firing, byte-identical to BEFORE.

  (d) STATUS AUTO-CLEAR SUPPRESSED while blocked. The tail of `_move_toward`
      clears route_blocked whenever a move is chosen; that now runs only when
      `route_blocked_now` is false. Otherwise the flag would be set and cleared
      inside the same call. This gives the intended hysteresis: blocked while the
      route is gone, cleared on the first step taken with a live route.

  (e) GUARD REMOVED from `_mark_route_blocked`. The
      `if not self._cell_contains_active_fire(own cell)` veto is gone;
      idempotence (do nothing if already route_blocked) is kept.
      Rationale in Part 1 section 6. In short: both of the guard's plausible
      purposes are already covered elsewhere (`_blocked_replacement_attempted`
      dedupes the replacement; `_check_fire_casualties` overwrites status to
      "dead" anyway), and firing here gets the replacement dispatched one phase
      earlier because `_on_firefighter_route_blocked` runs
      `_process_rescue_incidents()` synchronously inside `schedule.step()`,
      whereas the casualty path runs later in the post-move cycle.
      After the change the TRIGGER decides when to raise the flag and
      `_mark_route_blocked` just raises it. A unit standing in fire that still
      has a live path does not fire - the guard was never what protected that.

INTERACTION WITH THE THREE CALL SITES - as required, stated explicitly, and now
with measured firing counts from the 18-run AFTER campaign:

  site 473  survival-retreat fall-through
        Already reachable. Now also fires on the no-path case.
        AFTER: 11 firings (6 old-condition, 5 new-condition-only). INTENDED.

  site 522  moving to victim
        Previously UNREACHABLE BY CONSTRUCTION. Now reachable.
        AFTER: 2 firings, both with `scored` non-empty and no live path - i.e.
        pure new-trigger firings that could not have occurred before. INTENDED,
        and the entire point of the change: this is the only site that can fire
        while the unit is still clear of fire, so it is the only possible source
        of lead time.

  site 557  exiting with victim
        DELIBERATELY UNCHANGED, via the `not self.exiting` guard on the new
        condition. AFTER: 1 firing, old-condition only - identical to BEFORE.
        Reason (Part 1 section 4): the replacement pathway's response is
        `unassign`, which clears rescued_victim / exiting / exit_target. For a
        unit CARRYING a victim that drops the victim mid-fire, and worse,
        agents.py:539-543 only finalises a rescue when rescued_victim is not
        None - so a blocked-then-recovered exiting unit would reach the boundary
        and the rescue would silently not count. That is a retreat/exit-routing
        problem, not a signal problem. Scoped out and reported instead.

NOT CHANGED: `_survival_move`, `_assigned_one_step_retreat`, the idle-retreat
state machine, the tier pools, cell selection, rescue_planner.py, dispatch and
pairing logic, and every consumer of the signal.

6. WHY THE THREE OUTCOME CHANGES HAPPENED - traced, not guessed
================================================================================
The brief asks whether movements are "because route_blocked now triggers
replacement/rerouting logic downstream, or for some other reason". All three are
the former, and each exposes a consumer defect rather than a trigger defect.

CASE 1 - D/east seed 101 at eval defaults, ff_deaths 1 -> 2.
  Traced step by step on both trees (outputs/_rb_seed101_{before,after}.txt):
      BEFORE: step 15 ff_unit_0 dispatched. Step 42 `_mark_route_blocked` IS
              called at (42,41) but the guard SUPPRESSES it - status stays
              en_route. Step 42 ff_unit_0 dies at (42,41). ff_unit_1 survives.
      AFTER:  step 15 ff_unit_0 dispatched. Step 38 route_blocked FIRES at
              (42,41) - four steps earlier - the unit is unassigned and its
              victim reset to pending. Step 42 ff_unit_0 dies anyway at (41,41).
              ff_unit_1's trajectory diverges from step 45 and it is overrun at
              (19,43) at step 201.
  So the trigger was right (ff_unit_0's route really was gone) and the earlier
  firing did not kill it - it died at the identical step in both runs. The extra
  death is ff_unit_1's, 159 steps later, downstream of the re-pairing churn the
  unassign set off. Rescued and victims-dead are unchanged.

CASE 2 - campaign D/east seed 333, r-1 d+1 ff-1. THE LATCH, IN THE WILD.
  This is the clearest evidence in the whole exercise, and it is a consumer bug:
      BEFORE: ff_unit_1 keeps working, contributes a rescue, dies at step 207.
              -> r2 d2 ff2
      AFTER:  route_blocked fires for ff_unit_1 at step 45 at (0,25). It is
              unassigned, and it then sits at (1,25) with status route_blocked
              for the remaining 195 steps - alive, mobile, and permanently
              undispatchable. It never dies (ff 2 -> 1) and it never rescues
              anyone again (r 2 -> 1, d 2 -> 3).
  The campaign confirms it directly: end-of-run latched units = 0 before, 1
  after, and that one unit is exactly east/333 ff_unit_1.
  MECHANISM (static analysis in Part 1 section 4, confirmed here and by
  outputs/_rb_consumer_probe.py): the flag has only three clearing paths -
  agents.py:1005 (end of a successful `_move_toward`), wildfire_model.py:2099
  (recycle after a COMPLETED rescue), and death. The replacement pathway's
  unassign (wildfire_model.py:3431-3463) sets target_pos=None and exiting=False
  while DELIBERATELY preserving the route_blocked status - there is an explicit
  `if status != "route_blocked":` at :3454. With no target and not exiting,
  `advance()` can never reach any `_move_toward` call site again, so :1005 can
  never run; and :2389 / :3330 both refuse to re-pair a route_blocked unit, so
  :2099 can never run either. The unit is retired for the rest of the run.
  In scenario D there are only TWO firefighters, so two firings can retire the
  whole fleet. This latch converted a firefighter death into a victim death.

CASE 3 - campaign D/east seed 808, r-1 ff+1.
  route_blocked fires for ff_unit_1 at step 153 while it is standing in fire, and
  it dies 3 steps later at 156. Same shape as case 1: a correct, late
  identification whose replacement churn costs a rescue.

WHAT NONE OF THE THREE ARE: a false positive. Every one of the 14 firings had a
genuinely unreachable target under the model's own definition of reachability.
The trigger is not what is misbehaving.

11. WHAT THE NEXT ROUND SHOULD SCOPE (evidence-backed, in priority order)
================================================================================
  1. FIX THE LATCH. Highest value and small. A firefighter whose route_blocked
     was acted on by the replacement pathway must return to service. Either
     clear the status inside the unassign at wildfire_model.py:3454, or give
     `advance()` a clearing path for a unit that has no target. Until this is
     fixed, a correct trigger is net-negative, which is why the outcome numbers
     here moved slightly the wrong way despite a strictly better signal.
  2. REACHABILITY-AWARE REPLACEMENT. The replacement dispatched after a
     route_blocked currently ignores whether the REPLACEMENT can reach the
     victim - the planner still sorts by Manhattan distance. Now that the model
     can answer "is this reachable", section 6 case 1 shows a second unit being
     sent toward a target that was just proven unreachable. This is the pairing
     tiebreaker the death investigation described and deferred; it is now much
     cheaper to justify because the primitive exists.
  3. EXITING-UNIT HANDLING. Decide what a blocked unit carrying a victim should
     do. It must not be "unassign", which drops the victim and breaks rescue
     finalisation. Until then the new condition stays scoped to the approach.
  4. RETREAT / STANDBY POSTURE. Unchanged from the death investigation's
     conclusion and untouched here. The lead-time data in section 3 supports it:
     by the time a route is topologically gone, the fire is already on the unit.
     Surviving requires being somewhere better earlier, not being told sooner.
