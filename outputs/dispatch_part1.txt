1.1 BRIEF: "select_rescue_assignment sends the NEAREST AVAILABLE firefighter by
    straight-line Manhattan distance, ties to the lower id, with no path test."
    VERDICT: CONFIRMED, one wording correction.
    - RP:689-695 is the whole scoring rule:
        dist = _manhattan_distance(victim_pos, ff_pos)
        if best_dist is None or dist < best_dist or (dist == best_dist and ff_s < str(best_ff))
      _manhattan_distance (RP:421-425) is plain grid L1.  It is NOT straight-line
      (Euclidean) distance; it ignores fire, smoke and obstacles.  No path test
      anywhere in the planner, the executor or the assign handler (WM:4891-5009).
    - Ties go to the lexicographically smaller id STRING.  At the shipped team
      sizes (at most 3 units, ff_unit_0..2) that equals numeric order; past 10
      units "ff_unit_10" < "ff_unit_2".
    - "Available" = RP:625-641 (not dead, not assigned, not route_blocked,
      snapshot "available", on grid) over WM:3578-3590 (not dead, status not in
      {dead, route_blocked}, not assigned, not exiting, not rescue_completed,
      pos not None).
    - The victim position used is the TRUE live marker cell (WM:3623-3626).
    PAPER: describe the baseline as "greedy nearest-unit dispatch on grid (L1)
    distance, blind to fire".  Never call it straight-line distance.

1.2 BRIEF: "Victims are handled ONE AT A TIME, in the order they are detected."
    VERDICT: ONE AT A TIME - CONFIRMED.  DETECTION ORDER - ONLY PARTLY.
    - Every production call passes a single victim_id (WM:3977, :5586), so the
      planner's needy list has at most one entry; each decision binds one pair
      against a fresh snapshot (sequential greedy).
    - The FIRST dispatch of a victim follows detection: victim_confirmed is
      enqueued once per victim at first detection (WM:1447-1498) and the queue
      drains FIFO (WM:4018-4025).  Within one detection pass the order is UAV
      schedule order x victim index (WM:1423-1426), not "first seen".
    - A victim whose first dispatch was a "delay" (no free unit) has NO pending
      record: delay is a no-op (RE:346-347) and victim_confirmed never repeats.
      It is retried only by _try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims
      (WM:3039-3059), which walks managed_victims in VICTIM-INDEX order
      (victim_0 first), not detection order, and only when kicked by a unit's
      recycle / off-grid return (WM:3360-3361) or a route_blocked recovery
      (WM:3210-3211).
    - So when a unit frees up, the LOWEST-INDEX waiting victim gets it.  Evidence
      cases 2 (step 51) and 3 (step 63) are exactly this (section 3).
    - Called WITHOUT a victim id the planner would also pick the lowest id
      (RP:613, :623) - no production call does that.
    PAPER: the baseline serves waiting victims in victim-index order.  Under ring
    placement the index fixes the spawn cell (WM:953-955; in D victim_0 (40,25),
    victim_1 (25,48), victim_2 (10,25), victim_3 (25,3)), so the baseline's
    re-dispatch priority is a hidden function of geometry in every ring result
    reported so far.

1.3 BRIEF: "An assignment is NEVER reconsidered: a firefighter stays bound to its
    victim even when stalled, and a free, closer firefighter is never used."
    VERDICT: CONFIRMED for stalls and for better-placed units; bindings do end
    on six other events.
    - Nothing releases or rebinds a unit for lack of progress or because a free
      unit is closer: _try_dispatch skips a victim with an active unit
      (WM:3057-3058), the planner skips it (RP:603-605), the executor refuses
      "already_assigned" (RE:384-386), the assign handler refuses to rebind a
      unit bound to another victim (WM:4913-4918).  No firefighter stall or
      oscillation detector exists (only the idle-retreat latch, AG:3185-3290).
    - Bindings DO end on: (a) the FIRST route_blocked raise on an (ff, victim)
      pair - unassign + immediate replacement (WM:3954-3985); (b) firefighter
      death (WM:5751-5797); (c) victim death and (d) rescue completion -
      _release_other_claimants (WM:3880-3951); (e) the 30-step escape sweep -
      unassign then mark unreachable (WM:5536-5576); (f) the stale-clear mode 2
      (WM:4350-4369, not the shipped mode 1).
    - A REPEATED route_blocked raise on the same pair returns early (WM:3961-3963;
      _blocked_replacement_attempted is never cleared, WM:425).  The unit stays
      bound but is hidden from _find_active_firefighter_for_victim
      (WM:4039-4041), so a later kick can bind a SECOND unit - double claims are
      permitted by design (dd0a1b9; the model-level assign accepts two live
      claimants, tests/test_phantom_rescue_release.py:138-142).  A latched unit
      does not stay latched: the tail of its own _move_toward relabels it
      "assigned" on the first move that finds a fire-free path (AG:3692-3695), so
      such a double claim becomes TWO ACTIVE binders - recorded in case 2
      (victim_3, steps 238-256, [RescueInvariant] "assigned but firefighters=
      ['ff_unit_0', 'ff_unit_1']" in its pool log).
    PAPER: "assignments are never reconsidered for progress or placement" is the
    accurate statement; "never reconsidered" alone is false (route_blocked,
    casualty and terminal releases exist).

2. OTHER FACTS THE DESIGN RESTS ON
================================================================================
2.1 FIREFIGHTER MOVEMENT IS A MEMORYLESS GREEDY MOVER (AG:3594-3696).  Each step
    it scores the 4 in-grid, non-burning neighbours in tiers: T1 improving and
    clean; T2 "maintaining" - ALWAYS EMPTY, an orthogonal step changes L1 by
    exactly +-1; T3 any clean cell - therefore always a step AWAY; T4 least risk
    (fire 1e6, fire-adjacent 100, smoke 10, AG:3091-3099).  At a barrier across
    the line to the target it falls into a two-step loop (T3 back, T1 forward)
    with a fire-free path still existing, so no route_blocked is raised.  An
    assigned unit that lands fire-adjacent does a survival retreat instead
    (AG:2867-2906).  A PICKUP happens only on a clean victim cell (the survival
    check runs before the pickup test, AG:2867 vs :2913).  Speed is 0 or 1 cell
    per step.  Units stack freely (MultiGrid, no occupancy test; docked UAVs are
    not obstacles to firefighters).
    PAPER: dispatch cannot fix the mover's livelock; this round does not change
    movement (out of scope), so the residual stall cost is a movement-layer
    limitation the paper must state.
2.2 PATH SEARCH.  _path_exists_avoiding_fire (AG:3461-3496): 4-connected BFS,
    ONLY actively burning cells impassable (burnt, smoky, fire-adjacent cells
    passable), destination counted even if burning, source never tested, returns
    a BOOL.  The mover calls it every approach step and raises route_blocked
    when it fails (AG:3612-3616, :3647-3650).  NO function returns a firefighter
    path LENGTH (_safe_path_reachable_cells WM:5265-5291 returns a set;
    exit_leg_first_step AG:2341-2366 returns a first cell).
    Against a fixed burning set AND a stationary victim, steps taken >= BFS
    length over non-burning cells (every mover refuses burning cells, moves <= 1
    cell/step): BFS length is an ADMISSIBLE (never over-estimating) travel time
    - but only a lower bound, and the greedy mover can exceed it without limit
    (2.1).  A victim fleeing toward the unit (<= 1 cell/step, within its 6-cell
    leash) can shorten it by at most its displacement.
    PAPER: a route-aware cost had to be built (none existed); the paper calls it
    "a lower bound on travel time to the victim's current cell", not an
    estimate of arrival time.
2.3 FIRE KNOWLEDGE TODAY.  Firefighters read the TRUE current burning set
    (_fire_cells scans the schedule's Fire agents, AG:3043-3053) and true
    per-cell smoke (AG:3055-3082), with no delay; the route_blocked
    revalidation (WM:3185-3195) and the escape sweep (WM:5249-5291) use the same
    truth.  Ignition and burn-out happen only on the global tick every
    FIRE_SPREAD_SPEED = 3 steps (AG:97-125); an idle unit's extinguish (shipped
    ON, AG:142-156) can end burning on ANY step; smoke on burnt cells changes
    every step.  This is covered
    only implicitly by paper assumption A3 ("fire perimeter known in near real
    time (today = truth)", outputs/fix3b_part1.txt:661), which was written for
    the search side; docs/04_clean_separation_table.md:101 even describes fire
    as beliefs.
    PAPER: the paper must state, as an assumption, that the firefighter team and
    its dispatcher see the current fire perimeter in real time (A3 extended to
    firefighters); the docs' belief wording is inconsistent with the code.
2.4 VICTIMS.  A victim dies only when its own cell is burning at the post-move
    casualty sweep (WM:5647-5737) - no smoke, timer or health.  It flees one cell
    per step when within Manhattan 3 of fire (VICTIM_FLEE_TRIGGER_DISTANCE = 3),
    within a leash of 6 cells (VICTIM_FLEE_MAX_DISPLACEMENT = 6, CFV:324-325),
    never while in custody (AG:2456-2457, :2643-2675).  The flee step maximises
    distance to fire, so it is often SIDEWAYS relative to an approaching unit.  Dispatch uses the
    detected victim's TRUE live cell and the bound unit re-targets to it every
    step (AG:2811-2850) - paper assumption A7.
2.5 BINDING STATE.  The authoritative binding is on the firefighter marker:
    assigned, rescued_victim, target_pos, exiting, exit_target,
    rescue_completed.  The victim side (rescue_assigned, status "assigned",
    firefighter_id) are mirrors; firefighter_id is never cleared (stale after
    any unassign).  Every BIND goes through the executor
    (RE:execute_physical_command -> WM:apply_physical_rescue_command); a
    completer's binding is also cleared directly by recycle (WM:2622-2628) and
    absence removal (WM:2827-2833).  The model-level assign accepts a second
    live claimant on a victim (pinned, tests/test_phantom_rescue_release.py
    :138-142); only the executor's apply_physical_pairing_decision refuses
    "already_assigned" (RE:384-386).  assign
    refuses a unit bound to a different victim (WM:4913-4918); unassign clears
    the marker binding but NOT its status (WM:5011-5080) - a released unit left
    "en_route" reads as busy (the 70e1b33 latch shape) unless relabelled, as
    _release_other_claimants does (WM:4180-4183).  reset_victim_pending relabels
    the victim "confirmed" and is meant only for a live victim (WM:5045-5073).
    PAPER: none beyond "every binding change goes through one audited command
    path"; the stale back-pointer is an implementation detail J overwrites.
2.6 _release_other_claimants (WM:4049-4222) CANNOT DO WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS OF IT
    AS WRITTEN.  It releases EVERY bound, non-dead, non-completer unit of a
    victim (keep_ff_id spares one unit only while it is exiting with no
    completer present, WM:4140-4146), assumes the victim is TERMINAL (metadata

    Section 7 resolves this (decision D-3).
    PAPER: the brief's "freed through the existing release path" needs a
    one-keyword extension of that path; the paper can say releases reuse the
    audited path.
2.7 INFORMATION AT THE DECISION LEVEL IS DETECTED-ONLY; AT THE READ LEVEL IT IS

4. THE DESIGN IN ONE PAGE
================================================================================
One pure, deterministic dispatcher J (new module src_extension/planning/
joint_dispatch.py: no model, grid or RNG access) decides every binding while
Limit 2 is on.  The model builds J's input - a detected-only view, route-aware
travel times, the current bindings and their progress state - and applies J's
output through the existing executor.  J runs at two fixed points per step,
both inside EXISTING stage methods (no stage is added; both pinned stage orders
stay untouched), never during agent advance:
  J-pre   end of WildFireModel._run_execution (WM:2355), the last pre-move
          binding opportunity before schedule.step().            FILL only.
  J-post  end of WildFireModel._sync_firefighter_marker_status (WM:5833-5836),
          after both post-move incident drains, the casualty sweep and the
          route_blocked revalidation.     PROGRESS -> FILL -> REPLACE -> FILL.
LIMIT 2 (FILL).  Free units are matched to unserved detected victims at once:
  maximise the number served, then use the fewest re-used pairs, then minimise
  the total route-aware travel time, then the longest one, then integer ids.
  Travel time = BFS distance over non-burning cells - exactly the unit's own
  route test, so J never binds a pair that test would reject on the same fire.
  Every legacy binder is closed under the switch; releases stay where they are.
LIMIT 3 (REPLACE).  A free unit with a CLEAN approach to the victim may replace
  an incumbent that is STALLED (no progress of its own for S = 10 counted steps,
  with fire changes and victim motion factored out) or that it beats by a
  MARGIN of M = 5 steps held over P = 3 consecutive evaluations.  Carriers and
  finishers are never touched.  The new unit is bound before the old one is
  released, in one frame, through _release_other_claimants (extended with a
  single-unit keyword), so a victim is never left without a unit.
NO FLIP-FLOP, BY CONSTRUCTION.  A per-run LEDGER counts binds per (unit,
  victim) pair.  A REPLACE may use only a pair never bound before; a fill binds
  only a victim that has no active unit.  Theorem (6.9): no unit can return to
  a victim, nor a victim to a unit, except after an outside fire event (a
  route_blocked raise or a death) removed or latched the unit in between; binds
  per victim are bounded; replacements per victim are at most N_ff - 1.
SWITCHES.  DISPATCH_JOINT (Limit 2) and DISPATCH_REASSIGN (Limit 3) ship 0 and
  turn on only on an exact 1; DISPATCH_REASSIGN acts only when DISPATCH_JOINT is
  on.  DISPATCH_JOINT = 0 alone restores the base commit's dispatch exactly.
NOT CHANGED: firefighter movement, the fire, victims, UAVs and searchers, the
  route_blocked raise and its handler's unassign, the latch fixes, the absence,
  the carrying leg, the escape sweep, select_rescue_assignment itself.
PAPER: one dispatcher, two switches, two fixed decision points per step, and a
proved no-reversal property - the four sentences the paper needs.

5. LIMIT 2 - JOINT, ROUTE-AWARE ASSIGNMENT
================================================================================
5.1 SCOPE.  The brief says "match all DETECTED, unrescued victims not in custody
    to all available firefighters".  Read literally, a victim already being
    approached is "unrescued and not in custody"; matching it again would be a
    reassignment with no hysteresis, contradicting Limit 3.  Available units are
    by definition unbound (WM:3578-3590).  So Limit 2 matches FREE units to
    UNSERVED victims; changing an existing binding is Limit 3's job alone
    (decision D-13).
    PAPER: Limit 2 is "joint assignment of idle units to waiting victims";
    reconsidering an assignment is a separate mechanism with its own guarantee.

5.2 THE SETS (built by the model at each J point; reads in section 9).
    F_free  units passing _firefighter_available_for_dispatch (WM:3578-3590):
            alive, status not dead/route_blocked, not assigned, not exiting,
            not rescue_completed, on grid.
    W       UNSERVED victims: id in _detected_victim_ids (confirmed union the
            runtime-record ids, local_adaptation_generator.py:1076-1092); not
            rescued, dead, cancelled or unreachable (state flags AND marker
            status - the planner's needy semantics, RP:593-602, which
            _victim_needs_rescue alone does not give, WM:3592-3609); and NO
            LIVING unit (not dead by flag or status) has rescued_victim == v
            (the _bound scan of WM:4106-4112 restricted to living units).
    W_L     LATCHED-HELD victims: detected and needy, whose only living binders
            are route_blocked units (the repeated-raise state, WM:3961-3963).
            Under Limit 2 alone they are treated as unserved and get a second
            claimant (today's dd0a1b9 design, made prompt); under Limit 3 a
            LATCH-FILL replaces the latched binder (6.6).
    Custody: a victim whose binder is exiting or co-located (agents
            Victim._in_firefighter_custody, AG:2643-2675) or rescue_completed is
            never in W or W_L.

5.3 ROUTE-AWARE TRAVEL TIME d(f, v).
    - One single-source BFS from each victim of interest (W, W_L and the
      victims of contestable bindings), 4-connected, bounds by
      grid.out_of_bounds, over cells NOT in the current burning set B (the same
      truth _fire_cells reads, AG:3043-3053).  Burnt, smoky and fire-adjacent
      cells are passable.  The victim's own cell is the source and is never
      tested (it may burn - the destination exemption of
      _path_exists_avoiding_fire, AG:3477-3491).  d(f, v) = the BFS distance at
      the unit's cell (0 if it stands on v).  Living units stand on non-burning
      cells at both J points (a unit on a burning cell dies at the post-move
      sweep, AG fire commits only inside schedule.step), so the reversed search
      equals the unit-source search of the route test.  One BFS per victim
      gives d to every unit.  No random draw.
    - EQUIVALENCE: d(f, v) < infinity  <=>  _path_exists_avoiding_fire(f, v, B).
      So J never binds a pair that the unit's own per-step route test would
      reject against the fire J saw.  (The fire may tick, or a unit may
      extinguish, between J and the next advance, exactly as today.)
    - ADMISSIBLE for a fixed burning set and a stationary victim: every mover
      refuses burning cells and moves at most one cell per step, so realised
      steps to pickup >= d + 1 (the pickup step).  A victim fleeing toward the
      unit shortens this by at most its displacement (<= 6, its leash).  It is
      a LOWER bound only: burn-out and extinguishing shorten routes, ignitions
      lengthen them (d is recomputed at every J point), and the greedy mover can
      exceed it without limit (2.1).  Limit 3's stall rule is the design's
      answer to that failure; the instrument records d at binding against
      realised steps to pickup, with its sign.
    - REJECTED: a "clean-cell" passable set (no fire-adjacent, no smoke cells -
      the mover's tiers T1-T3 and the MODE-2 carry) as the TRAVEL TIME.  It
      over-estimates where the mover takes a T4 shortcut (not admissible), and
      it would call victims unreachable that route_blocked and the escape
      sweep's geo_reachable (WM:5265-5291) call reachable - such a victim would
      wait unbound while its isolation streak keeps resetting.  (Clean cells ARE
      used, separately, to judge whether a stall can be helped - 6.3, 6.4.)
    PAPER: "route-aware travel time = shortest fire-free path length on the
    current fire, a lower bound on travel time to the victim's current cell;
    the same reachability notion the units, the route_blocked signal and the
    isolation write-off use".

5.4 THE OBJECTIVE.  Over allowed pairs (finite d, ledger rule 5.6), choose the
    matching M of F_free to W (plus W_L, 5.2) that is lexicographically best by:
      L1  maximise |M|                               (serve as many as possible)
      L2  minimise the number of RE-USED pairs (b(f, v) >= 1, 5.6)
      L3  minimise sum of d(f, v) over M             (total time to reach)
      L4  minimise max of d(f, v) over M             (worst case, tie-break)
      L5  smallest sorted list of (victim index, unit index), parsed as
          INTEGERS (removes the "ff_unit_10" < "ff_unit_2" string order, RP:693)
    WHY:
    - L1 first: no reachable victim waits while a free unit could reach it.
      This closes the missing-kick gap (1.5a) by construction.  Because L1
      precedes L2, a re-used pair can be chosen when every MAXIMUM matching
      needs one, even if some other victim has a fresh pair (free A, B; waiting
      v, w; (A,v), (A,w) fresh, (B,v) re-used, B cannot reach w: the maximum
      matching {A->w, B->v} is chosen).  That is intended: coverage first.
    - L3: the PRIMARY measure is detection-to-rescue time.  For a victim served
      now, its past wait is fixed and its pickup step and carry leg depend on
      the victim, not on which unit comes (MODE 2 carries to the nearest
      boundary from the pickup cell); the only unit-dependent part is the time
      to reach.  Minimising the total is therefore the myopic choice that
      minimises the mean - the shortest-processing-time argument for a sum of
      completion times.  With every waiting victim served (|W| <= |F_free|) L3 is
      pure pairing; on the route metric (a shortest-path metric, so the triangle
      inequality holds) it never chooses a crossed pairing that an uncrossed one
      beats.
    - L4 only breaks ties: as a primary key min-max has large plateaus (many
      matchings share the worst pair) and needs a second key anyway, and with
      more victims than units it would still pick the nearest victims.
    - The brief's two examples are L3 and L4; L3 is the one aligned with the
      primary measure (decision D-2).
    WHEN VICTIMS OUTNUMBER FREE UNITS (|W| > |F_free|) L1 fills every unit and
    L3 then serves the victims nearest BY ROUTE: easy rescues first, a far
    victim (often one on the fire side, often the critical one) waits for the
    next freed unit.  Time already waited counts for nothing.  This replaces
    today's victim-INDEX order (1.2), which is neither first-come nor nearest.
    This is a real policy choice - decision D-1:
      L3 (recommended): aligned with the primary measure; robust to 1-step
        detection jitter (the choice depends on positions, not on which victim
        was seen one step earlier - the evidence's common signature, section 3);
        shorter legs mean less exposure (the evidence's long legs were 147-273
        steps).
      FCFS (alternative): neutral between easy and critical rescues, so it does
        not pre-empt the supervisor's question (section 11); it keeps today's
        default's pairing in case 3, where L3 reproduces the pre-untune pairing
        that ended with two firefighter deaths (3, 14.8, K3).  FCFS KEY: L1, L2,
        then the lexicographically smallest sorted list of DETECTION STEPS of
        the served victims (same-step detections tie), then L3, L4, L5.  The
        detection step is recorded by the model when victim_confirmed is
        enqueued (WM:1447-1498) as a new per-victim field that the builder
        reads for detected ids only.
    PAPER: under L3 the system DOES treat easy and hard rescues differently when
    units are scarce - it serves the nearest-by-route first.  The paper must say
    so; "nothing distinguishes them" would be false after this round.

5.5 THE SOLVER.  Exhaustive enumeration of injective partial maps F_free -> W:
    at the shipped sizes at most sum_k C(3,k) P(5,k) = 1 + 15 + 60 + 60 = 136
    configurations, scored by the L1-L5 key.  Exact, deterministic, no RNG,
    under 1 ms.  The function refuses (raises ValueError, no silent fallback) if
    the configuration count exceeds 100 000 - a team size this project does not
    ship; a Hungarian encoding of L1-L5 is the documented path if teams grow.
    PAPER: "exact joint assignment by enumeration (at most 136 candidates at the
    team sizes studied)".

5.6 THE LEDGER AND ALLOWED PAIRS.  The model keeps b(f, v) = the number of
    successful binds of the pair (f, v) in this run.  It is incremented at the
    SINGLE SINK - the successful assign branch of apply_physical_rescue_command -
    whenever DISPATCH_JOINT is effective, so every bind is counted whoever
    issues it.  A pair is ALLOWED for:
      a REPLACE (Limit 3, stall or margin)  only if b(f, v) = 0     (fresh)
      a FILL of an unserved victim (W)      for any b; L2 prefers fresh pairs
      a LATCH-FILL of a W_L victim          only if b(f, v) <= 1 before it
    WHY THE FILL IS NOT CAPPED: a REPLACE uses a pair's first bind without any
    raise, so a later first route_blocked raise on that pair falls on a SECOND
    bind and unbinds; a hard cap of two binds per pair could then leave a
    reachable victim with no allowed unit while a living unit keeps it
    geo_reachable (its isolation streak resets, the escape sweep never writes
    it off) - a run that never finishes.  The no-reversal theorem does not need
    the cap (6.9); finiteness comes from the permanent
    _blocked_replacement_attempted (WM:425, :3961-3963), which lets each pair be
    UNBOUND by a raise at most once.
    WHY THE LATCH-FILL IS CAPPED: without it two units whose routes keep
    closing could alternate on a latched victim for as long as the fire keeps
    blocking them (each change driven by a repeated raise).  With it every pair
    enters a latched victim at most twice.
    WHY ALLOW RE-USE AT ALL: in case 2 victim_3 was finally rescued by a pair
    whose route had been blocked once (ff_unit_1: block 216, re-bound 237,
    rescued 257).  A one-bind rule would have lost that rescue.
    PAPER: the ledger is the anti-reversal device; its price is that a latched
    victim can wait for its latched unit when every other unit has used its two
    latch-fills - measured as ledger-blocked victim-steps (14.7 M4).

5.7 SOLVE POINTS AND WHAT REPLACES LEGACY PAIRING (DISPATCH_JOINT = 1 only).
    - The generic pairing tail of _handle_rescue_incident (WM:3976-3985) is
      skipped for every incident type that reaches it (victim_confirmed,
      route_blocked, firefighter_casualty): the handler records the victim's
      pending cause (initial / blocked / casualty) for the reason string and
      returns.  Every state change BEFORE that tail is kept: the route_blocked
      first-raise unassign with reset_victim_pending (WM:3965-3974) and its
      repeated-pair early return (WM:3961-3963); the victim_dead and
      rescue_complete releases (WM:3880-3951) are separate branches, unchanged.
    - _try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims (WM:3039-3059) returns at once;
      both its callers (WM:3211, :3361) run earlier in the same post-move cycle
      as J-post.
    - _dispatch_firefighter_to_victim (WM:5578-5593) returns without binding,
      which also closes the dormant _sync_victim_agent_status fallback
      (WM:6036-6047, off by _allow_sync_victim_dispatch_fallback = False, WM:441)
      and the test-only helpers - so J is the only binder even if that flag is
      ever set.  (RescueExecutor.execute_physical_dispatch has no caller; the
      sink-counted ledger would still count it.)
    - So no pairing happens mid-advance any more (P2 in 1.4): the route_blocked
      unassign stays inline in the blocked unit's own move (the 808 mechanism -
      the unassign in the raise's call stack - is untouched), and the

    - TIMING versus today: identical for post-move detection, casualty

    PAPER: dispatch decisions move out of the agents' move phase into two fixed
    points; the only timing cost is one step for some route_blocked
    replacements.

5.8 EVENTS (the brief's list, made exact).  J-pre and J-post do not trust an
    event list for completeness: each evaluates the precondition
      PRE = (F_free non-empty AND W union W_L non-empty)
            OR (Limit 3 on AND a contestable binding exists, 6.2)
    and solves whenever PRE holds (cost O(units + victims) when it does not).
    The events that make PRE true, with where they occur, are:
      E1 new detection - victim_confirmed (AM:101 at step 1; AM:171 after).
      E2 unit freed - off-grid return or recycle (AM:144, WM:2951-3037);
         release on victim death (_release_other_claimants, WM:3891, inside the

         completion (WM:3914, :3948); revalidation recovery (WM:3199, AM:184);
         escape-sweep unassign (WM:5537-5551, after the cycle -> next J-pre);
         D6 relabel / stale clear (WM:4184-4205, :4299-4385); a Limit 3 release.
      E3 victim loses its unit - first route_blocked raise of its unit,
         including the HOLD-0 carrier drop (mid-advance; J-post same step); its

      E4 route change - the burning set changes on a fire tick (every 3 steps)
         or by an extinguish on any step.  For a BOUND unit "a route closing" is
         already the unit's own route test failing in its move = E3; no new
         detector is added.  For free units E4 can make a waiting victim
         reachable; PRE is re-evaluated every step, so it is caught at the next
         J point.
      E5 (Limit 3) incumbent progress state - a stall count reaching S, a margin

      killed" frees nothing - the event is that its victim needs a unit (E3).
    PAPER: dispatch becomes "evaluated at two fixed points per step, acting
    whenever an idle unit and a reachable waiting victim coexist" - not

5.9 UNMATCHED VICTIMS WAIT; DISPATCH WRITES NO VICTIM OFF.  A victim in W with no
    allowed pair stays waiting (today's "delay", but now re-evaluated at every J
    point).  J issues only assign and unassign commands - never
    mark_unreachable, and it never calls select_rescue_assignment, so no reason
    string can reach the planner's empty-pool write-off (RP:665-666).
    CONSEQUENCE (decision D-6): today's casualty-replacement write-off - a

    not happen under Limit 2; the victim waits.  The escape sweep is unchanged:
    a victim no living non-carrier unit can reach still accumulates its 30-step
    isolation streak and is written off "no_firefighter_available" or
    "geographically_isolated" (RP:468-563), so a team that is all dead or cut
    off still terminates.  The instrument counts each would-have-been write-off
    so the report can attribute its effect.
    Also changed by Limit 2 (structural, predicted): an assignment into a closed
    route (the rb campaign's assign_into_blocked_route) cannot happen at the
    moment of assignment; today the planner sends the Manhattan-nearest unit,
    which raises route_blocked on its next move.
    PAPER: under Limit 2 dispatch never gives a victim up; only the isolation
    sweep does, and only for a victim no living unit can reach; "unreachable"
    counts then mean isolation write-offs only.

    PAPER: dispatch-count tables gain one row, "reassignment"; the existing
    rows keep their meaning.

================================================================================
6. LIMIT 3 - REASSIGNMENT, HYSTERESIS, AND THE NO-FLIP-FLOP PROOF
================================================================================
6.1 SCOPE.  An incumbent unit A bound to victim v may be replaced by a FREE unit
    B (one in F_free left after the fill at J-post - a "spare", which by L1 can
    reach no waiting victim).  Not in this round (decision D-14):
    - CROSS-SWAPS (two bound units exchanging victims in one decision): outside
      the brief's two triggers and no evidence case needs one.
    - PRE-EMPTION (taking a unit off one victim to serve another): it leaves a
      victim uncovered; case 1's step-16 flip would need it and therefore stays
      unfixed.
    - pulling a unit that a waiting victim needs: impossible, spares are by
      construction units no waiting victim can use.
    A CARRIER IS NEVER REASSIGNED: exiting, rescue_completed, off-grid and dead
    units are never incumbents, challengers or donors (an unassign of a carrier
    clears exiting and drops the victim, WM:5021-5025 - the HOLD-0 drop shape).
    PAPER: "reassignment = an idle unit takes over from a stalled or clearly
    worse-placed one; no two bound units exchange victims in one decision (over
    several decisions an idle unit can take over a victim another unit was
    freed from); a carried victim is never touched".

6.2 CONTESTABLE BINDING.  (A, v) is contestable at J-post iff v is detected and
    needy, A is v's only living binder, A is alive, on grid, not exiting, not
    rescue_completed, NOT on v's cell (d(A, v) > 0: a co-located binder is
    custody, pickup on its next advance), and A's status is en_route/assigned
    (an ACTIVE incumbent).  A route_blocked binder is a LATCHED incumbent and is
    handled by the fill (6.6), not here.

6.3 PROGRESS AND STALL STATE (new model state per binding, reset at every bind).
    The rule must credit the unit only for its OWN movement: a fire change
    (either sign, on a tick or by an extinguish) and a victim move (in any
    direction - the flee step is often sideways) must neither count as progress
    nor as stalling.  At each J-post, for each contestable (A, v):
      D_t   = the BFS distances from v's CURRENT cell over the CURRENT burning
              set (the same BFS 5.3 runs - no extra search);
      d_t   = D_t(A_t)           (A's current cell)
      x_t   = D_t(A_{t-1})       (A's PREVIOUS cell, same fire, same victim cell)
      e_t   = x_t - d_{t-1}      the change NOT caused by A: fire and victim
                                 motion measured at A's old cell
      best  <- best + e_t        shift the reference by the exogenous change
      if d_t < best : PROGRESS   best <- d_t ; k <- 0
      else          :            k <- k + 1  (unless FROZEN, below)
      d_t = 0 resets k (A reached the cell; 6.2 excludes it next frame).
      If d_t or x_t is infinite (A's route is closed on this fire), the state is
      frozen and the binding is not evaluated this frame; the unit's own route
      test raises route_blocked at its next move (E3).
    A two-cell loop alternates d, d+1 with e = 0 and never beats best: k grows by
    1 per step.  A loop beside a front that adds +4 every tick: e = +4 on the
    tick, best shifts with it, k still grows.  A victim drifting sideways: x_t
    absorbs the drift, k still grows.  Burn-out shortening the route: e < 0,
    best falls with it, no false progress.  (The model's existing "approaching"
    flag, WM:5470-5484, reads a loop as approaching every other step and is not
    used.)
    FROZEN: k does not advance on a step at which NO unit (incumbent or spare)
    has a CLEAN APPROACH to v.  CLEAN(c) = not burning, not smoky and not
    4-adjacent to a burning cell - the negation of the assigned-unit survival
    trigger (AG:3110-3132; pickup happens only after that check passes, AG:2867
    vs :2913).  CLEAN APPROACH(u, v) = v's cell is CLEAN and a 4-connected path
    over CLEAN cells leads from u's cell (exempt as the source) to v.  One extra
    BFS per contestable victim, from v over CLEAN cells, decides it for every
    unit at once.  When no unit has a clean approach (v's cell unclean, every
    neighbour unclean, or an unclean band separating v from every unit) no unit
    could pick v up, so replacing A cannot help and A is not blamed.  When some
    spare has a clean approach but A does not, k counts: A's position is the
    problem.

6.4 STALL RULE.  A is STALLED when k >= S, S = DISPATCH_STALL_STEPS = 10.
    A stalled A is replaced by a spare B iff ALL hold:
        (B, v) is fresh (b = 0);
        B has a CLEAN APPROACH to v (6.3) - this also screens out a challenger
          that would livelock at the same barrier;
        d(B, v) < d(A, v) + k.
    The stalled unit is charged the time it has demonstrably lost: its true
    remaining time is at least its route distance plus an unknown non-negative
    extra, and k counted steps without progress are evidence of at least k.  A
    spare farther than that is not expected to arrive sooner and is not sent.
    A stall with no qualifying spare releases nothing: the unit keeps the
    victim (a victim never loses its unit to nobody).
    WHY S = 10.
    - LOWER BOUND: with fire changes and victim motion factored out (6.3) and
      unhelpable steps frozen, the no-progress spans that normal movement still
      produces are one T3-then-T1 out-and-back (2 steps), one survival
      out-and-back (2) and the pickup step (1) - at most about 5 in a row.
      S = 10 leaves a factor of two.
    - UPPER BOUND: the approach-leg two-cell episodes in the evidence lasted
      10-13 steps in cases 1, 2 and the ut0r2 arm of case 3 (139-150, 168-180,
      182-191), and route_blocked came late (case 2: 184, death 201).  S = 10
      acts at the end of the first such episode.  In ut2r2 the loop lasted 6
      steps (150-155) and ended in route_blocked, so S = 10 would NOT have acted
      there; route_blocked did.
    - S = 10 equals the project's firefighter-oscillation window (10 steps).
    These numbers come from the diagnosed mechanisms and the recorded
    trajectories, NOT from any screen outcome; S is never tuned on Part 3.
    PAPER: "a stall = 10 steps in which the unit's own moves bring it no closer,
    with fire and victim motion factored out, while some unit could approach the
    victim cleanly".

6.5 MARGIN RULE.  A non-stalled contestable A is replaced by a spare B iff, at
    each of the last P = 3 consecutive J-post evaluations,
        d(B, v) + M <= d(A, v),   M = DISPATCH_MARGIN_STEPS = 5,
    with (B, v) fresh.  The persistence counter is kept per (v, B); it resets
    whenever v's binder changes and whenever B is not a spare at an evaluation,
    so a binding younger than P steps can never be margin-replaced (the
    COMMITMENT period).
    WHY M = 5: in one step motion alone can move the gap d(A,v) - d(B,v) against
    the challenger by at most 4 (the victim moves one cell: +-1 on each
    distance; A advances one: -1; B is pushed back one: +1).  A replacement
    decided at J-post of step t first acts at the advance of t+1, so M = 4 + 1
    keeps it strictly better after one step of worst-case adverse motion.
    WHY P = 3 = FIRE_SPREAD_SPEED: the advantage must survive one full fire
    period of unit and victim motion.  A route lengthened BY FIRE persists (fire
    does not un-burn within 3 steps), so it will trigger a replacement once it
    has lasted P evaluations - intended: A's route really got longer.
    CONSISTENCY CHECK (not tuning): the evidence's near-ties do not qualify
    (case 1 step 99: Manhattan 39 vs 40; case 3 step 131: 29 vs 31); case 1's
    gap at step 120 (28 vs 37, about 9) would qualify if route distances track
    Manhattan and it persists 3 steps.
    PAPER: "a reassignment needs the newcomer to be at least 5 steps closer by
    route for 3 consecutive steps, or the incumbent to be stalled".

6.6 LATCHED INCUMBENT (route_blocked but still bound, the repeated-raise state).
    It found no fire-free path at its most recent move; its status clears itself
    on the first move that finds one (AG:3692-3695), and it is invisible to
    _find_active_firefighter_for_victim (WM:4039-4041).  Its victim is in W_L.
    Under Limit 3 the FILL may give a W_L victim a free unit B with
    b(B, v) <= 1 before the bind - a LATCH-FILL - executed as an atomic
    replacement (7.1, reason joint_replace_latched) so the victim ends with ONE
    binder.  The released latched unit becomes an UNASSIGNED route_blocked unit -
    exactly the population 70e1b33's revalidation clears (WM:3101-3109) - so no
    new latch shape is created.  With no allowed free unit the latched unit is
    kept, and may recover by itself.  Under Limit 2 alone W_L victims get a
    second claimant instead (today's dd0a1b9 hedge, made prompt), and a
    recovering latched unit then makes TWO ACTIVE binders, as today (1.3) -
    decision D-7.
    PAPER: in the full design no victim has both a latched and an active unit;
    that double claim is a property of the baseline (and of Limit 2 alone).

6.7 ORDER INSIDE J-POST AND PER-FRAME LIMITS.
    1 PROGRESS: update (best, k, d_prev, A_prev, persistence) for every
      contestable binding; compute CLEAN APPROACH where needed.
    2 FILL: F_free x (W union W_L), objective 5.4; under Limit 3 a W_L bind is a
      LATCH-FILL, under Limit 2 alone a second claim.
    3 REPLACE: spares left after step 2 against stalled incumbents first (largest
      k, then largest d(A,v) - d(B,v), then integer ids), then margin
      incumbents (largest gain, then integer ids).  At most one replacement per
      victim and per spare in a frame.
    4 SECOND FILL: units released in step 3 x victims still in W (a released unit
      may reach a victim nobody else could), with the ledger rules.
    Every unit is bound at most once per frame and every victim changes binder
    at most once per frame.  J-pre runs step 2 only.

6.8 HYSTERESIS / COMMITMENT, IN ONE LIST.
    (a) the incumbent is protected by M = 5 steps AND P = 3 evaluations
        (margin), or by S = 10 steps without own progress plus the lost-time
        charge and the clean-approach test (stall);
    (b) a binding younger than P steps cannot be margin-replaced;
    (c) a REPLACE may only use a pair NEVER bound before (ledger b = 0);
    (d) a FILL binds only a victim with no living binder (W); a LATCH-FILL only a
        victim whose living binders are all latched, with b <= 1 before it;
    (e) at most one change per victim per frame;
    (f) Limit 2 alone never unbinds anyone; with Limit 3 the only J unbinds are
        REPLACEs and LATCH-FILLs.
    The anti-reversal guarantee rests on (c), (d) and (f), not on the size of M
    or S.  M, S and P decide how OFTEN replacements happen, never whether they
    can reverse.

6.9 PROOF THAT NO ASSIGNMENT CAN FLIP BACK AND FORTH.
    Setting: DISPATCH_JOINT = 1, so J is the only binder (5.7) and every bind is
    counted (5.6).  A victim's BINDER SEQUENCE lists the units bound to it in
    order; a unit's VICTIM SEQUENCE likewise.  THREE BIND KINDS: FILL (v in W),
    LATCH-FILL (v in W_L; the latched binder is released in the same frame),
    REPLACE (stall or margin; the active binder is released in the same frame).
    OUTSIDE EVENTS - the ones the dispatcher does not choose: (o1) a binder's
    FIRST route_blocked raise on that pair (the handler's unassign); (o2) its
    REPEATED raise (it stays bound, status route_blocked = latched); (o3) its
    death; (o4) the victim turning terminal (rescued, dead, unreachable - which
    ends the victim's sequence).  Firefighting writes and fire ticks only cause
    these through the units' own route tests.
    INVARIANT L: every bind of (f, v) increments b(f, v); b never decreases.
    LEMMA 1 (REPLACEs bounded).  REPLACEs on a victim v number at most N_ff - 1:
      each uses a pair (B, v) with b = 0 and raises it to 1; v's first bind
      already used one such pair; there are N_ff pairs (., v).
    LEMMA 2 (binds bounded).  v becomes unbound only by (o1) - at most once per
      pair, because _blocked_replacement_attempted is permanent (WM:425,
      :3961-3963), so at most N_ff times - or (o3), at most N_ff times; a REPLACE
      never unbinds v (Lemma 3).  So FILLs of v number at most 1 + 2 N_ff.
      LATCH-FILLs of v number at most 2 N_ff (each raises some b(B, v) from 0 or
      1).  So binds of v <= 1 + 2 N_ff + (N_ff - 1) + 2 N_ff = 5 N_ff (15 in
      A-C, 10 in D) and a run is finite in binds.
    LEMMA 3 (coverage).  A REPLACE or LATCH-FILL binds the new unit before it
      releases the old one, in one frame (7.1), so it never leaves v without a
      binder; a FILL binds only a victim with no living binder.
    THEOREM (no reversal by a cost decision).  Suppose v's binder sequence
    contains A, then later another unit, then A again (A ... X ... A), and let X
    be the binder immediately before A's return.
    (i) A's return has b(A, v) >= 1 before it, so it is not a REPLACE; it is a
        FILL or a LATCH-FILL.
    (ii) A FILL needed v in W: by Lemma 3 no J decision removed X, so X was
        removed by an outside event (o1) or (o3).  A LATCH-FILL needed v in W_L:
        X had latched by its own repeated raise (o2).
    (iii) Hence every return of a unit to a victim is preceded by an outside
        fire event that removed or latched the intermediate binder.  No
        comparison of costs can ever send a unit back.
    The same argument holds for a unit's victim sequence f: v ... w ... v, since
    a unit changes victim only through a release followed by a FILL or
    LATCH-FILL of another victim, and returning to v requires v to be in W or
    W_L again - i.e. v's binder at that time was removed or latched by an
    outside event.
    REVERSALS THAT REMAIN POSSIBLE (bounded by Lemma 2, none chosen on cost):
      R1 A -> (A raises, o1) -> B -> (B raises or dies) -> A again by FILL once
         A's route has reopened (revalidation cleared it).
      R2 A -> (replaced by B, Limit 3) -> (B raises or dies) -> A by FILL.
      R3 A latched (o2) -> LATCH-FILL B -> (B raises or latches) -> A by FILL or
         LATCH-FILL.
      R4 a unit v1 -> v2 -> v1 through outside losses and fills.
    IMPOSSIBLE: REPLACE A->B followed by REPLACE B->A; any return whose
    intermediate binder was removed only by J; a FILL onto a victim that has an
    active binder; a LATCH-FILL bringing in a pair for the third time.
    Under DISPATCH_JOINT = 0 none of this applies and today's dispatch runs
    unchanged (today's reversals through route_blocked recovery and the
    _try_dispatch kick remain possible exactly as now).
    PAPER: the anti-ping-pong property is a theorem about the dispatcher, not a
    tuning outcome; the paper can state it with its proof and its exact
    exceptions (returns forced by fire events, finitely many per victim).

6.10 WHAT LIMIT 3 CANNOT FIX.
    - A challenger uses the same greedy mover and can still livelock (the
      clean-approach test only removes the obvious cases); the ledger forbids a
      third REPLACE on the same pair.  Cured only by a movement change (out of
      scope).
    - Pre-emption: a unit bound to a far victim when a nearer victim is detected
      a few steps later (case 1, steps 16/21) keeps its victim (D-14).
    - When every unit is stalled at once there is no spare (case 2, 121-200).
    PAPER: the reachable gain is bounded by the mover and by team size; the
    paper must not present the dispatch fix as a cure for movement livelock.

7. RELEASE AND REBIND MECHANICS; BINDING INVARIANTS
================================================================================
7.1 ATOMIC REPLACEMENT (A -> B on v; REPLACE or LATCH-FILL), one call frame, no
    movement in between:
    1 PRE-CHECK every executor condition: B passes the free predicate, is not
      route_blocked and is unbound; v is detected and needy; A is v's binder and
      not exiting / rescue_completed / dead; (B, v) allowed by the ledger.
    2 ASSIGN B -> v FIRST, through RescueExecutor.execute_physical_command
      (action assign, the reason of 5.10).  The model's assign checks only the
      unit's own binding (WM:4908-4918) and accepts a second live claimant on a
      victim (pinned, tests/test_phantom_rescue_release.py:138-142), so it
      succeeds while A is still bound; it overwrites every victim-side field,
      including firefighter_id = B (fixing the stale back-pointer, 2.5).  The
      planner-level already_assigned guard of apply_physical_pairing_decision
      (RE:384-386) is deliberately not used here: J has validated the pair.  If
      the assign is refused, NOTHING has changed and the replacement aborts
      (failure-atomic; logged, counted).
    3 RELEASE A through _release_other_claimants(vid, victim_marker, keep_ff_id
      = "", reason = <the same reason>, only_ff_id = A) - the EXISTING release
      path, extended with one keyword (decision D-3).  With only_ff_id set its
      loop considers only that unit; everything else is today's body: the
      executor unassign with metadata {} (correct here: v now belongs to B, and
      reset_victim_pending would relabel it "confirmed" over B's binding,
      WM:5045-5072), the en_route/assigned -> "available" relabel and knowledge
      sync (WM:4180-4183; without it A reads busy - the 70e1b33 latch shape),
      the D6 branch for a route_blocked A (work_left is True because v is live
      and detected, so the flag is left for the revalidation, WM:4184-4205), and
      the release counters (WM:4211-4221).  Its default (only_ff_id = "")
      reproduces today's three call sites exactly.
    4 STATE: the new binding (B, v) starts with best = d(B, v), k = 0, d_prev =
      d(B, v), A_prev = B's cell, persistence counters cleared (the same
      initialisation for every bind, fills included).
    The transient two-binder state exists only inside this frame; the invariant
    checker (AM:186) runs after J-post.

7.2 WHY NOT THE ALTERNATIVES.
    - _release_other_claimants AS WRITTEN releases every non-completer binder and
      would release B too; it cannot name one unit (2.6).
    - RELEASE FIRST, THEN ASSIGN (with rollback) leaves v unbound inside the
      frame and needs reset_victim_pending; assign-first never does.
    - A brand-new primitive would duplicate the release body; the brief asks for
      the existing path.
    PAPER: releases reuse the existing audited path; nothing new to state.

7.3 FILL BINDS go through RescueExecutor.apply_physical_pairing_decision with a
    RescueDecision(assign) built by J (selected_option_id "joint_pairing").  Its
    guards (needs-rescue, already_assigned) pass because v has no active binder.
    Under Limit 2 alone a W_L second claim passes too (the latched binder is
    invisible to the guard, WM:4039-4041).  Under Limit 3 a W_L bind is a
    LATCH-FILL (7.1).

7.4 BINDING INVARIANTS (checked at every frame boundary in tests and by the
    instrument, with BOTH switches on unless stated):
    I1 at most one ACTIVE (non-route_blocked, alive, assigned) binder per victim.
       (NOT under Limit 2 alone, nor today: a latched second claimant recovers
       into an active one, 1.3, 6.6.)
    I2 every unit is bound to at most one victim (assign refuses a rebind,
       WM:4913-4918) - in every configuration.
    I3 NO DISPATCH ABANDONMENT: no J action leaves a victim that had a living
       binder before the frame without one after it - DISPATCH_JOINT = 1.
    I4 FULL FILL: at the end of each J call no free unit and unserved victim form
       an allowed pair with finite d (follows from L1) - DISPATCH_JOINT = 1.
    I5 LEDGER: every REPLACE (reasons reassign_stall / reassign_margin) bound a
       pair with b = 0 before it; every LATCH-FILL (joint_replace_latched) a pair
       with b <= 1 before it.
    I6 CUSTODY: no exiting / rescue_completed / co-located unit was ever a J donor
       or target.
    I7 no victim ends a frame with a latched binder AND an active binder.

7.5 "NO VICTIM BOUND TO TWO UNITS, AND NONE TO NONE" - what the design
    guarantees and what it does not.
    - Two units: with BOTH switches on, I1 and I7 hold because J is the only
      binder, fills only victims with no living binder, and resolves a W_L
      victim by an atomic replacement.  It does NOT hold today or under Limit 2
      alone: a latched binder relabels itself "assigned" on its first move with a
      fire-free path (AG:3692-3695), so a second claimant makes two ACTIVE
      binders (case 2, victim_3, steps 238-256).
    - None: I3 - no DISPATCH decision ever strips a victim of its unit.  A victim
      can still be without a unit because no unit can reach it, all are busy,
      or every allowed pair is used; those are waits, measured (14.6 G-B f,
      14.7 M3, M4), not abandonments.
    PAPER: "no victim is ever left with two active units or stripped of its unit
    by a dispatch decision" - true of the full design, with the proof above.

7.6 UNCHANGED, DELIBERATELY.  _blocked_replacement_attempted stays permanent
    (Lemma 2's bound depends on it).  select_rescue_assignment, the route_blocked
    handler's unassign, the casualty sweep's unassign with reset_victim_pending,
    and the escape sweep are untouched; apply_physical_rescue_command gains one
    gated line (the ledger increment, 5.6) and nothing else.
    VictimState.firefighter_id is not cleared on unassign (every J bind
    overwrites it).

11. DESIGN QUESTION ONLY - CRITICAL VICTIMS (NOT IN THE DESIGN)
================================================================================
11.1 TODAY: nothing distinguishes an easy rescue from a critical one; dispatch
     is nearest-unit for whichever victim's incident is handled, and waiting
     victims go in index order.  UNDER THIS DESIGN (L3, decision D-1) the
     system serves the nearest-by-route victim first when units are scarce: it
     favours EASY rescues.  Any urgency term would act on exactly that choice.
11.2 THE ESTIMATE.  Urgency(v) = T_fire(v), the estimated time until the fire
     reaches the victim's cell.  The Bayesian round's fire_arrival_estimate T(c)
     (operator-side elliptical Huygens envelope over the BELIEVED burning cells
     and the constant wind; literature parameters; no random draws; never reads
     a simulator fire constant) is the natural source; it exists only after that
     round merges.  A cheaper proxy is the Manhattan distance to the nearest
     burning cell (Victim._min_fire_distance, AG:2691).
11.3 HOW IT COULD ENTER THE OBJECTIVE (forms, least to most invasive):
     U1 ON-TIME COUNT: a level between L1 and L3 - maximise the number of matched
        victims with d(f, v) + 1 < T_fire(v) (reachable before the fire, by the
        estimate).  Changes which victims are served first when scarce;
        pairing otherwise unchanged.
     U2 WEIGHTED TIME: replace L3 by sum of w(v) x d(f, v), w(v) = 1 + kappa x
        exp(-T_fire(v) / tau) (the front-priority weight form, with the
        Bayesian round's fixed kappa, tau).  Changes pairing too.
     U3 EARLIEST DEADLINE FIRST: when scarce, serve victims in increasing slack
        T_fire(v) - min_f d(f, v).
     U4 TRIAGE: deprioritise (never write off) victims with negative slack for
        every unit.
11.4 COST.  Computation: T at most 5 detected cells per J call - about a
     millisecond, or free if the search module already holds the envelope that
     step.  Matching size unchanged.  Methodological cost: a new arm and its own
     decision rule; its parameters are the Bayesian round's, validated for a
     search weight, NOT for a per-victim deadline.
11.5 RISKS.
     - Model mismatch: this fire ticks every 3 steps (AG:97-125), biases only
       exactly-in-line upwind sources (MU 0.9, CFV:67; AG:250-266), spreads with
       distance^-2 weights within Moore radius 3 (AG:29, :53-86) and can jump up
       to 3 cells per tick downwind; an elliptical envelope with literature
       parameters will mis-rank victims - the Bayesian round already measures
       its estimate as late by a median of about 50 steps.
     - Victims flee within Manhattan 3 of fire (leash 6), so T at the current
       cell understates survival time and keeps moving.
     - A fire-adjacent or smoky victim cannot be picked up (2.1): urgency sends
       units to wait beside the front.  Critical victims sit where units die:
       firefighter deaths in the evidence came en route on long legs, on a fresh
       assignment, and after a return (section 3).
     - Triage (U4) is ethically loaded and is a new give-up path; the paper would
       have to state the triage policy.
     - Instability: urgency moves every fire tick; it must NEVER enter Limit 3's
       rules, or it becomes a new reason to reverse assignments.
     - The primary measure can worsen while deaths fall (a critical victim's
       rescue takes longer): rescued, dead and firefighter deaths would have to
       be primary for that arm.
11.6 RECOMMENDATION.  Not in this round.  If D-11 approves, Part 3 classifies,
     POST HOC and without any behaviour change, each death of a detected victim
     by the TRUE arrival - the death step itself: FEASIBLE-CRITICAL if at
     detection some living unit had d + 1 <= (death step - detection step), else
     FUTILE.  The estimator's T_fire at detection is recorded alongside (on a
     copy, never through the search module's cache) only to report its bias.  If
     feasible-critical deaths are at least 25% of detected-victim deaths on BOTH
     seed sets, propose an urgency round using U1 in the fill only, never in
     Limit 3, no triage, its own switch and arm, firefighter deaths as a primary
     guard.
PAPER: until ruled, the paper states that the dispatcher has no urgency notion
and, under L3, favours the nearest rescue when units are scarce; the record-only
classification gives the supervisor's question a measured answer.

            living binders of any status; (b) victim-steps with >= 2 ACTIVE
            binders - structural 0 in dp1, dp0 reported (today's latched second
            claim can recover into two active binders, 1.3); (c) dispatch
            abandonment I3 - 0 in dp1; (d) captured RescueInvariant lines - must
            not rise (not structural: a kept latched-only binder prints
            "assigned but firefighters=[]"); (e) inline_violations; (f)
            "abandoned waiting": victim-steps where a detected needy victim has
            no ACTIVE binder while a free unit has a finite d to it - split into
            ledger-allowed (structural 0 in dp1, I4) and ledger-blocked (must
            not rise).
     G-T    reassignment flip-flops: (a) RETURNS (A..X..A on a victim, v..w..v on
            a unit) whose intermediate binder X (for a unit sequence: v's binder
            at the time of the return) had NO outside event before the return -
            outside event = a route_blocked-handler unassign of X, X observed
            route_blocked while still bound in the per-step binder rows, or X's
            death - 0 in dp1 (the theorem); (b) all RETURNS of any cause - must
            not rise; (c) ledger violations - a reassign_stall / reassign_margin
            bind on a pair with b >= 1, a joint_replace_latched bind on a pair
            with b >= 2 - 0.
     G-RB   route_blocked gate (scenario D, 240 steps, shards a/b/c/s: 18 (wind,
            seed) runs over 13 distinct seeds; outputs/_ffr_rbgate.sh lines run
            through the pool, _rblatch_campaign2.py from each checkout).  The
            campaign has NO CRN path: the shards run CRN OFF, so per-seed signs
            are partly noise once the fire stream diverges.  Rule (decision
            D-15): RECOMMENDED - pooled rescued over the 18 runs in dpG1 >= dpGR,
            0 latched, and every per-seed loss diagnosed from the campaign's
            assigns/fires/recoveries/deaths lists (a loss whose victim saw no
            dispatch decision differing from dpGR before the fire stream first
            diverged is reported as noise); ALTERNATIVE - the inherited rule, any
            NEW per-seed loss fails.
     G-S    full suite at the final source on an idle machine: exactly B's
            recorded failing set (expected {T5}; C10).
     G-INV  T-INV and the unit invariants green.
     PAPER: firefighter oscillation and binding integrity become reported safety
     outcomes for the first time (D-9); the route_blocked gate is a CRN-off
     per-seed regression screen.
14.7 MEASURES (reported; the decision rule in section 15 uses M1, M1c, M2, M7).
     M1 PRIMARY - detection-to-rescue time: for each victim rescued in BOTH arms
        (paired by set, placement, scenario, wind, victim id - same spawn, same
        CRN fire draws), delta = (rescue - detection) in dp1 minus in dp0.  For
        each cell c with at least one such victim, m_c = the mean delta over
        them.  T(set) = the UNWEIGHTED mean of m_c over that set's contributing
        cells; T(pooled) likewise over both sets.  Cells without a pair are
        listed and excluded.  Also reported: victim-pooled mean, median,
        per placement, per scenario, and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the m_c
        (reported, not gated).
     M1c CENSORED, all detected victims: (rescue step, or 361 if not rescued by
        360) - detection, paired the same way, aggregated by the same per-cell
        statistic - the guard against "faster on the easy victims while losing
        the hard ones".  Victims detected in only one arm are listed and
        excluded from pairing.
     M2 rescued, dead, firefighter deaths, never_detected, unreachable by cause.
     M3 firefighter-idle steps while a detected victim waits, sampled as G-B(f):
        (a) UNIT-steps free (dispatch predicate) while a detected needy victim

        counterfactual = the legacy select applied in victim-index order to the

     M5 latency decomposition: detection -> assignment -> pickup -> rescue;
        realised steps to pickup minus d at binding, with its sign.
     M6 escape-sweep write-offs, would-have-been casualty write-offs,
        assignments into a closed route (instrument BFS verdict at assign;
        expected 0 in dp1).
     M7 per-step dispatch cost: per run R = sum of J ms / (wall_s x 1000); J ms

     that carry it listed - the paper reports it with its per-set values.

17. RISKS
================================================================================
  K1 BFS is optimistic exactly where the mover livelocks; J may mis-rank units
     where routes bend.  Measured (M5); the stall rule bounds the damage.
  K2 Easy-first (L3) defers far, often critical, victims; dead or censored time
     could rise.  Guarded by S4 and S5; D-1 offers FCFS.
  K3 Case 3 at the defaults moves toward the pre-untune pairing that cost two
     firefighter deaths; firefighter deaths could rise.  Guarded by S5.
  K4 Firefighter oscillation is gated for the first time; Limit 2 sends units on
     different routes and could raise a positional count with no defect - a false
     FAIL is possible and is accepted (the brief: none may rise).
  K5 Dropping the casualty write-off (D-6) keeps victims needy longer; a risk for
     G-N, bounded by the escape sweep.
  K6 More escape-sweep write-offs (unreachable victims no longer get futile
     bindings that reset their streak); "unreachable" then counts isolation
     write-offs only.
  K7 A latched victim can wait for its latched unit when every other unit has
     used its two latch-fills (ledger-blocked, M3/M4).
  K8 A spare taken by Limit 3 stops fighting fire; the fire responds to
     firefighter writes even under CRN.
  K9 A higher-index route_blocked replacement acts one step later.
  K10 Instrument blind spots (by-name imports, stderr) - addressed in 14.4; an
     unaddressed one would bias G-B.
  K11 The clean-approach test reads true smoke centrally (9.2); a belief-based
     smoke picture would make it more conservative.
  K12 The route_blocked gate runs CRN off; per-seed losses can be noise (D-15).
PAPER: K2, K3 and K6 change what the paper's headline counts mean and must be
discussed with the results; the others are engineering risks.

19. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER - SUMMARY
================================================================================
  - The baseline must be described as it is: greedy nearest-unit dispatch on
    grid (L1) distance, blind to fire, one victim at a time, waiting victims in
    victim-INDEX order (a hidden function of ring geometry), no reconsideration
    for progress or placement, six decision points, two gaps (waiting victims
    not re-offered to some freed units; an immediate casualty write-off), and
    double claims that can become two active units.  (1.1-1.5)
  - The earlier dispatch-reachability null (outputs/dispatchreach_report.txt)
    was scenario D only, binary reachability, at commitment, at c4d5a25, with a
    pool of at most 2; it does not cover joint pairing or reconsideration and
    does not transfer to A-C, where the pool is 3.  (1.5b)
  - The evidence shows a mechanism (pairing flips on 1-5 step detection shifts;
    livelocked approaches with an idle unit available), not an effect size;
    cases 1-2 are CRN-off pre-untune runs; case 3's firefighter deaths belong to
    the pre-untune arm.  Only the screen sizes it.  (3)
  - Properties the paper can state with proofs and tests: no reassignment can
    reverse except after a fire event removed or latched the intermediate unit;
    binds per victim are bounded; no dispatch decision strips a victim of its
    unit; in the full design no victim has two active units; carried victims are
    never touched; the dispatcher reads only the detection flag of an
    undetected victim.  (6.9, 7.4, 9.1)
  - Under L3 the system favours the nearest rescue when units are scarce; the
    paper must say so, and the critical-victim question stays open (or is
    answered by the post-hoc classification).  (5.4, 11)
  - The fire-knowledge assumption (A3 for firefighters and dispatch, including
    smoke) must be stated.  (9.2)
  - The fix is bounded by the greedy mover and by team size; it is not a cure
    for movement livelock, and pre-emption is out of scope.  (6.10)
  - Every headline number measured before this round is at the old dispatch;
    if the switches flip, earlier dispatch-dependent numbers become references
    only, as at every previous flip.
