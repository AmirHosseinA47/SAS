0. METHOD, AND WHY NO RUN WAS NEEDED
================================================================================

0.1 THE RECONSTRUCTION
The harness records, per run, `burn_intervals` (per cell, the half-open [start,
end) step intervals during which it was observed burning) and `fire_ground_final`
(per cell, [has_burned, burnt, burning] at the horizon). At the shipped default
FF_FIREFIGHT_EXTINGUISH = FF_FIREFIGHT_FIREBREAK = 0, so nothing but burning ever
consumes fuel. Therefore, for any step t:
    BURNING  <- t lies inside one of the cell's intervals
    VIRGIN   <- no interval starts at or before t
    BURNT    <- fire_ground_final marks the cell burnt, and t is at or after the
                END of its LAST interval (burnt is absorbing: fuel is gone)
    SCORCHED <- everything else, i.e. it has burned, has fuel left, is not
                burning now. This is the category that re-ignites.

0.2 THE RECONSTRUCTION IS EXACT, NOT ASSUMED - THREE INDEPENDENT CHECKS
V1  Against the harness's live per-UAV burning bit (uav_actions[t][i][2]):
        orchestrator's implementation : 0 disagreements / 85,440 UAV-steps / 89 runs
        sub-agent A1's implementation : 0 disagreements / 291,840 UAV-steps / 304 runs
    The step alignment is step_number = uav_steps index + 1; offsets -2,-1,+1,+2
    give 5841 / 3236 / 3530 / 7128 disagreements, so the alignment is forced by
    the data, not chosen.
    WHAT V1 DOES NOT PROVE, and an earlier draft of this report got wrong. That
    bit is NOT an independent witness. In _ffr_harness.py the per-step set
    `burning_now` is built once and used BOTH to open/close burn_intervals AND
    (as `burning_cells`) to compute the uav_actions bit. So V1 establishes the
    interval encode/decode and the index offset exactly - which is what the
    off-by-one risk needed - but it says nothing about the fire model, and it
    gives NO support at all to the scorched/burnt split. That split rests
    entirely on V2 and V3 below, which are genuinely independent of it.
V2  Fuel arithmetic. Every cell whose final state is burnt must have burned for
    fuel x FIRE_SPREAD_SPEED = {7..10} x 3 = {21,24,27,30} steps. Over 349,066
    final-burnt cells in 304 runs the totals are exactly 21/24/27/30, with ONE
    systematic exception: the original ignition cell, which is constructed burning
    before step 1 and so lands one step short at {20,23,26,29} (261 cells, ~1 per
    run). No other exception exists.
V3  Absorbing-state check: no cell is ever burnt at t and burning at t' > t.

0.3 A SECOND ALIGNMENT THAT HAD TO BE PINNED DOWN (and nearly produced a
    false result here, exactly as the round brief warns)
`selected_dir` is recorded post-step. It is NOT "the direction the UAV will take
next"; it is the direction ALREADY APPLIED during the step just recorded, moving
the UAV from its previous cell to its recorded cell. Tested on 11,472 UAV-steps:
    hypothesis A (dir@i moved p[i-1] -> p[i]) : 93.3% landed on the intended cell,
                                                6.7% refused,  0.0% UNEXPLAINED
    hypothesis B (dir@i applies next step)    : 39.5% UNEXPLAINED
A is correct and accounts for every single UAV-step. Under the wrong alignment a
"tracker holds pointing off-grid" artefact appears that does not exist. Every
off-grid figure in this report uses A, and is restricted to steps on which the
UAV DEMONSTRABLY DID NOT MOVE - so it is a measured refusal, not an inference.

0.4 WHAT IS A HOOKED DECISION HERE (the project's five-times failure mode)
Used as decisions, never inferred: `uav_actions[t][i][1]`, the action label taken
straight from model.latest_execution_result['local']['uav_results'][uid]['action'];
and `partition_steps[t]['targets'][uid]`, the UAV's actual current target cell.
Used as observations: positions, selected_dir, base_state, battery.
Derived by pure arithmetic on two recorded observations (not inferred): whether a
refused move's target was off the grid.

3.1 DEFINITIONS (both reported, because they answer different questions)
  D1 CONSECUTIVE-CATEGORY RUN: a maximal run of consecutive steps during which
     the UAV is on the category. This is what the brief literally asks for.
  D2 SAME-CELL OCCUPANCY RUN: a maximal run of consecutive steps on the SAME
     CELL that touches the category at least once. This is what "parked on it"
     means, and it is the one that exposes the defect, because a pinned UAV's
     cell cycles burning -> scorched -> burning underneath it and D1 chops that
     single 27-step event into several shorter runs.
  A D2 run is further classified by the MEASURED reason the move failed:
     TRANSIT      - the cell changes every step (it is crossing)
     OFF-GRID     - the UAV did not move and its applied direction pointed off
                    the grid, so agents.py move() refused it on out_of_bounds
     UAV-BLOCKED  - the UAV did not move and the target was on-grid but occupied
                    by another UAV, so not_UAV_adjacent refused it
  Both refusal reasons are computed from two recorded quantities (position and
  the applied selected_dir); neither is inferred from where the UAV ended up.

  somewhere it went.

  And when a target does happen to be burning, it is abandoned fast: target
  change rate on the next step is 41.1% for burning targets against 13.8%
  scorched, 11.9% burnt and 7.6% virgin.

WHAT THE SEARCHER IS DOING ON EACH KIND OF GROUND (hooked action labels)
    on BURNING  ground: victim_search_hazard_retreat 525 of 854 (61%),
                        retarget_to_interior 226, escape_bfs 96
    on SCORCHED ground: retarget_to_interior 1,328 of 2,173 (61%),
                        escape_bfs 477, hazard_retreat 289
  On scorched ground the searcher is overwhelmingly doing ORDINARY SEARCH. On
  burning ground it is overwhelmingly in HAZARD RETREAT. That is the whole story
  of this round in two lines.

ANSWER: NOT TARGETED. The searcher picks burning cells as targets at one fifth of
the rate chance would give (1.18% against 5.92% available) - burning ground is
strongly and deliberately avoided at selection time. Scorched cells are picked at
0.97 of the map rate: statistically indistinguishable from indifference. The
searcher is neither drawn to nor repelled by scorched ground - which is what a
mostly-absent signal predicts (section 5: smoke marks recent scorch, nothing
marks stale scorch, and most scorched exposure is on stale ground).

Burning-ground exposure is therefore arrival, not selection: 97% of it is
single-step transit (section 3.3), and the concentrated remainder is the pin,
where the searcher is not pursuing anything - it is issuing an illegal move.

================================================================================
5. REQUIREMENT 5 - DOES THE SINGLE-BIT SENSOR MATTER?
================================================================================

The sensor limitation is real and confirmed in the pinned source.
`UAV.surrounding_states()` (agents.py:398-412) encodes each observed cell as
`int(agent.is_burning() is True)`. `_strict_victim_hazard_level`
(uav_executor.py:1844-1851) grades a cell out-of-bounds 3 / burning 2 / smoke 1 /
else 0. Sub-agents A3 and A4 enumerated every route by which ground state can
reach a UAV decision and found no channel anywhere - not the visibility model,
not the shared operational picture, not fire_runtime_model - that carries
has_burned, fuel or burnt to a UAV. As a GROUND-STATE read, scorched is
genuinely invisible.

BUT THERE IS A SECOND CHANNEL, AND AN EARLIER DRAFT OF THIS REPORT MISSED IT.
SMOKE. Smoke is attached to each Fire cell, activates once the cell has burned
and persists for `fuel` ticks after it stops (agents.py Smoke.smoke_step), and
`_strict_victim_hazard_level` grades a smoky cell as hazard level 1 - so it DOES
reach the searcher's decision, through the retreat's own filters. Measured on the
UAV's own cell, dcD + d4D:

    P(smoke | ground category)     searcher      tracker
      burning                        53.4%        57.0%
      scorched                       13.4%        17.0%
      burnt                           0.4%         0.4%
      virgin                          0.00%        0.00%       (exactly zero)

  Smoke is a ONE-WAY MARKER: it never appears on virgin ground, so smoke implies
  the cell has burned. And it decays, so it marks only RECENTLY scorched ground:

    P(smoke | scorched), by age of the scorch   searcher   tracker
      stopped burning <= 3 steps ago              28.9%     57.8%
      4-9 steps ago                               21.8%     49.2%
      10-21 steps ago                             15.1%     20.6%
      22+ steps ago                                0.6%      3.1%
      (n = 568 / 344 / 245 / 951 searcher-steps)

  So the correct statement is NOT "scorched is invisible". It is: scorched ground
  carries a partial, decaying, one-way signal via smoke, strong on freshly
  scorched ground and gone after about 20 steps; the majority of the searcher's
  scorched exposure (951 of 2,108 steps) is on ground old enough to carry no
  signal at all.

THE OBSERVABLE PREDICTION. Measured selection-time ratios: scorched 0.97, virgin
1.02 - close to indifference, which is what a mostly-absent signal predicts. And
the bit it DOES have is demonstrably in use: burning 0.20 at selection, 0.68 at
occupancy.

CONTROLLED FOR THE SPATIAL CONFOUND. Scorched ground clusters where the fire has
been, so raw ratios could hide an effect. Conditioning occupancy on the recorded
distance to the nearest burning cell (fire_dist, de-duplicated, 57 tuples):

    fire_dist band   n steps    scorched obs/exp = ratio    virgin ratio
       1-2             4,252    1,070 / 1,748  = 0.61          1.50
       3-5             3,278      302 /   384  = 0.79          1.07
       6-8             2,408      161 /   175  = 0.92          1.04
       9+             14,888      640 /   788  = 0.81          1.02

  In EVERY band the searcher is on scorched ground LESS than availability, never
  more. There is no hidden attraction that proximity was masking.

BUT THE BLINDNESS IS NOT THE PROBLEM, AND CLOSING IT WOULD NOT HELP.
  (a) UAVs are fire-immune. `_check_fire_casualties` (wildfire_model.py:5143)
      builds its fire set from is_burning() and marks only victim markers and
      firefighter markers dead - verified by reading the whole function in the
      pinned checkout; there is no UAV branch and no other UAV death path.
      Standing on scorched ground costs a UAV precisely nothing.
  (b) The scorched exposure is at 0.90 of availability - there is no excess to
      remove.
  (c) Every long stuck event happens on BURNING ground, which the searcher
      already sees perfectly well, and is caused by a control-flow fall-through
      (section 6), not by a perception gap.
  (d) The part of scorched ground that is most dangerous - freshly burned, and
      so most likely to re-ignite - is the part ALREADY signalled, by smoke, at
      29% on the searcher's own cell within 3 steps. The gap that remains is
      over old scorch, which re-ignites least and harms nobody anyway.
ANSWER TO REQUIREMENT 5: NO, the sensor limitation does not matter here, but the
reason is narrower than "the searcher is blind to scorched". It is partially
sighted - via smoke, on recent scorch - and what it cannot see is the stale
scorch that carries the least risk. The exposure is not attributable to the
sensor either way: it sits at or below availability, and the stuck events are on
burning ground the searcher sees perfectly. This also matches the
already-measured result that a scorched hazard penalty (#9-A1) was harmful, and
explains why: it would pay a real cost to avoid ground that harms nobody, on top
of a smoke signal that already covers the risky subset.

WHAT DIFFERS BETWEEN THE ROLES - THE CODE REASON
`_apply_victim_searcher_hazard_gate` (uav_executor.py:2307-2402) is reached only
by victim searchers. Its documented job is to be the "final safety pass on a
victim searcher's chosen direction". Its candidate filters are correctly
bounds-checked (`_strict_victim_hazard_level` returns 3 for out-of-bounds, so
`_retreat_to_safe_interior_direction` can never RETURN an off-grid direction).
The failure is in what happens when it can return nothing:

    uav_executor.py:2318-2321
        if <the cell I am standing on is hazardous>:
            retreat = self._retreat_to_safe_interior_direction(agent)
            if retreat is not None:
                return retreat, "victim_search_hazard_retreat"
            return chosen_dir, "victim_search_hazard_retreat"   <-- UNVALIDATED
    uav_executor.py:2399-2402  (same shape; best_dir defaults to chosen_dir)

When every in-bounds neighbour is hazardous or edge-blocked, the retreat returns
None and the gate hands back the upstream direction UNCHANGED, while stamping it
with the label `victim_search_hazard_retreat`. Sub-agent A3, reading source
independently and without seeing any of the measurements, flagged exactly this
line as "an action label that reads like a retreat and is not one". The hazard
geometry that produced the fall-through does not change from step to step, so the
same illegal direction is re-emitted every step and the UAV is pinned until the
fire around it burns out.

Trackers are immune because the gate is victim-searcher code. Its primary
dispatch call site is explicitly role-guarded (uav_executor.py:419, `elif
self._role_is_victim_searcher(role) and action != "hold"`) and its five other
call sites sit inside the victim-search planning path. This round did not
exhaustively prove every one of those five is unreachable by a tracker; what it
did establish directly is the outcome - zero off-grid refusals in 23,422
tracker-steps across both arms.

Contributing factor, identified in source by A3 and consistent with the data but
NOT isolated by this round: at VICTIM_SEARCHER_HAZARD_RETREAT_RANGE = 99 (the
shipped default) the boundary terms in the retreat scorer are zeroed, and when no
hazard is in range all four directions tie and direction 0 - EAST - wins on
first-wins ordering (uav_executor.py:2286-2288; pinned by
tests/test_victim_searcher_hazard_retreat.py:87-96). Measured: searcher direction
choice is east-biased (29.8% east against 25.0% uniform), searchers spend 11.0% of
steps in the x>=45 rim at dcD against 4.9% at dcoff, and 6 of the 10 pins are on
the east edge - including 2 under SOUTH wind, so it is not purely the fire being
blown east. Treat this as a plausible contributing mechanism, not a proven one.

WHERE THE ASYMMETRY DOES NOT HOLD - partial verdict (D)
On the berth-jam mode (section 3.5) trackers are affected slightly MORE than
searchers: 20 events / 74 burning steps against 13 / 68. "Trackers are fine" is
true of the searcher-specific livelock and false of stuck-in-a-burning-depot.

A POINTED NOTE ON THE ROUND'S OWN PREMISE - AND THE TRAP IN IT.
The brief says local hazard avoidance for searchers is "already measured
ineffective". The situation is sharper and more awkward than that. The always-on
hazard retreat that actually shipped (VICTIM_SEARCHER_HAZARD_RETREAT_RANGE = 99,
documented in common_fixed_variables.py:157-163 as an accident made deliberate)
is the PROXIMATE CAUSE of every long searcher pin in the corpus - all 113
off-grid refusals at dcD carry its label, and trackers have zero. The feature
added to keep searchers out of fire is what holds them in it.

AND YET IT IS THE BEST SETTING MEASURED. On the same 23 tuples (canonical 13 +
fresh 10), searcher steps on burning ground by retreat range:

    range 99  (always on, SHIPPED)   267 / 10,320 = 2.59%
    range 6   (local avoidance)      320 / 10,320 = 3.10%
    range 0   (edge-only gate)       356 / 10,320 = 3.45%
    (8520706 baseline, accidentally always-on: 260 / 10,320 = 2.52%)

Turning the retreat DOWN makes aggregate exposure WORSE, by up to +0.86 points.
This is consistent with the source's own note ("0 ... measured at +1.1 points of
searcher steps on a burning cell") and with the range-6 arm recovering only part
of the benefit.

So the two effects run in opposite directions: the always-on retreat cuts the
common case (the 97% single-step transits) and creates the rare catastrophic case
(the boundary pin). THE FIX MUST THEREFORE NOT BE "TURN THE RETREAT DOWN" - that
is a third already-measured-harmful option, alongside the two the brief names. It
must keep the retreat's aggregate benefit and remove only its failure mode, which
is exactly what a legality guard on the fall-through does.

LIMITS OF THIS ROUND - what is NOT established
================================================================================
 1. WHICH of the two fall-through returns (:2321 or :2402) fires on a given
    pinned step is DERIVED from the code plus the recorded neighbour states, not
    hooked. For the observed pins every in-bounds neighbour is burning or
    edge-blocked, which forces the retreat to None on both paths, so the
    conclusion does not depend on which - but a fix round should hook the two
    return sites directly rather than inherit this derivation. One instrumented
    run on seed 404 south would settle it.
 2. The east tie-break (section 6) is a plausible contributing mechanism
    identified in source and consistent with three measurements; it has not been
    isolated, and this round does not claim it as a cause.
 3. The cost analysis is underpowered (section 7) and cannot rule out a real
    outcome cost.
 4. Cross-commit comparison against 8520706 is confounded by everything shipped
    between (base station, dock fix, latch 808, dimension fix, base-mark). All
    causal claims in section 2 use the same-commit dcoff/dcD pair instead, where
    the fires are bit-identical.
 5. Only east and south winds exist in the corpus. West and north are unmeasured,
    and the east tie-break makes a west-wind check genuinely informative.
 6. Arms with firefighting ON (fm*, f3GATE, f2c*) are excluded from every
    category figure, because firefighter fuel writes break the burnt/scorched
    derivation of section 0.1.
 7. PROVENANCE OF THE COVERAGE RESULT, which is what makes the verdict (A)
    rather than (B). The mean-instantaneous-coverage correlations in section 7
    come from a second implementation, not from _sf_analyze.py; what I
    re-implemented independently is the searcher-footprint measure (partial
    -0.275, p=0.068), which agrees in sign and magnitude. The two are not fully
    independent - both replay the same recorded positions - so treat them as one
    finding confirmed twice, not two findings. It is also one significant result
    among ~150 correlation tests; it replicates across three pools and four
    metrics, which is why I stand behind it, but a fix round should re-measure
    coverage as a pre-registered gate item rather than inherit this number.
