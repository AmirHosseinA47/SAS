HEADLINE
================================================================================
THE LATCH IS GONE, CATEGORICALLY - not merely in the seed that motivated it.

Across the 18-run sample, route_blocked fired 15 times. Every single firing now
ends in one of two ways:
    3 recovered  (the unit became dispatchable again)

1. WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED (Part 3)
================================================================================
Scope held exactly as agreed. wildfire_model.py ONLY. Untouched: the unassign
branch, agents.py (the trigger, out of scope), rescue_planner.py,
adaptation_manager.py, rescue_executor.py.

PIECE 1 - new method _revalidate_route_blocked_firefighters(), placed beside
_try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims (wildfire_model.py:2139). For every
firefighter marker it:
    skips unless marker.status == "route_blocked"
    skips if dead / assigned / exiting / pos is None
    skips if TRAPPED - every in-bounds neighbour cell holds active fire. This is
      the same condition agents.py uses to raise the flag via `if not scored`,
      and it is the guard that keeps a doomed unit from testing "reachable" at
      distance zero against a victim in its own cell (section 5 explains why
      this was added).
    tests marker._path_exists_avoiding_fire(unit_cell, victim_cell, fire_cells)
      against the LIVE cell of every victim for which _victim_needs_rescue is
      True - the identical BFS the trigger fix validated, so the recheck cannot
      disagree with the trigger about what "reachable" means
    leaves the flag SET when nothing is reachable
    otherwise sets marker.status = "available" and calls
      _sync_firefighter_operational_knowledge([ff_id])
  and, if anything was cleared, calls _try_dispatch_unresolved_confirmed_victims()
  once - without which the clear is cosmetic, because dispatch in this model is
  purely incident-driven and nothing periodically looks for idle units.

PIECE 2 - one-line hook at the top of the existing per-step post-move stage
_sync_firefighter_marker_status() (wildfire_model.py:4188). Deliberately NOT a
new AdaptationManager stage: tests/test_adaptation_manager_cycle.py:219 asserts
model.call_log == list(POST_MOVE_STAGE_ORDER) by exact equality, so a new stage
would have been a NEW test failure. The placement is also correct on the merits -
it runs after _check_fire_casualties and after both _process_rescue_incidents
passes, so the replacement pairing caused by a unit's own failure has already
been made before that unit is ever reconsidered. That ordering, not a timer, is
what preserves the anti-self-replacement intent documented in Part 1 section 2.

NO COOLDOWN, as agreed in the Part 2 revision. The live BFS plus the trapped
guard already refuse to clear a unit whose situation has not actually improved.

Incidental: the patch script initially rewrote the file from CRLF to LF while the
rest of the tree is CRLF (core.autocrlf=true). Caught and restored; the committed
diff is 80 insertions, 0 deletions, and no line-ending churn.

      seed 808  step  40 ff_unit_0 blocked -> RECOVERED step  42  (+2)

      seed 333  step  45 ff_unit_1 blocked -> RECOVERED step  45  (+0, same step)

    FATE OF ALL 15 FIRINGS - the decisive table:
      3 recovered.
      12 died 0-8 steps after firing, so no recovery was possible:
         101 +0, 202 +3, 303 +8, 404 +1, 505 +2, 606 +6, 808/ff1 +3, 909 +0,
         333/ff0 +3, 333/ff1(2nd) +0, south101 +8, south404 +0.
      0 alive-but-latched.
    The twelve non-recoveries are the fix being correctly conservative, not
    under-firing: each of those units was dead within eight steps.

    Note that recovery is not the same as benefit. In seeds 808 and 222 the
    recovered unit was returned to the pool but no confirmed victim was free to
    give it (both had just been assigned to the other firefighter), and it died
    3 steps later. Outcomes there are unchanged - correctly so, since no
    dispatch followed and status alone does not move an agent. Only east/333
    converted a recovery into a rescue.

5b. OVERCORRECTION - units re-dispatched too early into an unsafe route.
    Measured at the moment of every successful assign, with the same live BFS:
    is the newly assigned target reachable from the unit's cell right now?

      total assigns, 18 runs                            : 70
      assigns into an ALREADY-BLOCKED route             :  1
      of which by a unit that had previously recovered  :  0   <-- the gate

    The single unreachable-at-assign dispatch is east/333 step 44, ff_unit_1 ->
    victim_0 at (49,29), reason=initial, after_recovery=False. That is the
    PRE-EXISTING dispatch that CAUSED the original latch - it occurs identically
    at f2827ed - not something this fix introduced.
    ff_unit_1's later step-136 dispatch, the one it eventually died on, WAS
    reachable at assign time; the route closed afterwards as the fire advanced.
    So the unit was not sent somewhere hopeless: the world changed under it.

    The residual risk flagged in the Part 2 design - that clearing proves a path
    to SOME victim while the planner may pick a different one - did not
    materialise anywhere in 18 runs. It remains theoretically possible and is
    the thing to re-measure if this fix is ever extended.

6. THE ff_deaths TRADEOFF, STATED EXPLICITLY
================================================================================
ff_deaths 14 -> 15. The entire increase is one unit: east/333 ff_unit_1.

  at f2827ed : latched at step 45, sat unharmed at (1,25) for 195 steps, never
               dispatched again, survived to the end of the run. victim_1 and
               victim_3 both burned to death while reachable from its cell.
               -> rescued 1, victims dead 3, ff_deaths 1
  with the fix: recovered at step 45, rescued victim_3 at step 96, took victim_1
               at step 136 on a route that was open when it was assigned, and
               was overrun at step 153 when the fire closed it.
               -> rescued 2, victims dead 2, ff_deaths 2

This is precisely the distinction the brief drew: a unit that recovers, saves
someone, and then dies trying to save a second person is not the same failure as
a unit that never tries. The trade on this seed is one firefighter's life for
one victim's life plus one additional rescue. Whether that trade is desirable is
a mission-policy question, not a code-correctness question, and it is flagged
here rather than buried.

There is no evidence of a broader safety regression: ff_deaths are unchanged on
all 17 other seeds, and no recovered unit was ever dispatched into a route that
was already blocked.

8. A CORRECTION TO MY OWN MEASUREMENT
================================================================================
The first campaign harness inferred route_blocked firings and recoveries by
sampling each firefighter's status once per step. That undercounts, because a
block-and-recover that completes inside a single step is invisible to it - and
that is the fix's FASTEST path, exactly what happens to east/333 ff_unit_1 at
step 45. The first pass reported 10 fires / 2 recoveries.
The campaign was re-run with hooks placed directly on _mark_route_blocked and on
the revalidation pass, giving the exact 15 fires / 3 recoveries used throughout
this report. All 18 outcome triples were bit-identical across the two runs,
which confirms the instrumentation is pure passthrough and the results
reproducible. The outcome numbers were never affected - they come from
_build_evaluation, not from the sampler.

COST: the revalidation pass was called 4320 times across the 18 runs and found a
route_blocked unit present on only 41 of those calls (0.95%). The other 99.05%
return at the first guard having done nothing. The BFS runs only for a blocked,
untrapped unit against victims still needing rescue.

9. DEFECTS FOUND AND DELIBERATELY NOT FIXED (out of this round's scope)
================================================================================
Both are recorded in outputs/rblatch_part1.txt section 8d with evidence.

  (a) ff_marker.target_pos IS NEVER REFRESHED. It is written with a real value in
      exactly one place, wildfire_model.py:3348 at assign time, and otherwise only
      set to None. The trigger's BFS therefore tests a path to the cell the victim
      occupied AT DISPATCH. In east/333 ff_unit_0 carried target_pos=(40,25) for
      twenty steps after leaving it. This is trigger-side and was excluded by the
      brief. The consumer-side recheck implemented here deliberately BFSes to live
      victim positions, so it does not inherit the staleness.

  (b) UNASSIGNING AN EXITING UNIT DROPS THE VICTIM IT IS CARRYING
      (rescued_victim=None, exiting=False at :3443-3444). In east/333 that dropped
      victim_0 three steps before both it and ff_unit_0 died. The trigger fix's own
      comment says the replacement pathway is the wrong response for a unit
      carrying a victim, but the "nowhere to step" path still routes exiting units
      into it. Worth its own round.
