VERDICT: (H) HOLD-CAUSED.

The target-hold added by fix14 Part 3 is the sole cause. With the hold
neutralized on the same tree, seed 303 rescues 4 and detects every victim; with
it enabled, the same seed rescues 2 and loses victim_1 and victim_2 to
never_detected. This is not seed-specific bad luck: the hold's segment lengths
on A/south are an order of magnitude longer than the churn it was designed
against, on all five seeds. Seed 303 is where that cost happens to land on
victims rather than on empty ground.

3. THE MECHANISM ON SEED 303

   Searcher dwell by 10x10 block over 240 steps (x_block, y_block):

     hold   : (1,1)=92  (2,2)=40  (3,2)=23  (4,3)=20  (0,1)=17  (2,1)=16
              (1,0)=14  (4,2)=9   (1,2)=8   (2,0)=1
     nohold : (2,3)=74  (3,2)=59  (2,4)=38  (2,2)=32  (1,3)=18  (1,2)=10  (1,3)=9

   The hold arm spends 92 of 240 steps inside x 10-19, y 10-19 and never works
   the northern half. The nohold arm works the y 20-49 band, which is where
   victim_1 (32,46) and victim_2 (13,34) actually are.

   The hold segments that produce this (init = Manhattan distance to target when
   the hold was issued, best = closest it ever got, closed = fraction of the gap
   closed before release or end of run):

       steps      n   target    init  best  closed
         2- 27   26   ( 8, 11)   32     9    71.9%
        29- 40   12   ( 8, 45)   43    43     0.0%
        42- 84   43   ( 8, 45)   48    33    31.2%
        86-130   45   (41, 45)   67    54    19.4%
       132-178   47   (41, 45)   59    40    32.2%
       180-240   61   (41, 45)   39     9    76.9%

   Six holds over 240 steps; 234 of 240 steps were spent under an active hold.
   Release reasons for the whole run: {'unsafe': 1, 'stalled': 4} - ARRIVAL NEVER
   FIRED ONCE. The searcher was continuously committed to a target it never
   reached.

   Why the release conditions do not bite:
     - timeout: budget = COMMIT_TIMEOUT_MULTIPLIER (2.0) x initial Manhattan
       distance. With init 39-67 the budget is 78-134 steps, longer than any of
       these segments and comparable to half the run. It never fires.
     - stalled: COMMIT_PROGRESS_WINDOW = 12 consecutive non-closing steps, and
       commit_no_progress RESETS on any single step that beats the best distance
       so far. A searcher crawling toward a distant corner beats its best
       distance every few steps, so the stall counter is repeatedly reset;
       observed commit_no_progress sat at 11 (one short of release) for long
       stretches of segments 2-5.
     - arrival: needs to be within COMMIT_ARRIVAL_DIST 2.0 of the target, which
       never happens on this run.

   So on this run the hold behaves as an open-ended commitment: it picks a far
   corner (8,45) then (41,45), pins the searcher to it for 43-61 steps at a
   time, closes 19-32% of the gap, is released for stalling, and then re-commits
   to the SAME corner. From step 86 to the end of the run the target is (41,45)
   continuously - 155 steps aimed at the far north-east corner - while victim_2
   sits at (13,34) in the west the searcher has just left, and victim_1 at
   (32,46) is approached only at the very end, too late (terminal=210).


4. IS THIS SEED-SPECIFIC, OR A GENERAL COST ON THIS COMBO?

   General. The hold's own design note (local_adaptation_generator.py:1000-1015)
   calibrated against measured churn of 42-61 target segments per 240 steps with
   mean holds of 3.3-5.7 steps. On A/south the hold produces:

     seed  segs  mean hold  max hold  release reasons                arrivals
      101    4     59.0       170     arrival 1, stalled 2              1
      202    4     59.0        82     arrival 2, unsafe 1               2
      303    6     39.0        61     unsafe 1, stalled 4               0
      404    3     79.0       141     arrival 1, stalled 1              1
      505    4     59.0       102     unsafe 1, arrival 2               2

   Mean hold length on this combo is 39-79 steps, roughly 10x the 3.3-5.7 the
   mechanism was sized for, and 3 of 5 seeds contain a single hold longer than
   100 steps. Only 6 of the 21 holds issued across the five seeds ended in
   arrival. The low-closure tail is visible on other seeds too - seed 404 holds
   (41,45) for 141 steps and closes 2.8% of the gap; seed 505 holds (30,45) for
   102 steps and closes 16.3%; seed 101 holds (41,45) for 170 steps.

   Corroborating aggregate: acted-target changes over the run drop from 41-68
   (nohold) to 11-47 (hold), and the searcher's coverage becomes more contiguous
   but narrower - on seed 303 obs_frac_all falls from 0.740 (nohold) to 0.648
   (hold) even though the searcher's own obs_frac RISES from 0.437 to 0.552. The
   hold makes the searcher sweep a tighter, more coherent region, and on this
   combo that region overlaps ground the other UAVs already cover.

   What makes 303 the seed that pays: its two northern/western victims (32,46)
   and (13,34) sit outside the south-west block the searcher is pinned to for
   the first 130 steps, and the run terminates at 210. On the other four seeds
   the same oversized holds happen to run through or near the victims (or the
   victims are found by other UAVs), so the cost shows up as slower coverage
   rather than as a miss. Seeds 404 and 505 in fact GAIN a rescue under the hold
   (1->2 and 3->4), which is why A/south nets only -1 against fix13 rather than
   more.

   This is the hold's declared tradeoff - completion rate against exploration
   breadth - landing badly on a combo where the coverage argmax repeatedly
   selects unreachable far-corner targets.
